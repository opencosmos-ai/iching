# 咎 — `blame`

**100 times in the judgments and line texts, 93 of them in one phrase.** 无咎, "no blame", occurs 92
times, and 13.1 writes it 無咎. That makes it the most repeated phrase in the book. The rest are
无大咎 twice (18.3, 44.3, "no great blame"), 何咎 twice (17.4, 38.5, "what blame?"), 匪咎 (14.1, "it is
not blame"), 為咎 (43.1, "makes for blame") and 何其咎 (9.1, "what blame is there in it?"). Every one
of them reads with *blame*.

**Its place in the set.** 繫辭上 ch 3 gives it a tier of its own, after 吉凶 (losing and getting)
and 悔吝 (small blemishes): 无咎者，善補過也, "no blame is being good at mending a fault." The same
chapter has 震无咎者存乎悔, "what rouses one to no blame lies in regret." So 咎 is not the
outcome and not the flaw. It is **the accounting**: what you would be held to for a fault (過) left
unmended. See [`WORKLIST-CLOSED.md` § A4](../WORKLIST-CLOSED.md#a4--the-verdict-vocabulary--done).

## The evidence, and the fork in it

**The graph and two witnesses read harm.** 說文: 災也。从人从各。各者，相違也, "calamity. From 人
and 各; 各 is being at odds." Across the 56 lines whose only verdict graph is 咎, McClatchie
writes *injury* 19 times, *danger* 4, *detriment*, *harm* and *calamity* once each, against
*blame* 20 and *error* 4. De Harlez writes *sans dommage* and *évitera les maux*, or paraphrases
(*c'est bien*), or drops it: 41 of his 52 have none of *faute*, *faillir*, *blâme*, *erreur*,
*mal* or *reproche*. Legge reads conduct: *no error* 45 of 54, *blame* 4, *mistake* 3.

**The core text settles the fork inside itself, and the Wings agree.** Three lines keep 咎 apart
from harm. In 28.6 and 47.2 the outcome is 凶 and there is still no 咎, and the 小象傳 at 28.6 says
過涉之凶，不可咎也, "the ominousness of wading over cannot be blamed." 14.1 sets the two words side
by side: 无交害，匪咎, "no dealings with harm; it is not blame." So 咎 is not the outcome. Under the
lens, the Wings' 善補過 leads. The harm reading is named here and set aside, and
[`divergence-stays-open`](../method.md#6-the-principles) keeps it in the notes at 14.1.

## The ruling — `blame`, Shalom's call, 2026-10-20

**Two English words fit every line: *blame* and *fault*.** *No fault* and *who would fault him?*
(又誰咎也, the 小象傳 at 13.1, 40.3 and 60.3) are as natural as *no blame* and *who would blame him?*
The choice is not made by the lines. It is made by the set, and by the other book.

**For *blame*: the core text sets 咎 against 譽.** 2.4 括囊，无咎无譽 ("tying up the sack: no blame,
no praise") and 28.5 无咎无譽 put 咎 opposite 譽 (說文: 稱也, "to praise"). *Blame* and *praise* is
English's own pair, and where the Chinese sets two words as opposites the English may follow
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-polarity-is-in-the-lines)). *Fault* is not the opposite of praise.
*Blame* also leaves *fault* free for 過 in the Wings' moral sense (補過, "mending faults"; 有過則改,
"having faults, change them", 42's 大象), so that you commit a fault and, if you mend it, there is
no blame. And the 64 readings already say *no blame*, 211 times.

**The Tao Te Ching keeps its own usage, and the difference is deliberate.** It renders 咎 *fault*
(ch 9, 自遺其咎; ch 46, 咎莫大於欲得) and 尤 *blame* (ch 8, 無尤). That was the case for *fault*
here. Shalom's ruling: **the Laozi is a different context, so it may use the word differently.**
The parent's entries for 咎 and 尤 carry `binds: [taoteching]`, so their locks stay in that book and
nothing binds across the two. A reader comparing them will find 咎 *blame* here and *fault* there,
and both are intended.

**What it leaves inside this book: 尤 needs a word that is not *blame*.** 尤 occurs six times in the
小象傳: 終无尤也 ("in the end, no 尤") at 22, 23, 39, 50 and 56, and 中无尤也 at 26. Inside one
book, two graphs cannot share *blame*. *Fault* is wanted for 過, so it cannot take 26's present
gloss "no fault" either. *Reproach* is unclaimed. The question is decided when the Wings are
rendered, not here.

## What is ruled out, and why

- **"error", "mistake"** (Legge) name the going wrong, which is 過, and not what you are held to.
  繫辭上 ch 3 separates the two in one clause, 善補過.
- **"injury", "harm", "detriment"** (McClatchie) give the outcome reading that 28.6, 47.2 and 14.1
  refuse. *Harm* is also 害's word, and 害 stands in 14.1 beside 咎.
- **None of them is on the `forbidden:` list.** A verdict entry binds every hexagram that cites it,
  and 過 and 害 may need these words in the same files.

## The seam

**无 and 無.** 13.1 writes 無咎 where the other 92 write 无咎. 王弼's text writes 无咎 there, so the
variant is probably the transcription's ([`WORKLIST.md`](../WORKLIST.md) B10). Either way, the
parent's own 無 flexes to a plain *no* (ch 8 無尤, "no blame"; ch 10 無疵, "without blemish"), so
*no blame* holds however A11 rules. **The phrase does not wait on A11.**
