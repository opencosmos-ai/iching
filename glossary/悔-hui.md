# 悔 — `regret`

**34 times in the judgments and line texts, and most often as it leaves.** 悔亡, "regret goes," occurs
19 times. Then 无悔 5 times and 13.6's 無悔 once ("no regret"), 有悔 4 ("there is regret"), 无袛悔 once
(24.1), and 悔 on its own four times (16.3, 37.3, 47.6, 50.3). It comes with 吉 in 12 lines, as in
貞吉悔亡 (31.4, 34.4, 57.5): the regret goes, and the way ahead is auspicious.

**Its place in the set.** 繫辭上 pairs it with 吝 twice: 悔吝者，憂虞之象也, "悔 and 吝 are the images
of worry and apprehension" (ch 2), and 悔吝者，言乎其小疵也, "悔 and 吝 speak of small blemishes"
(ch 3). The same chapter gives 悔 a direction, 震无咎者存乎悔: "what rouses one to no blame lies in
regret." **悔 is a small trouble that faces toward mending.** Its English has to be small, inward,
and distinct from 吝's. See [`WORKLIST-CLOSED.md` § A4](../WORKLIST-CLOSED.md#a4--the-verdict-vocabulary--done).

## The evidence

**The graph.** 說文: 悔恨也。从心𣫭聲, "rue; from 心 (the heart), sounded 𣫭." It is a feeling, and the
heart radical says where it sits.

**The witnesses converge, with one exception, and the exception is a property of its word.**

| | renders 悔 as |
|---|---|
| Legge | *repentance*, in all 32 lines aligned; 悔亡 is always "occasion for repentance will disappear" |
| McClatchie | *regret*, in all 31 legible; 悔亡 is "no regret ensues", "the absence of regret" |
| de Harlez | *regret* 7, *repentir* 3, of 13 |

## The ruling — `regret`, Shalom's call, 2026-10-20

**Regret is the convergent word, and nothing in either book holds it.** The Tao Te Ching translation
never uses *regret*, and the 64 readings already do, 80 times, with 悔亡 as *regret goes* 36 times. It
is the right size for 小疵, a small blemish. It is a feeling, which is where 說文 puts 悔. And it can
go, which 悔亡 needs 19 times.

**悔亡 is "regret goes"** while 亡 is unruled. 亡 is also *lose* and *flee* elsewhere in the core (56.5
一矢亡, "one arrow lost"), and the formula takes 亡's English when that is decided.

## What is ruled out, and why

- **"repentance"** imports penitence: a turning from sin, with confession and absolution behind it.
  It makes a 小疵 into a sin, and it brings a theology the Chinese lacks. Legge uses it 32 times out
  of 32.
- **"remorse"** is the guilt of a grave wrong. It is far heavier than the Wings' small blemish.
- **Both are on the `forbidden:` list.** Neither word is the plain English for any neighbouring
  graph, so barring them from every file that cites this entry costs nothing.

## The seam

**24.1 无袛悔** turns on 袛, which this entry does not rule. 24's reading takes the phrase as "it
does not come to regret," with 王弼's 幾悔而反 ("on the edge of regret, it turns back"). That is a
reading of 袛, not of 悔, and it is decided in A8 with 24's line texts. *Regret* holds either way.
