# 吝 — `constriction`

**20 times in the judgments and line texts, the rarest of the six, and the hardest.** 往吝 ("going on:
constriction") 3 times, counting 4.1's 以往吝. Then 貞吝 4 times, 小吝 twice (21.3, 45.3, "a little
constriction"), 終吝 once (37.3, "in the end, constriction"), 往見吝 once (18.4, "going on, you meet
constriction"), and 吝 on its own 9 times.

**Its place in the set.** It is 悔's partner in 繫辭上: both are images of worry and apprehension
(憂虞, ch 2), and both speak of small blemishes (小疵, ch 3). So the set constrains it twice over. It
must be as small as *regret*, and it must not be *regret*. See [`悔-hui.md`](悔-hui.md) and
[`WORKLIST-CLOSED.md` § A4](../WORKLIST-CLOSED.md#a4--the-verdict-vocabulary--done).

## The evidence — two strands, and one word for both

**The Wings read the place running out.** The 小象傳 glosses 吝 with 窮 ("to run out, to come to the
end with no way on") three times: 往吝窮也 (3.3, "going on, constriction: it runs out"), 上窮吝也
(44.6, "at the top it runs out: constriction"), and 志窮也 (57.3, "the will runs out").

**王弼 reads the heart narrowing.** His notes gloss it as narrowness of heart, 用心褊狹 (13.2, "using
the heart narrowly") and 用心不弘 (28.4, "using the heart not broadly"). They call it base or
petty, 鄙 (4.4, 20.1, 32.3) and 賤 (31.3, 35.6, 40.3). And at 64.1 they call it obstinacy that
turns back only once it is hemmed in.

**The dictionary and 說卦 read a holding back.** 說文: 恨惜也。从口文聲。易曰：以往吝,
"rueful grudging; from 口, sounded 文. The Changes says: 以往吝." Xu Shen quotes this book's 4.1
under the entry. 說卦 ch 11 lists among earth's attributes 為吝嗇, "it is stinginess."

**The three translators split three ways, and each split lands on another member's word.**

| | renders 吝 as | and the trouble with it |
|---|---|---|
| Legge | *regret*, in 19 of 20; *stingy* at 22.5 | *regret* is 悔's. Legge keeps them apart only by making 悔 *repentance* |
| McClatchie | *shame* or *disgrace*, in all 20 | *shame* is 羞's. 32.3 has both: 或承之羞，貞吝, and he writes *shame* twice |
| de Harlez | *honte* 3, *blâme* 1, *regret* 1 | both of the above, and 咎's word besides |

## The ruling — `constriction`, Shalom's call, 2026-10-20

**It is one word for both strands.** The place narrows until it runs out (窮), and the heart narrows
(褊狹). *A constricted spirit* is ordinary English, and so is a constricted passage. It also takes
in 說文's holding back, since a grudging hand is a closed one.

**It reads as a verdict.** Constriction happens to you, so it can stand after the colon every time:
往吝, "going on: constriction"; 終吝, "in the end, constriction"; 小吝，无咎, "a little constriction;
no blame." *Narrowness* was the other candidate, and it is Wang Bi's own word. Shalom set it aside
because it reads like a trait, and a verdict is not a trait.

**It fits the book's bodily images.** At 21.3 the biter meets poison (遇毒，小吝), at 31.3 the touch is
in the thigh, and at 44.6 the meeting is at the horns.

**It collides with nothing.** It is unused in both books.

## What is ruled out, and why

- **"stinting"**, the working gloss until 2026-10-20 and the only word on the `forbidden:` list. It
  carries the stinginess of 吝嗇 and nothing of 窮. So where 吝 is something met rather than done
  (4.4 困蒙, 47.4 困于金車, 64.1 濡其尾, 18.4 往見吝), it names a behaviour the line does not
  describe. No other graph wants the word, so barring it costs nothing.
- **"regret"** is 悔's, and the Wings set 悔吝 as two.
- **"shame", "humiliation"**: *shame* is 羞's, inside 32.3's own line, and *humiliation* is its
  heavier synonym.
- **"restriction", "constraint"** sit on 節 (60). Its proposed name is *Restraint*, and the Chinese
  treats the two in opposite ways: 安節，亨 (60.4) and 甘節，吉 (60.5) praise 節, and 吝 is a blemish.
  Both words also put the limit outside the person, where Wang Bi puts it inside.
- **"distress", "trouble", "impasse"** spend words the 64 names need
  ([`already-spoken-for`](../method.md#6-the-principles)).
- **"meanness"** is exactly 鄙 in its old sense of small, poor and grudging. Its modern sense is
  *unkind*, and that misleads.
- **"chagrin"** is 恨, the feeling. It loses both strands, and beside *regret* it reads as the same
  feeling twice.
- **"falling short"** is 窮 without the heart, and it is a phrase that 小吝 and 往見吝 would have to
  bend around.
- **None of these but *stinting* is on the `forbidden:` list.** They are the plain English of graphs
  that stand in the same lines as 吝 (羞 at 32.3) or will want them later (節).

## The seam

**At 4.4 and 47.4, 吝 follows 困**, whose proposed name is *Hemmed In*: 困蒙，吝 and 困于金車，吝.
"Hemmed in…: constriction" comes close to saying one thing twice. The Chinese has the same
closeness, because the 小象 itself glosses 吝 with 窮. The notes at 4 and 47 should say that the
second word is the verdict on the first.
