## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **Thunder** (震 · yang · moving out).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake and thunder ↔ wind, both yang.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 小過，小者過而亨也 (*xiǎo guò, xiǎo zhě guò ér hēng yě*), "小過: the small go over, and there is 亨 (*hēng*, its English pending)"; 有飛鳥之象焉 (*yǒu fēi niǎo zhī xiàng yān*), "there is the image of a flying bird in it"; 上逆而下順也 (*shàng nì ér xià shùn yě*), "going up is against the grain, going down goes along." The 大象 (*dà xiàng*): 山上有雷，小過；君子以行過乎恭，喪過乎哀，用過乎儉 (*shān shàng yǒu léi, xiǎo guò; jūnzǐ yǐ xíng guò hū gōng, sāng guò hū āi, yòng guò hū jiǎn*), "thunder above the mountain: 小過; in conduct the noble one goes over into respect, in mourning over into grief, in spending over into thrift." The tradition reads an excess allowed only in the downward, lesser direction.

**What the polarity implies.** Both trigrams are yang, and a yang trigram, 繫辭下 (*xìcí xià*) says, is 陽一君而二民 (*yáng yī jūn ér èr mín*), "one ruler and two subjects" — one firm line among open ones. Two yang trigrams therefore build a figure of four open lines and two firm: `001100`. 28 大過 (*dà guò*) is the mirror case: two yin trigrams, wind and lake, building four firm lines around two open ones. The great exceed there because yin trigrams are made of firm lines; the small exceed here because yang trigrams are made of open ones. Each pole, doubled across two spectrums, produces its opposite in excess. The firm lines of mountain (its top) and thunder (its base) press together at the centre — a body with open lines spread either side, which is the 彖's bird. Stopping within, moving out without: a sound passes over a height that does not move. The structure suggests the figure's measure is that the mountain lets thunder pass over, not rise. The lines keep to it: 過其祖，遇其妣 (*guò qí zǔ, yù qí bǐ*), "he passes his grandfather and meets his grandmother" — a going-over that lands a step lower — is without fault; 弗遇過之，飛鳥離之 (*fú yù guò zhī, fēi niǎo lí zhī*), "he does not meet it but goes past it; the flying bird is netted", is ominous. Where 28's ridgepole bends under too much that is firm, 62's bird fails by climbing on too little.

**For the name.** *Small Exceeding* — the signature supports it and ties it harder to 28: the two figures are the two ways a pole yields its opposite line in excess, so 大 (*dà*, "great") and 小 (*xiǎo*, "small") must stay one pair of words. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Small Overstepping* — survives: 過其祖 "oversteps his grandfather", 弗過防之 (*fú guò fáng zhī*) "he does not overstep, but guards against it" — only if 28 becomes *Great Overstepping*; *Flying Low* — fails the lines: supplied by the 彖's bird, it drops 過 (*guò*), the verb four lines turn on.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A time for small things to go a little over, and only downward: the bird that keeps low lives,
the bird that climbs is netted.** The judgment says it outright — 可小事，不可大事 (*kě xiǎo shì, bù
kě dà shì*, "small affairs can be done; great affairs cannot") — and then gives the bird. 說文
(*shuōwén*) has 過 (*guò*) as 度也 (*dù yě*, "to pass across"), walking (辵, *chuò*) for its sense.
The lines turn that one verb against another, 遇 (*yù*, "to meet"), four times: pass by, or meet.
Where a line passes and then meets, it is safe; where it passes and meets nothing, it falls.
*Small Exceeding* (proposed).

### The polarities in it

The mountain within stops at its closed top; thunder without moves out from its firm base. Their
firm lines press together in the middle of `001100`, and the open lines spread either side — the
彖 (*tuàn*)'s 有飛鳥之象焉 (*yǒu fēi niǎo zhī xiàng yān*, "there is the image of a flying bird in
it"): a body of two firm lines, two wings of two open ones. **Here the structure and the text agree
closely.** The wingtips, lines 1 and 6, are the two ominous lines, both flying. The centres, lines
2 and 5, are soft, and the 彖 draws the judgment from that: 柔得中，是以小事吉也 (*róu dé zhōng, shì yǐ
xiǎo shì jí yě*, "the soft hold the centres, so small affairs are auspicious"). The firm lines sit
at the seam, out of the centre, and both are warned.

### In nature

Thunder over a mountain is a summer afternoon's weather. Air driven up a slope builds cloud over
the ridges, and the storm breaks there first; in the valley you hear it pass along the heights
before any rain arrives. The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees it plainly: 山上有雷 (*shān shàng yǒu léi*,
"thunder above the mountain"). The mountain does not move; the sound goes over it.

Watch birds before such a storm. Swallows and martins drop and feed close over fields and water,
because the insects they hunt have come down in the heavy air. A bird caught high in a thunderhead
is in real danger; birds that come through a storm are the ones that went low and sheltered. That
is the judgment's image exactly: 飛鳥遺之音，不宜上宜下 (*fēi niǎo yí zhī yīn, bù yí shàng yí xià*,
"the flying bird leaves its sound behind: not fitting to go up, fitting to go down"). Anyone who
has heard geese pass over at night knows the first half — the sound falls to the ground from a
bird you never see.

The fifth line adds weather that promises and does not give: 密云不雨，自我西郊 (*mì yún bù yǔ, zì wǒ xī
jiāo*, "dense clouds, no rain, from our western outskirts") — the same words as the judgment of
hexagram 9. Heaped cloud that builds all afternoon and drifts off dry is a common sight in a dry
season; the text leaves it at that.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 小過：亨。利貞。可小事，不可大事。飛鳥遺之音，不宜上宜下，大吉 (*xiǎo guò: hēng. lì
zhēn. kě xiǎo shì, bù kě dà shì. fēi niǎo yí zhī yīn, bù yí shàng yí xià, dà jí*) — "Small
exceeding: it gets through. Advantageous to hold firm. Small affairs can be done, not great ones.
The flying bird leaves its sound: not up, but down — greatly auspicious." The 彖 reads the name as
小者過 (*xiǎo zhě guò*, "the small go over"): the small are the subject, not the size of the fault.

**Line 1.** 飛鳥以凶 (*fēi niǎo yǐ xiōng*, "a flying bird — and so, ominous"). The lowest line is
already in the air. 王弼: 進而之逆，无所錯足 (*jìn ér zhī nì, wú suǒ cuò zú*, "going forward into what
runs against it, with nowhere to set its feet").

**Line 2** passes and meets. 過其祖，遇其妣；不及其君，遇其臣；无咎 (*guò qí zǔ, yù qí bǐ; bù jí qí jūn, yù
qí chén; wú jiù*, "passing the grandfather, meeting the grandmother; not reaching the ruler,
meeting the minister — no blame"). 說文 has 妣 (*bǐ*) as 殁母也 (*mò mǔ yě*, "a mother who has died").
王弼 reads it as going over and landing one step lower: 過而不至於僣 (*guò ér bù zhì yú jiàn*, "going
over, but not as far as usurping"). de Harlez reads a proverb instead — avoid one thing and you meet
its like. Both readings survive the words.

**Line 3** will not pass. 弗過防之，從或戕之，凶 (*fú guò fáng zhī, cóng huò qiāng zhī, xiōng*, "not
passing, guarding against it; following, someone may strike them down — ominous"). The firm line
at the top of the mountain stands on guard, and harm comes from behind.

**Line 4** meets without passing. 无咎，弗過遇之。往厲必戒，勿用永貞 (*wú jiù, fú guò yù zhī. wǎng lì bì
jiè, wù yòng yǒng zhēn*, "no blame: not passing, it meets. Going on is peril — be on guard. Do not
hold firm for good"). The counsel is local and temporary: hold here, and do not make holding a
rule.

**Line 5** takes the small thing. 公弋取彼在穴 (*gōng yì qǔ bǐ zài xué*, "the lord, with a corded
arrow, takes that one in its hole"). 弋 is the fowler's weapon; 王弼 reads 在穴者隱伏之物也 (*zài xué
zhě yǐn fú zhī wù yě*, "what is in the hole is a thing hidden and lying low"). The soft centre acts
small, and does not wait for the rain.

**Line 6** passes and meets nothing. 弗遇過之，飛鳥離之，凶，是謂災眚 (*fú yù guò zhī, fēi niǎo lí zhī,
xiōng, shì wèi zāi shěng*, "meeting nothing, it passes on; the flying bird is netted — ominous;
this is called disaster and blight"). 說文 gives 眚 (*shěng*) as 目病生翳也 (*mù bìng shēng yì yě*, "an
eye disease, a film growing over it"): a blight that begins in the one who cannot see. The 小象
(*xiǎo xiàng*): 已亢也 (*yǐ kàng yě*, "already too high").

### In a life now

This is the figure for a season when the large move is not available: the first months after an
illness, a small business in a hard year, a family after a death, a new person in an old
institution. It permits going over — but toward the small, the lower, the more careful. The
大象 gives three cases: 君子以行過乎恭，喪過乎哀，用過乎儉 (*jūnzǐ yǐ xíng guò hū gōng, sāng guò hū āi,
yòng guò hū jiǎn*, "in conduct the noble one goes over into respect, in mourning over into grief,
in spending over into frugality"). Err, if you must, toward too much courtesy, too much grief, too
little spent. The warning is the high flier: the plan that climbs past every point where it could
land.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The core text and the Laozi share a word, and part on what to do with it.** Chapter 63 in
Shalom's translation: *"The world's great affairs always begin slight."* The graphs are 大事
(*dà shì*, "great affairs"), the same pair as 62's judgment, 不可大事 (*bù kě dà shì*, "great affairs
cannot be done") — both locked, *great* and *affairs*, in both books. The Laozi's counsel is to do
the great while it is still small, and so never meet it great. 62 says something plainer and more
limited: this is not the time for great affairs at all. The Laozi turns small into a method; 62
leaves it as a season, and 彖's 與時行也 (*yǔ shí xíng yě*, "going with the time") says it will change.

### Words kept

- 小過 (*xiǎo guò*) — *Small Exceeding*, proposed; 小 *small* and 大 *great*, locked, so 28 and 62 stay one pair
- 過 (*guò*) — *go over*, *pass*; set against 遇 (*yù*) *meet*, and the pair kept visible in every line
- 事 (*shì*) — *affairs*, locked
- 公 (*gōng*) — *lord*, locked, an office
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss; 永貞 (*yǒng zhēn*) *hold firm for good*
- 儉 (*jiǎn*) — *frugality*, as in the Laozi's chapter 67
- 厲 · 凶 · 无咎 (*lì · xiōng · wú jiù*) — *peril · ominous · no blame*, working glosses
