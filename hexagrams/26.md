## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth under mountain ↔ lake, both poles yang.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 能止健，大正也 (*néng zhǐ jiàn, dà zhèng yě*, "able
to hold vigour: great rightness") and 剛上而尚賢 (*gāng shàng ér shàng xián*, "the firm is on top, and
the worthy are honoured"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 天在山中 (*tiān zài shān zhōng*, "the sky inside
the mountain"), and the noble one 多識前言往行，以畜其德 (*duō shí qián yán wǎng xíng, yǐ chù qí dé*,
"learns many words of the past and deeds gone by, to keep integrity in store"). So the tradition
reads a great force held, and laid up, by something firmer — and 雜卦 (*záguà*) adds 大畜時也 (*dà
chù shí yě*, "大畜 is timing"): the holding is a matter of when.

**What the polarity implies.** Read it against 9 小畜 (*xiǎo chù*, "small keeping"), which has the
same sky within. There the keeper is wind — yin, open at the base — and one open line restrains five
firm ones: the clouds gather and cannot rain, and the cart breaks, 輿說輻 (*yú tuō fú*, "the cart
sheds its spokes", 9.3). Here the keeper is mountain — yang, closed at the top — and sky meets a lid
of its own kind. The keeping is great because what keeps is as firm as what is kept, and the same
cart image turns from accident to choice: 輿說輹 (*yú tuō fù*, "the cart takes off its axle-block",
26.2), which the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) calls 中无尤 (*zhōng wú yóu*, "in the centre, no fault").

The lines show how firm holds firm: not force against force, but early, at the root — 童牛之牿
(*tóng niú zhī gù*, "the board on a young ox's horns", 26.4), 豶豕之牙 (*fén shǐ zhī yá*, "the tusks of
a gelded boar", 26.5) — and both sit in the two open lines, the only yielding places in the figure.
Then, at the mountain's odd line, the lid itself: 何天之衢，亨 (*hé tiān zhī qú, hēng*, "what a
crossroads of the sky — it gets through", 26.6). The structure suggests great keeping ends not when
the lid breaks but when the lid turns out to be a road.

**For the name.** **Great Keeping** — supports: the lid keeps in both senses the 大象 wants, holding
back and laying up, and the pairing with 9 is exactly what the signature shows — one sky, a soft
keeper and a firm one. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Great Restraint* — survives the six
lines: 有厲利已 (*yǒu lì lì yǐ*, "there is peril; it is favourable to stop", 26.1) and the ox's
horn-board are restraint, though it cannot serve the verb in 畜牝牛 (*chù pìn niú*, "keep a cow", 30)
or 9's lines; *Great Store* — fails the lines: nothing in the six lines is laid up, and the word lives
only in the 大象's 畜其德 (*chù qí dé*, "keep integrity in store").

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Great strength kept the way a herder keeps strong animals — checked early, at the root, trained
daily, and let out when the road opens.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 畜 (*chù*) as 田畜也 (*tián chù yě*,
"the keeping of the fields"): livestock, and the keeping of it. The six lines are a stockman's year:
stop at the first peril, unhitch the cart, drill the horses, board the young ox's horns, geld the
boar — and then 何天之衢 (*hé tiān zhī qú*, "what a crossroads of the sky"). Nothing in the lines is
destroyed. The force is kept whole by being kept in hand, and the keeping ends in a road.

### The polarities in it

Sky within, mountain without. What is kept is all firm; what keeps it is closed at its top edge,
firm too. So this keeping is not softness containing strength, as in 9 小畜 (*xiǎo chù*, "small
keeping"), where wind holds the sky and the cart breaks. Here firm holds firm, and it does so not by
pressing down but through the two open lines, 4 and 5 — the only yielding places in the figure,
and exactly where the ox and the boar are handled (see *The signature*, above). **The strength
below is kept by a light hand set early, and the mountain's firm top, which looked like a lid, turns
out to be the way through.**

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 天在山中 (*tiān zài shān zhōng*, "the sky inside the mountain"). Go into a
mountain valley before sunrise on a still night in autumn. Cold air has drained down the slopes in
the dark and pooled in the basin, held there by the ridges, often filled with mist — the sky,
literally, kept inside the mountain. When the sun reaches the valley floor it lifts and goes.
Mountains keep water the same way: the winter's snow and rain held in rock and soil, let out as
springs in the dry months, long after the weather that brought it.

The lines' animals are observed exactly. A calf's horns start as buds, and a stockman deals with
them then, not when they are sharp; line 4's 童牛 (*tóng niú*, "the young ox") is the point. 說文 gives
牿 (*gù*) as 牛馬牢也 (*niú mǎ láo yě*, "a pen for oxen and horses"), where the old commentaries read a
board across the horns — either way, the containing is done while the animal is young. Line 5's 牙
(*yá*, "tusk") is in 說文 牡齒也，象上下相錯之形 (*mǔ chǐ yě, xiàng shàng xià xiāng cuò zhī xíng*, "the male's
tooth; it draws the upper and lower crossing"): a boar's tusks grow against each other and hone each
other sharp. 豶 (*fén*) is 羠豕也 (*yí shǐ yě*, "a gelded pig") — and a gelded boar is calmer and its
tusks grow less. The danger is dealt with at the source, and the tusk stays.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 大畜：利貞，不家食吉，利涉大川 (*dà chù: lì zhēn, bù jiā shí jí, lì shè dà chuān*) —
"Great keeping. Holding firm is advantageous. Not eating at home: auspicious. It is advantageous to
cross the great river." What is kept is not to be eaten privately. The 彖 (*tuàn*) reads 不家食 as
養賢也 (*yǎng xián yě*, "nourishing the worthy"): the store goes to a wider table.

**Line 1**, firm, at the root of the sky. 有厲利已 (*yǒu lì lì yǐ*, "there is peril; stopping is
advantageous"). The strength is new and the way is shut; 王弼 says line 4 is what holds it, so
pressing on is peril.

**Line 2**, firm, in the lower centre. 輿說輹 (*yú tuō fù*, "the cart takes off its axle-block").
說文 has 輹 (*fù*) as 車軸縛也 (*chē zhóu fù yě*, "the binding of the axle"). With it out, the cart goes
nowhere — and it was taken out, not broken. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) says 中无尤也 (*zhōng wú yóu yě*,
"in the centre, no fault").

**Line 3**, firm, at the top of the sky. 良馬逐，利艱貞。曰閑輿衛，利有攸往 (*liáng mǎ zhú, lì jiān zhēn.
yuē xián yú wèi, lì yǒu yōu wǎng*, "good horses race; hardship and holding firm are advantageous.
Practise the chariot and its guard; it is advantageous to have somewhere to go"). Now the horses
run, but under drill. 閑 (*xián*) is in 說文 闌也，从門中有木 (*lán yě, cóng mén zhōng yǒu mù*, "a barrier:
wood inside a gate"), and 王弼 keeps that: 閑閡也 (*xián hé yě*, "*xián* is an obstruction") — the chariot is
fenced even as it goes.

**Line 4**, open, the first of the mountain. 童牛之牿，元吉 (*tóng niú zhī gù, yuán jí*, "the young ox's
horn-board — auspicious from the source"). The keeping of line 1, done where it costs least.

**Line 5**, open, the upper centre. 豶豕之牙，吉 (*fén shǐ zhī yá, jí*, "the tusks of a gelded boar —
auspicious"). 王弼 matches it to line 2: 柔能制健 (*róu néng zhì jiàn*, "the soft can govern the
vigorous"). The tusk is not pulled; its cause is dealt with.

**Line 6**, firm, the mountain's top. 何天之衢，亨 (*hé tiān zhī qú, hēng*, "what a crossroads of the
sky — it gets through"). 說文 gives 衢 (*qú*) as 四達謂之衢 (*sì dá wèi zhī qú*, "open in four
directions"). 王弼 reads 何 (*hé*) as an exclamation; others take it as 荷 (*hè*, "to shoulder"), so that the line
carries the sky's crossroads. Either way the lid has become a road, and 王弼 says why: 畜極則通 (*chù jí
zé tōng*, "keeping, at its utmost, opens through").

So the kept lines (1–3) are counselled to stop, unhitch and drill; the keeping lines (4–5) are
auspicious; and the top, where keeping ends, gets through.

### In a life now

This is the figure for strength that is not yet to be spent: a gifted child, a new team with money
and nerve, one's own anger or ambition, a book half known. It does not counsel suppression. It
counsels what a good stockman does — handle it early, while the horns are buds (line 4); deal with
the cause, not the tusk (line 5); take the axle-block out yourself rather than let the cart break
(line 2); and when it does run, drill it daily (line 3). The judgment adds that what is kept is not
for eating at home: a store of skill or means is for a wider table. And 雜卦 (*záguà*) says 大畜時也
(*dà chù shí yě*, "great keeping is timing"): there is a day the keeping ends, and the road is
open in four directions.

The 大象 turns it to the store one keeps in oneself: 君子以多識前言往行，以畜其德 (*jūnzǐ yǐ duō shí qián
yán wǎng xíng, yǐ chù qí dé*, "the noble one learns many words of the past and deeds gone by, to keep
their integrity").

### An echo from the Laozi

**The same verb, and a different hand.** The Laozi's chapter 51 opens: *"The Tao gives birth to the
countless things, integrity rears them."* The verb is 畜 (*xù*, "rear"), with 德 (*dé*, "integrity")
as its subject — 德畜之 (*dé xù zhī*, "integrity rears them") — and Shalom's *rears* answers to it. The
graph is this hexagram's name, in the core text. The 大象's 畜其德 (*chù qí dé*, "keep their
integrity") turns the Laozi's clause round: there integrity rears the things; here a person rears
integrity.

They part on the hand. Chapter 51's rearing is *"growing them without ruling them."* This
hexagram's keeper boards the ox's horns and gelds the boar, and the core text calls it auspicious.
Chapter 64's *"Handle it before it exists"* is closer to lines 4 and 5 — but that is a thematic
likeness only; 64 has none of these graphs. **The Laozi lets things be; 大畜 keeps them, and the
reading leaves the difference standing.**
