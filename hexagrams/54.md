## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake and thunder ↔ wind; yin within, yang without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 gives the figure cosmic weight and then a warning. 歸妹，天地之大義也
(*guī mèi, tiān dì zhī dà yì yě*, "the younger sister's marriage is the great rightness of sky and earth"),
because 天地不交，而萬物不興 (*tiān dì bù jiāo, ér wànwù bù xīng*, "if sky and earth do not join, the countless
things do not rise"). It reads the two actions as 說以動 (*yuè yǐ dòng*, "delighted, and so moving"), but
征凶，位不當也 (*zhēng xiōng, wèi bù dàng yě*, "going on is ill: the places are not right"). The 大象傳 has
澤上有雷 (*zé shàng yǒu léi*, "thunder above the lake"), and from it 君子以永終知敝 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yǒng zhōng zhī
bì*, "the noble one sees through to the far end and knows where it wears out").

**What the polarity implies.** This is 53 turned over, and the order goes with it. There, a hold gave way
to an entering; here, an opening lies under a strike. Lake within is open at its top, the third line;
thunder without is firm at its base, the fourth. At the centre an open mouth meets a force coming down.
The inside assents, and the movement belongs to someone else. That is the younger sister's case exactly:
she does not go, she is sent, and to a place already ranked. The 彖傳 names the fault structurally, 柔乘剛
(*róu chéng gāng*, "the yielding rides on the firm"): the open lines sit above firm ones they cannot carry.
What the lines prize is making do in a place not chosen: 跛能履 (*bǒ néng lǚ*, "lame, yet able to walk"),
眇能視 (*miǎo néng shì*, "one-eyed, yet able to see"). The top line shows the release with nothing behind
it: 女承筐无實 (*nǚ chéng kuāng wú shí*, "the woman holds up a basket with nothing in it"). The structure
suggests this against 53. Consent that comes before the ground is set is still consent. But it cannot carry
what is put on it, and the 大象傳's 知敝 (*zhī bì*), "knowing where it wears out", is the honest response.

**For the name.** **The Younger Sister's Marriage** — the signature supports it: a yielding inner position
set in motion by another is the 妹 (*mèi*, "younger sister") and 娣 (*dì*, "secondary wife") system the
lines turn on. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Given in Marriage* — survives: 帝乙歸妹 (*Dì Yǐ guī
mèi*, "Di Yi gives his younger sister in marriage"; 帝乙 is a Shang ruler's name), 歸妹愆期 (*guī mèi qiān qí*,
"the younger sister's marriage overruns its time"); *Consent* — fails the lines: they rank and time the
marriage (歸妹以娣, *guī mèi yǐ dì*, "married as a secondary wife"), and no line asks her.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Entering an order someone else set, at a rank not chosen — and making it walk.** The judgment is
flat: 歸妹：征凶，无攸利 (*guī mèi: zhēng xiōng, wú yōu lì*, "the younger sister's marriage: marching
on is ominous; nothing is advantageous"). Then the first line turns it: married as the junior,
跛能履，征吉 (*bǒ néng lǚ, zhēng jí*, "lame, yet able to walk: going on is auspicious"). 說文
(*shuōwén*) gives 歸 (*guī*, "marrying") as 女嫁也 (*nǚ jià yě*, "a woman marrying"), 妹 (*mèi*,
"younger sister") as 女弟也 (*nǚ dì yě*, "a woman's younger sibling"), and 娣 (*dì*, "junior wife")
the same way, built from woman (女) and younger sibling (弟).

**The seam has to be named.** The lines are built on a Zhou institution: a bride went to her
husband's house with a younger sister or cousin as a secondary wife, ranked below her, and no woman
in it chose. The core text neither praises nor questions this. This reading does not turn it into a
story of consent, and does not pretend it is about something else. What carries across is the
situation: going into a household, an office, a country whose order was fixed before you came,
below its top, on a timetable not your own.

### The polarities in it

Lake within, thunder without: 53 turned over. The lake is open at its top and receives; the thunder
is firm at its base, strikes, and moves off (see *The signature*, above). The movement belongs to
the upper figure; the lower gives way to it. 王弼 reads the pair as ages: 兊爲少隂震爲長陽 (*duì wéi
shào yīn, zhèn wéi zhǎng yáng*, "the lake is the young yin, thunder the elder yang"). The 彖 (*tuàn*)
names the result 說以動 (*yuè yǐ dòng*, "delighted, and so moving") — the lake moves only because
something else did — and the fault 柔乘剛 (*róu chéng gāng*, "the soft rides on the firm"). Here
the text refines the structure rather than repeating it. Its good lines are the two firm ones at the
bottom, lame and one-eyed, which make do; its empty one is the soft line at the very top.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 澤上有雷 (*zé shàng yǒu léi*, "thunder above the lake"). Stand by open
water in a storm: the sound comes across the surface from a long way off, the water shivers, and
the lake does nothing — it cannot follow. The storm passes on over the hills and the lake is left
as it was. 說卦 (*shuōguà*) ch 5 sets thunder in the east, where things first come out, and the lake
at 正秋 (*zhèng qiū*, "full autumn"): spring's sound over autumn's water.

The fifth line holds the finest image in the figure: 月幾望 (*yuè jī wàng*, "the moon almost
full"). Look at the moon a night or two before full. It rises in the east in late afternoon, bright
but not quite round, and along its shadowed edge the craters and mountains stand out in relief. At
full, the sunlight falls straight on and the face goes flat; nothing casts a shadow. The nearly full
moon shows more. The line prizes that — the height not quite taken — and the same phrase is in 9.6.

The top line gives the rite. 說文 glosses 刲 (*kuī*, "to stab") as 刺也 (*cì yě*, "to pierce"), and
quotes this very line: 士刲羊 (*shì kuī yáng*, "the man stabs the sheep"). A sheep stabbed that does
not bleed is already dead — no offering at all.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 歸妹：征凶，无攸利 — "The younger sister's marriage: marching on is ominous;
nothing is advantageous." 征 (*zhēng*) is setting out on one's own force. In a place ordered by
others, that is the one thing that fails.

**Line 1** is the lowest rank, taken. 歸妹以娣，跛能履，征吉 (*guī mèi yǐ dì, bǒ néng lǚ, zhēng jí*,
"the younger sister married as junior wife: lame, yet able to walk; going on is auspicious"). 說文
gives 跛 (*bǒ*, "lame") as 行不正也 (*xíng bù zhèng yě*, "walking not straight"). The judgment's
march fails; this limp arrives.

**Line 2** is the lower centre. 眇能視，利幽人之貞 (*miǎo néng shì, lì yōu rén zhī zhēn*, "one-eyed,
yet able to see; holding firm is advantageous for one kept apart"). 說文: 眇 (*miǎo*) is 一目小也
(*yī mù xiǎo yě*, "one eye small"); 幽 (*yōu*) is 隱也 (*yǐn yě*, "hidden"). The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*):
未變常也 (*wèi biàn cháng yě*, "the abiding has not changed").

**Line 3** reaches up and comes back. 歸妹以須，反歸以娣 (*guī mèi yǐ xū, fǎn guī yǐ dì*, "the younger
sister married while waiting; she goes back, and is married as junior wife"). 須 (*xū*) is disputed;
說文's 面毛也 (*miàn máo yě*, "hair of the face") does not help, and 王弼 reads it as waiting —
進未值時故有須也 (*jìn wèi zhí shí gù yǒu xū yě*, "she advanced before the time came, so there is
waiting"). At the seam, the higher place is tried and the lower one taken. No verdict is given.

**Line 4** is late, and not lost. 歸妹愆期，遲歸有時 (*guī mèi qiān qí, chí guī yǒu shí*, "the
marriage overruns its time; a late marriage has its time"). 說文 gives 愆 (*qiān*) as 過也 (*guò yě*,
"to go past").

**Line 5** is the height, worn plainly. 帝乙歸妹，其君之袂，不如其娣之袂良，月幾望，吉 (*Dì Yǐ guī
mèi, qí jūn zhī mèi, bù rú qí dì zhī mèi liáng, yuè jī wàng, jí*, "Dì Yǐ gives his younger sister
in marriage: the first wife's sleeves were not as fine as the junior's; the moon almost full —
auspicious"). 帝乙 is a name, a Shang ruler; 11.5 tells the same wedding, 帝乙歸妹，以祉，元吉
(*Dì Yǐ guī mèi, yǐ zhǐ, yuán jí*, "…with blessing: auspicious from the source"). The one at the
top of the rank dresses below her junior.

**Line 6** is the form with nothing in it. 女承筐无實，士刲羊无血，无攸利 (*nǚ chéng kuāng wú shí, shì
kuī yáng wú xuè, wú yōu lì*, "the woman holds up a basket with nothing in it; the man stabs the
sheep and no blood comes; nothing is advantageous"). The rite is performed, and empty.

### In a life now

This is the figure for anyone who joins an order that was there first: marrying into a family with
its own ways, a step-parent, a second hire into a team built round a founder's favourite, a junior
partner, an immigrant in a new country, a younger sibling, a second marriage. The text counsels
stance, not revolt or resignation. Do not march to take the top (the judgment). Walk, even lame;
see, even with one eye; keep steady out of sight (lines 1, 2). If you reach too high, come back
without shame (line 3). If the time is late, it is not gone (line 4). If you do hold the high
place, wear it plainly (line 5). And fear the empty basket above all — the arrangement kept up
after there is nothing in it (line 6).

The 大象 turns it toward seeing ahead: 君子以永終知敝 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yǒng zhōng zhī bì*, "the noble one
makes the end long, and knows where things wear out"). 敝 (*bì*) is worn cloth. The 雜卦 (*záguà*)
calls this figure 女之終也 (*nǚ zhī zhōng yě*, "a woman's ending"), and the 彖 calls it 人之終始也
(*rén zhī zhōng shǐ yě*, "the ending and beginning of people"). To enter an arrangement well is to
know from the start how it could fray.

### Words kept

- 歸妹 (*guī mèi*) — **The Younger Sister's Marriage**, the proposed name (not yet approved)
- 歸 (*guī*) — *marrying*, a woman's going to a husband's house (說文 女嫁也)
- 娣 (*dì*) — *junior wife*, a kinswoman of the bride ranked below her; never *concubine*, which names another institution
- 帝乙 (*Dì Yǐ*) — a name (locked), not *god*
- 常 (*cháng*) — *the abiding*, never *eternal* (locked)
- 征 (*zhēng*) — *marching on*, *going on*: setting out on one's own force
- 无攸利 (*wú yōu lì*) — *nothing is advantageous*; 利 *advantage* (locked)
- 貞 · 吉 · 凶 (*zhēn · jí · xiōng*) — *holding firm · auspicious · ominous*, working glosses (A4, A6)
