## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking
in). **Doubled** — water ↔ fire, the yang pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 習坎，重險也 (*xí kǎn, chóng xiǎn yě*, "the pit
repeated: danger doubled"), 水流而不盈，行險而不失其信 (*shuǐ liú ér bù yíng, xíng xiǎn ér bù shī qí
xìn*, "water flows and does not fill up; it goes through danger and does not lose its trust"), and
維心亨，乃以剛中也 (*wéi xīn hēng, nǎi yǐ gāng zhōng yě*, "only the heart gets through — because the firm
is central"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 水洊至 (*shuǐ jiàn zhì*, "water comes, and comes again"), and
the noble one 常德行，習教事 (*cháng dé xíng, xí jiào shì*, "makes integrity and conduct abiding, and
practises the affairs of teaching"). The tradition reads doubled danger passed through by a firm
heart, and makes the doubling itself — 習 (*xí*, "repeat, practise") — the lesson.

**What the polarity implies.** Water is a firm line held inside two open ones, and doubled, its two
firm lines land on the second and fifth places — the centre of each trigram. Every strength in the
figure is a centre; every edge is open. That is the 彖's 剛中 (*gāng zhōng*, "the firm is central")
read straight off the figure, and it says where the danger is: not in the water's core but in the
open lines around it. The open lines are where one falls — 入于坎窞 (*rù yú kǎn dàn*, "into the hollow
of the pit", 29.1), 寘于叢棘 (*zhì yú cóng jí*, "set among thorns", 29.6) — and the firm ones are where
there is footing: 求小得 (*qiú xiǎo dé*, "seek, and get a little", 29.2), 坎不盈 (*kǎn bù yíng*, "the
pit does not fill", 29.5).

Doubled, water also has nothing to attach to — descending into descending, it has only its own
middle. The structure suggests the way through is to be the firm line: to keep moving as water does,
never filling, never stopping. At the seam, where two open lines meet between the pits, it leaves one
opening: 樽酒簋貳，用缶，納約自牖 (*zūn jiǔ guǐ èr, yòng fǒu, nà yuē zì yǒu*, "a jug of wine, two
baskets of grain, in earthenware, handed in through the window", 29.4) — the plain gift that gets in
where nothing else can. And it closes the upper canon: 1 and 2 opened it with the spectrum that has no
odd line; 29 and 30 end it with the spectrum whose odd line is in the middle, each pole alone and
twice — 水火不相射 (*shuǐ huǒ bù xiāng shè*, "water and fire do not quench each other", 說卦 ch 3).

**For the name.** **The Pit** — supports: doubled sinking-in is a pit within a pit, and every line uses
坎 (*kǎn*, "pit") as a place one enters. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *The Deep* — survives:
入于坎窞 reads "into the hollow of the deep" and 坎不盈 "the deep does not fill", though it loses the
dug edge of 窞 (*dàn*, "hollow, pit-bottom"); *Sinking* — fails the lines: 坎有險 (*kǎn yǒu xiǎn*, "the
pit has danger", 29.2) and 坎不盈 need a place that can be full, not a motion — and it is the
signature's own action, which is reason enough to drop it.
