## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking
in). **Doubled** — water ↔ fire, the yang pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 習坎，重險也 (*xí kǎn, chóng xiǎn yě*, "the pit
repeated: hazard doubled"), 水流而不盈，行險而不失其信 (*shuǐ liú ér bù yíng, xíng xiǎn ér bù shī qí
xìn*, "water flows and does not fill up; it goes through hazard and does not lose its trust"), and
維心亨，乃以剛中也 (*wéi xīn hēng, nǎi yǐ gāng zhōng yě*, "only the heart gets through — because the firm
is central"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 水洊至 (*shuǐ jiàn zhì*, "water comes, and comes again"), and
the noble one 常德行，習教事 (*cháng dé xíng, xí jiào shì*, "makes integrity and conduct abiding, and
practises the affairs of teaching"). The tradition reads doubled danger passed through by a firm
heart, and makes the doubling itself — 習 (*xí*, "repeat, practise") — the lesson.

**What the polarity implies.** Water is a firm line held inside two open ones, and doubled, its two
firm lines land on the second and fifth places — the centre of each trigram. Every strength in the
figure is a centre; every edge is open. That is the 彖's 剛中 (*gāng zhōng*, "the firm is central")
read straight off the figure, and it says where the danger is: not in the water's core but in the
open lines around it. The open lines are where one falls — 入于坎窞 (*rù yú kǎn dàn*, "into the hollow
of the pit", 29.1), 寘于叢棘 (*zhì yú cóng jí*, "set among thorns", 29.6) — and the firm ones are where
there is footing: 求小得 (*qiú xiǎo dé*, "seek, and get a little", 29.2), 坎不盈 (*kǎn bù yíng*, "the
pit does not fill", 29.5).

Doubled, water also has nothing to attach to — descending into descending, it has only its own
middle. The structure suggests the way through is to be the firm line: to keep moving as water does,
never filling, never stopping. At the seam, where two open lines meet between the pits, it leaves one
opening: 樽酒簋貳，用缶，納約自牖 (*zūn jiǔ guǐ èr, yòng fǒu, nà yuē zì yǒu*, "a jug of wine, two
baskets of grain, in earthenware, handed in through the window", 29.4) — the plain gift that gets in
where nothing else can. And it closes the upper canon: 1 and 2 opened it with the spectrum that has no
odd line; 29 and 30 end it with the spectrum whose odd line is in the middle, each pole alone and
twice — 水火不相射 (*shuǐ huǒ bù xiāng shè*, "water and fire do not quench each other", 說卦 ch 3).

**For the name.** **The Pit** — supports: doubled sinking-in is a pit within a pit, and every line uses
坎 (*kǎn*, "pit") as a place one enters. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *The Deep* — survives:
入于坎窞 reads "into the hollow of the deep" and 坎不盈 "the deep does not fill", though it loses the
dug edge of 窞 (*dàn*, "hollow, pit-bottom"); *Sinking* — fails the lines: 坎有險 (*kǎn yǒu xiǎn*, "the
pit has hazards", 29.2) and 坎不盈 need a place that can be full, not a motion — and it is the
signature's own action, which is reason enough to drop it.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A pit inside a pit, and the only way out is through.** The core text uses 坎 (*kǎn*) as a place
eight times over — something you enter, sink into, lie down in, climb out of. 說文 (*shuōwén*)
gives the graph as 陷也 (*xiàn yě*, "a falling-in"), earth (土) dug open, and it gives the pit's
floor its own word, 窞 (*dàn*): 坎中小坎也, "a small pit within the pit". The hexagram is not about
danger in general. It is about being **in** it — already down, with more hollow beneath — and about
what can still be done from there.

### The polarities in it

Water within, water without: one pole, twice. Each trigram is a firm line held between two open
ones, and doubled, the two firm lines fall in the centre of each half (see *The signature*, above).
So the strength of this figure is never at an edge. The edges give way; the centres hold. That is
where the reading starts: **in a pit you cannot trust the walls — only the middle, and whatever
keeps moving.**

### In nature

Watch water in a gully after rain. It does not climb out of the low place; it fills the hollow to
its lip and then goes on, over, down to the next one. It never stays, and it is never full for
long. The Wings saw exactly this: 水流而不盈 (*shuǐ liú ér bù yíng*, "water flows and does not fill
up", 彖), and 水洊至 (*shuǐ jiàn zhì*, "water comes, and comes again", 大象) — the second wave arriving
on the first, as rain does on a streambed already running.

The first word of the judgment holds another creature. 習 (*xí*), "repeat, practise", is in 說文
數飛也 (*shuò fēi yě*, "to fly again and again") — wings over a graph for the young bird beating
them before it can fly. 習坎 (*xí kǎn*, "the pit, doubled") is the pit met again, and the pit
**practised**: the fledgling's lesson, learned the only way it can be, by repeating what it
cannot yet do. 王弼 reads it the same way — 習謂便習之 (*xí wèi biàn xí zhī*, "習 means becoming
practised at it").

Look, too, at the fourth line's window, 牖 (*yǒu*). 說文 gives it as a lattice cut through a wall,
and adds 牖，所以見日 (*yǒu, suǒ yǐ jiàn rì*, "the window: that by which one sees the sun"). The only
opening this hexagram offers is a small one, set high, facing the light.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 習坎：有孚，維心亨。行有尚 (*xí kǎn: yǒu fú, wéi xīn hēng. xíng yǒu shàng*) — "The
pit, doubled. There is confidence; it is the heart that gets through. Going on is honoured." In
the core text the whole weight falls on 心 (*xīn*, "heart"): not a plan, not strength, not the
walls — the middle of the person, the firm line in the middle of the water.

**Line 1** falls. 習坎，入于坎窞，凶 (*xí kǎn, rù yú kǎn dàn, xiōng*, "the pit, doubled: into the
hollow at the pit's floor — ominous"). The first move in danger is the most dangerous: going
further down.

**Line 2** is the first firm line, and it is modest. 坎有險，求小得 (*kǎn yǒu xiǎn, qiú xiǎo dé*,
"the pit has hazards; seek, and get a little"). From the centre of the lower pit there is no
escape yet — only small gains, one foothold at a time.

**Line 3** is between the two pits, with nothing firm to stand on. 來之坎坎，險且枕，入于坎窞，勿用
(*lái zhī kǎn kǎn, xiǎn qiě zhěn, rù yú kǎn dàn, wù yòng*, "coming and going, pit on pit; hazard,
and lying back in it; into the hollow — do not act"). 枕 (*zhěn*) is a pillow: the hazard of
getting comfortable in it. The line's counsel is stillness.

**Line 4** is the one kindness in the figure. 樽酒簋貳，用缶，納約自牖，終无咎 (*zūn jiǔ guǐ èr,
yòng fǒu, nà yuē zì yǒu, zhōng wú jiù*, "a jug of wine, two baskets of grain, in plain
earthenware, handed in through the window — in the end, no blame"). No ceremony, no bronze —
the plainest vessels, passed through the smallest opening. Where two open lines meet between the
pits, something gets in.

**Line 5** is the second firm centre, and it holds. 坎不盈，祗既平，无咎 (*kǎn bù yíng, zhī jì
píng, wú jiù*, "the pit does not fill; it is only brought level — no blame"). The water does not
overflow; it reaches the lip and evens out. Enough, and not more.

**Line 6** is caught at the top, where everything is open. 係用徽纆，寘于叢棘，三歲不得，凶 (*xì
yòng huī mò, zhì yú cóng jí, sān suì bù dé, xiōng*, "bound with black cords, set among the
thorns; three years and not found — ominous"). To reach the rim of a pit is not to be out of it.

### In a life now

This is the figure for a long illness, a debt that compounds, grief that returns in waves, a
country or a household in trouble that does not end on schedule. It does not promise rescue. It
names three things that still work from inside: **keep the middle** (the judgment's heart);
**take the small gain and do not grasp for the large** (line 2, and line 5's "enough"); and
**accept the plain gift through the small opening** — the meal a neighbour leaves, the one honest
conversation, the letter that arrives (line 4). Its sharpest warning is line 3's pillow: that
the pit becomes where you live, and you stop moving.

The 大象 turns all of this toward the long run: 君子以常德行，習教事 (*jūnzǐ yǐ cháng dé xíng, xí
jiào shì*, "the noble one makes integrity and conduct abiding, and practises the work of
teaching"). Danger met again and again becomes practice; what is practised can be taught.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a graph here, and part over an image.** The Laozi's chapter 15 ends: *"Those
who keep the Tao safe like this have no wish to be full. / Precisely because they are never full,
/ they can stay covered, and never newly made."* The word is 盈 (*yíng*, "full") — the same graph as
29.5's 坎不盈, "the pit does not fill", in the core text itself. In both books, not-filling is the
condition of going on.

But the water is not the same water. Chapter 78's water is *"softer and more yielding"* than
anything, and it overcomes the hard. 坎's water is seen from the one who has fallen into it: the
hollow that holds you, not the softness that wears stone down. **The difference is the point, and
this reading does not smooth it over** ([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md)
§ 6, failure 2). If there is a bridge, it is the judgment's 心 — the firm centre that keeps
moving, as water does, without being full.

- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked)
