## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving
out). **Crossed** — thunder ↔ wind, the yin pole within, the yang pole without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 恆，久也 (*héng, jiǔ yě*, "恆 is lasting"), 剛上而柔下，雷風相與，巽而動
(*gāng shàng ér róu xià, léi fēng xiāng yǔ, xùn ér dòng*, "the firm above, the yielding below; thunder
and wind give to each other; entering, and moving out"), and 終則有始也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ yě*, "at the
end there is a beginning") — 四時變化，而能久成 (*sì shí biàn huà, ér néng jiǔ chéng*, "the four seasons
change, and so can complete for long"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 雷風 (*léi fēng*, "thunder, wind"),
and the noble one 以立不易方 (*yǐ lì bù yì fāng*, "stands and does not change direction"). The
tradition reads lasting as a process that renews itself, not a state that stays.

**What the polarity implies.** The one figure named for lasting is built from the two images that
never stay: wind, which slips in beneath, and thunder, which breaks out from the base. Both odd lines
are base lines — the open first, the firm fourth — so each trigram's defining line is its
foundation, and constancy here is a matter of what is underneath. Wind within enters and roots;
thunder without moves out and sounds. In and out, in one rhythm — and the 說文 (*shuōwén*) picture of
the graph agrees: 从心从舟，在二之閒上下 (*cóng xīn cóng zhōu, zài èr zhī jiān shàng xià*, "heart and
boat, going up and down between two lines").

The lines fail at exactly those bases. At wind's open foot, 浚恆，貞凶 (*jùn héng, zhēn xiōng*,
"dredging for constancy: holding firm is ominous", 32.1) — the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) says 始求深也 (*shǐ qiú
shēn yě*, "seeking depth at the start"): entering too far, too soon. At thunder's firm foot, 田无禽
(*tián wú qín*, "hunting, no game", 32.4): moving out with nothing to meet. At thunder's top, 振恆，凶
(*zhèn héng, xiōng*, "shaken constancy — ominous", 32.6). The structure suggests constancy is neither
movement but their keeping time — direction held while everything moves. Against 31 it completes the
hinge: there the youngest pair, one side still, the man below; here the eldest pair, 剛上而柔下 (*gāng
shàng ér róu xià*, "the firm above, the yielding below"), 31's order turned over — lasting because
both move.

**For the name.** **Constancy** — deepens: the signature makes it constancy of direction between two
movers, not of state, and the lines' warnings against over-depth and shaking confirm it. **Candidates
the signature suggests:** *Continuance* — survives: 不恆其德 (*bù héng qí dé*, "not continuing in his
integrity", 32.3) and 恆其德 (*héng qí dé*, "continuing in his integrity", 32.5) both read, and it
carries the 彖's 終則有始; *Renewal* — fails the lines: 不恆其德，或承之羞 (*bù héng qí dé, huò chéng
zhī xiū*, "not constant in integrity, and shame may follow", 32.3) would blame what the name praises.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Constancy is a direction kept, not a place held — and it fails at the ends: dug in too deep at the
start, shaken loose at the finish.** The judgment ties lasting to going: 利有攸往 (*lì yǒu yōu wǎng*,
"advantageous to have somewhere to go"). 說文 (*shuōwén*) draws 恆 (*héng*, "constancy", proposed)
as 从心从舟，在二之閒上下 (*cóng xīn cóng zhōu, zài èr zhī jiān shàng xià*, "from heart and from boat,
going up and down between two lines"), and adds 心以舟施，恆也 (*xīn yǐ zhōu shī, héng yě*, "the heart
going as a boat goes: that is constancy"). A ferry between two banks, crossing and crossing again.
And the lines are hard on it: only one of the six, the second, is good without condition. The rest
show the ways constancy goes wrong.

### The polarities in it

Wind within, thunder without: the two poles whose odd line is at the base, crossed. Both trigrams
are defined by what lies underneath — wind open at its foot, thunder firm at its — and the text's
failures fall on those feet: line 1 digs, line 4 hunts an empty field. The figure named for lasting
is built from the two images that never stay, and the 彖 (*tuàn*) reads it as their keeping time
together: 雷風相與 (*léi fēng xiāng yǔ*, "thunder and wind give to each other"). **Nothing in this
figure is still; constancy is two movements keeping time with each other.** Here 王弼 pulls the other
way — see line 6 — and the text sits between them: it condemns shaking, not moving.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) is two words, 雷風 (*léi fēng*, "thunder, wind"). Watch a summer storm come in.
The wind arrives first — a sudden cold gust, leaves flipped pale side up, dust lifting off the road —
pushed out ahead of the rain by the storm's own falling air; the thunder follows. Neither stays; the
storm is over in an hour. Yet the storms come back every summer, in the same weeks, from the same
quarter. That is lasting as the 彖 sees it: 四時變化，而能久成 (*sì shí biàn huà, ér néng jiǔ chéng*,
"the four seasons change, and so can complete for long"), and 終則有始也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ yě*, "at the
end there is a beginning").

Then look at 說文's ferry. A boat crossing a wide river does not hold still; it is pushed off line
by the current every minute and corrected every minute, and its course is the same each day. The
大象's direction is a boat too: 方 (*fāng*, "direction") is in 說文 併船也 (*bìng chuán yě*, "boats
lashed side by side"). Two boats in one name and image: a coincidence of the dictionary,
noted, not built on.

And line 4's field. Game moves with the season, from summer grazing to winter cover; a hunter who
keeps to the ground where the deer were in June will find it empty in November. The 小象 (*xiǎo
xiàng*) says exactly that: 久非其位，安得禽也 (*jiǔ fēi qí wèi, ān dé qín yě*, "long in a place not its
own — how would it take game?").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 恆：亨，无咎。利貞，利有攸往 (*héng: hēng, wú jiù. lì zhēn, lì yǒu yōu wǎng*) —
"Constancy. It gets through; no blame. Advantageous to hold firm; advantageous to have somewhere to
go." Holding firm and going somewhere, in one breath.

**Line 1** — 浚恆，貞凶，无攸利 (*jùn héng, zhēn xiōng, wú yōu lì*, "dredging for constancy: holding
firm is ominous; nothing is advantageous"). 說文 gives 浚 (*jùn*) as 杼也 (*zhù yě*, "to scoop out").
The 小象: 始求深也 (*shǐ qiú shēn yě*, "seeking depth at the start"); 王弼: 求深窮底 (*qiú shēn qióng
dǐ*, "seeking depth, scraping the bottom"). Wanting at once what only years can give.

**Line 2** — 悔亡 (*huǐ wáng*, "regret goes"). Two characters, the whole line. The firm centre of the
wind; the 小象: 能久中也 (*néng jiǔ zhōng yě*, "it can last in the middle").

**Line 3** — 不恆其德，或承之羞，貞吝 (*bù héng qí dé, huò chéng zhī xiū, zhēn lìn*, "not constant in
one's integrity: someone may hand one shame; holding firm is stinting"). 說文's 羞 (*xiū*) is 進獻也
(*jìn xiàn yě*, "to present", as food is) and 承 (*chéng*) is 奉也 (*fèng yě*, "to hold up in both
hands"): shame served like a dish. The 小象 gives it the phrase it gave 30's burning fourth line, 无所容也
(*wú suǒ róng yě*, "there is nowhere that holds it").

**Line 4** — 田无禽 (*tián wú qín*, "the hunt: no game"). Thunder's firm base moves out and meets
nothing. Constancy in the wrong place is only staying.

**Line 5** — 恆其德，貞，婦人吉，夫子凶 (*héng qí dé, zhēn, fùrén jí, fūzǐ xiōng*, "constant in
integrity, holding firm: for the wife auspicious, for the husband ominous"). The core text makes the
verdict turn on the role: the same steadiness serves one place and fails another. The 小象 explains it
in its own age's terms of marriage — 從一而終也 (*cóng yī ér zhōng yě*, "following one to the end") for
her, 夫子制義 (*fūzǐ zhì yì*, "the husband decides what is right") for him — and that explanation is the
Wing's. What the line itself says is narrower, and still sharp: constancy is not the same thing in every
place.

**Line 6** — 振恆，凶 (*zhèn héng, xiōng*, "constancy shaken — ominous"). 說文 gives 振 (*zhèn*) as also
奮也 (*fèn yě*, "to rouse"): constancy stirred up, at the top of thunder. The 小象: 大无功也 (*dà wú gōng
yě*, "great, and nothing achieved"). 王弼 draws the Laozi's lesson — 靜者可乆之道也 (*jìng zhě kě jiǔ
zhī dào yě*, "stillness is the Tao of what can last") — which is his, not the figure's: nothing in
this figure is still.

Constancy fails by excess at the start, by lapse, by inertia, and by agitation — and survives in the
middle.

### In a life now

This is the figure for a marriage in its twentieth year — 序卦 (*xùguà*) sets it here because 夫婦之道，不可以不久也
(*fū fù zhī dào, bù kě yǐ bù jiǔ yě*, "the Tao of husband and wife cannot but last") — and for any
long practice: an instrument, a craft, a garden, a friendship, the care of someone ill for years, an
institution. It counsels against four mistakes. **Do not demand at the start the depth only years
give** (line 1). **A lapse in integrity is served back to you** (line 3). **Loyalty to a place the
life has left is not constancy** (line 4). **Do not overhaul in agitation at the end** (line 6). And
it gives one plain good: **keep to the middle**, and regret goes (line 2).

The 大象 turns it to practice: 君子以立不易方 (*jūnzǐ yǐ lì bù yì fāng*, "the noble one stands and
does not change direction"). Not *does not change* — 方 (*fāng*, "direction") is a heading. Everything
around the noble one moves, as wind and thunder do, and so may they; the heading holds.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One shared saying, and two reasons for it.** Chapter 7: *"The sky is lasting, the earth enduring.
/ Sky and earth can last and endure / because they do not live for themselves."* The 彖 agrees that
they last — 天地之道，恆久而不已也 (*tiān dì zhī dào, héng jiǔ ér bù yǐ yě*, "the Tao of sky and earth is
constant, lasting, and never stops") — and the characters, 天地 (*tiān dì*, "sky and earth") and 久
(*jiǔ*, "lasting"), are in the layer being read, a Wing. The English answers to them.

But the reasons differ. The Laozi's sky and earth last because they have no self to serve. The 彖's
last because 終則有始也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ yě*, "at the end there is a beginning") — by turning over,
like the seasons. One is selflessness, the other rhythm; this reading does not merge them.

One contact was considered and set aside. This project's own glossary for 常 (*cháng*) records that
the Mawangdui manuscripts of the Laozi wrote 恆 (*héng*, "constancy") where the received text writes
常 (*cháng*, "abiding") — changed under the Han because 恆 (*héng*) was the personal name of the reigning ruler. So this hexagram's name
once stood in the Laozi's most quoted lines. But Shalom's translation renders the received 常
(*cháng*, "abiding"), so the English does not answer to 恆 (*héng*, "constancy"); it fails the second
question, and it is a fact about manuscripts, not an echo.

### Words kept

- 恆 (*héng*) — *constancy*, the proposed name (not yet approved); never *abiding* (that is 常, *cháng*, locked — and 說文 glosses 恆 as 常也, *cháng yě*, so the two need keeping apart); never *eternal*
- 久 (*jiǔ*) — *lasting*: the 彖's and 序卦's gloss, not the core text's word
- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked)
- 道 (*dào*) — *the Tao* in the 彖, 序卦 and 王弼; the core text of this hexagram does not write it
- 方 (*fāng*) — *direction*, a heading
- 婦人 · 夫子 (*fùrén · fūzǐ*) — *the wife · the husband*: the text's own roles, not this reading's
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 貞凶 (*zhēn xiōng*) is "the inquiry: ominous"
- 吉 · 凶 · 吝 · 无咎 (*jí · xiōng · lìn · wú jiù*) — *auspicious · ominous · stinting · no blame*; 悔亡 (*huǐ wáng*) *regret goes* — working glosses (A4)
- 靜 (*jìng*) — *stillness*
