## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving
out). **Crossed** — thunder ↔ wind, the yin pole within, the yang pole without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 恆，久也 (*héng, jiǔ yě*, "恆 is lasting"), 剛上而柔下，雷風相與，巽而動
(*gāng shàng ér róu xià, léi fēng xiāng yǔ, xùn ér dòng*, "the firm above, the yielding below; thunder
and wind give to each other; entering, and moving out"), and 終則有始也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ yě*, "at the
end there is a beginning") — 四時變化，而能久成 (*sì shí biàn huà, ér néng jiǔ chéng*, "the four seasons
change, and so can complete for long"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 雷風 (*léi fēng*, "thunder, wind"),
and the noble one 以立不易方 (*yǐ lì bù yì fāng*, "stands and does not change direction"). The
tradition reads lasting as a process that renews itself, not a state that stays.

**What the polarity implies.** The one figure named for lasting is built from the two images that
never stay: wind, which slips in beneath, and thunder, which breaks out from the base. Both odd lines
are base lines — the open first, the firm fourth — so each trigram's defining line is its
foundation, and constancy here is a matter of what is underneath. Wind within enters and roots;
thunder without moves out and sounds. In and out, in one rhythm — and the 說文 (*shuōwén*) picture of
the graph agrees: 从心从舟，在二之閒上下 (*cóng xīn cóng zhōu, zài èr zhī jiān shàng xià*, "heart and
boat, going up and down between two lines").

The lines fail at exactly those bases. At wind's open foot, 浚恆，貞凶 (*jùn héng, zhēn xiōng*,
"dredging for constancy: holding firm is ominous", 32.1) — the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) says 始求深也 (*shǐ qiú
shēn yě*, "seeking depth at the start"): entering too far, too soon. At thunder's firm foot, 田无禽
(*tián wú qín*, "hunting, no game", 32.4): moving out with nothing to meet. At thunder's top, 振恆，凶
(*zhèn héng, xiōng*, "shaken constancy — ominous", 32.6). The structure suggests constancy is neither
movement but their keeping time — direction held while everything moves. Against 31 it completes the
hinge: there the youngest pair, one side still, the man below; here the eldest pair, 剛上而柔下 (*gāng
shàng ér róu xià*, "the firm above, the yielding below"), 31's order turned over — lasting because
both move.

**For the name.** **Constancy** — deepens: the signature makes it constancy of direction between two
movers, not of state, and the lines' warnings against over-depth and shaking confirm it. **Candidates
the signature suggests:** *Continuance* — survives: 不恆其德 (*bù héng qí dé*, "not continuing in his
integrity", 32.3) and 恆其德 (*héng qí dé*, "continuing in his integrity", 32.5) both read, and it
carries the 彖's 終則有始; *Renewal* — fails the lines: 不恆其德，或承之羞 (*bù héng qí dé, huò chéng
zhī xiū*, "not constant in integrity, and shame may follow", 32.3) would blame what the name praises.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Constancy is a direction kept, not a place held — and it fails at the ends: dug in too deep at the
start, shaken loose at the finish.** The judgment ties lasting to going: 利有攸往 ("advantageous to have
somewhere to go"). The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, draws 恆 ("constancy", proposed)
as 从心从舟，在二之閒上下 ("from heart and from boat, going up and down between two lines"), and adds 心以舟施，恆也
("the heart going as a boat goes: that is constancy"). A ferry between two banks, crossing and
crossing again. And the lines are hard on it: only one of the six, the second, is good without
condition. The rest show the ways constancy goes wrong.

### The polarities in it

Wind within, thunder without: the two poles whose odd line is at the base, crossed. Both trigrams
are defined by what lies underneath — wind open at its foot, thunder firm at its — and the text's
failures fall on those feet: line 1 digs, line 4 hunts an empty field. The figure named for lasting
is built from the two images that never stay, and the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, reads it as
their keeping time together: 雷風相與 ("thunder and wind give to each other"). **Nothing in this figure
is still; constancy is two movements keeping time with each other.** Here Wang Bi pulls the other
way — see line 6 — and the text sits between them: it condemns shaking, not moving.

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, is two words, 雷風 ("thunder, wind"). Watch a summer
storm come in. The wind arrives first — a sudden cold gust, leaves flipped pale side up, dust
lifting off the road — pushed out ahead of the rain by the storm's own falling air; the thunder
follows. Neither stays; the storm is over in an hour. Yet the storms come back every summer, in the
same weeks, from the same quarter. That is lasting as the Tuan sees it: 四時變化，而能久成 ("the four seasons
change, and so can complete for long"), and 終則有始也 ("at the end there is a beginning").

Then look at the Shuowen's ferry. A boat crossing a wide river does not hold still; it is pushed off
line by the current every minute and corrected every minute, and its course is the same each day.
The Great Image's direction is a boat too: 方 ("direction") is in the Shuowen 併船也 ("boats lashed side
by side"). Two boats in one name and image: a coincidence of the dictionary, noted, not built on.

And line 4's field. Game moves with the season, from summer grazing to winter cover; a hunter who
keeps to the ground where the deer were in June will find it empty in November. The Small Image, the
Wing on each line, says exactly that: 久非其位，安得禽也 ("long in a place not its own — how would it take
game?").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 恆：亨，无咎。利貞，利有攸往 ("Constancy. It gets through; no blame. Advantageous to hold firm;
advantageous to have somewhere to go."). Holding firm and going somewhere, in one breath.

**Line 1** — 浚恆，貞凶，无攸利 ("dredging for constancy: holding firm is ominous; nothing is advantageous").
The Shuowen gives 浚 ("dredging") as 杼也 ("to scoop out"). The Small Image: 始求深也 ("seeking depth at
the start"); Wang Bi: 求深窮底 ("seeking depth, scraping the bottom"). Wanting at once what only years
can give.

**Line 2** — 悔亡 ("regret goes"). Two characters, the whole line. The firm centre of the wind; the
Small Image: 能久中也 ("it can last in the middle").

**Line 3** — 不恆其德，或承之羞，貞吝 ("not constant in one's integrity: someone may hand one shame; holding
firm is stinting"). The Shuowen's 羞 ("shame") is 進獻也 ("to present", as food is) and 承 ("hand") is 奉也
("to hold up in both hands"): shame served like a dish. The Small Image gives it the phrase it gave
30's burning fourth line, 无所容也 ("there is nowhere that holds it").

**Line 4** — 田无禽 ("the hunt: no game"). Thunder's firm base moves out and meets nothing. Constancy
in the wrong place is only staying.

**Line 5** — 恆其德，貞，婦人吉，夫子凶 ("constant in integrity, holding firm: for the wife auspicious, for the
husband ominous"). The core text makes the verdict turn on the role: the same steadiness serves one
place and fails another. The Small Image explains it in its own age's terms of marriage — 從一而終也
("following one to the end") for her, 夫子制義 ("the husband decides what is right") for him — and that
explanation is the Wing's. What the line itself says is narrower, and still sharp: constancy is not
the same thing in every place.

**Line 6** — 振恆，凶 ("constancy shaken — ominous"). The Shuowen gives 振 ("shaken") as also 奮也 ("to
rouse"): constancy stirred up, at the top of thunder. The Small Image: 大无功也 ("great, and nothing
achieved"). Wang Bi draws the Laozi's lesson — 靜者可乆之道也 ("stillness is the Tao of what can last") —
which is his, not the figure's: nothing in this figure is still.

Constancy fails by excess at the start, by lapse, by inertia, and by agitation — and survives in the
middle.

### In a life now

This is the figure for a marriage in its twentieth year — the Xugua, the Wing on the sequence, sets
it here because 夫婦之道，不可以不久也 ("the Tao of husband and wife cannot but last") — and for any long
practice: an instrument, a craft, a garden, a friendship, the care of someone ill for years, an
institution. It counsels against four mistakes. **Do not demand at the start the depth only years
give** (line 1). **A lapse in integrity is served back to you** (line 3). **Loyalty to a place the
life has left is not constancy** (line 4). **Do not overhaul in agitation at the end** (line 6). And
it gives one plain good: **keep to the middle**, and regret goes (line 2).

The Great Image turns it to practice: 君子以立不易方 ("the noble one stands and does not change
direction"). Not *does not change* — 方 ("direction") is a heading. Everything around the noble one
moves, as wind and thunder do, and so may they; the heading holds.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One shared saying, and two reasons for it.** Chapter 7: *"The sky is lasting, the earth enduring.
/ Sky and earth can last and endure / because they do not live for themselves."* The Tuan agrees
that they last — 天地之道，恆久而不已也 ("the Tao of sky and earth is constant, lasting, and never stops") —
and the characters, 天地 ("sky and earth") and 久 ("lasting"), are in the layer being read, a Wing. The
English answers to them.

But the reasons differ. The Laozi's sky and earth last because they have no self to serve. The
Tuan's last because 終則有始也 ("at the end there is a beginning") — by turning over, like the seasons.
One is selflessness, the other rhythm; this reading does not merge them.

One contact was considered and set aside. This project's own glossary for 常 ("abiding") records that
the Mawangdui manuscripts of the Laozi wrote 恆 ("constancy") where the received text writes 常
("abiding") — changed under the Han because 恆 ("constancy") was the personal name of the reigning
ruler. So this hexagram's name once stood in the Laozi's most quoted lines. But Shalom's translation
renders the received 常 ("abiding"), so the English does not answer to 恆 ("constancy"); it fails the
second question, and it is a fact about manuscripts, not an echo.

### Words kept

- 恆 ("Constancy") — the proposed name (not yet approved); never *abiding* (that is 常 ("abiding"),
  locked — and the Shuowen glosses 恆 ("constancy") as 常也 ("abiding"), so the two need keeping
  apart); never *eternal*
- 久 ("lasting") — the Tuan's and the Xugua's gloss, not the core text's word
- 德 ("integrity") — never *virtue* (locked)
- 道 ("the Tao") — in the Tuan, the Xugua and Wang Bi; the core text of this hexagram does not write
  it
- 方 ("direction") — a heading
- 婦人 ("the wife") · 夫子 ("the husband") — the text's own roles, not this reading's
- 貞 ("holding firm") — working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 貞凶 ("holding firm is ominous") is
  "the inquiry: ominous"
- 吉 ("auspicious") · 凶 ("ominous") · 吝 ("stinting") · 无咎 ("no blame"); 悔亡 ("regret goes") — working
  glosses (A4)
