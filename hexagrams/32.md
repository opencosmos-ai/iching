## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving
out). **Crossed** — thunder ↔ wind, the yin pole within, the yang pole without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 恆，久也 (*héng, jiǔ yě*, "恆 is lasting"), 剛上而柔下，雷風相與，巽而動
(*gāng shàng ér róu xià, léi fēng xiāng yǔ, xùn ér dòng*, "the firm above, the yielding below; thunder
and wind give to each other; entering, and moving out"), and 終則有始也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ yě*, "at the
end there is a beginning") — 四時變化，而能久成 (*sì shí biàn huà, ér néng jiǔ chéng*, "the four seasons
change, and so can complete for long"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 雷風 (*léi fēng*, "thunder, wind"),
and the noble one 以立不易方 (*yǐ lì bù yì fāng*, "stands and does not change direction"). The
tradition reads lasting as a process that renews itself, not a state that stays.

**What the polarity implies.** The one figure named for lasting is built from the two images that
never stay: wind, which slips in beneath, and thunder, which breaks out from the base. Both odd lines
are base lines — the open first, the firm fourth — so each trigram's defining line is its
foundation, and constancy here is a matter of what is underneath. Wind within enters and roots;
thunder without moves out and sounds. In and out, in one rhythm — and the 說文 (*shuōwén*) picture of
the graph agrees: 从心从舟，在二之閒上下 (*cóng xīn cóng zhōu, zài èr zhī jiān shàng xià*, "heart and
boat, going up and down between two lines").

The lines fail at exactly those bases. At wind's open foot, 浚恆，貞凶 (*jùn héng, zhēn xiōng*,
"dredging for constancy: holding firm is ominous", 32.1) — the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) says 始求深也 (*shǐ qiú
shēn yě*, "seeking depth at the start"): entering too far, too soon. At thunder's firm foot, 田无禽
(*tián wú qín*, "hunting, no game", 32.4): moving out with nothing to meet. At thunder's top, 振恆，凶
(*zhèn héng, xiōng*, "shaken constancy — ominous", 32.6). The structure suggests constancy is neither
movement but their keeping time — direction held while everything moves. Against 31 it completes the
hinge: there the youngest pair, one side still, the man below; here the eldest pair, 剛上而柔下 (*gāng
shàng ér róu xià*, "the firm above, the yielding below"), 31's order turned over — lasting because
both move.

**For the name.** **Constancy** — deepens: the signature makes it constancy of direction between two
movers, not of state, and the lines' warnings against over-depth and shaking confirm it. **Candidates
the signature suggests:** *Continuance* — survives: 不恆其德 (*bù héng qí dé*, "not continuing in his
integrity", 32.3) and 恆其德 (*héng qí dé*, "continuing in his integrity", 32.5) both read, and it
carries the 彖's 終則有始; *Renewal* — fails the lines: 不恆其德，或承之羞 (*bù héng qí dé, huò chéng
zhī xiū*, "not constant in integrity, and shame may follow", 32.3) would blame what the name praises.
