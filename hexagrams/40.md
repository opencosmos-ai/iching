## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 *kǎn* · yang · sinking in). Without: **thunder** (震 *zhèn* · yang · moving out).
**Across** — the yang pole of water ↔ fire within, the yang pole of thunder ↔ wind without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 reads the two actions as an escape: 解，險以動，動而免乎險，解 (*xiè, xiǎn
yǐ dòng, dòng ér miǎn hū xiǎn, xiè*, "release: in hazard, and so moving; moving, and so free of hazard —
release"). It widens to a season: 天地解，而雷雨作，雷雨作，而百果草木皆甲坼 (*tiān dì xiè, ér léi yǔ zuò; léi
yǔ zuò, ér bǎi guǒ cǎo mù jiē jiǎ chè*, "sky and earth release, and thunder and rain rise; thunder and
rain rise, and the husks of every fruit, grass and tree split open"). The 大象: 雷雨作，解；君子以赦過宥罪
(*léi yǔ zuò, xiè; jūnzǐ yǐ shè guò yòu zuì*, "thunder and rain rise: release; the noble one pardons
faults and forgives crimes"). For the tradition, release is the spring storm — and mercy.

**What the polarity implies.** 39 turned over. The water that lay ahead now lies beneath and behind,
and above it is thunder, moving out. Both trigrams are yang, one ruling firm line each, and the figure
(`010100`) shows what happens to that hardness: the water's firm line, trapped in the middle of the pit,
reappears two steps up as thunder's firm line at the base — the push from beneath. The same hard thing
that was held is now the foot that kicks off. That is the 彖's split husk exactly: a hard core inside
something enclosing it, pressing out.

The lines put the release at that very place. 解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*, "release your big toe") is 九四, the
firm base line of thunder, and 說卦 says 震為足 (*zhèn wéi zú*, "thunder is the foot"): the leg hobbled in
39 is untied at the line the figure makes the foot. The structure also accounts for the 大象's pardon —
water sinking in is the weight of the fault, thunder moving out is its being let go — and for 雜卦's 解緩也
(*xiè huǎn yě*, "release is slackening"): after the storm, the air loosens. And the judgment says release
is a moment, taken quickly: 有攸往，夙吉 (*yǒu yōu wǎng, sù jí*, "if there is somewhere to go, going early is
auspicious").

**For the name.** **Release** — the signature supports and deepens it: a hard core that moves from being
trapped to being the push is release, not rescue, which keeps *Deliverance*'s devotional register out.
(*Releasing* is this project's English for 兌's action 說 (*yuè*); 40 has no lake in its signature, so the
principle does not bar it, but the two will sit close in the set.) **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Untying*, *Breaking Open* — *Untying* survives the lines, and answers 39's hobble directly: 解而拇 (*xiè ér
mǔ*, "untie your big toe"), 君子維有解 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè*, "the noble one, bound, has an untying"); *Breaking
Open*, the 彖's 甲坼 (*jiǎ chè*, "husks splitting"), fails the lines — 解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*) cannot be "break
open your big toe", and 君子維有解 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè*) needs something bound, not something shut.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Release is the knot coming loose — take it early, and if there is nowhere to go, go home.** The
Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 解 ("release") as 判也 ("to divide"), and reads the
graph's parts as 刀判牛角 ("a knife parting the horn from the ox") — the butcher's separation, along the
places where a thing already comes apart. The core text uses the word for what is tied: 解而拇 ("loosen
your big toe"), 君子維有解 ("the noble one, bound, has a loosening"). And the judgment is about timing:
无所往，其來復吉。有攸往，夙吉 ("with nowhere to go, coming back is auspicious; with somewhere to go, early is
auspicious"). Release is a moment, not a place to stay.

### The polarities in it

Water within, thunder without: 39 turned over (see *The signature*, above). The hazard that lay
ahead now lies below and behind, and above it something moves out. The Tuan, the Wing on the
judgment, says it as a sequence: 險以動，動而免乎險 ("in hazard, and so moving; moving, and so free of
hazard"). The firm line held in the middle of the water becomes, in the upper half, the firm line at
thunder's base — the hard thing that was trapped is now the foot that pushes off. The lines put the
loosening exactly there: 解而拇 ("loosen your big toe") is line 4, thunder's first line, and the
Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams, says 震為足 ("thunder is the foot").

### In nature

雷雨作 ("thunder and rain rise"): the first storms of spring. Before them the air is heavy and close;
after them it slackens, and the word of the Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams, for this
hexagram is exactly that — 解緩也 ("release is slackening"). The Tuan watches what the rain does to the
ground: 百果草木皆甲坼 ("the husks of every fruit, grass and tree split open"). That is how seeds wake. The
coat takes up water, swells and splits, and the first root comes out through the crack; look at a
bean or an acorn a day or two after heavy rain on warm soil.

The lines have their creatures. Foxes, 狐 ("fox"), work the fields at dusk and dawn, listening for
voles and pouncing; Wang Bi calls the fox 隱伏之物 ("a thing that hides and lies low"), and the Shuowen
says 鬼所乘之 ("ghosts ride it"). The falcon, 隼 ("falcon"), of the top line perches wherever is highest
— cliff, tower, or, as here, 高墉 ("a high wall"); peregrines still take to city walls and church
towers for the view. And the judgment's 夙 ("early") has its hour: the dawn after a night storm, the
air washed, the ground soft enough to show every track.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 解：利西南 ("Release. Advantageous to the southwest"), then the timing already quoted.
It keeps 39's southwest and drops its "not to the northeast", and Wang Bi notices: 故不言不利東北也 ("so it
does not say 'not advantageous to the northeast'"). After release, no direction is closed.

**Line 1.** 无咎 ("no blame"). Two characters and nothing else. The knot has just come loose, and
there is nothing to add. The Small Image, the Wing on each line, places it 剛柔之際 ("where firm and
soft meet").

**Line 2.** 田獲三狐，得黃矢，貞吉 ("hunting, takes three foxes and gets a yellow arrow — holding firm,
auspicious"). The firm centre of the water: one who knows the hazard from inside catches what hides
in it. The Shuowen's 黃 ("yellow") is 地之色也 ("the colour of earth"), and Wang Bi reads the arrow as
straightness and yellow as the middle.

**Line 3.** 負且乘，致寇至，貞吝 ("carrying a load on the back and riding in a carriage — it brings the
raiders; holding firm, stinting"). The Shuowen draws 負 ("to carry on the back") as 人守貝 ("a person
guarding valuables"). Keeping both the burden and the seat, at the top of the hazard, invites the
robbery; the Small Image says 自我致戎，又誰咎也 ("I brought the attack on myself — whom else to blame?").
Here holding firm is the fault: the release is refused.

**Line 4.** 解而拇，朋至斯孚 ("loosen your big toe — friends arrive, and there is confidence"). The
Shuowen's 拇 ("big toe") is 將指也 ("the leading digit"). The foot hobbled in 39 is untied here, and
39's 朋來 ("friends come") returns as 朋至 ("friends arrive").

**Line 5.** 君子維有解，吉；有孚于小人 ("the noble one, bound, has a loosening — auspicious; there is confidence
even among small people"). 維 ("bound") is a cord. The yielding centre does not snap it; it finds
where it gives. The Small Image: 小人退也 ("small people withdraw").

**Line 6.** 公用射隼，于高墉之上，獲之，无不利 ("the lord shoots a falcon on top of a high wall, and takes it —
nothing not advantageous"). At thunder's top, the last knot is not untied but brought down. The
Shuowen gives 墉 ("wall") as 城垣也 ("a city wall"), and the Small Image says 以解悖也 ("to release what is
twisted").

### In a life now

This is the figure for the morning after: the diagnosis that proved treatable, the debt cleared, the
long argument finally over, the drought broken. The text is exact about what to do with it. If there
is nowhere you need to go, go home and settle again (the judgment's 來復 ("coming back")). If there is
something to do, do it early, before the slackness sets in. Put down what you were carrying, and do
not try to keep your seat as well (line 3). Loosen what binds the foot, and company comes (line 4).
And some tangles do not come undone: they have to be brought down, once and cleanly (line 6).

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, turns release toward others: 君子以赦過宥罪 ("the noble one
pardons faults and forgives crimes"). **That is the Wing's reading; the core text does not say it**
— the core's release includes a hunt and a shot. But the image fits the storm. The Shuowen draws 罪
("crime") as 捕魚竹网 ("a bamboo net for catching fish"), and gives 赦 ("pardon") as 置也 ("to set down").
To pardon is to let the net go slack.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A graph shared, and a disagreement about knots.** Chapter 27, in Shalom's translation: *"Masterful
tying uses no ropes, yet cannot be untied."* The last word is 解 ("untied") — read one way there,
another as this hexagram's name, the same graph — and it stands in 40's core text at lines 4 and 5.
Shalom's *untied* answers to it; the test passes. Chapters 4 and 56 have the graph again:
*"untangles the knots."*

They part over the cord. The Laozi's master ties without cords, so there is nothing to undo, and its
untangling is done by blunting the sharp and joining the dust. 40's noble one is already bound — 維
("a cord") — and the text does not wish the cord away; it finds the loosening. And its release ends
with an arrow in a falcon on a wall. The Laozi's untying is soft; the I Ching's includes a shot.
Neither is softened here to match the other.
