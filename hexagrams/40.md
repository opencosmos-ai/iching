## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 *kǎn* · yang · sinking in). Without: **thunder** (震 *zhèn* · yang · moving out).
**Across** — the yang pole of water ↔ fire within, the yang pole of thunder ↔ wind without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 reads the two actions as an escape: 解，險以動，動而免乎險，解 (*xiè, xiǎn
yǐ dòng, dòng ér miǎn hū xiǎn, xiè*, "release: in hazard, and so moving; moving, and so free of hazard —
release"). It widens to a season: 天地解，而雷雨作，雷雨作，而百果草木皆甲坼 (*tiān dì xiè, ér léi yǔ zuò; léi
yǔ zuò, ér bǎi guǒ cǎo mù jiē jiǎ chè*, "sky and earth release, and thunder and rain rise; thunder and
rain rise, and the husks of every fruit, grass and tree split open"). The 大象: 雷雨作，解；君子以赦過宥罪
(*léi yǔ zuò, xiè; jūnzǐ yǐ shè guò yòu zuì*, "thunder and rain rise: release; the noble one pardons
faults and forgives crimes"). For the tradition, release is the spring storm — and mercy.

**What the polarity implies.** 39 turned over. The water that lay ahead now lies beneath and behind,
and above it is thunder, moving out. Both trigrams are yang, one ruling firm line each, and the figure
(`010100`) shows what happens to that hardness: the water's firm line, trapped in the middle of the pit,
reappears two steps up as thunder's firm line at the base — the push from beneath. The same hard thing
that was held is now the foot that kicks off. That is the 彖's split husk exactly: a hard core inside
something enclosing it, pressing out.

The lines put the release at that very place. 解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*, "release your big toe") is 九四, the
firm base line of thunder, and 說卦 says 震為足 (*zhèn wéi zú*, "thunder is the foot"): the leg hobbled in
39 is untied at the line the figure makes the foot. The structure also accounts for the 大象's pardon —
water sinking in is the weight of the fault, thunder moving out is its being let go — and for 雜卦's 解緩也
(*xiè huǎn yě*, "release is slackening"): after the storm, the air loosens. And the judgment says release
is a moment, taken quickly: 有攸往，夙吉 (*yǒu yōu wǎng, sù jí*, "if there is somewhere to go, going early is
auspicious").

**For the name.** **Release** — the signature supports and deepens it: a hard core that moves from being
trapped to being the push is release, not rescue, which keeps *Deliverance*'s devotional register out.
(*Releasing* is this project's English for 兌's action 說 (*yuè*); 40 has no lake in its signature, so the
principle does not bar it, but the two will sit close in the set.) **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Untying*, *Breaking Open* — *Untying* survives the lines, and answers 39's hobble directly: 解而拇 (*xiè ér
mǔ*, "untie your big toe"), 君子維有解 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè*, "the noble one, bound, has an untying"); *Breaking
Open*, the 彖's 甲坼 (*jiǎ chè*, "husks splitting"), fails the lines — 解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*) cannot be "break
open your big toe", and 君子維有解 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè*) needs something bound, not something shut.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Release is the knot coming loose — take it early, and if there is nowhere to go, go home.**
說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 解 (*xiè*) as 判也 (*pàn yě*, "to divide"), and reads the graph's parts as
刀判牛角 (*dāo pàn niú jiǎo*, "a knife parting the horn from the ox") — the butcher's separation,
along the places where a thing already comes apart. The core text uses the word for what is tied:
解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*, "loosen your big toe"), 君子維有解 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè*, "the noble one, bound,
has a loosening"). And the judgment is about timing: 无所往，其來復吉。有攸往，夙吉 (*wú suǒ wǎng, qí
lái fù jí. yǒu yōu wǎng, sù jí*, "with nowhere to go, coming back is auspicious; with somewhere to
go, early is auspicious"). Release is a moment, not a place to stay.

### The polarities in it

Water within, thunder without: 39 turned over (see *The signature*, above). The hazard that lay
ahead now lies below and behind, and above it something moves out. The 彖 says it as a sequence:
險以動，動而免乎險 (*xiǎn yǐ dòng, dòng ér miǎn hū xiǎn*, "in hazard, and so moving; moving, and so
free of hazard"). The firm line held in the middle of the water becomes, in the upper half, the
firm line at thunder's base — the hard thing that was trapped is now the foot that pushes off. The
lines put the loosening exactly there: 解而拇 (*xiè ér mǔ*, "loosen your big toe") is line 4,
thunder's first line, and 說卦 says 震為足 (*zhèn wéi zú*, "thunder is the foot").

### In nature

雷雨作 (*léi yǔ zuò*, "thunder and rain rise"): the first storms of spring. Before them the air is
heavy and close; after them it slackens, and 雜卦's word for this hexagram is exactly that — 解緩也
(*xiè huǎn yě*, "release is slackening"). The 彖 watches what the rain does to the ground: 百果草木皆甲坼
(*bǎi guǒ cǎo mù jiē jiǎ chè*, "the husks of every fruit, grass and tree split open"). That is how
seeds wake. The coat takes up water, swells and splits, and the first root comes out through the
crack; look at a bean or an acorn a day or two after heavy rain on warm soil.

The lines have their creatures. Foxes (狐 *hú*) work the fields at dusk and dawn, listening for voles
and pouncing; 王弼 calls the fox 隱伏之物 (*yǐn fú zhī wù*, "a thing that hides and lies low"), and 說文
says 鬼所乘之 (*guǐ suǒ chéng zhī*, "ghosts ride it"). The falcon (隼 *sǔn*) of the top line perches
wherever is highest — cliff, tower, or, as here, 高墉 (*gāo yōng*, "a high wall"); peregrines still
take to city walls and church towers for the view. And the judgment's 夙 (*sù*, "early") has its
hour: the dawn after a night storm, the air washed, the ground soft enough to show every track.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 解：利西南 (*xiè: lì xīnán*, "Release. Advantageous to the southwest"), then the
timing already quoted. It keeps 39's southwest and drops its "not to the northeast", and 王弼 notices:
故不言不利東北也 (*gù bù yán bù lì dōngběi yě*, "so it does not say 'not advantageous to the
northeast'"). After release, no direction is closed.

**Line 1.** 无咎 (*wú jiù*, "no blame"). Two characters and nothing else. The knot has just come
loose, and there is nothing to add. The 小象 places it 剛柔之際 (*gāng róu zhī jì*, "where firm and soft
meet").

**Line 2.** 田獲三狐，得黃矢，貞吉 (*tián huò sān hú, dé huáng shǐ, zhēn jí*, "hunting, takes three
foxes and gets a yellow arrow — holding firm, auspicious"). The firm centre of the water: one who
knows the hazard from inside catches what hides in it. 說文's 黃 (*huáng*) is 地之色也 (*dì zhī sè yě*,
"the colour of earth"), and 王弼 reads the arrow as straightness and yellow as the middle.

**Line 3.** 負且乘，致寇至，貞吝 (*fù qiě chéng, zhì kòu zhì, zhēn lìn*, "carrying a load on the back
and riding in a carriage — it brings the raiders; holding firm, stinting"). 說文 draws 負 (*fù*) as 人守貝
(*rén shǒu bèi*, "a person guarding valuables"). Keeping both the burden and the seat, at the top of
the hazard, invites the robbery; the 小象 says 自我致戎，又誰咎也 (*zì wǒ zhì róng, yòu shéi jiù yě*,
"I brought the attack on myself — whom else to blame?"). Here holding firm is the fault: the
release is refused.

**Line 4.** 解而拇，朋至斯孚 (*xiè ér mǔ, péng zhì sī fú*, "loosen your big toe — friends arrive, and
there is confidence"). 說文's 拇 (*mǔ*) is 將指也 (*jiāng zhǐ yě*, "the leading digit"). The foot
hobbled in 39 is untied here, and 39's 朋來 (*péng lái*, "friends come") returns as 朋至 (*péng zhì*,
"friends arrive").

**Line 5.** 君子維有解，吉；有孚于小人 (*jūnzǐ wéi yǒu xiè, jí; yǒu fú yú xiǎo rén*, "the noble one,
bound, has a loosening — auspicious; there is confidence even among small people"). 維 (*wéi*) is a
cord. The yielding centre does not snap it; it finds where it gives. The 小象: 小人退也 (*xiǎo rén tuì
yě*, "small people withdraw").

**Line 6.** 公用射隼，于高墉之上，獲之，无不利 (*gōng yòng shè sǔn, yú gāo yōng zhī shàng, huò zhī, wú
bù lì*, "the lord shoots a falcon on top of a high wall, and takes it — nothing not advantageous").
At thunder's top, the last knot is not untied but brought down. 說文 gives 墉 (*yōng*) as 城垣也 (*chéng
yuán yě*, "a city wall"), and the 小象 says 以解悖也 (*yǐ xiè bèi yě*, "to release what is twisted").

### In a life now

This is the figure for the morning after: the diagnosis that proved treatable, the debt cleared,
the long argument finally over, the drought broken. The text is exact about what to do with it. If
there is nowhere you need to go, go home and settle again (the judgment's 來復, *lái fù*, "coming
back"). If there is something to do, do it early, before the slackness sets in. Put down what you
were carrying, and do not try to keep your seat as well (line 3). Loosen what binds the foot, and
company comes (line 4). And some tangles do not come undone: they have to be brought down, once and
cleanly (line 6).

The 大象 turns release toward others: 君子以赦過宥罪 (*jūnzǐ yǐ shè guò yòu zuì*, "the noble one
pardons faults and forgives crimes"). **That is the Wing's reading; the core text does not say it** —
the core's release includes a hunt and a shot. But the image fits the storm. 說文 draws 罪 (*zuì*) as
捕魚竹网 (*bǔ yú zhú wǎng*, "a bamboo net for catching fish"), and gives 赦 (*shè*) as 置也 (*zhì yě*,
"to set down"). To pardon is to let the net go slack.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A graph shared, and a disagreement about knots.** Chapter 27, in Shalom's translation:
*"Masterful tying uses no ropes, yet cannot be untied."* The last word is 解 — read *jiě* there,
*xiè* as this hexagram's name, the same graph — and it stands in 40's core text at lines 4 and 5.
Shalom's *untied* answers to it; the test passes. Chapters 4 and 56 have the graph again: *"untangles
the knots."*

They part over the cord. The Laozi's master ties without cords, so there is nothing to undo, and
its untangling is done by blunting the sharp and joining the dust. 40's noble one is already bound —
維 (*wéi*, "a cord") — and the text does not wish the cord away; it finds the loosening. And its
release ends with an arrow in a falcon on a wall. The Laozi's untying is soft; the I Ching's
includes a shot. Neither is softened here to match the other.

### Words kept

- 解 (*xiè*) — *release* (proposed); *loosen* where the lines tie (解而拇, *xiè ér mǔ*); not
  *deliverance*, whose register is devotional
- 公 (*gōng*) — *lord* (locked, an office) · 利 (*lì*) — *advantageous* (locked)
- 君子 · 小人 (*jūnzǐ · xiǎo rén*) — *the noble one · small people*
- 險 (*xiǎn*) — *hazard*, never *danger*
- 赦 · 罪 (*shè · zuì*) — *pardon · crime*, the 大象's words, not the core's
- 孚 · 貞 · 吝 · 无咎 (*fú · zhēn · lìn · wú jiù*) — *confidence · holding firm · stinting · no blame*,
  working glosses (A4, A6); at line 3, the older reading of 貞 — "the inquiry: stinting" — is as live
  as *holding firm*
