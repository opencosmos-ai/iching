## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake and water ↔ fire; yang within, yin without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 reads the traveller's small success as 止而麗乎明 (*zhǐ ér lì hū míng*,
"stopping, and attaching to clarity"), and places the good line outside: 柔得中乎外，而順乎剛 (*róu dé
zhōng hū wài, ér shùn hū gāng*, "the yielding gains the centre on the outside, and goes along with the
firm"). The 大象傳 sees 山上有火 (*shān shàng yǒu huǒ*, "fire on the mountain"), and from it 君子以明慎用刑，而不留獄
(*jūnzǐ yǐ míng shèn yòng xíng, ér bù liú yù*, "the noble one is clear and careful in using penalties,
and does not let cases linger").

**What the polarity implies.** Fire on a mountain does not stay. It clings to what it can reach, burns it
and moves on across ground that does not move. The mountain within is the traveller's own closed self,
shut at its top. The fire without is his whole outer life, hollow at its centre: whatever he attaches
himself to belongs to someone else. The lines draw the cost: what he attaches to, he burns. 旅焚其次 (*lǚ fén qí cì*,
"the traveller burns his lodging"), and at the top 鳥焚其巢 (*niǎo fén qí cháo*, "the bird burns its nest"),
then 旅人先笑后號咷 (*lǚ rén xiān xiào hòu háotáo*, "the traveller laughs first and wails after"). Even
the good line is a single shot: 射雉一矢亡 (*shè zhì yī shǐ wáng*, "he shoots a pheasant and loses the one
arrow"). The structure suggests the traveller's rule. Hold within, attach lightly without; keep nothing
long, as the 大象傳 keeps no case. Turned over it is 55, where the light is inside and a house is heaped over
it. Here the self is inside and there is no house at all. 序卦 (*xùguà*, "the Sequence") sets the hinge between them: 窮大者必失其居
(*qióng dà zhě bì shī qí jū*, "who takes greatness to its end must lose his dwelling").

**For the name.** **The Traveller** — the signature supports it: a fixed self moving through places it can
only cling to is the traveller's condition, and 旅 (*lǚ*) is the subject of every line. **Candidates the signature
suggests:** *The Guest* — survives: 旅于處 (*lǚ yú chǔ*, "the guest at a resting-place"), 旅即次 (*lǚ jí cì*,
"the guest comes to a lodging"); *Wildfire* — fails the lines: the 大象傳's image, but 旅 (*lǚ*) is a person in every
line, and the fire in 旅焚其次 is his misfortune, not himself.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Away from home, among things that belong to others: small things get through, and only for the
one who holds firm.** The judgment says it twice over: 旅：小亨，旅貞吉 (*lǚ: xiǎo hēng, lǚ zhēn jí*,
"The traveller: small things get through; for the traveller, holding firm is auspicious"). 王弼 hears
the repeated 旅 (*lǚ*, "traveller") as a limit — 唯足以爲旅之貞吉 (*wéi zú yǐ wéi lǚ zhī zhēn jí*,
"it suffices only for a traveller's holding firm to be auspicious"): good fortune of a traveller's
size. 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives the graph as 軍之五百人爲旅 (*jūn zhī wǔ bǎi rén wéi lǚ*, "five hundred
men of an army make a 旅"), a banner (㫃) over people following it — a company on the move. In the
lines the company has shrunk to one person, a servant, a purse and an axe, lodging in places that
are someone else's. **The Traveller** *(proposed)* has no house; everything turns on how it treats
what it touches.

### The polarities in it

Mountain within, fire without. The mountain stops at its closed top — a self that keeps its own
counsel; the fire is hollow at its centre and lives by attaching to what it can reach, owning none
of it (see *The signature*, above). The 彖 (*tuàn*) gives the good form: 止而麗乎明 (*zhǐ ér lì hū
míng*, "stopping, and attaching to clarity"). The text sharpens the structure. Both burnings fall on
firm lines — line 3, the mountain's top at the seam, and line 6, the top of the fire — and both good
lines, 2 and 5, are the soft centres. In a place not your own, the yielding middle does well and the
firm edge sets things alight.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 山上有火 (*shān shàng yǒu huǒ*, "fire on the mountain"). Watch a hill fire,
or the photographs of one at night: a line of flame along a ridge, visible for miles. Fire runs
uphill fast, because the flames lean into the slope and dry the fuel above them; it burns what is
there and moves on, and it cannot stay, because it leaves nothing to hold it. That is the
traveller's condition and the traveller's danger in one image.

說卦 (*shuōguà*) ch 8 gives fire its creature: 離為雉 (*lí wéi zhì*, "fire is the pheasant"). The
pheasant is the fifth line's bird. It keeps to the ground and runs through cover, and when flushed
it bursts up almost vertically with a clatter of wings, flies low and fast, and drops back into the
brush. It is a hard shot, and an arrow that misses is lost in the undergrowth. The top line's bird
nests high — 說文 gives 巢 (*cháo*, "nest") as 鳥在木上曰巢 (*niǎo zài mù shàng yuē cháo*, "a bird's
place up in a tree is a 巢") — and a fire climbing the slope reaches the highest thing last and
takes it completely. Even the sound is exact: 說文 glosses 咷 (*táo*, "wailing") as a child crying
and not stopping.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 旅：小亨，旅貞吉 — "The traveller: small things get through; for the traveller,
holding firm is auspicious." Small is the right size.

**Line 1** is petty. 旅瑣瑣，斯其所取災 (*lǚ suǒ suǒ, sī qí suǒ qǔ zāi*, "the traveller fussing over
trifles: this is where calamity is taken on"). 說文 gives 瑣 (*suǒ*) as 玉聲也 (*yù shēng yě*, "the
sound of jade") — the small clink of pendants. Fretting over small things invites the large ones.

**Line 2** is the mountain's centre, and it settles. 旅即次，懷其資，得童僕貞 (*lǚ jí cì, huái qí zī,
dé tóng pú zhēn*, "the traveller comes to a lodging, keeps the means close, and gains a young servant
who holds firm"). 說文 gives 次 (*cì*, "lodging") as 不前 (*bù qián*, "not going forward") — a halt —
and 資 (*zī*, "means") as 貨也 (*huò yě*, "goods").

**Line 3** is the seam, and burns. 旅焚其次，喪其童僕，貞厲 (*lǚ fén qí cì, sàng qí tóng pú, zhēn lì*,
"the traveller burns the lodging, loses the young servant; holding firm is peril"). Here even
holding firm is dangerous. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) gives the cause: 以旅與下，其義喪也 (*yǐ lǚ yǔ xià,
qí yì sàng yě*, "dealing with those below as a stranger does, what binds them is lost").

**Line 4** has what it needs and is not at home. 旅于處，得其資斧，我心不快 (*lǚ yú chǔ, dé qí zī fǔ,
wǒ xīn bù kuài*, "the traveller at a resting-place, gets means and an axe: my heart is not glad").
王弼 reads the axe as for clearing thorns round the lodging. It is the one line that speaks as "I".

**Line 5** is the fire's soft centre. 射雉一矢亡，終以譽命 (*shè zhì yī shǐ wáng, zhōng yǐ yù mìng*,
"shooting a pheasant, one arrow lost; in the end, praise, and a charge spoken over one"). A small
loss, gracefully taken, and standing given by others.

**Line 6** burns at the top. 鳥焚其巢，旅人先笑后號咷。喪牛于易，凶 (*niǎo fén qí cháo, lǚ rén xiān
xiào hòu háotáo. sàng niú yú yì, xiōng*, "the bird's nest burns; the traveller laughs first and
wails after. The ox is lost at 易 — ominous"). 王弼 reads 易 (*yì*) as "ease": 喪牛於易，不在於難 (*sàng
niú yú yì, bù zài yú nán*, "the ox was lost in ease, not in hardship"). It may also be a place name,
as in 34.5 喪羊于易 (*sàng yáng yú yì*, "the sheep lost at Yì"). Either way, the ox — the working
wealth — goes when things seem easiest.

### In a life now

This is the figure for being where you do not belong by right: a new city, work inside someone
else's institution, a long hospital stay, a guest in a partner's family, a season of touring, life
as a newcomer in another country, fieldwork, exile. The text counsels a small, steady stance. Do not
fret over trifles (line 1). Keep your means close and treat well the people who help you (line 2);
the lodging burns when the guest behaves as though the house were theirs (line 3). Expect to have
what you need and still not feel at home (line 4). Take a small loss with grace (line 5). Beware the
height, where the laugh comes first (line 6). The 雜卦 (*záguà*) sets the pair side by side:
豐多故也，旅寡親也 (*fēng duō gù yě, lǚ guǎ qīn yě*, "abundance: many ties; the traveller: few
kin").

The 大象 turns the fire into justice: 君子以明慎用刑，而不留獄 (*jūnzǐ yǐ míng shèn yòng xíng, ér bù
liú yù*, "the noble one sees clearly and is careful in using penalties, and does not let cases
linger"). Like fire on a mountain — pass through, decide, do not hold anyone longer than needed.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Thematic only, and useful mostly for where the two books part.** The Laozi's chapter 47:
*"Without leaving home, you know the whole world. / … The further out you venture, the less you
know."* 旅 does not occur in the Laozi, so this fails the two-question test and is kept as
commentary. The parting is real. The Laozi's sage need not go; 56's traveller must, and the 彖 says
so without regret: 旅之時義大矣哉 (*lǚ zhī shí yì dà yǐ zāi*, "great indeed is the meaning of the
traveller's time"). The I Ching does not tell the traveller to stay home. It tells them how to be a
guest.
