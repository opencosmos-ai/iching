## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **water** (坎 · yang ·
sinking in). **Across** — thunder ↔ wind meets water ↔ fire, yang on both sides, the odd line at the
base within and at the middle without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 剛柔始交而難生，動乎險中 (*gāng róu shǐ jiāo ér nán
shēng, dòng hū xiǎn zhōng*), "firm and yielding first meet and difficulty is born; it moves in the
middle of the hazard"; and 雷雨之動滿盈 (*léi yǔ zhī dòng mǎn yíng*), "the moving of thunder and rain
fills everything to the brim." The 大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image) reads the upper trigram as
cloud, rain not yet fallen: 雲雷，屯；君子以經綸 (*yún léi, zhūn; jūnzǐ yǐ jīng lún*), "cloud and
thunder: 屯 (*zhūn*); the noble one, by it, lays the warp and twists the thread." The tradition reads
the first, crowded difficulty of things coming to be, and the work of ordering it.

**What the polarity implies.** Two yang trigrams, so two rulers — and they sit at different heights.
Within, the firm line is at the very bottom, pushing up and out; without, the firm line is held in
the middle of the water, sunk and closed round. Thunder rises, water descends: they move *into* each
other, which is why the 彖 finds everything full. But full is not open. The figure has exactly two
firm lines, and the lines say what each is doing: 初九 (*chū jiǔ*, the firm first line) 磐桓 (*pán
huán*), "circling round a great stone" — the push at the base cannot go yet; 九五 (*jiǔ wǔ*, the
firm fifth) 屯其膏 (*zhūn qí gāo*), "hoarding its fat" — the core above keeps its richness to itself.
The four open lines between are caught in the traffic, and three of them repeat 乘馬班如 (*chéng mǎ
bān rú*), "riding horses, milling about."

The structure suggests the difficulty here is not weakness but two initiatives not yet in touch:
a shoot with all its force at the tip, under a ground that has a hard, wet core. 4 turns this over —
the thunder at the base becomes a mountain at the top, *moving out* turned into *holding*, and the
force that was pushing up is now sunk and capped.

**For the name.** **Sprouting** — the signature deepens it: a firm line at the base moving out,
beneath a firm core sinking in, is the sprout under the crust that 說文 (*shuōwén*) pictures, and it
says the struggle is the sprout's own force, not a lack. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Crowding* — from 滿盈 (*mǎn yíng*, "filled to the brim") and two firm lines with the open ones
jammed between; survives the lines, including 乘馬班如 ("riding horses, milling about") and 屯其膏
(*zhūn qí gāo*), which reads naturally as crowding in, hoarding. *Breaking Through* — from thunder's
moving out; fails the lines: no line breaks through, and the top one ends 泣血漣如 (*qì xuè lián
rú*), "weeping blood in streams."
