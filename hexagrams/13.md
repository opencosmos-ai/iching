## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — water ↔ fire under sky ↔ earth.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) finds the name in one line: 柔得位得中，而應乎乾
(*róu dé wèi dé zhōng, ér yìng hū qián*, "the yielding gets its place and the centre, and answers
乾, the sky"). It reads the two actions as 文明以健 (*wén míng yǐ jiàn*, "pattern and clarity, with
vigour"), and ends 唯君子為能通天下之志 (*wéi jūnzǐ wéi néng tōng tiān xià zhī zhì*, "only the noble
one can get through to the aims of all under the sky"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 天與火 (*tiān yǔ
huǒ*, "sky with fire"), and the noble one 以類族辨物 (*yǐ lèi zú biàn wù*, "sorts clans by kind and
tells things apart") — a gathering that still knows what differs.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire rises, and the sky is above: this is a figure whose two
trigrams have the same heading. 11 is two things meeting from opposite sides; 13 is two things
going the same way, which is what 同 (*tóng*, "together, the same") says. Fire attaches — it has
nothing of its own and lives on what it clings to — and its odd line is the hollow at its middle,
line 2, the only yielding line in the figure. A gathering, the structure suggests, needs an empty
centre that others can come into. But attaching clings to what is nearest, and the lines mark that
exactly on fire's own line: 同人于宗，吝 (*tóng rén yú zōng, lìn*, "people together in the clan:
narrowness", 13.2). Left to itself, fire makes a faction. The vigour above is what carries it out,
and the lines travel outward with it — 于門 (*yú mén*, "at the gate", 13.1), through the ambush and
the walls, to 于郊 (*yú jiāo*, "at the outskirts", 13.6), and the judgment's 于野 (*yú yě*, "in the
open country"). Against 14, its figure turned over: here the one open line is at the heart of the
self (line 2); there it sits in the ruler's place (line 5).

**For the name.** **People Together** — supports, and the signature deepens *together* into
*going the same way*, which is why the name holds when the lines turn to armies. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Joining People* — survives: 同人于宗，吝 (*tóng rén yú zōng, lìn*) reads as "joining people in the clan:
narrowness", and 大師克相遇 (*dà shī kè xiāng yù*, "the great armies prevail and meet", 13.5) as the
joining of two forces; *Kindred* — fails the lines: the kin gathering 于宗 (*yú zōng*, "in the clan") is the one line marked
吝 (*lìn*, "narrowness"), so a kin word would name the figure by its fault.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**People together get through only in the open country — past the gate, past the clan, past the
wall — and the lines mark each place on the way where togetherness closes into a faction or a
fight.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 同 (*tóng*) as 合會也 (*hé huì yě*, "to meet and join"), and the
judgment puts the meeting in one place: 同人于野 (*tóng rén yú yě*, "people together in the open
country"). 野 (*yě*) is, in 說文, 郊外也 (*jiāo wài yě*, "beyond the outskirts") — ground that belongs
to no household and is fenced by no one. The six lines walk out toward it from a doorway, and not
one of them arrives. This hexagram is a direction more than a state: together, and going outward.

### The polarities in it

Fire within, sky without. Fire's one open line is at its centre, and here that line — line 2 — is
the only yielding line of the six. So the place where others can come in is the heart of the lower
figure, and that is exactly where the lines put the clan and call it 吝 (*lìn*, "stinting"). Fire
attaches to whatever is nearest; left to itself, the open centre fills with kin. The sky does not
attach; it carries, and it carries upward, the way fire already goes. 王弼 reads the 大象 as both
poles heading one way: 天體於上而火炎上 (*tiān tǐ yú shàng ér huǒ yán shàng*, "the sky's body is
above, and fire flames upward"). **The warmth of the gathering is fire's; the reach is the sky's;
and the figure asks the first to follow the second out of the gate.**

### In nature

Light a fire in the open at night and watch it. The flames go up whichever way the wood is laid,
and heat and smoke go on up into the dark: 天與火 (*tiān yǔ huǒ*, "sky with fire", 大象), fire under
the sky and moving toward it. Then watch the people. They come in out of the dark to the one lit
place and stand in a ring, facing inward, every face lit and every face different. The 大象 draws
that counsel from the image: 君子以類族辨物 (*jūnzǐ yǐ lèi zú biàn wù*, "the noble one sorts clans by
kind and tells things apart"). Firelight gathers people, and it also shows them apart.

The third line holds a hunter's picture. 說文 builds 伏 (*fú*, "lie in wait") from a person and a
dog, and gives 莽 (*mǎng*, "thicket") as 犬善逐菟艸中 (*quǎn shàn zhú tù cǎo zhōng*, "a dog good at
chasing hares through grass"). Anyone who has watched a hound in long grass knows the posture:
pressed flat, eyes up, able to hold still a very long time and never rise.

And the ground is graded. 說文 measures 郊 (*jiāo*, "the outskirts") as 距國百里爲郊 (*jù guó bǎi lǐ
wéi jiāo*, "a hundred *li* from the capital is the outskirts"), and 野 (*yě*, "open country") lies
beyond that. Gate, ancestral temple, city wall, outskirts, open country: the hexagram is a map of
how far a person goes from home.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 同人于野，亨。利涉大川，利君子貞 (*tóng rén yú yě, hēng. lì shè dà chuān, lì
jūnzǐ zhēn*) — "People together in the open country: gets through. Advantageous to cross the great
river; advantageous for the noble one to hold firm." Openness gets through, and it can cross the
widest water. On the older reading of 貞 (*zhēn*), the last clause is "an inquiry advantageous for
the noble one"; either way, the demand falls on the noble one, not on the crowd.

**Line 1.** 同人于門，無咎 (*tóng rén yú mén, wú jiù*, "people together at the gate: no blame"). The
first step is only through one's own door, and it is already right. The 小象: 出門同人，又誰咎也
(*chū mén tóng rén, yòu shéi jiù yě*, "going out of the gate to be with people — who would blame
that?").

**Line 2.** 同人于宗，吝 (*tóng rén yú zōng, lìn*, "people together in the ancestral temple:
stinting"). 說文's 宗 (*zōng*) is 尊祖廟也 (*zūn zǔ miào yě*, "the honoured temple of the
ancestors") — belonging decided by descent. This is the centre of the lower figure and the one
open line, and 王弼 names its fault 用心褊狹 (*yòng xīn biǎn xiá*, "using the heart narrowly"). The
only fault in the figure is the warmest-looking gathering in it.

**Line 3.** 伏戎于莽，升其高陵，三歲不興 (*fú róng yú mǎng, shēng qí gāo líng, sān suì bù xīng*,
"troops lying hidden in the thicket, climbing its high hill; three years, and not rising"). The top
of the lower figure, at the seam: suspicion hides, climbs to watch, and nothing happens for three
years. There is no verdict; the waiting is its own cost. The 小象 gives the cause: 敵剛也 (*dí gāng
yě*, "the other side is firm").

**Line 4.** 乘其墉，弗克，攻吉 (*chéng qí yōng, fú kè, gōng jí*). 說文's 墉 (*yōng*) is a city
wall. The printed punctuation reads "mounts its wall; does not take it; attacking: auspicious", but
王弼 reads 弗克攻，吉 (*fú kè gōng, jí*, "does not carry the attack: auspicious"), and the 小象 agrees:
困而反則也 (*kùn ér fǎn zé yě*, "hard-pressed, it turns back to the rule"). Up on the other side's
wall, it stops. The good fortune is in the stopping.

**Line 5.** 同人，先號啕而后笑。大師克相遇 (*tóng rén, xiān háo táo ér hòu xiào. dà shī kè xiāng yù*,
"people together: first wailing, then laughter. The great armies prevail, and meet"). The firm
ruler answers line 2 across the figure, but lines 3 and 4 — the ambush and the wall — stand between.
說文 enters the crying graph as 咷 (*táo*), a child who cries and will not stop. The meeting comes
only after force has been spent. The 繫辭上 hangs a saying on this line: 二人同心，其利斷金 (*èr rén
tóng xīn, qí lì duàn jīn*, "two people, one heart: their edge cuts metal").

**Line 6.** 同人于郊，無悔 (*tóng rén yú jiāo, wú huǐ*, "people together at the outskirts: no
regret"). The farthest the lines go, and still short of the judgment's 野 (*yě*, "open country"). The
小象 says it plainly: 志未得也 (*zhì wèi dé yě*, "the aim is not yet reached").

Five firm lines, and three of them (3, 4, 5) are soldiers. 王弼's closing note gives the reason:
凡處同人而不泰焉則必用師矣 (*fán chǔ tóng rén ér bù tài yān zé bì yòng shī yǐ*, "whenever people are
together and not at ease, armies follow"), and 愛國愈甚益爲它災 (*ài guó yù shèn yì wéi tā zāi*, "the
more fiercely one loves one's own state, the more it becomes disaster for others").

### In a life now

Every group that forms: a new team, a congregation, a marriage that joins two families, a
movement, a town meeting, a crowd that finds itself online. The hexagram's map is plain. Going out
of your own door toward others is already right. Gathering only among your own — of blood, of
class, of opinion — is the one thing it calls stinting. Suspicion that lies in the grass and climbs
to watch is not yet a war, but it can eat three years. Standing on the other side's wall and
choosing not to storm it is fortunate. Real meeting may come only after grief, and after both sides
have spent their strength. And even when all of it goes well, the open ground where anyone may stand
is further off than you have come.

The 大象 turns this toward practice: 類族辨物 (*lèi zú biàn wù*, "sort clans by kind, tell things
apart"). The togetherness it wants is not sameness. It is people seen for what each one is, in one
light.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The graph is shared, and the books part over it.** 同 (*tóng*) is this hexagram's name and the
first word of four of its lines — it is in the core. Shalom's translation of chapter 56 renders it
*the same as*: *"Dim your own light, be the same as the dust. / This is called profound sameness."*
And of those who have it: *"You cannot draw them near nor hold them off."* The word there for
*draw near* is 親 (*qīn*) — the very word the 雜卦 (*záguà*) uses to define this hexagram: 同人親也
(*tóng rén qīn yě*, "people together is closeness"). So the Wing's 同 is closeness, and the Laozi's
is a sameness that closeness cannot reach. The English rightly differs — *together* here, *the same
as* there — because the core's 同人 are distinct people going one way, and the 大象 insists on
telling them apart, where chapter 56 dims every light to the dust's. **This reading does not merge
them.** What the two books share is a distrust of the near circle: 13.2's clan is stinting, and
chapter 56's profound sameness cannot be drawn in.

### Words kept

- 同人 (*tóng rén*) — *People Together* (proposed); 同 (*tóng*) is *together* here, where the Tao Te Ching has *the same as*; never *Kindred*, which would name the figure by its one fault (line 2)
- 野 (*yě*) — *open country*; 郊 (*jiāo*) — *the outskirts*, nearer in; the two are kept apart, because the lines reach one and not the other
- 宗 (*zōng*) — *the ancestral temple*, *the clan*
- 心 (*xīn*) — *heart*, never *mind* (locked)
- 天 (*tiān*) — *sky*, never *Heaven* (locked)
- 大川 (*dà chuān*) — *the great river*; 君子 (*jūnzǐ*) — *the noble one* (working)
- 亨 · 貞 (*hēng · zhēn*) — *gets through · holding firm*, working glosses (A6); 吝 · 無咎 · 無悔 (*lìn · wú jiù · wú huǐ*) — *stinting · no blame · no regret*, working glosses (A4)
