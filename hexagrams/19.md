## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake × sky ↔ earth.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 剛浸而長 (*gāng jìn ér zhǎng*), "the firm soaks in and grows";
說而順 (*yuè ér shùn*), "releasing, and going along"; and a warning, 至于八月有凶，消不久也 (*zhì yú bā
yuè yǒu xiōng, xiāo bù jiǔ yě*), "come the eighth month there is misfortune: the waning is not long
off." The 大象傳: 澤上有地 (*zé shàng yǒu dì*), "above the lake there is earth," and 君子以教思无窮，
容保民无疆 (*jūnzǐ yǐ jiào sī wú qióng, róng bǎo mín wú jiāng*), "the noble one teaches and thinks
without running out, holds the people and keeps them safe without a border." The tradition reads the
firm advancing, the high bending to the low, and an advance that will turn.

**What the polarity implies.** Both poles are yin, so neither trigram has its one ruler. Yet the
figure's only two firm lines are inside, at the bottom of the lake (`110000`). The lake's strength
is its bed, and it is rising, while the earth above has no firm line at all. So the overseeing here
has an odd shape. The land stands over the water like a bank above a lake, which is 說文's 監臨
(*jiān lín*, "bending to look down"). But the force is in what is looked at, and it is coming up. The
structure suggests that to oversee, in this figure, is to be the shore: wide, yielding and
containing (容, *róng*, "hold") rather than commanding, while the living thing below presses up
through the openness. That also explains 甘臨 (*gān lín*, "sweet overseeing"), which gains nothing.
Ground that only goes along, over water that only releases, is all ease and no edge. Line 3, where
the lake's open top meets the earth, is where that happens. 19 turned over is 20. Here the firm is
gathering at the bottom and arriving; in 20 it has reached the top and is on show. The 雜卦 makes
them one sentence: 臨、觀之義，或與或求 (*lín guān zhī yì, huò yǔ huò qiú*), "the meaning of 臨 and
觀: one gives, one seeks."

**For the name.** **Overseeing**. The signature strains it slightly, because it puts the force
below and advancing, and *overseeing* names only the shore's side. But every line is a mode of the
one above (咸臨, 至臨, 知臨, 敦臨), so the lines hold the name where the figure pulls. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Approach* survives the lines (至臨, *zhì lín*, "an arriving approach"; 甘臨,
"a sweet approach"), but it loses the bending-down that 說文 gives. *Rising* fails the lines: 知臨，
大君之宜 (*zhī lín, dà jūn zhī yí*), "a knowing ___, fitting for a great lord," cannot take "a
knowing rising," and 46 升 already has rising.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**To stand over others is to come down to them, and the lines ask only how.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives
臨 (*lín*) as 監臨也 (*jiān lín yě*, "bending to look down over"), built on 臥 (*wò*), a person bowed
forward. The core text never says what overseeing **(proposed)** is for. Its six lines are six
manners of the one act — all together, all together, sweet, arriving, knowing, solid — and five of
them are good. Then the judgment sets a date on all of it: 至于八月有凶 (*zhì yú bā yuè yǒu xiōng*,
"come the eighth month, it turns ominous"). To be over others is a season, and it ends.

### The polarities in it

Within is the lake, open at its top: it releases. Without is earth, every line yielding: it goes
along. The figure's only two firm lines lie at the very bottom, in the lake's bed, and the 彖 sees
them moving — 剛浸而長 (*gāng jìn ér zhǎng*, "the firm soaks in and grows"). So the strength of this
figure is in what is looked down on, and it is rising; what does the looking is soft ground. **The
structure and the text pull a little apart here.** The lines are spoken from above — the fifth is
fitting for a great lord — while the figure puts the force below. Both are true at once: the one
who oversees stands over something stronger and still growing, and does best by holding it the way
a bank holds a lake.

### In nature

The 大象's image is 澤上有地 (*zé shàng yǒu dì*, "above the lake there is earth"): the shore. Stand
on a bank over still water and bend to look. You see your own face first, and then, past it, the
bed. That is the posture 說文 draws. The bank is wet from below as well: along a lake's margin water
rises through the soil to the lip, which is where the reeds and sedges grow, in ground that is
neither lake nor land.

The fourth line's word is a bird coming down. 至 (*zhì*) is in 說文 鳥飛从高下至地也 (*niǎo fēi cóng
gāo xià zhì dì yě*, "a bird flying from high down to the ground"). Watch a heron drop to a
lakeshore: it loses height in a long glide, then brakes with its whole wing and puts its feet on the
earth. That is 至臨 (*zhì lín*, "arriving overseeing") — coming all the way down.

And watch the season the judgment warns of. The days begin to shorten at midsummer, while the heat
goes on building for weeks after; the turn is already inside the fullness before anyone feels it.
The 彖 says so of the eighth month: 消不久也 (*xiāo bù jiǔ yě*, "the waning is not long off").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 臨：元亨。利貞。至于八月有凶 (*lín: yuán hēng. lì zhēn. zhì yú bā yuè yǒu xiōng*) —
"Overseeing: from the source, it gets through. Holding firm is advantageous. Come the eighth month,
it turns ominous." The four great words, and then a date.

**Line 1**, firm at the base, oversees with everyone. 咸臨，貞吉 (*xián lín, zhēn jí*, "overseeing all
together; holding firm, auspicious"). 說文 gives 咸 (*xián*) as 皆也 (*jiē yě*, "all"); 王弼 reads it
as 感 (*gǎn*, "stirred, moved") — overseeing that moves and is moved. Both readings are live.

**Line 2**, the second firm line, has the fullest good fortune. 咸臨，吉无不利 (*xián lín, jí wú bù
lì*, "overseeing all together: auspicious, nothing not advantageous"). The 小象 adds 未順命也 (*wèi shùn
mìng yě*, "it has not yet gone along with the allotted course"): the rising strength is not yet
spent.

**Line 3**, at the seam where the lake's open lip meets the earth, is the one failure. 甘臨，无攸利。
既憂之，无咎 (*gān lín, wú yōu lì. jì yōu zhī, wú jiù*, "sweet overseeing: nothing advantageous.
Once you are troubled by it, no blame"). 說文 draws 甘 (*gān*) as 从口含一 (*cóng kǒu hán yī*, "a
mouth holding something"); 王弼 reads it as flattery. Being liked is not overseeing. The remedy is to
be troubled.

**Line 4** comes down. 至臨，无咎 (*zhì lín, wú jiù*, "arriving overseeing: no blame"). The first line
of the earth, touching the lake directly: the bird on the ground. The 小象: 位當也 (*wèi dāng yě*,
"the place is fitting").

**Line 5**, the soft centre, knows. 知臨，大君之宜，吉 (*zhī lín, dà jūn zhī yí, jí*, "knowing
overseeing, fitting for a great lord: auspicious"). 王弼 reads the knowing as trust in the capable
below — 不爲而成 (*bù wéi ér chéng*, "completing without doing"). A yielding line over a rising firm
one: the knowledge is of what to leave alone.

**Line 6**, at the top of the earth, is ground at its thickest. 敦臨，吉无咎 (*dūn lín, jí wú jiù*,
"solid overseeing: auspicious, no blame"). 說文's entry for 敦 (*dūn*) is a different sense; 王弼 reads
it as 厚 (*hòu*, "thick"). The 小象: 志在內也 (*zhì zài nèi yě*, "the will is on what is within").

Every line is auspicious or blameless except the sweet one. The only ominous word in the hexagram is
in the judgment, and it is not a fault but a date.

### In a life now

This is the figure for taking charge of people: a new manager, a nurse running a ward for the first time, a
teacher with a new class, a parent of teenagers, a mayor, an editor. It gives no programme. It
gives manners, and ranks them: with everyone and moved by them (lines 1 and 2); coming all the way
down to where they are (line 4); knowing what to leave to them (line 5); solid, and minding what is
within (line 6). The one it refuses is the sweet one — oversight that wants to be liked — and it
says the cure is to worry about it. Its hardest counsel is the date. Whatever is rising beneath you
will not stay beneath you; the eighth month comes, and the position that suits now will not suit
then.

The 大象 turns it toward the long run: 君子以教思无窮，容保民无疆 (*jūnzǐ yǐ jiào sī wú qióng, róng bǎo
mín wú jiāng*, "the noble one teaches and thinks without running out, holds the people and keeps
them safe without a border"). Holding, not commanding: the bank's work.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Two contacts, one in each layer, and each parts from the Laozi somewhere.** In the core text, 19.6's
敦 (*dūn*) is the graph of chapter 15's *"Solid, like uncarved wood."* The English *solid* answers to
敦 there and can here: 敦臨, "solid overseeing." But the Laozi's solidity describes the ancients who
*"could not be read"*; this line's solidity is a way of standing over people, and it is auspicious in
the open.

In the Wings, the 彖 calls the firm growing 天之道 (*tiān zhī dào*, "the Tao of the sky") — a contact
recorded in [`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 4. Chapter 9: *"The work done,
the body withdraws. This is the way of nature."* Both books put the turn in nature's own going, not
in anyone's failure. They part on what to do: the 彖 names the waning as a threat to be seen coming,
and the Laozi's counsel is to withdraw before it arrives.

### Words kept

- 臨 (*lín*) — *overseeing*, the proposed name (not yet approved); the bending-down 說文 gives is the sense to keep
- 咸 (*xián*) — *all together*, with 王弼's *stirred* (感, *gǎn*) as the live alternative; it is also 31's name
- 甘 (*gān*) — *sweet*; the mouth holding something, and flattery in 王弼
- 至 (*zhì*) — *arriving*, the bird coming down to the ground
- 知 (*zhī*) — *knowing*
- 敦 (*dūn*) — *solid*, as the Tao Te Ching renders it in chapter 15
- 大君 (*dà jūn*) — *great lord*; not *ruler*, which is kept for 王 (*wáng*)
- 命 (*mìng*) — *the allotted course*, never *fate* (locked)
- 保 (*bǎo*) — *keep safe* (locked) · 容 (*róng*) — *hold*, *contain* (Laozi ch 16)
- 道 (*dào*) — *the Tao* in the 彖 (locked); the core text of 19 does not use it
- 八月 (*bā yuè*) — *the eighth month*; no calendar is imposed on it here
- 吉 · 凶 · 无咎 (*jí · xiōng · wú jiù*) — *auspicious · ominous · no blame*, working glosses (A4)
- 亨 · 元 · 貞 (*hēng · yuán · zhēn*) — *gets through · from the source · holding firm*, working glosses (A6)
