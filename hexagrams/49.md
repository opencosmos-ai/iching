## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Across** — water ↔ fire and mountain ↔ lake, both at their yin poles.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 sets the two against each other: 水火相息 (*shuǐ huǒ xiāng xī*,
"water and fire put each other out"), and 二女同居，其志不相得 (*èr nǚ tóng jū, qí zhì bù xiāng dé*,
"two women live under one roof, and their wills do not meet"). It ends well only because the change
is trusted — 革而信也 (*gé ér xìn yě*, "changed, and trusted") — and because 文明以說 (*wén míng yǐ yuè*,
"patterned and clear, and so it releases"). The 大象傳 gives the image 澤中有火 (*zé zhōng yǒu huǒ*, "fire
in the middle of the lake") and draws from it 君子以治歷明時 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhì lì míng shí*, "the noble one
puts the calendar in order and makes the seasons clear"): change as the turning of the year.

**What the polarity implies.** Both trigrams are yin — two firm lines round one open one — so nothing in
this figure strikes, holds or sinks. The work is done by heat that clings to what it touches, under a
surface that is open at the top. The odd line within is at the middle: the fire's hollow centre, heat
working at the core. The odd line without is at the top: the lake's open mouth, where whatever is loosened
comes away. Change starts at the centre and shows at the surface, and the lines climb exactly that way.
The first binds with the raw hide, 鞏用黃牛之革 (*gǒng yòng huáng niú zhī gé*, "bound with the hide of a
yellow ox") — the hide still whole and still useful. By the top the change is all coat: 大人虎變 (*dàrén
hǔ biàn*, "the great person changes as the tiger does"), 君子豹變，小人革面 (*jūnzǐ bào biàn, xiǎorén gé
miàn*, "the noble one changes as the leopard does; the small person changes the face"). The structure
suggests why the leopard is praised and the changed face is not: a coat grows from the inside out; a face
can be changed from the outside only.

**For the name.** **Shedding** — the signature deepens it: heat at the core, loosening at the open
surface, is how a hide sheds, and it keeps the name clear of 改 (*gǎi*, "alter") and 變 (*biàn*,
"transform") in the lines. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Molting* — survives the coat lines
(大人虎變, "the great person changes as the tiger does"), though there the verb is 變 (*biàn*, "transform"), not 革 (*gé*), and it
bends at 革言三就 (*gé yán sān jiù*, "talk of change comes round three times"), which is no animal's;
*Tanning* — fails the lines: the first line uses the hide already tanned as a binding, and 革言三就 is speech, not
leather.
