## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **Fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Crossed** — water ↔ fire, the sinking pole below the rising one, so they move apart.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 (*dà xiàng*): 火在水上，未濟；君子以慎辨物居方 (*huǒ zài shuǐ shàng, wèi jì; jūnzǐ yǐ shèn biàn wù jū fāng*), "fire above water: 未濟; the noble one carefully tells things apart and sets each where it belongs." The 彖 (*tuàn*): 雖不當位，剛柔應也 (*suī bù dāng wèi, gāng róu yìng yě*), "though the positions do not fit, firm and yielding answer each other." And 序卦 (*xùguà*) closes the book here: 物不可窮也，故受之以未濟；終焉 (*wù bù kě qióng yě, gù shòu zhī yǐ wèi jì; zhōng yān*), "things cannot be run out, so 未濟 follows — and there it ends."

**What the polarity implies.** Water sinks and fire rises; with water below and fire above, each moves away from the other. Nothing is cooked and nothing is quenched. The figure is 63 turned over, line for line: `010101` has every line out of place and every line still paired with its partner. Where 63 could only lose its order, 64 can only gain it — any move toward place is a move toward crossing. So the lines, which in 63 slide from 吉 (*jí*, "auspicious") to 厲 (*lì*, "peril"), climb here: 濡其尾，吝 (*rú qí wěi, lìn*), "its tail wet: stinting", at the base; 貞吉 (*zhēn jí*, "keeping to it, auspicious") at the second, fourth and fifth lines; 君子之光 (*jūnzǐ zhī guāng*), "the noble one's light", at the fifth. The little fox 汔濟 (*qì jì*), "nearly across", is in the water, not on the bank. The structure makes the ending a choice. The upper canon ends on water and fire each doubled; the lower ends on them crossed, and the direction rule puts the parting figure last. A book that closed on 63 would close on a meeting that can only decay. Closing on 64, it ends on a parting that can still meet — turn the figure over and it is 63 — and on the one figure that, in 序卦's words, cannot be run out. The book stops at the bank.

**For the name.** *Not Yet Across* — the signature deepens it: *not yet* is the book's last word because only the parted pair has somewhere left to go. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Unfinished* — survives: 未濟，征凶 (*wèi jì, zhēng xiōng*) "unfinished: setting out is ominous" reads, at the cost of the fox and the river that 汔濟 needs; *Parting* — fails the lines: it is the signature's word, not the text's, and 小狐汔濟 (*xiǎo hú qì jì*) "the little fox, nearly parting" says nothing.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi, the 3rd-century commentator, and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Not yet across — the little fox in the water, and the book ends there, because nothing can be run
to its end.** The judgment gives it whole: 小狐汔濟，濡其尾，无攸利 ("the little fox, nearly across, wets its
tail — nothing advantageous"). And yet it opens with 亨 ("gets through"). The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE
character dictionary, pictures 未 ("not yet") as 象木重枝葉也 ("a tree with branches and leaves layered") —
a tree in full leaf, its season not yet over. *Not Yet Across* (proposed): unfinished, and for that
reason still going.

### The polarities in it

Water within sinks; fire without rises. They move apart, and in `010101` every line stands out of
its place — the exact reverse of 63. But the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, sees what the reversal
keeps: 雖不當位，剛柔應也 ("though the positions do not fit, firm and yielding answer each other"). Nothing
is in order and everything is paired. **So the lines climb where 63's slid** — from a wet tail at
the base to 君子之光 ("the noble one's light") at the fifth. The structure says disorder; the lines say
the disorder is the room to move in.

### In nature

Look at the fox again, and this time at the river. 汔 ("nearly", or "the water dropped") is the hinge
of the judgment, and it reads two ways. The Tuan hears "nearly": 未出中也 ("it is not yet out of the
middle"). But the Shuowen gives it as 水涸也 ("the water dried up"), and Wang Bi reads it the same way:
小狐不能渉大川須汔然後乃能濟 ("the little fox cannot cross the great river; it must wait for the water to drop,
and only then can it cross"). That is a naturalist's reading. Rivers on the northern plains fall in
late autumn and winter; shoals come up, the channel narrows, ice forms at the edges. A young fox,
which by then has left its parents and is finding its own ground, meets the river at exactly that
season. Walk a riverbank after light snow and you may find its line of prints — neat and nearly
straight, each hind foot set in the fore print — running down to the water's edge. Sometimes they go
on across the ice. Sometimes they stop and turn back.

The image in the Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, is fire above water: 火在水上 ("fire above
water"). Watch a fire lit on a bank at dusk. Its light lies on the river and does not warm it; the
water runs dark underneath. Each is fully itself, and neither yet touches the other.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 未濟：亨。小狐汔濟，濡其尾，无攸利 ("Not yet across: it gets through. The little fox, nearly
across, wets its tail; nothing advantageous"). The Tuan reads the wet tail as 不續終也 ("it does not
carry through to the end"). Wang Bi: 濟未濟者必有餘力也 ("whoever would cross the not-yet-crossed must have
strength to spare").

**Line 1.** 濡其尾，吝 ("wets its tail — stinting"). Starting in the water, short of what the crossing
needs. The Small Image, the Wing on each line: 亦不知極也 ("it does not know its limit").

**Line 2.** 曳其輪，貞吉 ("dragging its wheel: holding firm, auspicious"). The same wheel as 63's first
line, one step higher, and here restraint is not only blameless but auspicious. A firm line at the
lower centre, holding back.

**Line 3.** 未濟，征凶，利涉大川 ("not yet across: setting out is ominous; advantageous to cross the great
river"). The line contradicts itself, and the contradiction should stay on the page. Wang Bi
resolves it without emending: this line cannot cross by its own strength, but carried by the firm
line below, 溺可得乎 ("how could it drown?"). March alone, ominous; cross with help, advantageous.

**Line 4.** 貞吉，悔亡，震用伐鬼方，三年有賞于大國 ("holding firm, auspicious, regret goes; roused, it attacks the
Ghost Country; in three years there is reward from the great state"). 63's third line, the same war,
now rewarded: out of the water trigram, the crossing turns.

**Line 5** is the height of the ending. 貞吉，无悔，君子之光，有孚，吉 ("holding firm, auspicious, no regret; the
noble one's light; there is confidence — auspicious"). A soft line in the ruler's place, and the
text's brightest word. Wang Bi reads it as giving work to the able and not doubting them: 付物以能而不疑也
("entrusting things to the able, and not doubting them").

**Line 6.** 有孚于飲酒，无咎，濡其首，有孚失是 ("there is confidence in drinking wine — no blame; but wetting the
head, there is confidence, and it loses what is right"). The celebration before the far bank. The
Small Image: 亦不知節也 ("it does not know measure").

### In a life now

This is the figure for the thesis two chapters short, the recovery not yet complete, the child
nearly grown, the reconciliation begun, the field sown and not reaped — and for any work that will
outlast the one doing it. Its counsel is not hurry and not despair. Hold the wheel back (line 2); do
not set out alone on strength you do not have (line 3); give the able their work and trust it (line
5); and do not toast the crossing mid-stream (line 6). Wait, if the river is high, for the water to
drop.

The Great Image closes the book on a practice: 君子以慎辨物居方 ("the noble one carefully tells things apart
and sets each where it belongs"). And the Xugua, the Wing on the sequence, gives the reason it
closes here at all: 物不可窮也，故受之以未濟；終焉 ("things cannot be run to their end, so Not Yet Across follows —
and there it ends").

### An echo from the Laozi

**Shared at the Wings' layer, and one near-miss to name.** Chapter 64 in Shalom's translation: *"Be
as careful at the end as at the beginning, / and nothing will be ruined."* The graphs are 慎
("careful") and 終 ("end"); 慎 ("careful") is the Great Image's first word of practice here, and 終
("end") is the Tuan's 不續終也 ("it does not carry through to the end"). So the echo is real — but it is
to the Wings, not to the fox. The same chapter says *"People often ruin their affairs just short of
completing them"*, which sounds like the fox 汔濟 ("nearly across") exactly; but the Laozi's graph for
"just short" is not 汔 ("nearly"), and that parallel is **thematic only**. And the books part: the
Laozi's sage would rather not begin what must be carried so far, and supports the countless things
*"and never pushes them"*; 64 rewards a war at its fourth line and a crossing made with help at its
third. (That both are numbered 64 is a coincidence of later chapter division, and means nothing.)
