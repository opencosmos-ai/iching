## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **Fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Crossed** — water ↔ fire, the sinking pole below the rising one, so they move apart.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 (*dà xiàng*): 火在水上，未濟；君子以慎辨物居方 (*huǒ zài shuǐ shàng, wèi jì; jūnzǐ yǐ shèn biàn wù jū fāng*), "fire above water: 未濟; the noble one carefully tells things apart and sets each where it belongs." The 彖 (*tuàn*): 雖不當位，剛柔應也 (*suī bù dāng wèi, gāng róu yìng yě*), "though the positions do not fit, firm and yielding answer each other." And 序卦 (*xùguà*) closes the book here: 物不可窮也，故受之以未濟；終焉 (*wù bù kě qióng yě, gù shòu zhī yǐ wèi jì; zhōng yān*), "things cannot be run out, so 未濟 follows — and there it ends."

**What the polarity implies.** Water sinks and fire rises; with water below and fire above, each moves away from the other. Nothing is cooked and nothing is quenched. The figure is 63 turned over, line for line: `010101` has every line out of place and every line still paired with its partner. Where 63 could only lose its order, 64 can only gain it — any move toward place is a move toward crossing. So the lines, which in 63 slide from 吉 (*jí*, "auspicious") to 厲 (*lì*, "peril"), climb here: 濡其尾，吝 (*rú qí wěi, lìn*), "its tail wet: stinting", at the base; 貞吉 (*zhēn jí*, "keeping to it, auspicious") at the second, fourth and fifth lines; 君子之光 (*jūnzǐ zhī guāng*), "the noble one's light", at the fifth. The little fox 汔濟 (*qì jì*), "nearly across", is in the water, not on the bank. The structure makes the ending a choice. The upper canon ends on water and fire each doubled; the lower ends on them crossed, and the direction rule puts the parting figure last. A book that closed on 63 would close on a meeting that can only decay. Closing on 64, it ends on a parting that can still meet — turn the figure over and it is 63 — and on the one figure that, in 序卦's words, cannot be run out. The book stops at the bank.

**For the name.** *Not Yet Across* — the signature deepens it: *not yet* is the book's last word because only the parted pair has somewhere left to go. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Unfinished* — survives: 未濟，征凶 (*wèi jì, zhēng xiōng*) "unfinished: setting out is ominous" reads, at the cost of the fox and the river that 汔濟 needs; *Parting* — fails the lines: it is the signature's word, not the text's, and 小狐汔濟 (*xiǎo hú qì jì*) "the little fox, nearly parting" says nothing.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Not yet across — the little fox in the water, and the book ends there, because nothing can be run
to its end.** The judgment gives it whole: 小狐汔濟，濡其尾，无攸利 (*xiǎo hú qì jì, rú qí wěi, wú yōu
lì*, "the little fox, nearly across, wets its tail — nothing advantageous"). And yet it opens with
亨 (*hēng*, "gets through"). 說文 (*shuōwén*) pictures 未 (*wèi*, "not yet") as 象木重枝葉也 (*xiàng mù
chóng zhī yè yě*, "a tree with branches and leaves layered") — a tree in full leaf, its season not
yet over. *Not Yet Across* (proposed): unfinished, and for that reason still going.

### The polarities in it

Water within sinks; fire without rises. They move apart, and in `010101` every line stands out of
its place — the exact reverse of 63. But the 彖 (*tuàn*) sees what the reversal keeps: 雖不當位，剛柔應也
(*suī bù dāng wèi, gāng róu yìng yě*, "though the positions do not fit, firm and yielding answer each
other"). Nothing is in order and everything is paired. **So the lines climb where 63's slid** —
from a wet tail at the base to 君子之光 (*jūnzǐ zhī guāng*, "the noble one's light") at the fifth.
The structure says disorder; the lines say the disorder is the room to move in.

### In nature

Look at the fox again, and this time at the river. 汔 (*qì*) is the hinge of the judgment, and it
reads two ways. The 彖 hears "nearly": 未出中也 (*wèi chū zhōng yě*, "it is not yet out of the
middle"). But 說文 gives 汔 as 水涸也 (*shuǐ hé yě*, "the water dried up"), and 王弼 reads it the same
way: 小狐不能渉大川須汔然後乃能濟 (*xiǎo hú bù néng shè dà chuān xū qì rán hòu nǎi néng jì*, "the
little fox cannot cross the great river; it must wait for the water to drop, and only then can it
cross"). That is a naturalist's reading. Rivers on the northern plains fall in late autumn and
winter; shoals come up, the channel narrows, ice forms at the edges. A young fox, which by then has
left its parents and is finding its own ground, meets the river at exactly that season. Walk a
riverbank after light snow and you may find its line of prints — neat and nearly straight, each
hind foot set in the fore print — running down to the water's edge. Sometimes they go on across the
ice. Sometimes they stop and turn back.

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*)'s image is fire above water: 火在水上 (*huǒ zài shuǐ shàng*). Watch a fire lit on a
bank at dusk. Its light lies on the river and does not warm it; the water runs dark underneath.
Each is fully itself, and neither yet touches the other.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 未濟：亨。小狐汔濟，濡其尾，无攸利 (*wèi jì: hēng. xiǎo hú qì jì, rú qí wěi, wú yōu
lì*) — "Not yet across: it gets through. The little fox, nearly across, wets its tail; nothing
advantageous." The 彖 reads the wet tail as 不續終也 (*bù xù zhōng yě*, "it does not carry through to
the end"). 王弼: 濟未濟者必有餘力也 (*jì wèi jì zhě bì yǒu yú lì yě*, "whoever would cross the
not-yet-crossed must have strength to spare").

**Line 1.** 濡其尾，吝 (*rú qí wěi, lìn*, "wets its tail — stinting"). Starting in the water, short
of what the crossing needs. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*): 亦不知極也 (*yì bù zhī jí yě*, "it does not know
its limit").

**Line 2.** 曳其輪，貞吉 (*yè qí lún, zhēn jí*, "dragging its wheel: holding firm, auspicious"). The
same wheel as 63's first line, one step higher, and here restraint is not only blameless but
auspicious. A firm line at the lower centre, holding back.

**Line 3.** 未濟，征凶，利涉大川 (*wèi jì, zhēng xiōng, lì shè dà chuān*, "not yet across: setting out
is ominous; advantageous to cross the great river"). The line contradicts itself, and the
contradiction should stay on the page. 王弼 resolves it without emending: this line cannot cross by
its own strength, but carried by the firm line below, 溺可得乎 (*nì kě dé hū*, "how could it
drown?"). March alone, ominous; cross with help, advantageous.

**Line 4.** 貞吉，悔亡，震用伐鬼方，三年有賞于大國 (*zhēn jí, huǐ wáng, zhèn yòng fá guǐ fāng, sān nián
yǒu shǎng yú dà guó*, "holding firm, auspicious, regret goes; roused, it attacks the 鬼方,
*Guǐfāng*; in three years there is reward from the great state"). 63's third line, the same war,
now rewarded: out of the water trigram, the crossing turns.

**Line 5** is the height of the ending. 貞吉，无悔，君子之光，有孚，吉 (*zhēn jí, wú huǐ, jūnzǐ zhī guāng,
yǒu fú, jí*, "holding firm, auspicious, no regret; the noble one's light; there is confidence —
auspicious"). A soft line in the ruler's place, and the text's brightest word. 王弼 reads it as
giving work to the able and not doubting them: 付物以能而不疑也 (*fù wù yǐ néng ér bù yí yě*, "entrusting things to the able, and not doubting them").

**Line 6.** 有孚于飲酒，无咎，濡其首，有孚失是 (*yǒu fú yú yǐn jiǔ, wú jiù, rú qí shǒu, yǒu fú shī shì*,
"there is confidence in drinking wine — no blame; but wetting the head, there is confidence, and it
loses what is right"). The celebration before the far bank. The 小象: 亦不知節也 (*yì bù zhī jié yě*,
"it does not know measure").

### In a life now

This is the figure for the thesis two chapters short, the recovery not yet complete, the child
nearly grown, the reconciliation begun, the field sown and not reaped — and for any work that will
outlast the one doing it. Its counsel is not hurry and not despair. Hold the wheel back (line 2);
do not set out alone on strength you do not have (line 3); give the able their work and trust it
(line 5); and do not toast the crossing mid-stream (line 6). Wait, if the river is high, for the
water to drop.

The 大象 closes the book on a practice: 君子以慎辨物居方 (*jūnzǐ yǐ shèn biàn wù jū fāng*, "the noble
one carefully tells things apart and sets each where it belongs"). And 序卦 (*xùguà*) gives the
reason it closes here at all: 物不可窮也，故受之以未濟；終焉 (*wù bù kě qióng yě, gù shòu zhī yǐ wèi jì;
zhōng yān*, "things cannot be run to their end, so 未濟 follows — and there it ends").

### An echo from the Laozi

**Shared at the Wings' layer, and one near-miss to name.** Chapter 64 in Shalom's translation:
*"Be as careful at the end as at the beginning, / and nothing will be ruined."* The graphs are 慎
(*shèn*, "careful") and 終 (*zhōng*, "end"); 慎 is the 大象's first word of practice here, and 終 is the
彖's 不續終也. So the echo is real — but it is to the Wings, not to the fox. The same chapter says
*"People often ruin their affairs just short of completing them"*, which sounds like the fox 汔濟
exactly; but the Laozi's graph for "just short" is not 汔, and that parallel is **thematic only**.
And the books part: the Laozi's sage would rather not begin what must be carried so far, and
supports the countless things *"and never pushes them"*; 64 rewards a war at its fourth line and a
crossing made with help at its third. (That both are numbered 64 is a coincidence of later
chapter division, and means nothing.)
