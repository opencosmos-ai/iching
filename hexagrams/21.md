## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind × water ↔ fire.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 reads the figure as a face: 頤中有物，曰噬嗑 (*yí zhōng yǒu wù,
yuē shì kè*), "there is a thing between the jaws: it is called biting through." Then it reads the
trigrams: 動而明，雷電合而章 (*dòng ér míng, léi diàn hé ér zhāng*), "moving out, and clarity: thunder
and lightning join and are made plain." The 大象傳: 雷電噬嗑；先王以明罰敕法 (*léi diàn shì kè; xiān
wáng yǐ míng fá chì fǎ*), "thunder and lightning: biting through; the former rulers made penalties
clear and set the laws in order." The tradition joins the jaw that closes on an obstacle to justice
done in plain sight.

**What the polarity implies.** Thunder within is force at the base. Fire without is hollow at its
middle, a firm rim around a gap. The two odd lines sit at different heights: the blow comes up from
the very bottom, and what it meets is a gap at the centre of the upper figure with something lodged
in it. Read whole, `100101` is a mouth: firm lines at bottom and top for the jaws, and the firm
fourth line as the thing caught between them. The structure suggests the bite is for joining, not
destroying. 序卦 says 嗑者合也 (*kè zhě hé yě*, "嗑 is joining"). Thunder moves out so that the thing in
the gap is taken and the jaws meet. Fire, which attaches, is what they meet on. That is why the
lines are about eating as much as punishment: dried meat, spoiled meat, a bronze arrowhead found in
the bone (得金矢, *dé jīn shǐ*, "gets a bronze arrow"). The body runs from end to end. 說卦 ch 9 has
震為足 (*zhèn wéi zú*, "thunder is the foot"), and line 1 is the foot in the stocks, 屨校滅趾 (*jù jiào
miè zhǐ*, "shod in the stocks, the toes lost to sight"). At the top the stocks reach the head, 何校滅耳
(*hè jiào miè ěr*, "wearing the cangue, the ears lost to sight"). Here justice is a bite that passes
through the whole body, foot to ear.

**For the name.** **Biting Through**. The signature supports it strongly: *through* answers the
thunder striking up from the base. It also deepens it, because the fire's attaching is the 嗑, the
jaws meeting, and the name carries that only by implication. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Bite and Close* survives the lines (噬膚滅鼻, *shì fū miè bí*, "bite the skin, the nose lost to
sight," keeps the bite, and the name keeps the close), at the cost of being two actions where the
book prefers one. *Judgement* fails the lines: 噬乾肉，得黃金 (*shì gān ròu, dé huáng jīn*, "bite
dried meat, get yellow bronze") is eating, and every 噬 line is.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Something hard is caught in the mouth: bite through it, and see what it was hiding.** In the core
text 噬 (*shì*, "bite") comes four times, in lines 2 to 5, and every time it bites food — soft
flesh, cured meat, dried meat on the bone — and three times it finds something inside: poison, a
bronze arrowhead, yellow bronze. 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 噬 as 啗也 (*dàn yě*, "to eat"). The second
graph is less settled. 說文 has 嗑 (*kè*) as 多言也 (*duō yán yě*, "much talk"); the 序卦 (*xùguà*)
reads it 嗑者合也 (*kè zhě hé yě*, "嗑 is closing"), and 王弼 agrees, 嗑合也 (*kè hé yě*, "嗑 is
joining"). The core never uses 嗑 outside the name, so the Wing's reading carries it: the jaws close
only when what is between them has been bitten through. The judgment adds the other half — 利用獄
(*lì yòng yù*, "advantageous to bring a case"). This is the figure for a matter that has to be
chewed through and judged.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, fire without. The firm line at the very bottom strikes up; the upper figure is
hollow at its centre, with firm lines round the gap, and it is the pole that attaches and makes
things plain. The 彖 (*tuàn*) says it in four characters: 動而明 (*dòng ér míng*, "moving out, and
clarity"). So the force in this figure is never hidden. It comes from the base and it lands where it
can be seen — the bite and the verdict done in the open, as thunder and lightning are one event.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sets the figure in a storm: 雷電噬嗑 (*léi diàn shì kè*, "thunder and
lightning: biting through"). Watch one from a window. The flash comes first and the sound after,
because light is almost instant and sound travels about a kilometre in three seconds; count between
them and you know how far off the strike was. The 彖 has 雷電合而章 (*léi diàn hé ér zhāng*,
"thunder and lightning join and are made plain") — two things the senses receive apart, which are
one discharge.

The lines themselves are at a meal, and they are exact about food. Soft meat you can bite into to
the face; cured meat is hard, and meat cured badly in winter can spoil at its centre, where the salt
and smoke did not reach (line 3's 臘 (*là*) is in 說文 the sacrifice after the winter solstice, the
season for curing). Tough, dried meat on the bone needs the back teeth, where the jaw bites hardest,
near its hinge. And hunters do find, in the flesh of game, an old arrowhead with the wound healed
round it — the animal carried it for years. Line 4's 得金矢 (*dé jīn shǐ*, "finds a bronze
arrowhead") is that find.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 噬嗑：亨。利用獄 (*shì kè: hēng. lì yòng yù*) — "Biting through *(proposed)*:
gets through. Advantageous to bring a case." 獄 (*yù*) is in 說文 two dogs with speech between
them — 二犬，所以守也 (*èr quǎn, suǒ yǐ shǒu yě*, "two dogs: that is what guards"). A dispute,
watched over. The biting is what makes the getting-through possible.

**Line 1** is in the stocks. 屨校滅趾，无咎 (*jù jiào miè zhǐ, wú jiù*, "shod in wood, the toes lost to
sight — no blame"). 校 (*jiào*) is 說文 木囚也 (*mù qiú yě*, "a wooden prison"). A small restraint at
the start. 王弼 reads it so: 過輕戮薄 (*guò qīng lù bó*, "the fault is light, the penalty slight") —
enough to stop the walking, and no more.

**Line 2** bites soft. 噬膚滅鼻，无咎 (*shì fū miè bí, wú jiù*, "bites into tender flesh, the nose lost
to sight — no blame"). The bite goes so deep the face goes in with it: too far, and still no blame.

**Line 3** meets poison. 噬臘肉，遇毒；小吝，无咎 (*shì là ròu, yù dú; xiǎo lìn, wú jiù*, "bites cured
meat and meets poison — a little stinting, no blame"). What is hard may also be bad inside. It costs
a little.

**Line 4**, first line of the upper figure, is the thing lodged between the jaws in the 彖's
picture — and in the lines, the biter who finds it. 噬乾胏，得金矢，利艱貞，吉 (*shì gān zǐ, dé jīn
shǐ, lì jiān zhēn, jí*, "bites dried meat on the bone, finds a bronze arrowhead; advantage in holding
firm through hardship — auspicious"). The hardest bite, and the first 吉 (*jí*, "auspicious").

**Line 5**, the centre above, finds the metal of the centre. 噬乾肉，得黃金，貞厲，无咎 (*shì gān ròu,
dé huáng jīn, zhēn lì, wú jiù*, "bites dried meat, finds yellow bronze; holding firm, peril — no
blame"). 黃 (*huáng*) is 說文 地之色也 (*dì zhī sè yě*, "the colour of the earth"). What is found at the
centre is precious, and holding it is still perilous.

**Line 6** is in the wood again, and now to the head. 何校滅耳，凶 (*hè jiào miè ěr, xiōng*, "wearing
the cangue, the ears lost to sight — ominous"). The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) gives the cause: 聰不明也 (*cōng
bù míng yě*, "the hearing was not clear"). What went unheard at the foot reaches the ears.

### In a life now

This is the figure for the obstruction that will not go away by itself: the grievance in a team no
one names, the estate that has to be settled, the audit, the family quarrel that sits at every meal.
The text does not counsel patience here; it counsels the bite, and a hearing. It is honest about
cost — what you chew through may be spoiled inside (line 3), and the hard work at the centre is still
perilous (line 5) — and it finds the hidden thing only by chewing. Its outer lines give the other
lesson: a consequence met small and early is a warning (line 1); the same wrong left until it
reaches the head is not (line 6).

The 大象 turns this toward practice: 先王以明罰敕法 (*xiān wáng yǐ míng fá chì fǎ*, "the former rulers
made penalties clear and set the laws in order"). Make the consequences plain before they are
needed, as the flash comes before the sound.

### Words kept

- 噬嗑 (*shì kè*) — *Biting Through*, the proposed name (not yet approved); 說文's *much talk* for 嗑 is
  recorded, not followed — the core never uses 嗑 alone
- 噬 (*shì*) — *bite*, in all four lines; never *punish*, which is the Wings' and 王弼's reading
- 獄 (*yù*) — *a case*, a dispute brought to judgment; *prison* is a later narrowing
- 明 (*míng*) — *clarity* / *clear*, never *illumination* (locked); here 明罰 (*míng fá*, "make penalties
  clear") and 聰不明 (*cōng bù míng*, "the hearing not clear")
- 金 (*jīn*) — *bronze*, not *gold*: the metal of the Western Zhou
- 亨 · 吉 · 凶 · 无咎 · 吝 · 厲 · 貞 (*hēng · jí · xiōng · wú jiù · lìn · lì · zhēn*) — *gets through ·
  auspicious · ominous · no blame · stinting · peril · holding firm*, working glosses (A4, A6)
