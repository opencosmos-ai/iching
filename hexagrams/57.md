## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **Wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Doubled** — thunder ↔ wind, the yielding pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) opens on the doubling: 重巽以申命 (*chóng xùn yǐ shēn mìng*), "巽 doubled, so as to send the word out again" — 命 (*mìng*) being what is spoken over a thing and sent with it — and 柔皆順乎剛 (*róu jiē shùn hū gāng*), "the yielding lines all go along with the firm." The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) makes it succession: 隨風，巽；君子以申命行事 (*suí fēng, xùn; jūnzǐ yǐ shēn mìng xíng shì*), "wind following wind: 巽; the noble one sends the word out again and carries out affairs." The tradition takes the figure as influence that works by repeating and by getting beneath, never by force.

**What the polarity implies.** Each trigram is two firm lines resting on one open line: the yielding at the bottom, taking the weight. Doubled, the figure shows that posture twice — getting under, and then under again. Thunder meets resistance and breaks it; wind entering wind meets nothing that is not also giving way, so its hazard is not collision but having no floor. The lines follow that exactly. It starts undecided, 進退 (*jìn tuì*), "advancing and retreating", which 說卦 (*shuōguà*) lists among wind's own marks: 為進退、為不果 (*wéi jìn tuì, wéi bù guǒ*), "it is advancing and retreating, it is not carrying through." In the middle it becomes 頻巽 (*pín xùn*), "kneeling again and again", and 吝 (*lìn*), "stinting." The same words, 巽在牀下 (*xùn zài chuáng xià*), "kneeling beneath the bed", are 吉 (*jí*), "auspicious", at the second line and 凶 (*xiōng*), "ominous", at the top, where the kneeler has 喪其資斧 (*sàng qí zī fǔ*), "lost the travelling axe." The structure suggests that getting beneath is right while the word it carries still has somewhere to arrive: at the fifth line it is sent 先庚三日，后庚三日 (*xiān gēng sān rì, hòu gēng sān rì*), "three days before 庚 (*gēng*, a day of the cycle), three days after." Taken all the way up, it is getting beneath with nothing left to carry.

**For the name.** *Kneeling* — the signature deepens it: 說文 (*shuōwén*)'s graph carries two kneeling figures, and the figure is two open bases under weight, so graph and figure double the same posture. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Getting Under* — survives: 巽在牀下 "getting under the bed" and 頻巽 "getting under again and again" both read, though it drops the courtesy *kneeling* keeps; *Pervading* — fails the lines: "pervading beneath the bed" (巽在牀下) says nothing, and it is wind's image, which the trigram already owns.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Getting low is how one gets in — and the same kneeling, beneath the same bed, is auspicious at the
second line and ominous at the top.** The core text says 巽在牀下 ("kneeling beneath the bed") twice,
word for word, and gives it opposite outcomes; everything in this figure turns on what separates
them. The judgment promises only 小亨 ("a small getting-through"): this is not the power that breaks
things open. The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, glosses the graph 具也 ("set out, made
ready") and builds it on a stand, 丌 ("stand"), under a pair of figures that modern palaeography
reads as kneeling (the Shuowen calls that pair only the sound). Something offered, low, and ready.
The Wings name the act: 巽者入也 ("Kneeling is entering", the Xugua, the Wing on the sequence), and 巽伏
("Kneeling lies hidden", the Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams).

### The polarities in it

Wind within, wind without: one pole twice, each trigram an open line carrying two firm ones. So the
figure has no firm base anywhere — nothing in it can push off and strike, as thunder does from its
firm bottom line. What it has is firmness at both centres, and the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment,
puts the weight there: 剛巽乎中正而志行 ("the firm kneels, centred and upright, and its intent is carried
out"). The lines agree. The kneeling that works is done by strength from the middle (lines 2 and 5,
both auspicious); the open line at the bottom only wavers, and the firm line at the top, kneeling
past every centre, loses the thing that made it firm.

### In nature

Watch what wind does to a building in a gale. It rarely pushes a wall over. It gets under the edge
of a slate, the lip of a tile, the eaves of a barn roof, and lifts from beneath; the damage after a
storm is at the edges, where the wind found a way in. It does this by coming again: the Great Image,
the Wing on the whole figure, gives the image 隨風 ("wind following wind"), and a strong wind is
exactly that — gust after gust in trains, each arriving before the last has settled, working at the
same gap.

Look too at trees on an exposed coast or a hill ridge. A steady wind from one quarter, year after
year, never breaks them; it shapes them, so that they lean and branch away from it, their crowns
drawn out like flags. That is the kind of influence 巽 ("Kneeling") has — not one blow, but the same
light pressure, repeated, getting into the growth itself. The Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams,
gives 巽 ("wind") its season and quarter: 齊乎巽 ("things are made even in wind"), 巽，東南也 ("wind is the
south-east") — late spring into summer, when growth is thick and the wind moves through all of it.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 巽：小亨。利有攸往。利見大人 ("Kneeling *(proposed)*: a small getting-through. Advantage in
having somewhere to go; advantage in seeing the great person."). Kneeling is always toward someone,
and always on the way somewhere.

**Line 1** cannot decide. 進退，利武人之貞 ("advancing, retreating; advantage in a soldier's holding firm").
The Shuogua lists 為進退、為不果 ("advancing and retreating, not carrying through") among wind's own marks.
What the open base lacks is a soldier's resolve.

**Line 2** is the kneeling that works. 巽在牀下，用史巫紛若，吉无咎 ("kneeling beneath the bed; using scribes and
spirit-mediums in great number — auspicious, no blame"). Under the bed is where the unseen trouble
is — sickness, fear, whatever cannot be faced standing. The Shuowen makes the 巫 ("spirit-medium")
one who 以舞降神 ("brings the numinous down by dancing"). Wang Bi reads the line as the lowest humility
turned toward the spirits and not toward power.

**Line 3** kneels with a frown. 頻巽，吝 ("kneeling again and again — stinting"). Wang Bi reads 頻
("again and again") as knitted brows, 頻蹙不樂 ("frowning, unhappy"): kneeling done because there is no
choice. The Small Image, the Wing on each line: 志窮也 ("the intent has run out").

**Line 4** comes home with something. 悔亡，田獲三品 ("regret goes; hunting, taking three kinds of game").
Wang Bi: one for the offerings, one for guests, one for the ruler's kitchen. The yielding line,
rightly placed under the firm fifth, is the one that brings back plenty.

**Line 5** is where the word goes out. 貞吉悔亡，无不利。无初有終，先庚三日，后庚三日，吉 ("holding firm, auspicious; regret
goes; nothing without advantage. No beginning, but an end: three days before 庚 (a day in the ten-day
cycle), three days after — auspicious"). Wang Bi makes 庚 ("the seventh day of the ten-day cycle")
the moment of announcement: 申命令謂之庚 ("sending out the word again is called 庚 (the seventh day)"). A
change pressed on people is not welcomed at first; given three days' notice and three days' grace,
it ends well. 18 蠱 ("Rot") has the same span around 甲 ("the first day of the cycle").

**Line 6** repeats line 2 exactly, and loses. 巽在牀下，喪其資斧，貞凶 ("kneeling beneath the bed, losing the
travelling axe; holding firm — ominous"). 56 旅 ("The Traveller"), the figure just before, has the
traveller 得其資斧 ("get the travelling axe"); here it is gone. Wang Bi: 斧所以斷者也 ("the axe is what one
cuts with") — what decides. Kneeling taken all the way up has nothing left to decide with.

### In a life now

This is the figure for influence without rank: the new person changing an old institution, the carer
at a bedside, the envoy, the teacher of a reluctant class, anyone who must get a thing accepted by
people who cannot be made to accept it. It counsels getting low, getting in, and coming again — and
it counsels timing: the word sent out with notice before and time after (line 5). It is honest about
the cost. Kneeling from a frown (line 3) stints. Kneeling as a habit, past the point where there is
anything to deliver, becomes the person who agrees with everyone and can no longer decide anything
(line 6). The test is line 2's and line 6's difference: does the one who kneels still carry the axe?

The Great Image turns this into practice: 君子以申命行事 ("the noble one sends the word out again, and
carries out affairs") — 命 ("the word sent") here what is spoken over a thing and sent with it, not a
decree. Influence that works is said more than once.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Partly shared, and they part at the floor.** The Laozi's chapter 66: *"Rivers and seas can rule
the hundred valleys / because they are masterful at staying below them."* The character is 下
("below"), and it is in 57's core text, 牀下 ("beneath the bed"); the English answers to it. But in 57
下 ("below") is first a place, and it becomes a stance only through Wang Bi's reading, so the kinship
is more thematic than textual. And the books part plainly: the Laozi sets no floor under lowness —
*"the large one ought to be underneath"* (ch 61). 57 sets one. At the top line, the same
getting-under is ominous. The I Ching's lowness has a limit, and this reading keeps it.
