## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × mountain ↔ lake.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 剝，剝也，柔變剛也 (*bō, bō yě, róu biàn gāng yě*), "stripping
is stripping: the yielding changes the firm." It reads the trigrams as counsel, 順而止之，觀象也 (*shùn
ér zhǐ zhī, guān xiàng yě*), "going along, and stopping it: watch the image," and it sets the whole in
a cycle: 君子尚消息盈虛，天行也 (*jūnzǐ shàng xiāo xī yíng xū, tiān xíng yě*), "the noble one values
waning and waxing, filling and emptying: that is the sky's going." The 大象傳: 山附地上 (*shān fù dì
shàng*), "the mountain leans on the earth," and 上以厚下，安宅 (*shàng yǐ hòu xià, ān zhái*), "those
above thicken those below and settle the house." The tradition sees the yielding climbing to the last
firm line. The advice is not to set out, to go along and hold, and to thicken the base.

**What the polarity implies.** Five open lines sit under one firm one (`000001`). The earth within
has no firm line at all. The only firm line in the whole figure is the mountain's lid, at the very
top, with nothing above to hold it and nothing below that will. The base does not fight. It gives
way, line by line, and what gives way underneath takes the top with it. The lines name this in
order: the bed stripped at the legs, at the frame, at the skin (剝牀以足 … 以辨 … 以膚, *bō chuáng
yǐ zú … yǐ biàn … yǐ fū*). The structure suggests stripping is what happens to a lid with no
ground under it. But the top still holds one thing: 碩果不食 (*shuò guǒ bù shí*), "the big fruit is
not eaten." 說卦 ch 11 has 艮…為果蓏 (*gèn wéi guǒ luǒ*, "mountain is fruit"), so the last firm line
is a fruit, with a seed in it. The same chapter has 坤…為大輿 (*kūn wéi dà yú*, "earth is the great
cart"), and that answers the rest of line 6: 君子得輿 (*jūnzǐ dé yú*), "the noble one gets a cart."
The earth that stripped the firm line is the earth that will carry it. Turn the figure over and the
fruit has fallen and lies at the bottom: that is 24. The 大象 has already said where to put the work.
厚下, "thicken what is below," is where the firm line is going next.

**For the name.** **Stripping**. The signature supports it. The figure is a lid whose ground gives
way from beneath, and *stripping* keeps the transitive agent the lines need. It is deepened by line
6: stripping reaches a core it does not eat. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Peeling*
survives the lines (剝牀以膚, "peel the bed to the skin"), and it carries the signature's own fruit,
but it is small and domestic for 剝廬 (*bō lú*, "strip the hut"). *Collapse* fails the lines: it is
intransitive, so "collapse the bed at the legs" does not parse, as `hexagram-names.md` already rules.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Everything is being taken away from below, and what is left at the top is a fruit no one eats.**
說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 剝 (*bō*) as 裂也 (*liè yě*, "to split"), a knife beside 彔 (*lù*), which it
glosses 刻割也 (*kè gē yě*, "carving, cutting"). The core text strips a bed in order — at the legs, at
the frame, at the skin — and then, at the top, stops: 碩果不食 (*shuò guǒ bù shí*, "the big fruit is not
eaten"). The 雜卦 (*záguà*) reads the figure another way, as 剝爛也 (*bō làn yě*, "stripping is
rotting"): not a blow but a decay. Both are in it. It is the season when things come apart, and the
one thing the stripping does not reach.

### The polarities in it

Earth within, mountain without. The inner figure has no firm line at all; it goes along with
whatever comes. The only firm line in the whole figure is the mountain's closed top edge. So the
figure is a lid with nothing under it that will hold — the soft gives way line by line, and the top
has only itself. The 彖 (*tuàn*) draws the counsel from this: 順而止之 (*shùn ér zhǐ zhī*, "go along,
and stop it"). Not resisting at the base, not advancing at the top: yielding where it is useless to
push, and holding where there is still something to hold.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 山附地上 (*shān fù dì shàng*, "the mountain leaning on the earth"). Look at a
cliff where a hard layer of rock lies over softer ones — a sea cliff, or the lip of a waterfall. Water
and frost wear the soft beds back underneath, the hard cap is left overhanging, and one winter it
falls. Cliffs of this kind are lost from below, not from the top. That is 23's shape exactly: five
soft lines beneath one hard, and the stripping working upward.

The fruit is as exact. Walk an orchard in late autumn, after the picking, and there is often one
apple left high in the crown where no ladder reached — held on after the leaves have gone. 碩 (*shuò*)
is in 說文 頭大也 (*tóu dà yě*, "big-headed"). Such a fruit falls in the end, and rots, as the 雜卦
says. The rot is not the finish: it frees the seed, and the seeds of apples and many temperate trees
need weeks of winter cold in the ground before they will sprout. The fruit not eaten is the one that
becomes a tree.

One more thing to notice is line 6's 廬 (*lú*). 說文 gives it as 寄也。秋冬去，春夏居 (*jì yě. qiū dōng
qù, chūn xià jū*, "a lodging: left in autumn and winter, lived in in spring and summer") — the field
hut used through the growing season and empty in the cold.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 剝 (*bō*, "Stripping" *(proposed)*), then 不利有攸往 (*bù lì yǒu yōu wǎng*, "no
advantage in having somewhere to go") — so the 彖 reads it, as one clause; the modern punctuation of
the core breaks it after 不利 (*bù lì*, "no advantage"). Either way, this is not a time to set out.

**Line 1** — 剝牀以足，蔑貞凶 (*bō chuáng yǐ zú, miè zhēn xiōng*, "the bed is stripped at the legs;
holding firm is pared away — ominous"). 王弼 reads 蔑 (*miè*) as 削 (*xuē*, "to pare"). If 貞 is taken
in its older sense, *the inquiry*, the line reads "the inquiry is set at nothing" — the omen already
bad. 牀 (*chuáng*) is 說文 安身之坐者 (*ān shēn zhī zuò zhě*, "what the body rests on"). The stripping
starts where a thing stands.

**Line 2** — 剝牀以辨，蔑貞凶 (*bō chuáng yǐ biàn, miè zhēn xiōng*, "stripped at the frame; holding
firm pared away — ominous"). 王弼: 辨者足之上也 (*biàn zhě zú zhī shàng yě*, "the frame is what is
above the legs"). One step higher, the same verdict.

**Line 3** — 剝之，无咎 (*bō zhī, wú jiù*, "strip it — no blame"). The one soft line that answers the
firm line at the top. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*): 失上下也 (*shī shàng xià yě*, "it parts from those above
and below"). Among the strippers, it sides with what is stripped, and is not blamed.

**Line 4** — 剝牀以膚，凶 (*bō chuáng yǐ fū, xiōng*, "stripped to the skin — ominous"). The bed is gone;
now the body. The 小象: 切近災也 (*qiè jìn zāi yě*, "cutting close to disaster").

**Line 5**, the centre above, turns. 貫魚，以宮人寵，无不利 (*guàn yú, yǐ gōng rén chǒng, wú bù lì*,
"fish on a string; favour as for the palace women — nothing without advantage"). 王弼 sees the five
soft lines 駢頭相次似貫魚也 (*pián tóu xiāng cì sì guàn yú yě*, "head to head, one after another, like
fish on a string"). The line that could lead the stripping instead puts the soft in order under the
firm, and the verdicts change.

**Line 6** — 碩果不食，君子得輿，小人剝廬 (*shuò guǒ bù shí, jūnzǐ dé yú, xiǎo rén bō lú*, "the big
fruit is not eaten; the noble one gets a cart, small people strip the hut"). The same last line
carries people or is torn down for timber. The small people strip the shelter they will need in
spring.

### In a life now

This is the figure for decline: an organisation losing its people from the bottom, a body in old age
or long illness, a house left unrepaired, a marriage worn thin, a season of losses one after another.
It does not counsel fighting — 不利有攸往, no advantage in setting out. It names what can still be
done. Stand, like line 3, with what is being stripped, even alone. Put what is left in order, like
line 5. And keep the seed: do not eat the last fruit, do not pull down the hut for firewood. What is
kept at the end of stripping is what the next season grows from.

The 大象 turns this to practice: 上以厚下，安宅 (*shàng yǐ hòu xià, ān zhái*, "those above thicken what
is below, and settle the house"). When everything is being taken from below, put the work into the
base.
