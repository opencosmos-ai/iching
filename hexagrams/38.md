## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 *duì* · yin · releasing). Without: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching).
**Across** — the yin pole of mountain ↔ lake within, the yin pole of water ↔ fire without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 reads the figure as two motions away from each other: 火動而上，澤動而下；
二女同居，其志不同行 (*huǒ dòng ér shàng, zé dòng ér xià; èr nǚ tóng jū, qí zhì bù tóng xíng*, "fire moves and
goes up, the lake moves and goes down; two women live together, and their aims do not go the same way").
Then it turns: 天地睽，而其事同也 (*tiān dì kuí, ér qí shì tóng yě*, "sky and earth are estranged, yet their
undertaking is one"). The 大象: 上火下澤，睽；君子以同而異 (*shàng huǒ xià zé, kuí; jūnzǐ yǐ tóng ér yì*, "fire
above, lake below: estrangement; the noble one is at one with others, and different"). For the
tradition, estrangement is the condition of any pair — and pairs still work.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire rises and the lake settles, and here fire is already above and the
lake already below: each follows its own nature, and each step of that widens the gap. Nobody is pushing
anyone away. Both trigrams are yin — 說卦's middle and youngest daughters, neither with a ruling firm line — so
nothing in the figure forces them apart but their direction. The structure suggests estrangement without
enmity: a drift, not a quarrel. It is 37 turned over, and the inversion is exact — the fire that was the
household's centre has gone outside and looks elsewhere (離為目 *lí wéi mù*, "fire is the eye"), and
within is a lake, open at the top, releasing, talking (兌為口 *duì wéi kǒu*, "the lake is the mouth"): a
mouth speaking to an eye that is looking away. 說文's gloss fits: 目不相聽也 (*mù bù xiāng tīng yě*, "eyes that
do not heed each other").

That is why the lines keep turning misrecognition into meeting: 匪寇婚媾 (*fěi kòu hūn gòu*, "not a raider —
a marriage match"). And the resolving verb is the lake's own: 先張之弧，后說之弧 (*xiān zhāng zhī hú, hòu tuō
zhī hú*, "first drawing the bow, then setting the bow down") — 說, read *tuō* there, "let go", is the same
character as the lake's action 說 (*yuè*, releasing). The structure suggests the estrangement is undone by
what was within from the start.

**For the name.** **Estrangement** — the signature supports and deepens it: two things drifting by their
own nature, a bond going apart, which *estrangement* carries better than *opposition*; 睽孤 (*kuí gū*,
"estranged and alone") holds. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Looking Apart*, *Drifting Apart* —
*Looking Apart*, the fire as the eye, survives the lines: 睽孤 (*kuí gū*, "looking apart, alone"), twice;
*Drifting Apart*, the 彖's two motions, also survives 睽孤 (*kuí gū*, "drifting apart, alone"), but it is
the 彖's mechanics made into a name — commentary shaped like an answer — and loses the 說文 eye.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Estrangement is a way of seeing — the other looks like a threat — and the lines undo it not by
argument but by meetings nobody arranged.** The graph 睽 (*kuí*) is an eye, 目 (*mù*), with 癸
(*guǐ*) for the sound, and 說文 (*shuōwén*) glosses it 目不相聽也 (*mù bù xiāng tīng yě*, "eyes that do
not heed each other"). The core text turns on two verbs. 見 (*jiàn*, "see") comes three times, and
each time what is seen is ugly or frightening: a hostile person, a mutilated man, a pig in mud. 遇
(*yù*, "meet") comes three times, and each meeting helps: a master in a lane, a worthy man, the rain.
Estrangement is seeing gone wrong; meeting is what corrects it. And the judgment keeps the hope
small: 小事吉 (*xiǎo shì jí*, "small affairs: auspicious").

### The polarities in it

Lake within, fire without — 37 turned over (see *The signature*, above). The fire that was the
household's hearth is now outside and above; within is a lake, open at the top. Each does only
what it does: the fire goes up, the lake settles, and the 彖 says it as a drift, not a quarrel —
火動而上，澤動而下 (*huǒ dòng ér shàng, zé dòng ér xià*, "fire moves and goes up, the lake moves and
goes down"). **Here the structure and the text part a little, and the text wins.** The figure shows
two things going apart. The six lines are full of things coming together: a horse returning, a
meeting in a lane, a friend found, kin at a meal, a wedding party, rain. The structure names the
condition; the lines are about what happens inside it.

### In nature

Stand at the edge of a still lake at dusk with a fire burning on the far shore. The flame goes up;
its image in the water goes down. It is the same fire, moving two ways at once, and the gap between
them widens as the flame climbs. That is 上火下澤 (*shàng huǒ xià zé*, "fire above, lake below") seen
plainly — and the 大象's 同而異 (*tóng ér yì*, "at one, and different") in a single picture.

The lines' animals behave exactly as animals do. Anyone who keeps horses knows the first line:
喪馬勿逐，自復 (*sàng mǎ wù zhú, zì fù*, "the lost horse — do not chase it; it comes back of itself").
Chase a loose horse and it runs; stop, and it drifts back to the herd, the feed and the ground it
knows. The pig at line 6, 豕負涂 (*shǐ fù tú*, "a pig carrying mud on its back"), is a pig after its
wallow: pigs barely sweat, and they cake themselves in mud to cool down and to keep off sun and
flies. In poor light a mud-caked pig is a shape, not an animal — which is the point.

And the bow. 繫辭下 says the first bows were taken from this figure: 弦木為弧 (*xián mù wéi hú*,
"they strung wood to make the bow"), 蓋取諸睽 (*gài qǔ zhū kuí*, "taken, perhaps, from estrangement").
A bow is opposition put to use — stave and string pull against each other, and the pull is its
force. An archer unstrings a bow that is not needed, because a bow left strung takes a set and
loses its cast. Line 6's 后說之弧 (*hòu tuō zhī hú*, "then unstringing the bow") is what bows need.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 睽：小事吉 (*kuí: xiǎo shì jí*) — "Estrangement. Small affairs: auspicious." No large
undertaking is promised. The core text says only: keep it small.

**Line 1.** 悔亡，喪馬勿逐，自復；見惡人无咎 (*huǐ wáng, sàng mǎ wù zhú, zì fù; jiàn è rén wú jiù*,
"regret goes; the lost horse — do not chase it, it comes back of itself; seeing a hostile person —
no blame"). The first counsel of estrangement is not to pursue — and not to refuse to look. The
小象: 以辟咎也 (*yǐ bì jiù yě*, "that is how blame is avoided").

**Line 2.** 遇主于巷，无咎 (*yù zhǔ yú xiàng, wú jiù*, "meets the master in a lane — no blame"). Not at
the front gate, not by appointment: 王弼 says 不期而遇 (*bù qī ér yù*, "they meet without having
planned it"). In estrangement the formal way is shut; the side street is open.

**Line 3.** 見輿曳，其牛掣，其人天且劓，无初有終 (*jiàn yú yè, qí niú chè, qí rén tiān qiě yì, wú chū
yǒu zhōng*, "sees the cart dragged back, its ox pulled up short, its driver marked on the head and
the nose cut off — no beginning, but an end"). The worst sight in the figure, at the seam between
the trigrams. 天 (*tiān*) here is not the sky: 說文 gives the graph as 顛也 (*diān yě*, "the crown of
the head"), and the line uses it for a punishment on the head. Everything is held back — and yet,
an end.

**Line 4.** 睽孤，遇元夫，交孚，厲无咎 (*kuí gū, yù yuán fū, jiāo fú, lì wú jiù*, "estranged and alone,
meets a worthy man; confidence exchanged — peril, no blame"). 說文's 孤 (*gū*) is 無父也 (*wú fù yě*,
"fatherless"): the orphan of the household just left behind. Two who are each alone find each
other, and 交孚 (*jiāo fú*, "confidence exchanged") runs both ways.

**Line 5.** 悔亡，厥宗噬膚，往何咎 (*huǐ wáng, jué zōng shì fū, wǎng hé jiù*, "regret goes; the kin bite
through the tender meat — going on, what blame?"). A meal among kin, and meat soft enough to bite
through: 王弼 reads 噬膚 (*shì fū*, "biting the skin") as 齧柔也 (*niè róu yě*, "biting what is
soft"). At the yielding centre, the way back is easy.

**Line 6** is the whole figure in one line. 睽孤，見豕負涂，載鬼一車，先張之弧，后說之弧，匪寇婚媾，往遇雨則吉
(*kuí gū, jiàn shǐ fù tú, zài guǐ yī chē, xiān zhāng zhī hú, hòu tuō zhī hú, fěi kòu hūn gòu, wǎng yù
yǔ zé jí*, "estranged and alone: sees a pig carrying mud, a cart loaded with ghosts; first draws the
bow, then unstrings it; not raiders — a marriage party; going on, meets rain, and it is
auspicious"). Every threat is misseen, and the seeing corrects itself in order: the mud, the
ghosts, the arrow almost loosed, and then the people are known. 說文 gives 媾 (*gòu*) as 重㛰也
(*chóng hūn yě*, "marrying again into the same house") and quotes this very line: the party is kin
renewing a tie. The rain ends it — the 小象's 群疑亡也 (*qún yí wáng yě*, "all doubts are gone").

### In a life now

Estrangement is the silence between siblings after a parent's death, the colleague one no longer
greets, a marriage lived in separate rooms, a country split so that each half sees the other as a
raiding party. The text does not promise reconciliation by effort. It counsels a stance. Do not
chase (line 1). Do not refuse to look (line 1, again). Take the meeting that happens in the side
street rather than waiting for a formal one (line 2). Expect small things to go well and nothing
large (the judgment). And distrust your own sight: what looks like a pig in mud, a cart of ghosts,
an enemy, may be your own people coming to renew the tie (line 6). The bow is drawn; the line
honours the one who unstrings it.

The 大象 names the practice: 君子以同而異 (*jūnzǐ yǐ tóng ér yì*, "the noble one is at one with
others, and different"). 王弼 makes it concrete: 同於通理，異於職事 (*tóng yú tōng lǐ, yì yú zhí shì*,
"one with them in the common order of things, different in their own work"). Estrangement is not
cured by becoming the same.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The words are shared, at the Wings' layer only.** Chapter 1, in Shalom's translation: *"These
two have the same origin, but differ in name."* The Chinese there sets 同 (*tóng*, "the same")
beside 異 (*yì*, "different") — the pair the 大象 uses in 同而異 (*tóng ér yì*, "at one, and
different"). The test: the characters are in 38's 大象, not its core text, and Shalom's *same …
differ* answers to them. The 彖 makes the same turn at the largest scale: 天地睽，而其事同也 (*tiān dì
kuí, ér qí shì tóng yě*, "sky and earth are estranged, yet their affairs are the same").

They part over what the two are. Chapter 1's pair is the nameless and the named, coming out of one
source before anything is divided. 38's are people — in the 彖, 二女同居 (*èr nǚ tóng jū*, "two
women living together") — already divided, and working anyway. The Laozi goes back to where the
two are one; the 大象 stays where they are two and asks only that they act together. This is
kinship at the Wing layer; the core text of 38 has no echo in the Laozi.

- 見 · 遇 (*jiàn · yù*) — *see · meet*, kept distinct: the figure turns on the difference
- 天 (*tiān*) at line 3 — *the crown of the head* (說文 顛也, *diān yě*), a punishment; the lock (*sky* /

- 事 (*shì*) — *affairs* (locked) · 天地 (*tiān dì*) — *sky and earth* (locked)
- 說 (*tuō*) at line 6 — *unstring*; the same graph is the lake's 說 (*yuè*, releasing) — name the sense
- 元夫 (*yuán fū*) — *a worthy man*; 元 here is an adjective, not the formula *from the source*
