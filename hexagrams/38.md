## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 *duì* · yin · releasing). Without: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching).
**Across** — the yin pole of mountain ↔ lake within, the yin pole of water ↔ fire without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 reads the figure as two motions away from each other: 火動而上，澤動而下；
二女同居，其志不同行 (*huǒ dòng ér shàng, zé dòng ér xià; èr nǚ tóng jū, qí zhì bù tóng xíng*, "fire moves and
goes up, the lake moves and goes down; two women live together, and their aims do not go the same way").
Then it turns: 天地睽，而其事同也 (*tiān dì kuí, ér qí shì tóng yě*, "sky and earth are estranged, yet their
undertaking is one"). The 大象: 上火下澤，睽；君子以同而異 (*shàng huǒ xià zé, kuí; jūnzǐ yǐ tóng ér yì*, "fire
above, lake below: estrangement; the noble one is at one with others, and different"). For the
tradition, estrangement is the condition of any pair — and pairs still work.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire rises and the lake settles, and here fire is already above and the
lake already below: each follows its own nature, and each step of that widens the gap. Nobody is pushing
anyone away. Both trigrams are yin — 說卦's middle and youngest daughters, neither with a ruling firm line — so
nothing in the figure forces them apart but their direction. The structure suggests estrangement without
enmity: a drift, not a quarrel. It is 37 turned over, and the inversion is exact — the fire that was the
household's centre has gone outside and looks elsewhere (離為目 *lí wéi mù*, "fire is the eye"), and
within is a lake, open at the top, releasing, talking (兌為口 *duì wéi kǒu*, "the lake is the mouth"): a
mouth speaking to an eye that is looking away. 說文's gloss fits: 目不相聽也 (*mù bù xiāng tīng yě*, "eyes that
do not heed each other").

That is why the lines keep turning misrecognition into meeting: 匪寇婚媾 (*fěi kòu hūn gòu*, "not a raider —
a marriage match"). And the resolving verb is the lake's own: 先張之弧，后說之弧 (*xiān zhāng zhī hú, hòu tuō
zhī hú*, "first drawing the bow, then setting the bow down") — 說, read *tuō* there, "let go", is the same
character as the lake's action 說 (*yuè*, releasing). The structure suggests the estrangement is undone by
what was within from the start.

**For the name.** **Estrangement** — the signature supports and deepens it: two things drifting by their
own nature, a bond going apart, which *estrangement* carries better than *opposition*; 睽孤 (*kuí gū*,
"estranged and alone") holds. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Looking Apart*, *Drifting Apart* —
*Looking Apart*, the fire as the eye, survives the lines: 睽孤 (*kuí gū*, "looking apart, alone"), twice;
*Drifting Apart*, the 彖's two motions, also survives 睽孤 (*kuí gū*, "drifting apart, alone"), but it is
the 彖's mechanics made into a name — commentary shaped like an answer — and loses the 說文 eye.
