## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching). Without: **earth** (坤 *kūn* · yin · going along).
**Across** — the yin pole of water ↔ fire within, the yin pole of sky ↔ earth without.

**What the tradition reads.** Both Wings reverse 35's sunrise: 明入地中，明夷 (*míng rù dì zhōng, míng yí*,
"clarity enters into the earth: clarity wounded"). The 彖 is unusually explicit about within and
without: 內文明而外柔順，以蒙大難，文王以之 (*nèi wén míng ér wài róu shùn, yǐ méng dà nàn, Wén Wáng yǐ zhī*,
"patterned and clear within, yielding and going along without — so one bears a great hardship; 文王, the
Zhou's Ruler Wen, did this"), and 晦其明也 (*huì qí míng yě*, "it darkens its clarity"). The 大象: 君子以蒞眾，
用晦而明 (*jūnzǐ yǐ lì zhòng, yòng huì ér míng*, "the noble one oversees the crowd — uses darkness, and so
is clear"). The tradition reads a time when clarity must hide to survive.

**What the polarity implies.** The same two strokes as 35, and only the inside has changed. In 35 fire
was without — clarity was the situation, the thing seen. Here it is within: clarity is the self, and
over it lies earth, all open, going along with whatever presses on it. 35 and 36 are day and night drawn
with the same two trigrams. And the structure suggests something the name only half says: the light is
not put out. Fire attaches, and it attaches now to what covers it — a banked fire, an ember under ash.
The hollow at the fire's centre, in 35 the open face of the sun, is here a hidden core. That is how the
tradition can say 用晦而明 (*yòng huì ér míng*, "using darkness, and so clear") without paradox, and why
the 小象 on 箕子 (*Jīzǐ*) insists 明不可息也 (*míng bù kě xī yě*, "clarity cannot be snuffed out").

The wound is in the relation: an earth that goes along with power strikes whatever light shows itself —
夷于左股 (*yí yú zuǒ gǔ*, "wounded in the left thigh"). 說卦's correspondences land on the exact lines:
離為雉 (*lí wéi zhì*, "fire is the pheasant"), and in the fire's bottom line 明夷于飛，垂其翼 (*míng yí yú fēi,
chuí qí yì*, "clarity wounded in flight, its wings drooping"); 坤為腹 (*kūn wéi fù*, "earth is the belly"),
and at the earth's bottom line 入于左腹 (*rù yú zuǒ fù*, "entering the left belly"). The top line tells 35
and 36 in one sentence, of the darkness itself: 初登于天，后入于地 (*chū dēng yú tiān, hòu rù yú dì*, "first
it climbed to the sky, then it went into the earth").

**For the name.** **Clarity Wounded** — the lines support it (夷 *yí* is a verb, "wound", in 夷于左股), but
the signature deepens it: the clarity is wounded from without and kept whole within, and *wounded*
alone may read as the light failing, which 明不可息 (*míng bù kě xī*, "clarity cannot be snuffed out")
denies. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Clarity Brought Low*, *Clarity Hidden* — *Clarity Brought
Low* survives the lines: 明夷于飛，垂其翼 (*míng yí yú fēi, chuí qí yì*, "clarity brought low in flight, its
wings drooping"), 箕子之明夷 (*Jīzǐ zhī míng yí*, "Jizi's clarity brought low"), though 夷于左股 (*yí yú zuǒ
gǔ*) as "brought low in the left thigh" strains; *Clarity Hidden*, which is the signature's banked fire
and the 彖's 晦 (*huì*, "darken"), fails the lines — 夷于左股 (*yí yú zuǒ gǔ*, "wounded in the left thigh")
is a wound, not a hiding, and the hiding is a Wing's reading.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Clarity struck down by what is over it, and kept alive by going out of sight.** 說文 (*shuōwén*)
gives 夷 (*yí*) as 平也 (*píng yě*, "level"), a person, 大 (*dà*), with a bow, 弓 (*gōng*): to level,
to bring low. The core text makes the sense bodily in its second line — 夷于左股 (*yí yú zuǒ gǔ*,
"wounded in the left thigh") — and 序卦 (*xùguà*) says it outright: 夷者傷也 (*yí zhě shāng yě*,
"夷 is wounding"). The judgment asks for one thing, holding firm through hardship. The lines tell
what that looks like under a ruler who strikes whatever shows: flight without food, a wound and a
horse to escape on, a hunt that takes the head, a way into the dark house and a way out, and one
named person who kept clarity by hiding it.

### The polarities in it

Fire within attaches; earth without goes along. The clarity is the inside of this figure, and what
lies over it is the one trigram that goes along with whatever presses on it — so the earth, here,
is the whole weight of a bad order, obeyed. The lines divide at the seam. The three of the fire
**act**: they fly, are wounded and ride out, go hunting. The three of the earth are **inside**: they
enter the belly of the house, sit beside the dark as 箕子 (*Jīzǐ*) did, or are the dark. The Wings
read the figure as hiding — 晦其明也 (*huì qí míng yě*, "it obscures its clarity"). **The core text
has more fight in it than that:** its third line hunts and takes the great head. Where the Wing and
the lines differ, the lines stand.

### In nature

Two lights go down in this figure, and both come back. The 大象 has the sun: 明入地中 (*míng rù dì
zhōng*, "clarity enters into the earth"). The top line has the moon. 說文 gives 晦 (*huì*) as 月盡也
(*yuè jìn yě*, "the moon used up") — the last night of the lunar month, when there is none. And the
old form of 明 (*míng*) in 說文 is moonlight through a window. So 不明晦 (*bù míng huì*) is, in its own
graphs, the moonless night. Watch for it: at the dark of the moon the moon is not gone. It stands
near the sun, rising and setting with it, lost in the glare. Two or three evenings later a thin
crescent shows low in the west just after sunset.

The first line is a bird. Its grammar is the grammar of a bird's name in flight — 明夷于飛，垂其翼
(*míng yí yú fēi, chuí qí yì*, "clarity wounded, in flight, drooping its wings") — and 說卦 (*shuōguà*)
gives fire the pheasant, 離為雉 (*lí wéi zhì*). A bird hurt in the wing flies low and heavy, one wing
hanging. There is a second thing worth watching, which the line does not say: ground-nesting birds —
plovers, partridges — drag one wing as if broken to draw a fox away from the nest. The injury you
can see is not always the whole story.

The third line is a hunt with dogs: 說文's 狩 (*shòu*) is 犬田也 (*quǎn tián yě*, "hunting with dogs"),
and it cites this very line. 說卦 ch 5 makes fire the trigram of the south.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 明夷：利艱貞 (*míng yí: lì jiān zhēn*) — "Clarity wounded: advantageous to hold firm
in hardship."

**Line 1.** 明夷于飛，垂其翼。君子于行，三日不食，有攸往，主人有言 (*míng yí yú fēi, chuí qí yì. jūnzǐ yú
xíng, sān rì bù shí, yǒu yōu wǎng, zhǔ rén yǒu yán*, "clarity wounded, in flight, drooping its wings.
The noble one on the road, three days not eating; wherever they go, the host has words"). The first
move is to leave early, hungry, and be looked at askance where one arrives.

**Line 2**, at the fire's centre. 明夷，夷于左股，用拯馬壯，吉 (*míng yí, yí yú zuǒ gǔ, yòng zhěng mǎ
zhuàng, jí*, "clarity wounded, wounded in the left thigh; use a rescuing horse, a vigorous one —
auspicious"). Hurt, but not where it stops one riding. The vigour of 34 is here, and it is the
horse's, used to get away.

**Line 3** is the fire at its top. 明夷于南狩，得其大首，不可疾貞 (*míng yí yú nán shòu, dé qí dà shǒu, bù
kě jí zhēn*, "clarity wounded on the southern hunt takes its great head; one cannot hurry holding
firm"). The only strike in the figure, and it lands. Even so: 王弼 says 不可速正 (*bù kě sù zhèng*,
"it cannot be set right quickly").

**Line 4** crosses into the earth. 入于左腹，獲明夷之心，于出門庭 (*rù yú zuǒ fù, huò míng yí zhī xīn, yú
chū mén tíng*, "entering the left belly, one gets the heart of clarity wounded — and goes out through
gate and courtyard"). Inside the dark house, one learns what is at its heart, and leaves. 說卦 gives
earth the belly, 坤為腹 (*kūn wéi fù*).

**Line 5** is the ruler's seat, and a minister sits in it. 箕子之明夷，利貞 (*Jīzǐ zhī míng yí, lì zhēn*,
"箕子's clarity wounded: advantageous to hold firm"). 箕子 (*Jīzǐ*) was kin to the last Shang ruler;
the histories say he feigned madness rather than serve or die. The 彖 names what he did:
內難而能正其志 (*nèi nàn ér néng zhèng qí zhì*, "hardship within the house, and able to keep one's
purpose upright"); the 小象: 明不可息也 (*míng bù kě xī yě*, "clarity cannot be snuffed out").

**Line 6** is the dark itself. 不明晦，初登于天，后入于地 (*bù míng huì, chū dēng yú tiān, hòu rù yú dì*,
"no clarity — obscure. First it climbed to the sky; then it went into the earth"). The 小象 reads the
climb as light — 照四國也 (*zhào sì guó yě*, "it shone on the four lands") — so what wounds all the
clarity below was once clear itself.

### In a life now

This is the figure for working under someone who punishes what is seen clearly: a regime, a company
where the truth costs the teller, a household with a cruel parent, a church or a party that has
turned. The text does not ask for martyrdom. It asks for holding firm in hardship, and it gives the
ways: leave early, even hungry, and accept being looked at askance; take the horse when it is
offered; if the chance comes to strike, strike at the head and do not expect everything to be set
right at once; if one is inside, learn the heart of the thing, and find the gate. And if none of
that is possible, 箕子's way: keep the purpose upright where no one can see it.

The 大象 turns this toward leading: 君子以蒞眾，用晦而明 (*jūnzǐ yǐ lì zhòng, yòng huì ér míng*, "the
noble one oversees the crowd — using obscurity, and so clear"). 王弼: 藏明於内乃得明也 (*cáng míng yú
nèi nǎi dé míng yě*, "hide clarity within, and clarity is had"). Not every clear thing seen needs to
be shown.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A graph shared, and a graph that only looks shared.** Chapter 41: *"The clear Tao seems murky… The
level Tao seems rough."* *Clear* there is 明 (*míng*), the first graph of this hexagram's name, in the
core text; the English answers to it; the echo passes both questions. In both books clarity can
look like its opposite and not be it.

But the next line is a trap. *Level* in chapter 41 is 夷 (*yí*) — the second graph of the name —
and there it means what 說文 says, *level*. In 36 the core text uses 夷 for a wound. Same graph,
different word. And the books part on why clarity looks dim: in the Laozi it is subtle by nature; in
明夷 (*míng yí*, "clarity wounded") it is dimmed because it is struck, by a named ruler in a named
age, and it survives by hiding and, once, by hunting. The Laozi's own chapter 36 has *the hidden
clarity* — a coincidence of numbering, and its 微 (*wēi*, "hidden, subtle") is not in this hexagram.

### Words kept

- 明夷 (*míng yí*) — *clarity wounded*, the proposed name (not yet approved)
- 明 (*míng*) — *clarity*, never *enlightenment*, never *illumination*, never *brilliance* (locked)
- 夷 (*yí*) — *wounded* in this hexagram, *level* in 說文 and the Laozi
- 晦 (*huì*) — *obscure*, *obscurity*, here: not *dark*, which is locked to 玄 (*xuán*); 說文 makes it the moonless night. **For Shalom to rule.**
- 心 (*xīn*) — *heart*, never *mind* (locked)
- 天 · 地 (*tiān · dì*) — *sky · earth* (locked)
- 眾 (*zhòng*) — *the crowd*, never *the multitude* (locked)
- 壯 (*zhuàng*) — *vigorous*, as in 34
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss (A6); 利艱貞 (*lì jiān zhēn*), "advantageous to hold firm in hardship"
