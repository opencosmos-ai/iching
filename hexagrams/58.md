## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Lake** (兌 · yin · releasing). Without: **Lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Doubled** — mountain ↔ lake, the open pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) glosses the name with the action and places it: 兌，說也。剛中而柔外 (*duì, yuè yě. gāng zhōng ér róu wài*), "兌 is 說 — delight, release, speech; firm at the centre and yielding outside." It follows delight into public use: 說以先民，民忘其勞 (*yuè yǐ xiān mín, mín wàng qí láo*), "when delight goes before the people, the people forget their toil." The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) reads the doubling as company: 麗澤，兌；君子以朋友講習 (*lì zé, duì; jūnzǐ yǐ péngyǒu jiǎng xí*), "lakes joined together: 兌; the noble one talks things over and practises with friends."

**What the polarity implies.** A lake is firm beneath and open at the top; its mouth is its surface. Doubled, there is no mountain anywhere in the figure — nothing that holds — so what keeps release from simply spilling cannot be a rim. The 彖 finds it underneath: the firm lines sit at the bottom and centre of each trigram (剛中), so this is delight with a bed under it, not a surface without depth. And since nothing restrains it, the lines sort delight by its source instead: 和兌 (*hé duì*), "in harmony", auspicious; 孚兌 (*fú duì*), with 孚 (*fú*, its English pending), auspicious; 來兌 (*lái duì*), "that comes to you", ominous; 商兌 (*shāng duì*), "bargained over", 未寧 (*wèi níng*), "not yet settled"; 引兌 (*yǐn duì*), "drawn out", with no verdict at all. The doubling itself is an exchange. 說卦 (*shuōguà*) gives the lake the mouth, 兌為口 (*duì wéi kǒu*), "兌 is the mouth", and says 說言乎兌 (*yuè yán hū duì*), "in 兌 things are glad and speak." Two lakes are two mouths: the 大象's 講習. The structure suggests the three senses of 說 are one act seen three ways — opening, being glad of it, saying it — and that this is the one figure where the act happens between two parties. Its danger is the fifth line's 孚于剝 (*fú yú bō*): 孚 given to what strips away — gladness and speech trusted where they should not be.

**For the name.** *Delight* — the signature strains it slightly: the doubling makes the figure an exchange, and *Delight* is felt by one party. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Talk* — survives: 和兌 "harmonious talk", 來兌 "talk that comes to you", 商兌，未寧 "bargaining talk, not yet settled", 引兌 "talk drawn out" — every line reads, and 商 (*shāng*, "to bargain, to weigh") reads better than under *Delight*; *Opening* — fails the lines: 孚兌 and 商兌 as "an opening with 孚" and "a bargained opening" lose what the modifiers qualify. (*Release*, the action's own word, is taken by 40 解.)
