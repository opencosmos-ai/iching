## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind × sky ↔ earth: a trigram open at the base under one with no odd line.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 姤，遇也，柔遇剛也 (*gòu, yù yě, róu yù gāng yě*, "*gòu* is
meeting: the yielding meets the firm"). It keeps the judgement's warning — 勿用取女，不可與長也 (*wù
yòng qǔ nǚ, bù kě yǔ cháng yě*, "do not take the woman: one cannot last long with her") — and then
widens the meeting to everything: 天地相遇，品物咸章也 (*tiān dì xiāng yù, pǐn wù xián zhāng yě*, "sky
and earth meet each other, and the kinds of things all show their pattern"). The 大象傳: 天下有風，
姤；后以施命誥四方 (*tiān xià yǒu fēng, gòu; hòu yǐ shī mìng gào sì fāng*, "under the sky there is
wind: *gòu*; the sovereign issues a charge and proclaims it to the four quarters"). The tradition
holds both at once: an encounter dangerous to hold on to, and a meeting through which everything is
reached.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 43 turned over. The one open line that was leaving at the top
of 43 is at the very bottom here, arriving. Wind is the sky with its base opened, and the sky above
has no opening at all — a wholly firm world with one soft place at its root. And the soft thing is
the one that moves: wind's action is entering. Nothing in the sky is open to receive it, and it does
not need to be; it comes in underneath. In 43 the strength was the self's and the opening the
world's; here the strength is the world's, and the opening is in the self.

Entering within, vigour without. That is the 大象傳's wind under the sky, reaching the four quarters
because it goes under everything rather than through anything. It is also the judgement's warning,
arrived at from the figure: 女壯 (*nǚ zhuàng*, "the woman is strong") — not because the one line is
great, but because it is already inside, at the foundation, and it is the only line that differs.
The first line reads like the figure's own answer: 系于金柅 (*xì yú jīn nǐ*, "tie it to a metal
brake"). The structure suggests an encounter nobody arranged: the strong did not seek it, and have
no gate to shut against it.

**For the name.** *Encounter* — the signature supports it: it draws an unsought meeting, which is
what *encounter* has over *meeting*. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Chance Meeting*,
*Infiltration* — *Chance Meeting* survives: 姤其角 (*gòu qí jiǎo*, "a chance meeting at its horns")
reads; *Infiltration* — the slight line slipping in at the base — fails the lines: 姤其角 (*gòu qí
jiǎo*, "meeting at its horns") happens at the top, head-on, and infiltrates nothing.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**An unsought meeting at the root: what has arrived is to be held — wrapped, tied, let ripen — and
not married.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 姤 (*gòu*) as 偶也 (*ǒu yě*, "a mate, a pairing"), from 女 (*nǚ*,
woman) with 后 (*hòu*) for the sound; every Wing that glosses it says 遇 (*yù*), which 說文 gives as
逢也 (*féng yě*, "to come upon"). The judgment is one image and one warning: 女壯，勿用取女 (*nǚ
zhuàng, wù yòng qǔ nǚ*, "the woman is vigorous; do not take the woman in marriage"). Then the lines
turn on a single verb, 包 (*bāo*, "to wrap"): 包有魚 (*bāo yǒu yú*, "in the wrapper, a fish"), 包无魚
(*bāo wú yú*, "in the wrapper, no fish"), 以杞包瓜 (*yǐ qǐ bāo guā*, "the wolfberry wrapping melons").
What is met is kept near and contained, not taken into the house.

### The polarities in it

Wind within, sky without: a trigram open at its base under one with no opening at all. This is 43
turned over — the yielding line that was leaving at the top arrives at the bottom (see *The
signature*, above). And the soft thing is the one that moves; wind's action is entering, and it gets
in under a world that did not open for it. That is why the woman is 壯 (*zhuàng*, "vigorous"): not
because one line is great, but because it is already inside, at the root, and it is the only line
that differs. Text and structure agree on the hazard. Then the 彖 does what the lines never do —
天地相遇，品物咸章也 (*tiān dì xiāng yù, pǐn wù xián zhāng yě*, "sky and earth meet each other, and
the kinds of things all show their pattern"), and 姤之時義大矣哉 (*gòu zhī shí yì dà yǐ zāi*, "great
indeed is the meaning of the time of meeting"). The core text warns against making the meeting
permanent; the Wing praises the meeting itself. Both stand.

### In nature

The 大象: 天下有風 (*tiān xià yǒu fēng*, "under the sky there is wind"). Notice where wind goes: under
doors, through the grass stems, round the trunk rather than through it. It reaches everything by going
under. 說文's gloss of 后 (*hòu*, "sovereign") reads almost as this image: 施令以告四方 (*shī lìng yǐ
gào sì fāng*, "issuing the call to tell the four quarters").

The lines hold a tethered pig and a hidden melon. 羸豕孚踟躅 (*léi shǐ fú chí zhú*, line 1): 說文 gives
羸 (*léi*) as 瘦也 (*shòu yě*, "lean"), and 王弼 reads 羸豕謂牝豕也 (*léi shǐ wèi pìn shǐ yě*, "the lean
pig means a sow"). A sow in season does not stand still; she paces the length of her rope and
presses at it. The line's 金柅 (*jīn nǐ*, "metal brake") is what holds her. At line 5 a melon vine
runs under 杞 (*qǐ*), which 說文 calls 枸杞也 (*gǒu qǐ yě*, "the wolfberry"), a thorny shrub. The fruit
swells hidden in leaf, its rind taking on its markings unseen — 含章 (*hán zhāng*, "holding the pattern
within"). Then 有隕自天 (*yǒu yǔn zì tiān*, "there is a falling from the sky"); 說文 quotes this very
phrase under 隕 (*yǔn*), 從高下也 (*cóng gāo xià yě*, "to come down from a height"). The text does not
say what falls. A ripe muskmelon parts from its own stalk at a touch, and that is one way to read it:
what ripens covered comes down in its time.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 姤：女壯，勿用取女 (*gòu: nǚ zhuàng, wù yòng qǔ nǚ*, "meeting: the woman is
vigorous; do not take the woman in marriage"). The 彖: 不可與長也 (*bù kě yǔ cháng yě*, "one cannot
last long together"). 王弼 makes it moral — 一女而遇五男 (*yī nǚ ér yù wǔ nán*, "one woman meeting
five men") — but the core text says only *vigorous* and *do not wed*: a meeting to meet, not to keep.

**Line 1** is the soft line itself, at the root. 系于金柅，貞吉，有攸往，見凶，羸豕孚踟躅 (*xì yú jīn nǐ,
zhēn jí, yǒu yōu wǎng, jiàn xiōng, léi shǐ fú chí zhú*, "tied to a metal brake — holding firm,
auspicious; if there is somewhere to go, ominous is what one sees; a lean pig, 孚, pacing and
stamping"). Here 孚 (*fú*) strains *confidence*: 王弼 reads 孚猶務躁也 (*fú yóu wù zào yě*, "孚 is like
restless eagerness").

**Line 2**, the firm centre below, is nearest the arrival and has it. 包有魚，无咎，不利賓 (*bāo yǒu yú,
wú jiù, bù lì bīn*, "in the wrapper, a fish — no blame; not advantageous for guests"). The 小象: 義不及賓也
(*yì bù jí bīn yě*, "it is right that it not reach the guests"). What came to you, keep by you.

**Line 3** is 43's fourth, turned over, in the same words. 臀无膚，其行次且，厲，无大咎 (*tún wú fū, qí
xíng zī jū, lì, wú dà jiù*, "buttocks without skin, the walking halting — peril, no great blame"). In
43 it could not push on; here it cannot reach the meeting — line 2 got there first. 小象: 行未牽也
(*xíng wèi qiān yě*, "its walking is not yet led").

**Line 4** is line 1's partner, too late. 包无魚，起凶 (*bāo wú yú, qǐ xiōng*, "in the wrapper, no fish;
rising up, ominous"). The 小象 names the loss: 遠民也 (*yuǎn mín yě*, "far from the people"). The
meeting that was its own went to another; to rise now is ominous.

**Line 5**, the firm centre above, is patience. 以杞包瓜，含章，有隕自天 (*yǐ qǐ bāo guā, hán zhāng, yǒu
yǔn zì tiān*, "the wolfberry wrapping melons; holding the pattern within; there is a falling from the
sky"). 章 (*zhāng*) is in 說文 樂竟爲一章 (*yuè jìng wéi yī zhāng*, "a piece of music played through is
one 章") — a pattern complete. The 小象: 志不舍命也 (*zhì bù shě mìng yě*, "the will does not let go
of what it was sent with").

**Line 6** meets head-on. 姤其角，吝，无咎 (*gòu qí jiǎo, lìn, wú jiù*, "meeting at its horns —
stinting; no blame"). At the top, with 王弼's 无所復遇 (*wú suǒ fù yù*, "nothing left to meet"), the
only meeting is horn to horn: narrow, and not a disaster.

### In a life now

This is the figure for what arrives unbidden and gets in underneath: an affair; a new colleague of
unusual force in a settled team; a diagnosis; a talent that shows up in a child; an idea from
outside that slips under an institution's walls. The text does not say refuse it. It says meet it
and do not wed it — not every true meeting is a thing to make permanent (the judgment). Tie what is
restless (line 1). Keep what came to you near, and do not hand it round (line 2). Losing touch with
those below you is ominous (line 4). Let what ripens covered ripen, and wait for it to fall (line
5).

The 大象 turns one encounter outward: 后以施命誥四方 (*hòu yǐ shī mìng gào sì fāng*, "the sovereign
sends out what is spoken and proclaims it to the four quarters"). 后 (*hòu*) is the ruler in Zhou
usage, not yet a queen. The single meeting becomes a word that reaches everywhere, as wind does.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A thin echo, and labelled as one.** Chapter 50: *"one who truly tends life / can travel the land
without meeting rhinos or tigers, … The rhino's horn has nowhere to thrust."* 角 (*jiǎo*, "horn") is
in 44's core text at line 6; *meeting* answers to 遇 (*yù*), which is the Wings' word for this
hexagram, not the core's — so the echo half passes the test and half is Wings-layer. **Where they
part:** the Laozi's traveller meets nothing and gives the horn no place. 44 says meetings come
unbidden, and the 彖 says they are how the kinds of things show their pattern; line 6 does meet the
horn, and the cost is only 吝 (*lìn*, "stinting"). The Laozi avoids the horn; the I Ching counts what
it costs to meet it and finds the cost small.

### Words kept

- 姤 (*gòu*) — *encounter*, the proposed name (not yet approved), keeping *meet* free for 遇 (*yù*)
- 女 (*nǚ*) — *woman*; 壯 (*zhuàng*) — *vigorous*, as at 43 (Shalom to rule; see there)
- 包 (*bāo*) — *wrap*, *the wrapper*, working
- 命 (*mìng*) — *what is spoken and sent*, the Tao glossary's sense (open there); never *decree*
- 后 (*hòu*) — *sovereign*, not *queen*; 天 (*tiān*) — *sky*, never *Heaven* (locked)
- 章 (*zhāng*) — *pattern*
- 孚 (*fú*) — *confidence*, working (A6) — and it strains at line 1
- 貞 · 厲 · 吝 · 凶 · 无咎 (*zhēn · lì · lìn · xiōng · wú jiù*) — *holding firm · peril · stinting ·
  ominous · no blame*, working glosses (A4, A6)
