## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind × sky ↔ earth: a trigram open at the base under one with no odd line.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 姤，遇也，柔遇剛也 (*gòu, yù yě, róu yù gāng yě*, "*gòu* is
meeting: the yielding meets the firm"). It keeps the judgement's warning — 勿用取女，不可與長也 (*wù
yòng qǔ nǚ, bù kě yǔ cháng yě*, "do not take the woman: one cannot last long with her") — and then
widens the meeting to everything: 天地相遇，品物咸章也 (*tiān dì xiāng yù, pǐn wù xián zhāng yě*, "sky
and earth meet each other, and the kinds of things all show their pattern"). The 大象傳: 天下有風，
姤；后以施命誥四方 (*tiān xià yǒu fēng, gòu; hòu yǐ shī mìng gào sì fāng*, "under the sky there is
wind: *gòu*; the sovereign issues a charge and proclaims it to the four quarters"). The tradition
holds both at once: an encounter dangerous to hold on to, and a meeting through which everything is
reached.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 43 turned over. The one open line that was leaving at the top
of 43 is at the very bottom here, arriving. Wind is the sky with its base opened, and the sky above
has no opening at all — a wholly firm world with one soft place at its root. And the soft thing is
the one that moves: wind's action is entering. Nothing in the sky is open to receive it, and it does
not need to be; it comes in underneath. In 43 the strength was the self's and the opening the
world's; here the strength is the world's, and the opening is in the self.

Entering within, vigour without. That is the 大象傳's wind under the sky, reaching the four quarters
because it goes under everything rather than through anything. It is also the judgement's warning,
arrived at from the figure: 女壯 (*nǚ zhuàng*, "the woman is strong") — not because the one line is
great, but because it is already inside, at the foundation, and it is the only line that differs.
The first line reads like the figure's own answer: 系于金柅 (*xì yú jīn nǐ*, "tie it to a metal
brake"). The structure suggests an encounter nobody arranged: the strong did not seek it, and have
no gate to shut against it.

**For the name.** *Encounter* — the signature supports it: it draws an unsought meeting, which is
what *encounter* has over *meeting*. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Chance Meeting*,
*Infiltration* — *Chance Meeting* survives: 姤其角 (*gòu qí jiǎo*, "a chance meeting at its horns")
reads; *Infiltration* — the slight line slipping in at the base — fails the lines: 姤其角 (*gòu qí
jiǎo*, "meeting at its horns") happens at the top, head-on, and infiltrates nothing.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**An unsought meeting at the root: what has arrived is to be held — wrapped, tied, let ripen — and
not married.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 姤 ("Encounter") as 偶也 ("a
mate, a pairing"), from 女 ("woman") with 后 ("sovereign") for the sound; every Wing that glosses it
says 遇 ("to meet"), which the Shuowen gives as 逢也 ("to come upon"). The judgment is one image and
one warning: 女壯，勿用取女 ("the woman is vigorous; do not take the woman in marriage"). Then the lines
turn on a single verb, 包 ("to wrap"): 包有魚 ("in the wrapper, a fish"), 包无魚 ("in the wrapper, no
fish"), 以杞包瓜 ("the wolfberry wrapping melons"). What is met is kept near and contained, not taken
into the house.

### The polarities in it

Wind within, sky without: a trigram open at its base under one with no opening at all. This is 43
turned over — the yielding line that was leaving at the top arrives at the bottom (see *The
signature*, above). And the soft thing is the one that moves; wind's action is entering, and it gets
in under a world that did not open for it. That is why the woman is 壯 ("vigorous"): not because one
line is great, but because it is already inside, at the root, and it is the only line that differs.
Text and structure agree on the hazard. Then the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, does what the lines
never do — 天地相遇，品物咸章也 ("sky and earth meet each other, and the kinds of things all show their
pattern"), and 姤之時義大矣哉 ("great indeed is the meaning of the time of meeting"). The core text warns
against making the meeting permanent; the Wing praises the meeting itself. Both stand.

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure: 天下有風 ("under the sky there is wind"). Notice where
wind goes: under doors, through the grass stems, round the trunk rather than through it. It reaches
everything by going under. The Shuowen's gloss of 后 ("sovereign") reads almost as this image: 施令以告四方
("issuing the call to tell the four quarters").

The lines hold a tethered pig and a hidden melon. 羸豕孚踟躅 ("a lean pig, confident, pacing and
stamping", line 1): the Shuowen gives 羸 ("lean") as 瘦也 ("lean"), and Wang Bi reads 羸豕謂牝豕也 ("the lean
pig means a sow"). A sow in season does not stand still; she paces the length of her rope and
presses at it. The line's 金柅 ("metal brake") is what holds her. At line 5 a melon vine runs under 杞
("wolfberry"), which the Shuowen calls 枸杞也 ("the wolfberry"), a thorny shrub. The fruit swells
hidden in leaf, its rind taking on its markings unseen — 含章 ("holding the pattern within"). Then
有隕自天 ("there is a falling from the sky"); the Shuowen quotes this very phrase under 隕 ("to fall"),
從高下也 ("to come down from a height"). The text does not say what falls. A ripe muskmelon parts from
its own stalk at a touch, and that is one way to read it: what ripens covered comes down in its
time.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 姤：女壯，勿用取女 ("meeting: the woman is vigorous; do not take the woman in marriage").
The Tuan: 不可與長也 ("one cannot last long together"). Wang Bi makes it moral — 一女而遇五男 ("one woman
meeting five men") — but the core text says only *vigorous* and *do not wed*: a meeting to meet, not
to keep.

**Line 1** is the soft line itself, at the root. 系于金柅，貞吉，有攸往，見凶，羸豕孚踟躅 ("tied to a metal brake —
holding firm, auspicious; if there is somewhere to go, ominous is what one sees; a lean pig,
confident, pacing and stamping"). Here 孚 ("confidence") strains *confidence*: Wang Bi reads 孚猶務躁也
("the word is like restless eagerness").

**Line 2**, the firm centre below, is nearest the arrival and has it. 包有魚，无咎，不利賓 ("in the wrapper, a
fish — no blame; not advantageous for guests"). The Small Image, the Wing on each line: 義不及賓也 ("it
is right that it not reach the guests"). What came to you, keep by you.

**Line 3** is 43's fourth, turned over, in the same words. 臀无膚，其行次且，厲，无大咎 ("buttocks without skin,
the walking halting — peril, no great blame"). In 43 it could not push on; here it cannot reach the
meeting — line 2 got there first. The Small Image: 行未牽也 ("its walking is not yet led").

**Line 4** is line 1's partner, too late. 包无魚，起凶 ("in the wrapper, no fish; rising up, ominous").
The Small Image names the loss: 遠民也 ("far from the people"). The meeting that was its own went to
another; to rise now is ominous.

**Line 5**, the firm centre above, is patience. 以杞包瓜，含章，有隕自天 ("the wolfberry wrapping melons;
holding the pattern within; there is a falling from the sky"). 章 ("pattern") is in the Shuowen 樂竟爲一章
("a piece of music played through is one section") — a pattern complete. The Small Image: 志不舍命也
("the will does not let go of what it was sent with").

**Line 6** meets head-on. 姤其角，吝，无咎 ("meeting at its horns — stinting; no blame"). At the top, with
Wang Bi's 无所復遇 ("nothing left to meet"), the only meeting is horn to horn: narrow, and not a
disaster.

### In a life now

This is the figure for what arrives unbidden and gets in underneath: an affair; a new colleague of
unusual force in a settled team; a diagnosis; a talent that shows up in a child; an idea from
outside that slips under an institution's walls. The text does not say refuse it. It says meet it
and do not wed it — not every true meeting is a thing to make permanent (the judgment). Tie what is
restless (line 1). Keep what came to you near, and do not hand it round (line 2). Losing touch with
those below you is ominous (line 4). Let what ripens covered ripen, and wait for it to fall (line
5).

The Great Image turns one encounter outward: 后以施命誥四方 ("the sovereign sends out what is spoken and
proclaims it to the four quarters"). 后 ("sovereign") is the ruler in Zhou usage, not yet a queen.
The single meeting becomes a word that reaches everywhere, as wind does.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A thin echo, and labelled as one.** Chapter 50: *"one who truly tends life / can travel the land
without meeting rhinos or tigers, … The rhino's horn has nowhere to thrust."* 角 ("horn") is in 44's
core text at line 6; *meeting* answers to 遇 ("to meet"), which is the Wings' word for this hexagram,
not the core's — so the echo half passes the test and half is Wings-layer. **Where they part:** the
Laozi's traveller meets nothing and gives the horn no place. 44 says meetings come unbidden, and the
Tuan says they are how the kinds of things show their pattern; line 6 does meet the horn, and the
cost is only 吝 ("stinting"). The Laozi avoids the horn; the I Ching counts what it costs to meet it
and finds the cost small.
