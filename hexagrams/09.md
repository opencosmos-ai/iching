## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth under thunder ↔ wind.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) reads the figure as one yielding line holding five
firm ones — 柔得位而上下應之 (*róu dé wèi ér shàng xià yìng zhī*, "the yielding gets its place, and
those above and below answer it") — and names its two actions: 健而巽 (*jiàn ér xùn*, "vigorous, and
entering"). It reads the judgment's clouds as force not yet spent: 密雲不雨，尚往也 (*mì yún bù yǔ,
shàng wǎng yě*, "dense clouds and no rain: it is still going"), 施未行也 (*shī wèi xíng yě*, "the
giving has not yet gone out"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 風行天上 (*fēng xíng tiān shàng*, "wind
moving above the sky"), and the noble one 以懿文德 (*yǐ yì wén dé*, "refines the integrity of
pattern") — a keeping that works on the surface, not on the bulk.

**What the polarity implies.** Wind is sky with its bottom line opened, and it touches the sky with
exactly that open line. So the figure's one yielding line sits on the seam, at line 4: the keeping
happens where the keeper is weakest. Wind does not stop vigour; it gets round it and over it, and
what it gathers is cloud — water carried, not yet dropped. The structure suggests 小 (*xiǎo*,
"small") is a matter of means, not of quantity: one open line restraining five firm ones by
pervading them rather than blocking them. Set it beside its partner in name, 26 大畜 (*dà chù*,
"great keeping"): there the sky is within a mountain, closed at the top, and the 彖 (*tuàn*) says 能止健
(*néng zhǐ jiàn*, "able to hold vigour"). 26 keeps by a lid; 9 keeps by a cover of air. That is why
9's store does not last — its top line has 既雨既處 (*jì yǔ jì chǔ*, "the rain has come, it has
settled") — and why both figures break a cart on the way: 輿說輻 (*yú tuō fú*, "the cart sheds its
spokes", 9.3) against 26's 輿說輹 (*yú tuō fù*, "the cart sheds its axle-block", 26.2).

**For the name.** **Small Keeping** — deepens: the signature shows why one word must serve 9 and 26
(both are sky held by something outside it), and why 小 (*xiǎo*, "small") is a soft hold that stores until it spills.
**Candidates the signature suggests:** *Small Store* — survives: 尚德載 (*shàng dé zài*, "integrity
is still carried as a load", 9.6) and 富以其鄰 (*fù yǐ qí lín*, "rich along with its neighbours",
9.5) both describe a store; *Clouds Held* — fails the lines: only the judgment has clouds, and the
top line lets them rain.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A rope on an ox, a cloud over dry ground: a small hold on a great strength, which gathers for a
while and then lets go.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 畜 (*chù*, "keeping") as 田畜也 (*tián chù yě*,
"field-keeping") — livestock and stores, what a farm holds. The core text keeps that picture in its
lines: 牽 (*qiān*, "led"), which 說文 draws as 引前也 (*yǐn qián yě*, "to lead forward"), the rope on
an ox (象引牛之縻也, *xiàng yǐn niú zhī mí yě*, "picturing the halter that leads an ox"), and 攣
(*luán*, "tied"), 係也 (*xì yě*, "to bind"). Over the whole stands the judgment's cloud that will
not rain. **Small Keeping** *(proposed)* is not a small amount kept. It is a great thing held by
slight means — and a hold of that kind is always provisional.

### The polarities in it

Sky within, wind without. The inside is all vigour, three firm lines that would rise; the outside
is the figure that enters and pervades, and it meets the sky with its one open line. So the keeper
is the weakest line in the figure, set on the seam (see *The signature*, above). It cannot stop the
sky and does not try. It covers it, as air covers ground, and what it gathers — cloud, a herd, a
store — it holds only as long as the vigour beneath is willing to be led. 雜卦 (*záguà*) says it in
three characters: 小畜寡也 (*xiǎo chù guǎ yě*, "Small Keeping is few") — one soft line against five.

### In nature

Watch a summer afternoon build over hills to the west. Cumulus rises and darkens, the air goes
heavy, and nothing falls; sometimes grey streaks hang from the cloud's base and vanish before they
reach the ground, rain evaporating in dry air on the way down. That is 密雲不雨，自我西郊 (*mì yún bù
yǔ, zì wǒ xī jiāo*, "dense clouds, no rain, from our western outskirts"). 說文 puts 郊 (*jiāo*) at
the edge of the state — 距國百里爲郊 (*jù guó bǎi lǐ wéi jiāo*, "a hundred *lǐ* from the capital is
the 郊") — so the cloud is seen far off, over the borderland, not overhead. In the Wei valley, the
Zhou homeland, the summer rain mostly comes on wind from the south and east; cloud massing in the
west can promise all day and give nothing.

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sets the figure as 風行天上 (*fēng xíng tiān shàng*, "wind moving above the
sky"): high wind that combs the tops of cloud into streamers long before the weather below changes.
And the top line ends on a moon: 月幾望 (*yuè jī wàng*, "the moon almost full"). Look east at dusk
a night or two before full moon — the moon is already up, swollen on one side, still gaining. The
line uses it as the image of a thing nearly at its fullest, which is exactly when to stop.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 小畜：亨。密雲不雨，自我西郊 (*xiǎo chù: hēng. mì yún bù yǔ, zì wǒ xī jiāo*) —
"Small Keeping: it gets through. Dense clouds, no rain, from our western outskirts." It gets through,
and it has not yet arrived. The 彖 (*tuàn*) reads the cloud as force still under way: 施未行也 (*shī
wèi xíng yě*, "the giving has not yet gone out").

**Line 1** 復自道，何其咎，吉 (*fù zì dào, hé qí jiù, jí*, "returning by its own road — what blame
in that? Auspicious"). The first firm line meets the cover and simply turns back on its own road.
Nothing is forced.

**Line 2** 牽復，吉 (*qiān fù, jí*, "led back — auspicious"). Now it takes the halter. The second
firm line, in the lower centre, comes back not of itself but on a rope; still auspicious.

**Line 3** 輿說輻，夫妻反目 (*yú tuō fú, fū qī fǎn mù*, "the cart sheds its spokes; husband and wife
turn their eyes away from each other"). The third firm line pushes against the seam, and the hold
turns to breakage: a cart that cannot roll, a household that cannot look at itself. No verdict —
only the cost of straining against a soft cover.

**Line 4** is the keeper itself. 有孚，血去惕出，无咎 (*yǒu fú, xuè qù tì chū, wú jiù*, "there is
confidence; the blood goes, the alarm leaves — no blame"). The one yielding line, under all that
pressure from below, holds by confidence and not by strength — and the fear passes.

**Line 5** 有孚攣如，富以其鄰 (*yǒu fú luán rú, fù yǐ qí lín*, "confidence, bound together; rich
along with its neighbours"). In the upper centre the tether becomes a bond: what is kept is shared.
說文 counts a 鄰 (*lín*) as 五家 (*wǔ jiā*, "five households") — a store held for a hamlet, not for
one house.

**Line 6** 既雨既處，尚德載，婦貞厲，月幾望，君子征凶 (*jì yǔ jì chǔ, shàng dé zài, fù zhēn lì, yuè jī
wàng, jūnzǐ zhēng xiōng*, "the rain has come, it has settled; integrity is still carried as a load;
for a wife, holding firm is peril; the moon almost full; for the noble one, setting out is
ominous"). The cloud breaks at last. 處 (*chǔ*) is 說文's 得几而止 (*dé jī ér zhǐ*, "reaching a stool
and stopping"): the rain sits down. The cart that shed its spokes at line 3 is loaded again (載,
*zài*, "carried"). But the keeping is at its fullest, and the line counsels against pressing on.
王弼 has the reason: 滿而又進必失其道 (*mǎn ér yòu jìn bì shī qí dào*, "full, and still advancing,
one is sure to lose the way").

### In a life now

This is the figure for any hold that is real but slight: managing people more gifted than you; a
parent with a teenager; a small party in a coalition; savings built slowly against a large need.
The keeper has no walls. The lines say what works: let the strong go their own road where they will (line 1), lead by a
light rope where they will not (line 2), and do not strain the hold until the axle breaks (line
3). Hold by confidence, not force (line 4); make what is gathered common (line 5). And when the
rain finally comes — the harvest, the result — the gathering is done. The moon almost full is the
time to stop adding.

The 大象 turns this toward what a small hold can shape: 君子以懿文德 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yì wén dé*, "the
noble one refines the pattern of their integrity"). When you cannot move the bulk, work on the
surface — the manner, the finish, the patterning — which a soft cover can reach.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Both books use this hexagram's graph, and put 德 beside it.** The Laozi's chapter 51 has 德畜之
(*dé xù zhī*): *"integrity rears them."* The top line of 9 has 德 (*dé*, "integrity") too, 尚德載,
"integrity is still carried as a load" — so both graphs are in the core text here, and the English
answers to them. Both have 畜 done by something slight: in the Laozi by integrity, which rears
without possessing; in 9 by one open line, which keeps without walls.

They part in what keeping is for. The Laozi's 畜 is nurture — the growing thing is raised and let
go. The keeping of 9 is restraint: the strong is held back, and the hold is strained, breaks a
cart, and ends in a warning. The Laozi reads 畜 as *rear*; this figure's lines read it as *keep*,
and this reading does not blur the two.

- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked)
