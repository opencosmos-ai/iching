## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × thunder ↔ wind.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 大觀在上，順而巽 (*dà guān zài shàng, shùn ér xùn*), "the
great watching is above: going along, and entering," and 下觀而化也 (*xià guān ér huà yě*), "those
below watch and are changed." The 大象傳: 風行地上 (*fēng xíng dì shàng*), "wind goes over the earth,"
and 先王以省方，觀民設教 (*xiān wáng yǐ xǐng fāng, guān mín shè jiào*), "the former rulers inspected
the regions, watched the people, and set up teaching." The tradition has watching run both ways: the
one above surveys the land, and the land looks up and is changed by what it sees.

**What the polarity implies.** There are two yin poles again, and now the two firm lines stand at
the very top (`000011`). They are the one visible thing in a figure of open lines, like a tower on a
plain. Wind over earth touches every surface of the land and strikes none of it, so watching here
is contact that leaves no mark. That is the difference from thunder. Earth within goes along; it is
ground that lets itself be seen. The wind trigram's single open line is its lowest, the one facing
the earth, so the only gap in the upper figure opens downward. The structure suggests that in
watching, the low enters the high as much as the high surveys the low. 說卦 ch 11 has 巽…為進退 (*xùn
wéi jìn tuì*, "wind is advancing and withdrawing"), and those are the very words of line 3: 觀我生，
進退 (*guān wǒ shēng, jìn tuì*), "watch what I bring forth: advance, withdraw." The watcher sways the
way wind does. The judgment catches the held breath: 盥而不荐 (*guàn ér bù jiàn*), "the hands are
washed, the offering not yet made." It is wind hanging over the ground before it moves anything. In
19 the firm arrived from below through a yielding medium. Here it has arrived, and it must bear
being looked at.

**For the name.** **Watching**. The signature supports it and shows why the seam matters. The
figure is looking in both directions, and the English carries only the watcher's side (the
traditional *guàn*, "display," is the other). **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Beholding*
survives the lines (童觀, *tóng guān*, "a child's beholding"; 觀我生, "behold what I bring forth"),
at a heavier register than the book's plain words. *The Lookout* fails the lines: 觀 is a verb in
lines 3 to 6, and 觀國之光 (*guān guó zhī guāng*, "watch the country's light") cannot become
"lookout the country's light."

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation. The
formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets through"), 孚
("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on them
([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Looking, at every distance — through a crack in the door, across a country, and back at one's own
life — until the one who looks is the one being looked at.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character
dictionary, gives 觀 ("Watching") as 諦視也 ("to look closely"), built on 見 ("see"). The core text is
made of that verb: four of the six lines begin with it. It opens on a held breath — the hands
washed, the offering not yet made, every head raised — and it closes on the watcher watching what
they have brought forth. The old reading, "display", is the other side of the same act, and the
figure keeps both.

### The polarities in it

Within is earth, all yielding: it goes along, ground that lets itself be seen. Without is wind: it
enters, touching every surface and striking none. The two firm lines stand at the very top, the one
upright thing on a plain of open lines. Here the structure and the text agree closely. The four
yielding lines are the ones that look — a child, someone at a crack, someone weighing a step, a
visitor to a country; the two firm lines at the top are the ones looked at, and what they do is look
back at themselves. The Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, says it in four characters: 下觀而化 ("those
below watch, and are changed").

### In nature

The image in the Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, is 風行地上 ("wind goes over the earth"). No
one has seen wind. What you see is what it passes over: a field of barley in early summer, the heads
bowing in long waves that run across the field faster than any single stalk moves, then standing
again behind it. Wang Bi took his picture of rule from that field — 上之化下猶風之靡草 ("the high changing
the low is like wind laying down grass"). To watch this figure properly, watch where the wind is by
what it bends.

The judgment's 顒 ("heads raised") is in the Shuowen 大頭也 ("a big head") — heads held up. Watch
grazing deer or sheep when something moves at the field's edge: every head lifts at once, ears
forward, and the whole herd is still, looking. The second line's 窺 ("watching through a gap") is the
Shuowen's 小視也 ("looking small"), built on 穴 ("a hole") — the eye at a gap in a door. And the
judgment's washing, 盥 ("the hands washed"), is in the Shuowen 澡手也 ("washing the hands"), drawn as
two hands and water held over a basin. The word the Shuowen uses for "held over" is 臨 ("Overseeing")
— the name of the hexagram before this one, turned the other way up.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 觀：盥而不荐，有孚顒若 ("Watching: the hands washed, the offering not yet laid down; there is
confidence, heads raised.") This is the moment before the act, when everyone is looking. Wang Bi
says the washing is the part worth watching, and quotes Confucius: 禘自旣灌而往者吾不欲觀之矣 ("at the great
sacrifice, from the pouring on, I have no wish to watch").

**Line 1** is furthest from what is seen. 童觀，小人无咎，君子吝 ("a child's watching: for small people, no
blame; for the noble one, stinting"). Seeing only as a child sees is fine for some, and too little
for others.

**Line 2** looks through a crack. 窺觀，利女貞 ("watching through a gap: advantageous for a woman's
holding firm"). The core text names someone kept inside the house, and finds the narrow view fitting
there; the Small Image, the Wing on each line, adds its own judgement, 亦可丑也 ("it can also be
shameful"). That verdict is the Wing's, not the line's.

**Line 3**, at the top of the earth, at the seam, turns the eye round. 觀我生，進退 ("watch what I bring
forth: advance, withdraw"). Wang Bi calls this 觀風者也 ("one who watches the wind"). Not yet a decision
— the weighing that comes before one.

**Line 4**, first of the wind, nearest the ruler, looks out. 觀國之光，利用賓于王 ("watch the country's light:
it is advantageous to be a guest of the ruler"). The Shuowen draws 光 ("light") as 从火在人上 ("fire above
a person"). The visitor who comes to see how a country is lit.

**Line 5**, firm and central, is the one on display. 觀我生，君子无咎 ("watch what I bring forth: for the
noble one, no blame"). The Small Image turns it outward: 觀我生，觀民也 ("to watch what I bring forth is to
watch the people"). The one looked at learns what they are from the faces looking back.

**Line 6** is looked at from everywhere. 觀其生，君子无咎 ("watch what they bring forth: for the noble one,
no blame"). Wang Bi glosses 生 ("bring forth") as 生猶動出也 ("bringing forth is like moving out, coming
forth"), and the Small Image says 志未平也 ("the will is not yet level"). At the top, the watching never
quite settles.

**There is no auspicious word and no ominous one anywhere in this hexagram** — only *no blame*,
*advantage*, and one *stinting*. Watching is not an outcome. It comes before one.

### In a life now

This is the figure for any life that is looked at and looks: a parent watched by a child who copies
everything; a new arrival reading a country's streets; someone who knows the world only through a
screen's narrow gap; anyone in public life. And it is the figure for the pause before an act — the
hands washed, the thing not yet done, while everyone waits. Its counsel is about the size and the
direction of the looking. A child's view and a crack's view are allowed, but they are not enough for
someone with responsibility. Before advancing or withdrawing, look at what your own life has put
into the world (line 3). If you are the one on show, read yourself in the people you affect (line
5).

The Great Image puts watching first and teaching after it: 先王以省方，觀民設教 ("the former rulers inspected
the regions, watched the people, and set up teaching"). Look before you tell.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The verb is shared, and the self that watches is not.** Chapter 16: *"The countless things arise
together, / Relying upon this, I observe their return."* The word is 觀 ("watch"), the graph of this
hexagram's name and of lines 1 to 6, in the core text itself. But this project's Laozi renders it
*observe*, and the proposed name here is *watching*; the English does not yet answer to the one
character in one way.

The pronouns part them further. The Laozi's watcher is 吾 ("I"), which the locked glossary reads as
*the self seeing*; 20's lines watch 我生 ("what I bring forth"), and 我 ("I") is *the self seen*.
Chapter 16's watching comes after emptying, and falls on the countless things going home to their
root. The watching of 觀 ("Watching") is among people — a child, a crack, a court, a ruler read in
the faces of the ruled — and it runs both ways. **The Laozi watches without being watched; this
hexagram cannot.**

- 生 ("bring forth") — never *generate* (locked); Wang Bi's gloss agrees
- 我 ("the self seen") — locked pairing with 吾 ("the self seeing")
