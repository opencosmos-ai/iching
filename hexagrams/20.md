## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × thunder ↔ wind.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 大觀在上，順而巽 (*dà guān zài shàng, shùn ér xùn*), "the
great watching is above: going along, and entering," and 下觀而化也 (*xià guān ér huà yě*), "those
below watch and are changed." The 大象傳: 風行地上 (*fēng xíng dì shàng*), "wind goes over the earth,"
and 先王以省方，觀民設教 (*xiān wáng yǐ xǐng fāng, guān mín shè jiào*), "the former rulers inspected
the regions, watched the people, and set up teaching." The tradition has watching run both ways: the
one above surveys the land, and the land looks up and is changed by what it sees.

**What the polarity implies.** There are two yin poles again, and now the two firm lines stand at
the very top (`000011`). They are the one visible thing in a figure of open lines, like a tower on a
plain. Wind over earth touches every surface of the land and strikes none of it, so watching here
is contact that leaves no mark. That is the difference from thunder. Earth within goes along; it is
ground that lets itself be seen. The wind trigram's single open line is its lowest, the one facing
the earth, so the only gap in the upper figure opens downward. The structure suggests that in
watching, the low enters the high as much as the high surveys the low. 說卦 ch 11 has 巽…為進退 (*xùn
wéi jìn tuì*, "wind is advancing and withdrawing"), and those are the very words of line 3: 觀我生，
進退 (*guān wǒ shēng, jìn tuì*), "watch what I bring forth: advance, withdraw." The watcher sways the
way wind does. The judgment catches the held breath: 盥而不荐 (*guàn ér bù jiàn*), "the hands are
washed, the offering not yet made." It is wind hanging over the ground before it moves anything. In
19 the firm arrived from below through a yielding medium. Here it has arrived, and it must bear
being looked at.

**For the name.** **Watching**. The signature supports it and shows why the seam matters. The
figure is looking in both directions, and the English carries only the watcher's side (the
traditional *guàn*, "display," is the other). **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Beholding*
survives the lines (童觀, *tóng guān*, "a child's beholding"; 觀我生, "behold what I bring forth"),
at a heavier register than the book's plain words. *The Lookout* fails the lines: 觀 is a verb in
lines 3 to 6, and 觀國之光 (*guān guó zhī guāng*, "watch the country's light") cannot become
"lookout the country's light."

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Looking, at every distance — through a crack in the door, across a country, and back at one's own
life — until the one who looks is the one being looked at.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 觀 (*guān*) as
諦視也 (*dì shì yě*, "to look closely"), built on 見 (*jiàn*, "see"). The core text is made of that
verb: four of the six lines begin with it. It opens on a held breath — the hands washed, the
offering not yet made, every head raised — and it closes on the watcher watching what they have
brought forth. The old reading *guàn*, "display", is the other side of the same act, and the figure
keeps both.

### The polarities in it

Within is earth, all yielding: it goes along, ground that lets itself be seen. Without is wind: it
enters, touching every surface and striking none. The two firm lines stand at the very top, the one
upright thing on a plain of open lines. Here the structure and the text agree closely. The four
yielding lines are the ones that look — a child, someone at a crack, someone weighing a step, a
visitor to a country; the two firm lines at the top are the ones looked at, and what they do is
look back at themselves. The 彖 says it in four characters: 下觀而化 (*xià guān ér huà*, "those below
watch, and are changed").

### In nature

The 大象's image is 風行地上 (*fēng xíng dì shàng*, "wind goes over the earth"). No one has seen
wind. What you see is what it passes over: a field of barley in early summer, the heads bowing in
long waves that run across the field faster than any single stalk moves, then standing again
behind it. 王弼 took his picture of rule from that field — 上之化下猶風之靡草 (*shàng zhī huà xià yóu
fēng zhī mǐ cǎo*, "the high changing the low is like wind laying down grass"). To watch this figure
properly, watch where the wind is by what it bends.

The judgment's 顒 (*yóng*) is in 說文 大頭也 (*dà tóu yě*, "a big head") — heads held up. Watch
grazing deer or sheep when something moves at the field's edge: every head lifts at once, ears
forward, and the whole herd is still, looking. The second line's 窺 (*kuī*) is 說文's 小視也 (*xiǎo
shì yě*, "looking small"), built on 穴 (*xué*, "a hole") — the eye at a gap in a door. And the
judgment's washing, 盥 (*guàn*), is in 說文 澡手也 (*zǎo shǒu yě*, "washing the hands"), drawn as two
hands and water held over a basin. The word 說文 uses for "held over" is 臨 (*lín*) — the name of the
hexagram before this one, turned the other way up.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 觀：盥而不荐，有孚顒若 (*guān: guàn ér bù jiàn, yǒu fú yóng ruò*) — "Watching: the
hands washed, the offering not yet laid down; there is confidence, heads raised." This is the moment
before the act, when everyone is looking. 王弼 says the washing is the part worth watching, and
quotes Confucius: 禘自旣灌而往者吾不欲觀之矣 (*dì zì jì guàn ér wǎng zhě wú bù yù guān zhī yǐ*, "at the
great sacrifice, from the pouring on, I have no wish to watch").

**Line 1** is furthest from what is seen. 童觀，小人无咎，君子吝 (*tóng guān, xiǎorén wú jiù, jūnzǐ
lìn*, "a child's watching: for small people, no blame; for the noble one, stinting"). Seeing only as
a child sees is fine for some, and too little for others.

**Line 2** looks through a crack. 窺觀，利女貞 (*kuī guān, lì nǚ zhēn*, "watching through a gap:
advantageous for a woman's holding firm"). The core text names someone kept inside the house, and
finds the narrow view fitting there; the 小象 adds its own judgement, 亦可丑也 (*yì kě chǒu yě*, "it can
also be shameful"). That verdict is the Wing's, not the line's.

**Line 3**, at the top of the earth, at the seam, turns the eye round. 觀我生，進退 (*guān wǒ shēng,
jìn tuì*, "watch what I bring forth: advance, withdraw"). 王弼 calls this 觀風者也 (*guān fēng zhě yě*,
"one who watches the wind"). Not yet a decision — the weighing that comes before one.

**Line 4**, first of the wind, nearest the ruler, looks out. 觀國之光，利用賓于王 (*guān guó zhī guāng,
lì yòng bīn yú wáng*, "watch the country's light: it is advantageous to be a guest of the ruler").
說文 draws 光 (*guāng*) as 从火在人上 (*cóng huǒ zài rén shàng*, "fire above a person"). The visitor who
comes to see how a country is lit.

**Line 5**, firm and central, is the one on display. 觀我生，君子无咎 (*guān wǒ shēng, jūnzǐ wú jiù*,
"watch what I bring forth: for the noble one, no blame"). The 小象 turns it outward: 觀我生，觀民也
(*guān wǒ shēng, guān mín yě*, "to watch what I bring forth is to watch the people"). The one looked
at learns what they are from the faces looking back.

**Line 6** is looked at from everywhere. 觀其生，君子无咎 (*guān qí shēng, jūnzǐ wú jiù*, "watch what
they bring forth: for the noble one, no blame"). 王弼 glosses 生 (*shēng*) as 生猶動出也 (*shēng yóu
dòng chū yě*, "生 is like moving out, coming forth"), and the 小象 says 志未平也 (*zhì wèi píng yě*,
"the will is not yet level"). At the top, the watching never quite settles.

**There is no auspicious word and no ominous one anywhere in this hexagram** — only *no blame*,
*advantage*, and one *stinting*. Watching is not an outcome. It comes before one.

### In a life now

This is the figure for any life that is looked at and looks: a parent watched by a child who copies
everything; a new arrival reading a country's streets; someone who knows the world only through a
screen's narrow gap; anyone in public life. And it is the figure for the pause before an act — the
hands washed, the thing not yet done, while everyone waits. Its counsel is about the size and the
direction of the looking. A child's view and a crack's view are allowed, but they are not enough
for someone with responsibility. Before advancing or withdrawing, look at what your own life has
put into the world (line 3). If you are the one on show, read yourself in the people you affect
(line 5).

The 大象 puts watching first and teaching after it: 先王以省方，觀民設教 (*xiān wáng yǐ xǐng fāng, guān
mín shè jiào*, "the former rulers inspected the regions, watched the people, and set up teaching").
Look before you tell.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The verb is shared, and the self that watches is not.** Chapter 16: *"The countless things arise
together, / Relying upon this, I observe their return."* The word is 觀 (*guān*), the graph of this
hexagram's name and of lines 1 to 6, in the core text itself. But this project's Laozi renders it
*observe*, and the proposed name here is *watching*; the English does not yet answer to the one
character in one way.

The pronouns part them further. The Laozi's watcher is 吾 (*wú*), which the locked glossary reads as
*the self seeing*; 20's lines watch 我生 (*wǒ shēng*, "what I bring forth"), and 我 (*wǒ*) is *the self
seen*. Chapter 16's watching comes after emptying, and falls on the countless things going home to
their root. 觀's watching is among people — a child, a crack, a court, a ruler read in the faces of
the ruled — and it runs both ways. **The Laozi watches without being watched; this hexagram cannot.**

### Words kept

- 觀 (*guān*) — *watching*, the proposed name (not yet approved); the traditional *guàn*, "display", is the other side, kept in the reading, not the name
- 生 (*shēng*) — *bring forth*, never *generate* (locked); 王弼's gloss agrees
- 我 (*wǒ*) — *the self seen* (locked pairing with 吾, *the self seeing*)
- 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler* (locked); 先王 (*xiān wáng*) — *the former rulers*
- 光 (*guāng*) — *light*; not *glory*, and not *clarity*, which is kept for 明 (*míng*)
- 窺 (*kuī*) — *watching through a gap*
- 盥 · 荐 (*guàn · jiàn*) — *the hands washed · the offering laid down*
- 孚 (*fú*) — *confidence*, working gloss (A6; *trust* belongs to 信)
- 神 (*shén*) — *numinous*, never *divine*, in the 彖's 神道 (*shén dào*, "the numinous Tao")
- 君子 · 小人 (*jūnzǐ · xiǎorén*) — *the noble one · small people*, working glosses
- 无咎 · 吝 · 貞 (*wú jiù · lìn · zhēn*) — *no blame · stinting · holding firm*, working glosses (A4, A6)
