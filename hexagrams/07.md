## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going
along). **Across** — water ↔ fire meets sky ↔ earth, yang within and yin without, the odd line at the
middle within and none without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 師，眾也。貞，正也。能以眾正，可以王矣 (*shī, zhòng
yě. zhēn, zhèng yě. néng yǐ zhòng zhèng, kěyǐ wàng yǐ*), "師 (*shī*) is the many; 貞 (*zhēn*) is being
upright; one who can make the many upright can rule." And: 剛中而應，行險而順 (*gāng zhōng ér yìng,
xíng xiǎn ér shùn*), "firm at the centre and answered; going into hazard, and going along." The 大象
(*dà xiàng*, the great image): 地中有水，師；君子以容民畜衆 (*dì zhōng yǒu shuǐ, shī; jūnzǐ yǐ róng
mín xù zhòng*), "within the earth there is water: 師; the noble one, by it, makes room for the folk
and keeps the many." The tradition reads the many organised round one firm centre.

**What the polarity implies.** One firm line in six, and it is not in the ruler's seat: it is 九二
(*jiǔ èr*), the centre of the lower trigram. The yielding fifth answers it — the 彖's 剛中而應. So the
force of the figure belongs to the commander in the field, not the ruler, and the line says so:
在師中吉 (*zài shī zhōng jí*), "in the midst of the army: auspicious." The image is groundwater —
hazard stored inside the body of the ground, invisible from outside. An army is the dangerous thing
held within the settled many: farmers who are also soldiers, earth that goes along wrapped round a
sunk, hard core. Everything therefore depends on that one line holding. 師出以律 (*shī chū yǐ lǜ*),
"the army goes out by regulation"; replace the one who commands and the open lines have nothing to
go along with — 弟子輿尸 (*dìzǐ yú shī*), "the younger son carts corpses."

Turn the figure over and it is 8: the single firm line moves from second to fifth, from commander to
ruler, and the water comes up out of the earth to lie on it. In 7 the many are held together under a
line below them; in 8 they are drawn toward a line above.

**For the name.** **The Army** — supported and deepened: one firm line holding five yielding ones
from the lower centre is the shape of a command, and the signature adds that an army is a hazard
kept inside a peaceable body. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *The Levy* — the many raised
from the land; survives the lines: 師出以律 "the levy goes out by regulation", 師或輿尸 (*shī huò yú
shī*) "the levy perhaps carts corpses", 長子帥師 (*zhǎngzǐ shuài shī*) "the eldest son leads the
levy." *Discipline* — from 律 (*lǜ*, "regulation") and the lone firm line; fails the lines at 師或輿尸,
since discipline does not cart corpses.
