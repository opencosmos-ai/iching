## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going
along). **Across** — water ↔ fire meets sky ↔ earth, yang within and yin without, the odd line at the
middle within and none without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 師，眾也。貞，正也。能以眾正，可以王矣 (*shī, zhòng
yě. zhēn, zhèng yě. néng yǐ zhòng zhèng, kěyǐ wàng yǐ*), "師 (*shī*) is the many; 貞 (*zhēn*) is being
upright; one who can make the many upright can rule." And: 剛中而應，行險而順 (*gāng zhōng ér yìng,
xíng xiǎn ér shùn*), "firm at the centre and answered; going into hazard, and going along." The 大象
(*dà xiàng*, the great image): 地中有水，師；君子以容民畜衆 (*dì zhōng yǒu shuǐ, shī; jūnzǐ yǐ róng
mín xù zhòng*), "within the earth there is water: 師; the noble one, by it, makes room for the folk
and keeps the many." The tradition reads the many organised round one firm centre.

**What the polarity implies.** One firm line in six, and it is not in the ruler's seat: it is 九二
(*jiǔ èr*), the centre of the lower trigram. The yielding fifth answers it — the 彖's 剛中而應. So the
force of the figure belongs to the commander in the field, not the ruler, and the line says so:
在師中吉 (*zài shī zhōng jí*), "in the midst of the army: auspicious." The image is groundwater —
hazard stored inside the body of the ground, invisible from outside. An army is the dangerous thing
held within the settled many: farmers who are also soldiers, earth that goes along wrapped round a
sunk, hard core. Everything therefore depends on that one line holding. 師出以律 (*shī chū yǐ lǜ*),
"the army goes out by regulation"; replace the one who commands and the open lines have nothing to
go along with — 弟子輿尸 (*dìzǐ yú shī*), "the younger son carts corpses."

Turn the figure over and it is 8: the single firm line moves from second to fifth, from commander to
ruler, and the water comes up out of the earth to lie on it. In 7 the many are held together under a
line below them; in 8 they are drawn toward a line above.

**For the name.** **The Army** — supported and deepened: one firm line holding five yielding ones
from the lower centre is the shape of a command, and the signature adds that an army is a hazard
kept inside a peaceable body. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *The Levy* — the many raised
from the land; survives the lines: 師出以律 "the levy goes out by regulation", 師或輿尸 (*shī huò yú
shī*) "the levy perhaps carts corpses", 長子帥師 (*zhǎngzǐ shuài shī*) "the eldest son leads the
levy." *Discipline* — from 律 (*lǜ*, "regulation") and the lone firm line; fails the lines at 師或輿尸,
since discipline does not cart corpses.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**An army is a hazard carried inside a people, and everything turns on who holds it.** The Shuowen,
the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 師 ("The Army") as a number — 二千五百人爲師 ("two thousand five
hundred people make an army") — and reads its parts as 眾意也 ("the sense of the many"). The core text
is almost entirely about command: the army going out by rule, one leader in its midst, the wrong
leader carting corpses, the ruler choosing between an eldest son and a younger. Its judgment names
the only thing it trusts: 丈人 ("the elder") — a grown person of standing. The many are dangerous; the
question the lines keep asking is who holds them.

### The polarities in it

Within is water: hazard, its one firm line at the centre. Without is earth, open all through, going
along. So the force of the figure sits low, at the second line, and the ruler's seat above is
yielding — it does not command in person; it answers and delegates. The Great Image, the Wing on the
whole figure, sees the shape exactly: 地中有水 ("within the earth there is water"). The army is the
dangerous thing kept inside a peaceable body, farmers who are also soldiers. Everything depends on
the one line that holds it — and when that line is wrongly filled, the open lines around it have
nothing to go along with.

### In nature

Under every field there is water. Dig a well in a dry plain and at some depth the sides begin to
darken and seep; that water was there all along, under the crops, unseen from above. It is what
keeps the field alive, and it is also what floods a cellar or undermines a bank when it rises. That
is the Great Image's image, and a good one for a levy: the strength of the many, stored where no one
sees it until it is drawn up.

The fifth line's 田有禽 ("there is game in the fields") is farm country too. The Shuowen gives 禽
("beast") as 走獸緫名 ("the general name for running beasts") — not birds; deer and boar in the millet.
Anyone who farms near woodland knows the night raids and the flattened rows. The hunt that clears
them used the same people, carts and drill as the campaign; in this line they are one act.

And the Shuowen has an unsparing picture in the third line's 尸 ("corpse"): 象臥之形 ("it pictures the
shape of one lying down"). The graph does not look away.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 師：貞丈人吉，无咎 ("The army. Holding firm, the elder: auspicious, no blame"). Here the
older sense of 貞 ("the inquiry") matters: "an inquiry about the elder — auspicious" reads as
naturally, and on either reading the good fortune is tied to one person. Wang Bi says what kind:
嚴莊之稱也 ("a name for the stern and grave").

**Line 1** is the going out. 師出以律，否臧，凶 ("the army goes out by regulation; if not well done,
ominous"). The Shuowen gives 律 ("regulation") as 均布也 ("spread evenly") — the rule that reaches every
rank alike. Wang Bi reads 否臧 ("not well done") as "whether it succeeds or not": 否臧皆凶 ("success and
failure both ominous") — an army out of order is ominous even when it wins.

**Line 2** is the one firm line, the centre of the water. 在師中吉，无咎；王三錫命 ("in the midst of the army:
auspicious, no blame; the ruler three times confers the allotted course"). The commander is in the
midst of the troops, not behind them, and the charge is spoken over them from above, again and
again.

**Line 3** is open at the seam, sitting on the firm line. 師或輿尸，凶 ("the army perhaps carts corpses:
ominous"). The first ominous outcome falls where the wrong kind of line takes the place of command.
The Small Image, the Wing on each line, adds 大无功也 ("greatly without achievement").

**Line 4** is the first line of earth: the army stops. 師左次，无咎 ("the army halts on the left: no
blame"). The Shuowen gives 次 ("halt") as 不前 ("not going forward"). Nothing is gained, and nothing is
lost; the Small Image says 未失常也 ("it has not lost the abiding"). Halting is a move.

**Line 5** is the ruler's seat, open, and the line where the decision is made.
田有禽，利執言，无咎。長子帥師，弟子輿尸，貞凶 ("there is game in the fields: advantage in grasping it — no blame. The
eldest son leads the army; the younger son carts corpses: holding firm, ominous"). There is a real
cause — the game is in the fields — and the open ruler must hand the command to someone. Wang Bi:
柔非軍帥 ("the soft is not a commander"). Choose rightly and there is no blame; choose wrongly, and
holding to that choice is ominous.

**Line 6** is the end of the campaign. 大君有命，開國承家，小人勿用 ("the great ruler speaks the allotted course:
states are opened, houses carried on; small people — do not use them"). The war ends in founding and
rewarding, and the line's one warning is about who receives what the war has won.

Both ominous outcomes carry the same cart of corpses (lines 3 and 5), and both come from the same
fault: the wrong one in command.

### In a life now

Not only war: anything where many people are raised at once to meet a hazard — a strike, a fire
crew, an emergency ward in a bad winter, a campaign, a family gathering round a parent who is dying.
The figure's counsel is organisational and blunt. **Go out by rule, evenly applied** (line 1). **Put
one experienced person in the midst of it, with real authority, and back them repeatedly** (line 2).
**If you are the one who delegates, the choice of who leads is the whole of your part** — the wrong
one fills the carts (line 5). **Know when to halt** (line 4). And when it is over, do not hand what
was won to small people (line 6). The Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams, sums up its mood in
one word: 師憂 ("the army: care, sorrow").

The Great Image turns the matter back to the people the army comes from: 君子以容民畜衆 ("the noble one
makes room for the folk and keeps the crowd"). 畜 ("keep") has a field, 田 ("field"), in it: the many
are kept as a farm keeps what it rears. The strength of a levy is the life of the land it is drawn
from.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share the graph, and see the army from opposite sides.** The Laozi's chapter 30:
*"Where armies camp, thorns and brambles grow. / After a big military campaign, a year of disaster
follows."* The word is 師 ("army"), the same graph that names this hexagram and stands in five of its
lines in the core text.

The Laozi stands outside the campaign and counts what grows after it. 師 ("The Army") stands inside
it and asks how it is done rightly: its second line is auspicious, and its fifth finds an army
sometimes warranted — there is game in the fields. The two meet only at the carts of corpses, which
the core text does not soften any more than the Laozi does. **That the I Ching counsels how to
command, where the Laozi counsels not to dominate, is a real difference and stays one** (see
[`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 2).

### Words kept

- 眾 ("the crowd / the many") — never *the multitude* or *the masses* (locked)
- 命 ("the allotted course") — never *mandate*, never *decree* (locked); here it strains toward a
  commission spoken over the one sent
- 王 ("ruler") — never *king* (locked); 大君 ("the great ruler") — which collides with 王 ("ruler") and
  needs a ruling
- 常 ("the abiding") — never *eternal* (locked)
- 貞 ("holding firm") — working gloss; the older reading, *the inquiry*, matters in the judgment
- 吉 ("auspicious") · 凶 ("ominous") · 无咎 ("no blame") — working glosses (A4)
- 小人 ("small people") · 丈人 ("the elder")
- 律 ("regulation"); 執 ("grasp") — locked, here in its plain sense, the hand on the game; never
  *cling*
