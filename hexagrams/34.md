## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 *qián* · yang · vigour). Without: **thunder** (震 *zhèn* · yang · moving out).
**Across** — the yang pole of sky ↔ earth within, the yang pole of thunder ↔ wind without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 reads the name as a sentence and the figure as its cause:
大壯，大者壯也。剛以動，故壯 (*dà zhuàng, dà zhě zhuàng yě. gāng yǐ dòng, gù zhuàng*, "great vigour: it is
the great that is vigorous. Firm, and so moving — therefore vigorous"). It then turns the strength
toward uprightness: 大者正也 (*dà zhě zhèng yě*, "the great is upright"). The 大象 draws the same line:
雷在天上，大壯；君子以非禮弗履 (*léi zài tiān shàng, dà zhuàng; jūnzǐ yǐ fēi lǐ fú lǚ*, "thunder above the
sky: great vigour; the noble one does not tread where the rites do not lead"). Strength at its height,
for the tradition, is safe only when it is upright.

**What the polarity implies.** Read from the bottom, the figure is four firm lines in a row and then
two open ones: the force climbs unbroken out of the sky within and into thunder, whose one firm line
sits at its base — at the exact seam where it meets the sky. Nothing in the figure stands between the
vigour and its outlet. Where 33 set the same sky *above* a mountain, out of reach, here the sky is
*within* and has already become motion. And above the thunder there is nothing firm at all — only the
two open lines, empty air.

The structure suggests the danger is not weakness but the lack of anything to stop against. A figure
with no mountain in it must bring its own stop, and that is why the lines keep supplying a fence: 羝羊觸藩，
羸其角 (*dī yáng chù fān, léi qí jiǎo*, "the ram butts the fence and tangles its horns"), and at the top,
不能退，不能遂 (*bù néng tuì, bù néng suì*, "it cannot go back, it cannot go through"). The 大象's 非禮弗履
(*fēi lǐ fú lǚ*, "not treading off the rites") arrives here from the other side — the rite is the fence
a strong thing chooses before it hits one. 雜卦 says it outright: 大壯則止 (*dà zhuàng zé zhǐ*, "great
vigour means stopping"). 33 holds within and lets the sky go; 34 has the sky within and must learn to
hold.

**For the name.** **Great Vigour** — the signature supports the sense but strains the word: *vigour* is
this project's English for 乾's own action, 健 (*jiàn*), and 34's signature contains 乾, so the name
reuses the reading's vocabulary for a figure that carries it — the case the principle's "not as a source
of vocabulary" clause warns about. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Great Force*, *Great Strength* —
*Great Force* survives the lines: 壯于趾 (*zhuàng yú zhǐ*, "force in the toes"), 小人用壯 (*xiǎorén yòng
zhuàng*, "the small person uses force"), 壯于大輿之輹 (*zhuàng yú dà yú zhī fù*, "force in the axle-block of
the great cart"); *Great Strength* also survives those three lines, but sits against the lock that gives
強 (*qiáng*) *strong*, so it needs a seam in the glossary entry.
