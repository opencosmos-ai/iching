## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out).
**Doubled** — the yang pole of thunder ↔ wind, twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象傳 reads the doubling as repetition: 洊雷，震 (*jiàn léi, zhèn*,
"thunder upon thunder: *zhèn*"), and from it 君子以恐懼修省 (*jūnzǐ yǐ kǒngjù xiū xǐng*, "the noble one, in
fear and dread, puts himself in order and looks within"). The 彖傳 makes the fear productive: 震來虩虩，恐致福也
(*zhèn lái xìxì, kǒng zhì fú yě*, "the clap comes, and people start in alarm: fear brings good fortune"),
and 驚遠而懼邇也 (*jīng yuǎn ér jù ěr yě*, "it startles the far and frightens the near"). The one who keeps
the rites is the one who 不喪匕鬯 (*bù sàng bǐ chàng*, "does not drop the ladle or the sacrificial wine").

**What the polarity implies.** Each trigram is one firm line under two open ones: a single force at the
base, driving up through what gives way. Doubled, the force comes twice, at the first line and the fourth,
and the lines show the two landing very differently. The first comes clean and ends in laughter: the first line is
good fortune. The second has no ground to strike from, because it rises off the two soft lines beneath it:
震遂泥 (*zhèn suì ní*, "the clap sinks into the mud"), and the 小象傳 (*xiǎo xiàng zhuàn*, the line commentary) says 未光也 (*wèi guāng yě*, "it has not
yet shone"). The structure suggests that thunder doubled is not thunder twice as strong. The first clap does
the work and the second is spent. Set it beside 52. 說卦 (*shuōguà*) calls 震 (*zhèn*) the first draw and 艮 (*gèn*) the third (一索, *yī
suǒ*; 三索, *sān suǒ*): force at the bottom here, closure at the top there. Thunder is best at its start;
the mountain, as 52 shows, is best at its end. And every line here is someone the clap happens to. The
actor is never inside the figure.

**For the name.** **Thunderclap** — the signature strains it a little: 說文 (*shuōwén*)'s single strike fits the first line, but the
doubling and the lines are repetition — 震蘇蘇 (*zhèn sūsū*, "the claps roll on, and he is dazed"), 震往來厲
(*zhèn wǎng lái lì*, "claps going and coming: peril"). **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Shock* —
survives, from 震來虩虩 ("shock comes, and people start") to 震遂泥 ("the shock bogs in the mud");
*Peals* — survives the rolling lines, but bends at the top line's 震不于其躬，于其鄰 (*zhèn bù yú qí gōng, yú qí lín*,
"it strikes not his own body but his neighbour's"), since a peal does not strike.
