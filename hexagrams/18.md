## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind × mountain ↔ lake.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 剛上而柔下，巽而止，蠱 (*gāng shàng ér róu xià, xùn ér zhǐ,
gǔ*), "the firm goes up and the yielding goes down; entering, then held: rot." It then turns the rot
into a hinge: 終則有始，天行也 (*zhōng zé yǒu shǐ, tiān xíng yě*), "an end, and then there is a
beginning: that is the sky's going." The 大象傳: 山下有風 (*shān xià yǒu fēng*), "below the mountain
there is wind," and 君子以振民育德 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhèn mín yù dé*), "the noble one shakes the people and
nurtures integrity." The tradition treats rot as work to be taken up (序卦: 蠱者事也, *gǔ zhě shì yě*,
"蠱 is affairs"), and taking it up closes one cycle and opens the next.

**What the polarity implies.** 18 is 17 turned over. It is also 17 with every line changed, which
is true of only four King Wen pairs (11/12, 17/18, 53/54, 63/64). The figure `011001` is open at the
base and closed at the lid, the exact reverse of 17's firm-foot-below, open-mouth-above. It is a
sealed jar with a seep in its floor: what gets in from below is shut in by what holds above.
說文's 皿蟲 (*mǐn chóng*, "worms in a vessel") is the figure drawn. Wind at the foot of a mountain has
nowhere to blow, and air that enters and is held goes stale. 說卦 ch 11 has 巽為臭 (*xùn wéi chòu*,
"wind is odour"). The firm has gone up and stayed up (剛上), so the top holds, the bottom seeps, and
nothing passes between them. That is how an inheritance rots: the father's order sealed over, the
son's life seeping in underneath. The structure suggests the cure uses the same action as the
fault. Entering is turned from seeping into reaching, and 振 (*zhèn*, "shaking") is wind's work done
on purpose. The lid is more than a seal, too. 說卦 ch 6: 終萬物、始萬物者，莫盛乎艮 (*zhōng wànwù,
shǐ wànwù zhě, mò shèng hū gèn*), "for ending the countless things and beginning them, nothing is
fuller than 艮." The mountain is where 終則有始 happens.

**For the name.** **Rot**. The signature deepens it. Rot is what happens to a closed thing that
something has got into, and the figure draws the jar and its seep. It also supports keeping a
physical word over an abstract one. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Festering* survives the
lines (幹父之蠱, *gàn fù zhī gǔ*, "set right the father's festering"; 裕父之蠱, *yù fù zhī gǔ*,
"indulge the father's festering"), but it is a process where the lines want a thing one can set
right. *The Sealed Jar* fails the lines: "set right the father's sealed jar" loses the spoiling, and
the jar is the signature's image, not the text's.
