## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching). Without: **wind** (巽 *xùn* · yin · entering).
**Across** — the yin pole of water ↔ fire within, the yin pole of thunder ↔ wind without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 sees a hearth: 風自火出，家人；君子以言有物，而行有恆 (*fēng zì huǒ
chū, jiā rén; jūnzǐ yǐ yán yǒu wù, ér xíng yǒu héng*, "wind comes out of fire: the household; the noble
one's words have substance, and conduct has constancy"). The 彖 maps the household onto within and
without — 女正位乎內，男正位乎外 (*nǚ zhèng wèi hū nèi, nán zhèng wèi hū wài*, "the woman's right place is
within, the man's right place is without") — and ends in order: 正家而天下定矣 (*zhèng jiā ér tiānxià dìng
yǐ*, "set the household right, and all under the sky is settled"). For the tradition the household is
the first order, and every wider one follows from it.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire within: a hollow held between two firm lines — an open, warm centre
enclosed by walls. Wind without: open at its base, entering, pervading. Put them together and the
figure is the hearth and its reach — the fire people gather to, and the heat and smoke that pass out
through whatever is open and into everything nearby. The structure suggests the household is defined
twice: by its enclosure, where the fire's firm top line meets the wind, and by its influence, which goes
out softly. The lines hold both: 閑有家 (*xián yǒu jiā*, "bar in the household") at the start, and 富家
(*fù jiā*, "enriching the household") once the wind is reached.

The 大象's 言有物 (*yán yǒu wù*, "words with substance") arrives from the structure: wind carries only
what the fire gave it, so what goes out of a household is as good as its hearth. Both trigrams are yin —
two daughters in 說卦's family — and 卦辭 asks for 利女貞 (*lì nǚ zhēn*, "favourable for a woman's
steadiness"); the figure has no single ruling line in it, and the one-ruler figure it needs, 王假有家
(*wáng jiǎ yǒu jiā*, "the ruler comes to the household"), arrives from outside. Turn it over and the fire
goes outside and looks away: 38. 雜卦: 睽外也，家人內也 (*kuí wài yě, jiā rén nèi yě*, "estrangement is the
outside; the household is the inside").

**For the name.** **The Household** — the signature supports it: a hearth and its reach is a household,
and *household* rather than *family* keeps both the bar of 閑有家 and the wealth of 富家. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Kin*, *The Hearth* — *Kin* survives the lines: 家人嗃嗃 (*jiā rén hè hè*, "the kin
shouting sternly"), though it loses the house in 富家 (*fù jiā*, "enriching the household"); *The Hearth*,
from 風自火出 (*fēng zì huǒ chū*, "wind comes out of fire"), fails the lines — 家人嗃嗃 cannot be "the hearth
shouting sternly", and 人 (*rén*, "people") is in the name.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A household is kept from its centre: bar the gate at the start, feed the middle, and what goes out
of the house will be what burns in it.** The graph 家 ("household") is a roof with a pig beneath it.
The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, glosses it 居也 ("a dwelling") and takes the pig for
the sound; whether the pig is sound or sense is argued, but the picture is there either way — the
most valuable animal, kept under the same roof as the people. The core text's six lines never leave
that house. They move from its gate, 閑有家 ("a bar across the household"), to its food, 在中饋 ("at the
centre, the feeding"), to its tone of voice, its stores, its visitor and its authority. Nothing in
it is heroic. It is about the small, repeated things by which a house holds together — and the name
keeps 人 ("people"): *the household* (proposed) is its people, not its walls.

### The polarities in it

Fire within, wind without. The fire is hollow at its centre and firm at its edges — a warm space
held inside walls; the wind is open at its base and goes into everything nearby. Together they make
a hearth and its reach, the household defined once by its enclosure and once by what it gives out
(see *The signature*, above). The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, reads the pair as cause
and effect — 風自火出 ("wind comes out of fire") — and that is the centre of this reading: **the inside
comes first, and the outside is made by it.** Both trigrams are yin, the middle and the eldest
daughter in the family of the Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams. The Tuan, the Wing on the judgment,
nonetheless seats the man at the firm fifth line and the woman at the yielding second; that is the
Wing's arrangement of the figure, not something the lines say.

### In nature

Light a fire in still air and it makes its own wind. The heated gases rise; cooler air is drawn in
low from every side to take their place; the draught feeds the flame, and the plume carries heat,
smoke and the smell of what is cooking up and away. 風自火出 ("wind comes out of fire") is exactly
observed: the wind comes out of the fire, and it carries only what the fire gives it. Walk past a
village on a winter evening and you can read its households by their smoke before you see a door —
which are lit, which are cooking, which are cold. That is the Great Image's 言有物 ("words that have
substance"): what leaves a house is made of what burns inside it.

The gate is in the lines. The Shuowen gives 閑 ("a bar") as 闌也 ("a barrier") and draws it plainly,
門中有木 ("a gate with timber across it"). Notice when gates are barred: at dusk, and in the cold
months, when the stock comes in. The household of this figure is most itself in winter — the door
shut, the fire fed, everyone within reach of the same heat.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 家人：利女貞 ("The household. Advantageous: a woman's holding firm"). Four characters,
and one of them is 女 ("a woman"). Read with the older sense of 貞 ("the inquiry") it is
"advantageous: an inquiry about a woman" — a question put about a bride or a wife — and then the
woman is what the question concerns, not whom it instructs. Either way the core text names a woman
here, and the English should not hide her.

**The seam, named.** The core text names three roles: a woman in the judgment, 婦子 ("wives and
children") at line 3, 王 ("the ruler") at line 5. The Wings build them into a doctrine — the Tuan's
女正位乎內，男正位乎外 ("the woman's right place is within, the man's right place is without"), which it calls
天地之大義 ("the great rightness of sky and earth"). Wang Bi reads line 2, which names no one, as 盡婦人之正義
("fulfilling the right conduct of a wife"). The text was written in and for a house where the inside
was women's work and the outside men's. This reading renders toward the universal — whoever keeps
the centre of a house, whoever feeds it — because every image in the lines, a gate, a hearth, a tone
of voice, a full store, holds for any household; and it says here that it has done so.

**Line 1.** 閑有家，悔亡 ("a bar across the household — regret goes"). A firm line at the base: set the
terms at the start. Wang Bi puts it in three characters, 法在始 ("rules belong at the beginning"); a
house that makes rules only after it has gone slack will regret them.

**Line 2.** 无攸遂，在中饋，貞吉 ("nothing to go off and finish; at the centre, the feeding — holding firm,
auspicious"). The yielding centre of the fire: the one who does not leave to accomplish things
elsewhere, and feeds everyone. The Shuowen's 饋 ("the feeding") is 餉也 ("providing food").

**Line 3.** 家人嗃嗃，悔厲吉；婦子嘻嘻，終吝 ("the household shouting sternly — regret, peril, auspicious; wives and
children giggling — in the end, stinting"). Firm on a firm place, at the top of the fire: the
hearth's heat at its edge. Both tones fail somewhere; the line counts the stern one recoverable and
the slack one not. Wang Bi says it flatly: 家與其瀆寧過乎嚴 ("a household would rather err toward sternness
than toward slackness"). But the Shuowen gives 嗃嗃 ("shouting sternly") as 嚴酷皃 ("the look of
harshness") — the cost is written into the word.

**Line 4.** 富家，大吉 ("enriching the household — greatly auspicious"). The wind's first line, yielding
and in its place. The Shuowen's 富 ("enriching") is 備也 ("provided for"): not wealth to display, but
stores laid in.

**Line 5.** 王假有家，勿恤，吉 ("the ruler comes to the household — do not worry; auspicious"). 假 ("to
arrive") is "to arrive", the Shuowen's second sense, 一曰至也 ("another meaning: to arrive"). At the
firm centre above, the widest order enters the smallest. The Small Image, the Wing on each line:
交相愛也 ("they love one another").

**Line 6.** 有孚威如，終吉 ("there is confidence, and an air of authority — in the end, auspicious").
Confidence comes first, authority second. The Shuowen glosses 威 ("authority") as 姑也 ("the husband's
mother") — the household's authority written with 女 ("woman") in it. The Small Image reads it 反身之謂也
("it means turning back on one's own body"), and Wang Bi gives the balance the whole figure needs:
凡物以猛爲本者則患在寡恩，以愛爲本者則患在寡威 ("whatever is rooted in fierceness suffers from too little kindness;
whatever is rooted in love suffers from too little authority").

### In a life now

The figure fits any household now: a couple, a parent alone with a child, grown children caring for
a parent, a shared flat, a crew on a boat, a house of monks. The Xugua, the Wing on the sequence,
gives the reason people turn to it: 傷於外者，必反其家 ("whoever is wounded outside must go back to their
household"). What the lines counsel is timing and tone. Make the rules at the beginning, not after
the damage (line 1). Honour whoever stays at the centre and feeds everyone — work that accomplishes
nothing visible and holds everything (line 2). If the tone must err, the text would rather it err
strict than slack, but it counts the regret and the peril of strictness honestly (line 3). Lay in
stores (line 4). Let authority begin with oneself (line 6).

The Great Image turns this into practice: 君子以言有物，而行有恆 ("the noble one's words have substance, and
their conduct has constancy"). A household is known by its smoke. Nothing goes out of it that was
not burning inside.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share the word 家 ("household") and a shape; they part over the family's virtues.**
Chapter 54, in [the Open Tao Te Ching](https://github.com/opencosmos-ai/taoteching), widens outward: *"Cultivate this in your body, and its
integrity becomes authentic. / Cultivate this in your family, and its integrity becomes abundant"* —
and on through community and nation to the world. The same widening is in 37's Wings, not its core:
the Small Image's 反身 ("turning back on one's own body") at line 6, and the Tuan's 正家而天下定矣 ("set the
household right, and the world is settled"). The test: 家 ("household") is in the core text three
times, and the English answers to it — though the Open Tao Te Ching's chapter 54 says *family* where this project
proposes *household*.

Where they part is sharp. The Tuan sets the household right by roles: 父父子子 ("the father a father,
the son a son"). Chapter 18 distrusts exactly that: *"When kin are out of harmony, devotion and
tenderness appear"* — named family virtues are what shows when the thing itself has gone. And Wang
Bi, on line 5, writes that when the household is right the 六親 ("the six kin") are 和睦 ("at peace") —
the Laozi's own 六親 ("the six kin"), read back the other way. The Laozi's household is cultivated;
the Tuan's is ordered. This reading does not choose between them.
