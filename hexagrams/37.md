## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching). Without: **wind** (巽 *xùn* · yin · entering).
**Across** — the yin pole of water ↔ fire within, the yin pole of thunder ↔ wind without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 sees a hearth: 風自火出，家人；君子以言有物，而行有恆 (*fēng zì huǒ
chū, jiā rén; jūnzǐ yǐ yán yǒu wù, ér xíng yǒu héng*, "wind comes out of fire: the household; the noble
one's words have substance, and conduct has constancy"). The 彖 maps the household onto within and
without — 女正位乎內，男正位乎外 (*nǚ zhèng wèi hū nèi, nán zhèng wèi hū wài*, "the woman's right place is
within, the man's right place is without") — and ends in order: 正家而天下定矣 (*zhèng jiā ér tiānxià dìng
yǐ*, "set the household right, and all under the sky is settled"). For the tradition the household is
the first order, and every wider one follows from it.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire within: a hollow held between two firm lines — an open, warm centre
enclosed by walls. Wind without: open at its base, entering, pervading. Put them together and the
figure is the hearth and its reach — the fire people gather to, and the heat and smoke that pass out
through whatever is open and into everything nearby. The structure suggests the household is defined
twice: by its enclosure, where the fire's firm top line meets the wind, and by its influence, which goes
out softly. The lines hold both: 閑有家 (*xián yǒu jiā*, "bar in the household") at the start, and 富家
(*fù jiā*, "enriching the household") once the wind is reached.

The 大象's 言有物 (*yán yǒu wù*, "words with substance") arrives from the structure: wind carries only
what the fire gave it, so what goes out of a household is as good as its hearth. Both trigrams are yin —
two daughters in 說卦's family — and 卦辭 asks for 利女貞 (*lì nǚ zhēn*, "favourable for a woman's
steadiness"); the figure has no single ruling line in it, and the one-ruler figure it needs, 王假有家
(*wáng jiǎ yǒu jiā*, "the ruler comes to the household"), arrives from outside. Turn it over and the fire
goes outside and looks away: 38. 雜卦: 睽外也，家人內也 (*kuí wài yě, jiā rén nèi yě*, "estrangement is the
outside; the household is the inside").

**For the name.** **The Household** — the signature supports it: a hearth and its reach is a household,
and *household* rather than *family* keeps both the bar of 閑有家 and the wealth of 富家. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Kin*, *The Hearth* — *Kin* survives the lines: 家人嗃嗃 (*jiā rén hè hè*, "the kin
shouting sternly"), though it loses the house in 富家 (*fù jiā*, "enriching the household"); *The Hearth*,
from 風自火出 (*fēng zì huǒ chū*, "wind comes out of fire"), fails the lines — 家人嗃嗃 cannot be "the hearth
shouting sternly", and 人 (*rén*, "people") is in the name.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A household is kept from its centre: bar the gate at the start, feed the middle, and what goes
out of the house will be what burns in it.** The graph 家 (*jiā*) is a roof with a pig beneath it.
說文 (*shuōwén*) glosses it 居也 (*jū yě*, "a dwelling") and takes the pig for the sound; whether the
pig is sound or sense is argued, but the picture is there either way — the most valuable animal,
kept under the same roof as the people. The core text's six lines never leave that house. They
move from its gate, 閑有家 (*xián yǒu jiā*, "a bar across the household"), to its food, 在中饋 (*zài
zhōng kuì*, "at the centre, the feeding"), to its tone of voice, its stores, its visitor and its
authority. Nothing in it is heroic. It is about the small, repeated things by which a house holds
together — and the name keeps 人 (*rén*, "people"): *the household* (proposed) is its people, not
its walls.

### The polarities in it

Fire within, wind without. The fire is hollow at its centre and firm at its edges — a warm space
held inside walls; the wind is open at its base and goes into everything nearby. Together they
make a hearth and its reach, the household defined once by its enclosure and once by what it
gives out (see *The signature*, above). The 大象 reads the pair as cause and effect — 風自火出
(*fēng zì huǒ chū*, "wind comes out of fire") — and that is the centre of this reading: **the
inside comes first, and the outside is made by it.** Both trigrams are yin, the middle and the
eldest daughter in 說卦's family. The 彖 nonetheless seats the man at the firm fifth line and the
woman at the yielding second; that is the Wing's arrangement of the figure, not something the
lines say.

### In nature

Light a fire in still air and it makes its own wind. The heated gases rise; cooler air is drawn
in low from every side to take their place; the draught feeds the flame, and the plume carries
heat, smoke and the smell of what is cooking up and away. 風自火出 (*fēng zì huǒ chū*, "wind comes
out of fire") is exactly observed: the wind comes out of the fire, and it carries only what the
fire gives it. Walk past a village on a winter evening and you can read its households by their
smoke before you see a door — which are lit, which are cooking, which are cold. That is the
大象's 言有物 (*yán yǒu wù*, "words that have substance"): what leaves a house is made of what burns
inside it.

The gate is in the lines. 說文 gives 閑 (*xián*) as 闌也 (*lán yě*, "a barrier") and draws it plainly,
門中有木 (*mén zhōng yǒu mù*, "a gate with timber across it"). Notice when gates are barred: at dusk,
and in the cold months, when the stock comes in. The household of this figure is most itself in
winter — the door shut, the fire fed, everyone within reach of the same heat.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 家人：利女貞 (*jiā rén: lì nǚ zhēn*) — "The household. Advantageous: a woman's
holding firm." Four characters, and one of them is 女 (*nǚ*, "a woman"). Read with the older sense of
貞 (*zhēn*) it is "advantageous: an inquiry about a woman" — a question put about a bride or a wife —
and then the woman is what the question concerns, not whom it instructs. Either way the core text
names a woman here, and the English should not hide her.

**The seam, named.** The core text names three roles: a woman in the judgment, 婦子 (*fù zǐ*, "wives
and children") at line 3, 王 (*wáng*, "the ruler") at line 5. The Wings build them into a doctrine —
the 彖's 女正位乎內，男正位乎外 (*nǚ zhèng wèi hū nèi, nán zhèng wèi hū wài*, "the woman's right place
is within, the man's right place is without"), which it calls 天地之大義 (*tiāndì zhī dà yì*, "the
great rightness of sky and earth"). 王弼 reads line 2, which names no one, as 盡婦人之正義 (*jìn fù
rén zhī zhèng yì*, "fulfilling the right conduct of a wife"). The text was written in and for a
house where the inside was women's work and the outside men's. This reading renders toward the
universal — whoever keeps the centre of a house, whoever feeds it — because every image in the
lines, a gate, a hearth, a tone of voice, a full store, holds for any household; and it says here
that it has done so.

**Line 1.** 閑有家，悔亡 (*xián yǒu jiā, huǐ wáng*, "a bar across the household — regret goes"). A firm
line at the base: set the terms at the start. 王弼 puts it in three characters, 法在始 (*fǎ zài shǐ*,
"rules belong at the beginning"); a house that makes rules only after it has gone slack will
regret them.

**Line 2.** 无攸遂，在中饋，貞吉 (*wú yōu suì, zài zhōng kuì, zhēn jí*, "nothing to go off and finish;
at the centre, the feeding — holding firm, auspicious"). The yielding centre of the fire: the one
who does not leave to accomplish things elsewhere, and feeds everyone. 說文's 饋 (*kuì*) is 餉也
(*xiǎng yě*, "providing food").

**Line 3.** 家人嗃嗃，悔厲吉；婦子嘻嘻，終吝 (*jiā rén hè hè, huǐ lì jí; fù zǐ xī xī, zhōng lìn*, "the
household shouting sternly — regret, peril, auspicious; wives and children giggling — in the end,
stinting"). Firm on a firm place, at the top of the fire: the hearth's heat at its edge. Both tones
fail somewhere; the line counts the stern one recoverable and the slack one not. 王弼 says it flatly:
家與其瀆寧過乎嚴 (*jiā yǔ qí dú nìng guò hū yán*, "a household would rather err toward sternness than
toward slackness"). But 說文 gives 嗃嗃 (*hè hè*) as 嚴酷皃 (*yán kù mào*, "the look of harshness") —
the cost is written into the word.

**Line 4.** 富家，大吉 (*fù jiā, dà jí*, "enriching the household — greatly auspicious"). The wind's
first line, yielding and in its place. 說文's 富 (*fù*) is 備也 (*bèi yě*, "provided for"): not wealth
to display, but stores laid in.

**Line 5.** 王假有家，勿恤，吉 (*wáng jiǎ yǒu jiā, wù xù, jí*, "the ruler comes to the household — do
not worry; auspicious"). 假 (*jiǎ*) is "to arrive", 說文's second sense, 一曰至也 (*yī yuē zhì yě*,
"another meaning: to arrive"). At the firm centre above, the widest order enters the smallest. The
小象: 交相愛也 (*jiāo xiāng ài yě*, "they love one another").

**Line 6.** 有孚威如，終吉 (*yǒu fú wēi rú, zhōng jí*, "there is confidence, and an air of authority
— in the end, auspicious"). Confidence comes first, authority second. 說文 glosses 威 (*wēi*) as 姑也
(*gū yě*, "the husband's mother") — the household's authority written with 女 (*nǚ*, "woman") in it.
The 小象 reads it 反身之謂也 (*fǎn shēn zhī wèi yě*, "it means turning back on one's own body"), and
王弼 gives the balance the whole figure needs: 凡物以猛爲本者則患在寡恩，以愛爲本者則患在寡威 (*fán
wù yǐ měng wéi běn zhě zé huàn zài guǎ ēn, yǐ ài wéi běn zhě zé huàn zài guǎ wēi*, "whatever is rooted
in fierceness suffers from too little kindness; whatever is rooted in love suffers from too little
authority").

### In a life now

The figure fits any household now: a couple, a parent alone with a child, grown children caring
for a parent, a shared flat, a crew on a boat, a house of monks. 序卦 gives the reason people turn
to it: 傷於外者，必反其家 (*shāng yú wài zhě, bì fǎn qí jiā*, "whoever is wounded outside must go back
to their household"). What the lines counsel is timing and tone. Make the rules at the beginning,
not after the damage (line 1). Honour whoever stays at the centre and feeds everyone — work that
accomplishes nothing visible and holds everything (line 2). If the tone must err, the text would
rather it err strict than slack, but it counts the regret and the peril of strictness honestly
(line 3). Lay in stores (line 4). Let authority begin with oneself (line 6).

The 大象 turns this into practice: 君子以言有物，而行有恆 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yán yǒu wù, ér xíng yǒu héng*,
"the noble one's words have substance, and their conduct has constancy"). A household is known by
its smoke. Nothing goes out of it that was not burning inside.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share the word 家 (*jiā*, "household") and a shape; they part over the family's
virtues.** Chapter 54, in Shalom's translation, widens outward: *"Cultivate this in your body, and
its integrity becomes authentic. / Cultivate this in your family, and its integrity becomes
abundant"* — and on through community and nation to the world. The same widening is in 37's Wings,
not its core: the 小象's 反身 (*fǎn shēn*, "turning back on one's own body") at line 6, and the 彖's
正家而天下定矣 (*zhèng jiā ér tiānxià dìng yǐ*, "set the household right, and the world is settled").
The test: 家 is in the core text three times, and the English answers to it — though Shalom's
chapter 54 says *family* where this project proposes *household*.

Where they part is sharp. The 彖 sets the household right by roles: 父父子子 (*fù fù zǐ zǐ*, "the
father a father, the son a son"). Chapter 18 distrusts exactly that: *"When kin are out of
harmony, devotion and tenderness appear"* — named family virtues are what shows when the thing
itself has gone. And 王弼, on line 5, writes that when the household is right the 六親 (*liù qīn*,
"the six kin") are 和睦 (*hé mù*, "at peace") — the Laozi's own 六親, read back the other way. The
Laozi's household is cultivated; the 彖's is ordered. This reading does not choose between them.

  yě*, "a woman holding a broom, sprinkling and sweeping") — the role is in the graph
- 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler*, never *king* (locked) · 天下 (*tiānxià*) — *the world* (locked)
