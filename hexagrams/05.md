## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour). Without: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth meets water ↔ fire, yang on both sides, no odd line within and the odd line
at the middle without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 需，須也，險在前也；剛健而不陷，其義不困窮矣 (*xū, xū
yě, xiǎn zài qián yě; gāng jiàn ér bù xiàn, qí yì bù kùn qióng yǐ*), "需 (*xū*) is waiting: the hazard
is in front; firm and vigorous and not sinking in, by rights it is not cornered." The 大象 (*dà
xiàng*, the great image): 雲上於天，需；君子以飲食宴樂 (*yún shàng yú tiān, xū; jūnzǐ yǐ yǐn shí yàn
lè*), "cloud rises above the sky: 需; the noble one, by it, eats and drinks, feasts and takes
pleasure." The tradition reads strength that waits for the hazard ahead to open, and waits well.

**What the polarity implies.** Three firm lines inside: the self is all vigour. Outside is the pole
that sinks in, its firm line hidden at its centre. Sky rises and water descends, so the two move
toward each other — but the 大象 stops the image at cloud, rain gathered and not yet fallen. The 彖
does something rare: it names both of the signature's actions and negates the outer one, 剛健而不陷
(*gāng jiàn ér bù xiàn*), "firm and vigorous, and not sinking in." That is the whole figure. The
vigour could plunge; it chooses not to take on the other trigram's action. The lines then measure
the distance to the hazard, step by step — 需于郊 (*xū yú jiāo*) at the outskirts, 于沙 (*yú shā*) in
the sand, 于泥 (*yú ní*) in the mud, 于血 (*yú xuè*) in blood — until the fifth, the firm core of the
water itself, where the waiting is 于酒食 (*yú jiǔ shí*), "at wine and food." The structure suggests
the reward is inside the hazard, reached by not rushing it.

Turn the figure over and it is 6: the hazard inside, the vigour outside, the two moving apart. In 5
strength waits on the world and is fed; in 6 a grievance presses on a strength that will not come
down.

**For the name.** **Waiting** — supported: the 彖 glosses 需 by 須 (*xū*, "wait"), and the signature
says why the vigour waits — its action and the outer one run opposite, and it declines to sink. It
deepens the word toward waiting as held strength rather than delay. **Candidates the signature
suggests:** *Biding* — survives every line: "biding at the outskirts … in the mud … at wine and
food." *Standing Off* — from 不陷 (*bù xiàn*, "not sinking in"); fails the lines at 需于酒食 (*xū yú
jiǔ shí*, "— at wine and food"), where nobody stands off from a meal, and at 入于穴 (*rù yú xué*),
"enters the hole."

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Strength stopped short of the water, waiting for its time — and fed while it waits.** In the
core text 需 (*xū*) is a verb of place, said six times: waiting at the outskirts, on the sand, in
the mud, in blood, at wine and food. 說文 (*shuōwén*) draws the graph as rain, 雨 (*yǔ*), over its sound,
and says what the picture means: 遇雨不進 (*yù yǔ bù jìn*, "meets rain and does not go on"). This is
not delay from weakness. It is someone able to go, held by weather they cannot command, who
counts the distance and does not waste the time.

### The polarities in it

Within is sky, three firm lines: nothing inside is lacking. Without is water, a hazard whose own
firm line is hidden at its centre. The 彖 (*tuàn*) says the whole figure in one clause — 剛健而不陷
(*gāng jiàn ér bù xiàn*, "firm and vigorous, and not sinking in") — the vigour could plunge into
what lies ahead, and does not take that motion on. And the reward is not on the far side of the
hazard: it is in its middle, at the fifth line, the water's firm core, where the waiting becomes a
meal. What waits well is not kept from the hazard; it is received by it.

### In nature

Watch cloud build over dry country in the weeks before the rains. The towers rise all afternoon,
darken, and break up at dusk without a drop; the farmer can do nothing about it, and still has to
eat. The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees exactly this: 雲上於天 (*yún shàng yú tiān*, "cloud rises above
the sky") — rain gathered and not yet fallen.

Then walk the lines as a path down to a river in the dry season. 郊 (*jiāo*), the outskirts, is in
說文 the open land a hundred *li* from the city: far from anything. Then 沙 (*shā*), sand — and
說文 has the naturalist's note, 水少沙見 (*shuǐ shǎo shā xiàn*, "when the water is low, the sand
shows"): the bars and banks a river exposes when it shrinks. Then 泥 (*ní*), mud, the soft margin
where the bank gives underfoot and a cart sinks to the axle. Anyone who has crossed a low river
knows the order: sand holds you, mud takes you, and it is at the mud that you are stuck in plain
view.

The judgment's first word has a creature in it. 說文 gives 孚 (*fú*) as 卵孚也 (*luǎn fú yě*, "the
hatching of eggs") — a claw over a child: a bird on the nest. A sitting duck or hen does not move
for weeks except to turn the eggs; if she leaves them they chill. That is the waiting this
hexagram means — still, and doing the one thing that time requires.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 需：有孚，光亨。貞吉，利涉大川 (*xū: yǒu fú, guāng hēng. zhēn jí, lì shè dà
chuān*) — "Waiting. There is confidence; shining, it gets through. Holding firm: auspicious.
Advantageous to cross the great river." The core text sets the crossing after the waiting: the
river is not refused, only not rushed.

**Line 1** is furthest out. 需于郊，利用恆，无咎 (*xū yú jiāo, lì yòng héng, wú jiù*, "waiting at
the outskirts: advantage in constancy — no blame"). 恆 (*héng*) is in 說文 a heart and a boat
between two lines, the banks: steady passage. Far from the hazard, the only task is to keep on.

**Line 2**, the centre of the sky, is nearer. 需于沙，小有言，終吉 (*xū yú shā, xiǎo yǒu yán, zhōng
jí*, "waiting on the sand: a little talk; in the end, auspicious"). Close enough now that people
comment, and complain. The centre holds, and the talk passes.

**Line 3**, at the seam, goes a step too far. 需于泥，致寇至 (*xū yú ní, zhì kòu zhì*, "waiting in
the mud: it brings raiders on"). The core text gives no verdict word, only the outcome. The 小象
(*xiǎo xiàng*) adds 自我致寇 (*zì wǒ zhì kòu*, "I brought the raiders myself") — the firmest line,
pressed hardest against the water, stuck where everyone can see.

**Line 4** is the first line of the water: the hazard reached. 需于血，出自穴 (*xū yú xuè, chū zì
xué*, "waiting in blood: coming out of the hole"). Hurt, and out.

**Line 5** is the water's firm centre, and the arc's reward. 需于酒食，貞吉 (*xū yú jiǔ shí, zhēn
jí*, "waiting at wine and food: holding firm, auspicious"). The waiting has become a meal; nothing
is chased. 王弼: 无所復須 (*wú suǒ fù xū*, "there is nothing left to wait for").

**Line 6** goes back in. 入于穴，有不速之客三人來，敬之終吉 (*rù yú xué, yǒu bù sù zhī kè sān rén
lái, jìng zhī zhōng jí*, "entering the hole: three guests come, not sent for; treat them with
respect, and in the end, auspicious"). Line 4 came out of the 穴 (*xué*); line 6 enters it — and
說文 gives 穴 as 土室也 (*tǔ shì yě*, "an earthen room"), the dug dwelling of the loess country, so
this may be going home as much as going under. The waiting ends not with the traveller's own
crossing but with what arrives. 王弼 reads the three guests as the sky's three firm lines coming up
of themselves; the core text says only that they come.

### In a life now

This is the figure for the weeks between the scan and the result; for the field before the rain;
for the manuscript that is not ready, the offer not yet made, the child not yet home. It asks two
things. **Know where you are standing** — the outskirts, the sand, or the mud — because the same
patience that is right at the outskirts becomes, at the mud, a way of getting stuck in public.
And **eat.** The 大象 turns the image straight to practice: 君子以飲食宴樂 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yǐn shí yàn
lè*, "the noble one eats and drinks, feasts and takes pleasure"). Waiting that forgets to live
while it waits is not what this figure counsels. The last line adds a third thing: what ends a
long wait is often not your move but someone else's arrival, uninvited — and the counsel is
respect.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One shared image, and it is a formula, so the contact is modest.** The Laozi's chapter 15
describes the masterful: *"Careful, like wading across a river in winter."* The graphs are 涉
(*shè*, "wade, cross") and 川 (*chuān*, "river") — both in the judgment of 需 (*xū*), 利涉大川 (*lì shè dà chuān*, "advantageous to cross the great river"), in the core text.
But that phrase recurs in some ten hexagrams; it is a stock formula, not this figure's own.

What the two books share is care at the water's edge. Where they part is plain: in the Laozi the
crossing is a likeness for how the masterful carry themselves, forever hesitant; in 需 the crossing
is an event, and the waiting exists to make it *advantageous*. The same chapter has *"Dignified,
like a guest"* — 客 (*kè*), the graph of line 6. In the Laozi the masterful one is the guest; here
the one who has waited is the host, and the guests are honoured. The echo is worth keeping for
that reversal, not for any agreement.
