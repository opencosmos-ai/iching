## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind and water ↔ fire, both at their yin poles.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 opens by calling the figure a picture: 鼎，象也 (*dǐng, xiàng yě*,
"the cauldron is an image"). Then it reads the trigrams as fuel and flame: 以木巽火，亨飪也 (*yǐ mù xùn
huǒ, pēng rèn yě*, "wood entering fire: that is cooking"). The 大象傳 has 木上有火 (*mù shàng yǒu huǒ*,
"fire above wood"), and from it 君子以正位凝命 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhèng wèi níng mìng*, "the noble one sets the
positions right and lets the allotted course set firm"). What is cooked is made to set.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 49 turned over, and the fire changes places. In 49 the fire was
within and the thing itself changed. Here the fire is without, and the vessel stands between the heat and
what it cooks. Wind within, open at the base, is wood fed in from below a little at a time. Fire without,
hollow at the centre, clings to what it is given. So the heat is steady and fed, never a blaze, and the
change goes on inside a container that does not change. That is the difference 雜卦 (*záguà*, "the Mixed Figures") names: 革去故也，鼎取新也
(*gé qù gù yě, dǐng qǔ xīn yě*, "*gé* removes the old; *dǐng* takes up the new"). The open base is also the weak
point, and the lines know it. The first line tips the vessel to empty it, 鼎顛趾，利出否 (*dǐng diān zhǐ, lì
chū pǐ*, "the cauldron upended on its feet: good for tipping out the spoiled"). The fourth breaks it:
鼎折足，覆公餗 (*dǐng zhé zú, fù gōng sù*, "the cauldron's leg breaks and the lord's stew spills"). The
pair's own seam is in the third line: 鼎耳革 (*dǐng ěr gé*, "the cauldron's ears are changed"). When 革 (*gé*) gets into the
vessel, 其行塞 (*qí xíng sè*, "its going is blocked").

**For the name.** **The Cauldron** — the signature supports it: the figure is the one object in which
entering and attaching are held apart and made to work, and the 彖傳 reads it as a picture of that object.
**Candidates the signature suggests:** *Cooking* — fails the lines: no line cooks; they name the vessel's
feet, belly, ears and carrying-bar (鼎黃耳金鉉, *dǐng huáng ěr jīn xuàn*, "the cauldron's yellow ears and
bronze bar"); *The Vessel* — survives every line (鼎有實, *dǐng yǒu shí*, "the vessel has its fill"), but
器 (*qì*) is locked to *vessel*, so it collides.
