## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Across** — water ↔ fire × mountain ↔ lake: firm at the centre within, open at the top without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 困，剛掩也 (*kùn, gāng yǎn yě*, "*kùn*: the firm is covered
over"); 險以說，困而不失其所 (*xiǎn yǐ yuè, kùn ér bù shī qí suǒ*, "hazard, and releasing; hemmed in,
yet not losing one's place"); 有言不信，尚口乃窮也 (*yǒu yán bù xìn, shàng kǒu nǎi qióng yě*, "there
are words, and they are not trusted: to rely on the mouth is to come to an end"). The 大象傳: 澤无水，
困；君子以致命遂志 (*zé wú shuǐ, kùn; jūnzǐ yǐ zhì mìng suì zhì*, "the lake has no water: *kùn*; the
noble one stakes the life allotted to them to carry the aim through"). The tradition reads a firm
person pressed and covered by lesser things, who keeps their footing and whose words do nothing.

**What the polarity implies.** 澤无水 (*zé wú shuǐ*, "the lake has no water") is the figure's own
arrangement: the water is below the lake, not in it. Water's action is sinking in, and it has sunk —
the lake above is open at the top and holds nothing, because what should fill it has gone beneath.
The firm line within sits at water's centre, the hidden core, and that is 剛掩 (*gāng yǎn*, "the firm
covered over"). Firm within, open without: substance inside, and outside a mouth with nothing to
offer. So 尚口乃窮 (*shàng kǒu nǎi qióng*, "relying on the mouth comes to an end") belongs to this
figure in particular: the lake's 說 (*yuè*) is release and speech, and speech is all the outer figure
has left.

The lines name what presses — 株木 (*zhū mù*, "a tree stump"), 石 (*shí*, "stone"), 蒺藜 (*jí lí*,
"caltrops"), 金車 (*jīn chē*, "a bronze carriage"), 葛藟 (*gé lěi*, "creeping vines") — and the first
line sinks just as water does: 入于幽谷 (*rù yú yōu gǔ*, "enters a dark valley"). The structure
suggests where it turns, too: the fifth line, in the lake, finds the lake's own action — 乃徐有說 (*nǎi
xú yǒu yuè*, "then, slowly, there is release"). Turn the figure over and it is 48, where water stands
above and something goes down to bring it up; the 雜卦: 井通，而困相遇也 (*jǐng tōng, ér kùn xiāng yù
yě*, "the well gets through, and *kùn* runs into things").

**For the name.** *Hemmed In* — the signature deepens it: the lines supply the enclosures, and the
signature adds that the pressure comes with draining — the core is sound but covered, and the vessel
above it is empty. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Pressed*, *Drained* — *Pressed* survives:
困于石 (*kùn yú shí*, "pressed by stone"), 困于酒食 (*kùn yú jiǔ shí*, "pressed by wine and food") and
困于葛藟 (*kùn yú gé lěi*, "pressed by creeping vines") all read; *Drained* — the lake with no water —
fails the lines: 困于石 (*kùn yú shí*, "drained by stone") and 困于葛藟 (*kùn yú gé lěi*, "drained by
creeping vines") are enclosures, not emptyings.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Hemmed in on every side, and talking will not get you out; what gets through is the firm centre,
slowly.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 困 (*kùn*, "hemmed in") as 故廬也 (*gù lú yě*, "an old hut") and draws it
从木在囗中 (*cóng mù zài wéi zhōng*, "a tree inside an enclosure"): a tree that has grown up within four
walls and has nowhere to spread. The core text presses a person the same way, line after line, with
困于 (*kùn yú*, "hemmed in by") and a list — a tree stump, wine and food, stone, a bronze carriage, a
crimson sash of office, creeping vines. Some of these are hardship and some are plenty and honour,
and the text treats them alike. The judgment names what does not help: 有言不信 (*yǒu yán bù xìn*,
"there are words, and they are not trusted"). And yet its first word after the name is 亨 (*hēng*,
"gets through").

### The polarities in it

Water within, lake without (see *The signature*, above). The firm line sits at the centre of the
lower trigram, sound; above it the lake is open at the top and holds nothing, because the water has
sunk beneath it — 澤无水 (*zé wú shuǐ*, "the lake has no water", 大象). Firm at the core, an empty mouth
at the edge. The 彖 (*tuàn*, the judgment commentary) reads the same arrangement twice: 剛掩也 (*gāng
yǎn yě*, "the firm is covered over") and 尚口乃窮也 (*shàng kǒu nǎi qióng yě*, "to rely on the mouth is
to come to an end"). **Here the text goes further than the structure.** The figure shows a firm
thing covered; the lines show that the covering can be anything, feasting and office included — line
2's red sash and line 5's crimson one press as hard as stone. Nothing in the trigrams says that. The
lines do, and the reading follows them.

### In nature

澤无水 (*zé wú shuǐ*, "the lake has no water"): walk to a pond in a long drought. The water does not
vanish all at once; it goes down — into the margins first, then into the mud, then below the bed as
the water table falls — until the surface is plates of cracked clay curling at their edges and the
fish are crowded in the last pool. The water is still there, under the ground. What is gone is the
part anyone could reach. That is the figure exactly: water within, a lake without, empty.

The lines are full of growth that hems. 葛 (*gé*) is kudzu, in 說文 絺綌艸也 (*chī xì cǎo yě*, "the plant
for fine and coarse cloth") — a vine that climbs a tree and lays its leaves over the crown until the
tree is in its shade; 藟 (*lěi*) is another wild vine. 蒺藜 (*jí lí*) is the caltrop, or puncture-vine:
it lies flat on dry, bare ground in late summer, and its seed-cases are hard burrs whose spines
point up however they fall. You cannot put your hand down on it, which is what 據于蒺藜 (*jù yú jí lí*,
"leaning on caltrops") describes.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 困：亨，貞大人吉，无咎，有言不信 (*kùn: hēng, zhēn dàrén jí, wú jiù, yǒu yán bù xìn*) — "Hemmed
in: it gets through. Holding firm, the great person: auspicious, no blame. There are words, and they
are not trusted." The good verdict comes before any line is read. But it is for the great person,
and it does not come by speaking.

**Line 1** sinks. 臀困于株木，入于幽谷，三歲不覿 (*tún kùn yú zhū mù, rù yú yōu gǔ, sān suì bù dí*, "the buttocks
hemmed in on a tree stump; entering a dark valley; three years without being seen"). 株 (*zhū*) is 說文
木根也 (*mù gēn yě*, "a tree's root end"). Sitting on what is left of a tree, then going lower still,
out of sight. The first line does as water does, and has no verdict.

**Line 2** is hemmed in by plenty. 困于酒食，朱紱方來，利用亨祀，征凶，无咎 (*kùn yú jiǔ shí, zhū fú fāng lái, lì yòng
xiǎng sì, zhēng xiōng, wú jiù*, "hemmed in by wine and food; the red sash is just arriving;
advantageous to make offering; setting out: ominous; no blame"). The red sash (紱, *fú*, the
knee-cover worn in office) is on its way — honour, which is also a hold. Here 亨 (*xiǎng*) stands for
享 (*xiǎng*, "to offer"), as 王弼's text writes it. The firm centre within is told: stay, and offer; do
not go.

**Line 3** has nothing to hold. 困于石，據于蒺藜，入于其宮，不見其妻，凶 (*kùn yú shí, jù yú jí lí, rù yú qí gōng, bù
jiàn qí qī, xiōng*, "hemmed in by stone; leaning on caltrops; entering one's house, not seeing one's
wife: ominous"). Hard in front, thorns under the hand, and the house empty. Soft in a firm place, at
the seam between water and lake: the one line ominous outright.

**Line 4** is slow. 來徐徐，困于金車，吝，有終 (*lái xú xú, kùn yú jīn chē, lìn, yǒu zhōng*, "coming slowly,
slowly; hemmed in by a bronze carriage: stinting; there is an end"). 徐 (*xú*) is in 說文 安行也 (*ān xíng
yě*, "to walk at ease"). The slowness is not the failure; the line reaches an end.

**Line 5** is the ruler's place, and the worst of it. 劓刖，困于赤紱，乃徐有說，利用祭祀 (*yì yuè, kùn yú chì fú, nǎi
xú yǒu yuè, lì yòng jì sì*, "nose cut, feet cut; hemmed in by the crimson sash; then, slowly, there
is release; advantageous to make sacrifice"). The mutilating punishments, at the firm centre of the
lake — and there, slowly, the lake's own action, 說 (*yuè*, "release"). The word 徐 (*xú*, "slowly")
comes twice in the figure, and both times it is how the pressure gives.

**Line 6** is at the tottering top. 困于葛藟，于臲卼，曰動悔。有悔，征吉 (*kùn yú gé lěi, yú niè wù, yuē dòng huǐ. yǒu
huǐ, zhēng jí*, "hemmed in by creeping vines, on a tottering height; it says: moving brings regret.
There is regret — setting out: auspicious"). The line quotes the fear, and answers it: have the
regret, then go. Line 2's setting out was ominous; line 6's is auspicious. Deep in the trouble,
stay; at its far end, move, regret and all.

### In a life now

A job that pays well and will not let you go. A debt. An injury that does not heal. A marriage gone
silent — line 3's house with no one in it. A reputation, and no one listening. The first counsel is
about speech: in this time explaining does not help; the more is said, the less it is believed (the
judgment). The second is about pace: the lines that come through are the slow ones (lines 4 and 5).
Where plenty or position is the trap, do not grab at escape — make the offering and wait (line 2).
And when one has been hemmed in so long that every movement looks like a mistake, the last line says
to move. The 大象 turns it to practice: 君子以致命遂志 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhì mìng suì zhì*, "the noble one stakes
the allotted course to carry the aim through") — when there is no room, spend what one has been
given on what one means.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a graph for the end of talk, and part over what the centre is.** The Laozi's
chapter 5 ends: *"Speaking of this is a dead end. / Instead, hold to the center."* Its *dead end* is
窮 (*qióng*, "come to an end") — the graph of the 彖's 尚口乃窮 (*shàng kǒu nǎi qióng*, "to rely on the
mouth is to come to an end"). The echo passes the two-question test at the Wing layer; the core
text's 有言不信 (*yǒu yán bù xìn*, "there are words, and they are not trusted") meets chapter 81's
*"Trustworthy words are not pretty"* only thematically.

Both books say that words run out and the centre holds. **But the centres differ.** The Laozi's is
the empty middle of a bellows — *"Empty but never depleted"*. This figure's is 剛中 (*gāng zhōng*,
"the firm at the centre", 彖): a solid line covered over, which holds because it is firm. Nor does
the I Ching make *speak less* a rule; it says that in this situation speech is not believed. The
difference stands.

### Words kept

- 困 (*kùn*) — *Hemmed In*, the proposed name (not yet approved); *Pressed* survives; *Drained* fails the lines; *exhaustion* is kept for 窮
- 窮 (*qióng*) — *come to an end*; in the Laozi's ch 5, *dead end*
- 信 (*xìn*) — *trust*, never *faith*, never *sincerity* (locked)
- 命 (*mìng*) — *the allotted course*, never *fate*, never *destiny* (locked)
- 說 (*yuè*) — *release*, the lake's action (the sense named: not *delight*, not *speech*)
- 剛 (*gāng*) — *firm*
- 亨 (*hēng*) — *gets through*, working gloss (A6); at 47.2 it stands for 享 (*xiǎng*), *offering*
- 凶 · 吝 · 悔 · 征 (*xiōng · lìn · huǐ · zhēng*) — *ominous · stinting · regret · setting out*, working glosses (A4)
- 大人 · 君子 (*dàrén · jūnzǐ*) — *the great person · the noble one*
