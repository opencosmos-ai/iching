## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going
along). **Across** — thunder ↔ wind × sky ↔ earth.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 剛反，動而以順行 (*gāng fǎn, dòng ér yǐ shùn xíng*), "the firm
comes back; moving out, and travelling by going along." It sets the return in a cycle, 七日來復，天行也
(*qī rì lái fù, tiān xíng yě*), "in seven days it comes back: that is the sky's going," and asks
復其見天地之心乎 (*fù qí jiàn tiān dì zhī xīn hū*), "in return, do we not see the heart of sky and
earth?" The 大象傳: 雷在地中，復 (*léi zài dì zhōng, fù*), "thunder inside the earth: return," and
先王以至日閉關，商旅不行，后不省方 (*xiān wáng yǐ zhì rì bì guān, shāng lǚ bù xíng, hòu bù xǐng fāng*),
"on the solstice the former rulers shut the passes; merchants and travellers did not go, and the
lord did not inspect the regions." The tradition reads the first firm line reappearing at the
bottom, the turn of the year, and meets it with stillness.

**What the polarity implies.** 23's single firm line stood at the top with nothing over it. Here
the same line stands at the bottom with everything over it (`100000`). Stripped away above, it comes
back below. It returns to the opposite end of the figure, not to where it was, which is what 序卦
says: 剝窮上反下 (*bō qióng shàng fǎn xià*), "stripping runs out at the top and turns back to the
bottom." The fruit of 23.6 has fallen and is in the ground. Of thunder, 說卦 ch 11 says 其於稼也，為反生
(*qí yú jià yě, wéi fǎn shēng*), "in grain, it is growing back." Thunder inside earth is a moving-out
that has not yet moved anything: force at the base, with five yielding lines that go along with it
instead of resisting. That is why the 大象 answers it by shutting the passes. The structure suggests
return is a change of position rather than a change of direction. What was the lid becomes the
root, and a root needs quiet. The lines then grade the returns by distance from that root. The
first, the firm line itself, is 不復遠 (*bù fù yuǎn*), "the return not far," and it has the book's
best fortune. The top, the line farthest from it, is 迷復 (*mí fù*), "a lost return," and it ends
in disaster. How far a line is from the bottom is how far it is from the return.

**For the name.** **Return**. The signature supports and deepens it: this is the firm line coming
home to the root by way of the far end. It agrees with the parent project's 復命, "returning to what
is given." **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Coming Back* survives the lines (休復, *xiū fù*,
"a resting coming-back"; 頻復, *pín fù*, "a repeated coming-back"; 中行獨復, *zhōng xíng dú fù*,
"walking in the middle, coming back alone"), plainer and weaker. *Regrowth*, taken from 反生, fails
the lines: 迷復, "a lost regrowth," and 獨復, "regrowing alone," turn a homecoming into sprouting,
and 3 屯 already claims sprouting.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation. The
formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets through"), 孚
("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on them
([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Coming back by the road you went out on — and the sooner you turn, the better.** The Shuowen, the
c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 復 ("Return") as 往來也 ("going and coming"), built on 彳 ("step"),
the walking stride. It is the plainest word for a walk home. The core text uses it nine times, the
name included, and every one of the six lines is a kind of coming back: from not far, at rest, again
and again, alone, steadily, and lost. The judgment opens on its ordinary rhythm — 出入无疾 ("going out
and coming in without sickness"). This hexagram grades returns, and says which ones fail.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, earth without. The one firm line stands at the very bottom, force at the base, and
the five lines above it are all soft: nothing resists it, and nothing has yet been moved. The Tuan,
the Wing on the judgment, says 剛反，動而以順行 ("the firm comes back; it moves out, and travels by going
along"). In 23 the firm line was a lid with nothing under it; here it is a root with everything over
it — back at the other end of the figure, small and low, and in need of quiet.

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, has 雷在地中 ("thunder inside the earth"), and it sets
the figure at 至日 ("the solstice day"). It does not say which solstice; one firm line under five soft
ones has always been read as midwinter. Thunder is almost never heard then — storms need warm, wet
air rising from the ground. The return of the light is as quiet. Near the winter solstice the day
lengthens by seconds, and unevenly: at mid-northern latitudes the earliest sunset falls in early
December and the latest sunrise in early January. The evenings lengthen while the mornings still
darken. The turn has happened before anyone can see it.

The Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams, ch 11 says of thunder 其於稼也，為反生 ("in grain, it is growing
back") — winter wheat, whose roots go on growing under frost with nothing to show above. And line
2's 休 ("rest") is the Shuowen's 从人依木 ("a person leaning on a tree").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 復：亨。出入无疾，朋來无咎。反復其道，七日來復，利有攸往 ("Return *(proposed)*: gets through. Going out and
coming in without sickness; friends come — no blame. Back and forth along its road; in seven days it
comes back. Advantageous to have somewhere to go.") 道 ("road") here is a road, the one walked out
and walked back. Seven days is near in time, as line 1 is near in place; and the return ends by
setting out.

**Line 1** is the firm line itself. 不復遠，无袛悔，元吉 ("coming back from not far; it does not come to
regret — from the source, auspicious"). Wang Bi: 幾悔而反 ("on the edge of regret, it turns back"). The
best fortune in the figure goes to the return made before the road was long.

**Line 2** rests. 休復，吉 ("a return at rest — auspicious"). It leans on the firm line just below; the
Small Image, the Wing on each line, has 以下仁也 ("by deferring to humaneness").

**Line 3**, at the top of the thunder, keeps turning. 頻復，厲无咎 ("returning again and again — peril, no
blame"). Wang Bi reads 頻 ("again and again") as 頻頻蹙之貌 ("the look of a frowning brow"). Anxious,
unsteady — and not blamed.

**Line 4**, in the middle of the five soft lines and the only one that answers line 1, has no
verdict at all. 中行獨復 ("walking in the middle, coming back alone"). The others go on; it turns.

**Line 5**, the centre above, is solid. 敦復，无悔 ("a steady return — no regret"). Wang Bi reads it as
居厚 ("dwelling in thickness"). Far from the root, it comes back by weight, not nearness, and earns
less than line 2.

**Line 6** is farthest, and lost. 迷復，凶，有災眚 ("a lost return — ominous; disaster from without and
blight from within"). 眚 ("blight") is the Shuowen's 目病生翳也 ("an eye disease, a film growing over
it"). Then: 用行師，終有大敗 ("if it sets troops marching, in the end a great defeat"), reaching the head of
the state, and 至于十年，不克征 ("for ten years, no march possible"). Seven days for the near return; ten
years for the forced one.

### In a life now

This is the figure for coming back: to health after an illness, to a practice dropped, to sobriety,
to a friendship after a quarrel, home after a long absence. It is precise about how. Turn early,
before the road is long (line 1). Lean on what is already sound (line 2). If the return is made
again and again, anxiously, that is peril and no blame (line 3). Come back even if no one near you
does (line 4). And do not force a return from far off: the lost return that sends out an army is the
worst line in the figure.

The Great Image closes on stillness as practice: 先王以至日閉關，商旅不行，后不省方 ("at the solstice the former
rulers shut the passes; merchants and travellers did not go, and the lord did not inspect the
regions"). When something has just come back, do not load it.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share this graph, and the English answers to it in both.** Chapter 16 of the Laozi:
*"The countless things arise together, / Relying upon this, I observe their return. / Things come to
their fullest, / Each returns again to its root. / Returning to the root is called stillness."* The
word is 復 ("return") — 觀復 ("observe their return") — the same graph that stands in every line of
24's core text, and *return* renders it in both. The Wings meet it too: the Tuan asks 復其見天地之心乎 ("in
return, do we not see the heart of sky and earth?"), and Wang Bi, reading the two books together,
answers with the Laozi's stillness: 復者反本之謂也 ("return means turning back to the root"), and 動息則靜
("when moving rests, there is stillness"). That is his reading, not the core's.

**Where they part.** The Laozi's return is universal — *each* returns — and it ends in stillness, to
be observed. The core text's is walked by one person, graded by distance and promptness, and it can
fail: 迷復 ("a lost return") ends in rout. Nor does it end at the root: 利有攸往 ("advantageous to have
somewhere to go") — the core's return comes back in order to go out again.

**And one parallel that does not hold.** 24's judgment has 反復其道 ("back and forth along its road");
the Laozi's chapter 40 opens *"Returning is the Tao's movement"*, with 反 ("returning") and 道 ("the
Tao") both present. But in the core text 道 ("road") is a road
([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 1), and the English here
must say *road*. The graphs match; the words do not.

### Words kept

- 復 ("Return") — the proposed name (not yet approved); *come back* where a verb reads better
- 道 ("road") — in the core text, at 反復其道 ("back and forth along its road"), never *the Tao* here;
  *the Tao* only in the Wings
- 天地 ("sky and earth") — never *heaven and earth* (locked); 心 ("heart") — never *mind* (locked)
- 仁 ("humaneness") — locked
- 靜 ("stillness") — Wang Bi's word, not the core's
- 元吉 ("from the source, auspicious") · 吉 ("auspicious") · 凶 ("ominous") · 无咎 ("no blame") · 悔
  ("regret") · 厲 ("peril") — working glosses (A4, A6)
