## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **Water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **Wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Across** — water ↔ fire and thunder ↔ wind, yang within and yin without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 (*dà xiàng*): 風行水上，渙；先王以享于帝立廟 (*fēng xíng shuǐ shàng, huàn; xiān wáng yǐ xiǎng yú dì lì miào*), "wind moves over the water: 渙; the former rulers made offerings to the god (帝, *dì*) and set up temples." The 彖 (*tuàn*): 剛來而不窮 (*gāng lái ér bù qióng*), "the firm comes and does not run out", and 利涉大川，乘木有功也 (*lì shè dà chuān, chéng mù yǒu gōng yě*), "it is favourable to cross the great river: riding on wood gets results" — 說卦 (*shuōguà*) makes wind wood, 巽為木 (*xùn wéi mù*). The tradition reads a dispersal answered twice: by gathering at the temple, and by crossing on a boat.

**What the polarity implies.** Water within is firm at its centre and sinks in: it is what has settled, pooled, gone down into the pit. Wind without is open at its base and enters from above, so the figure lays the wind's open underside straight onto the water. Wind does not stop water; it breaks the surface, drives it apart in ripple and spray, and dries what was wet. That is the 渙 of the lines — what had pooled or set is loosened: 渙汗其大號 (*huàn hàn qí dà hào*), "his great cry pours out like sweat"; 渙其群 (*huàn qí qún*), "he scatters his flock", the figure's best omen; 渙其血，去逖出 (*huàn qí xuè, qù tì chū*), "he scatters his blood; goes, keeps far, gets out." Water is the danger trigram (陷, *xiàn*, "sinking in"), and the structure suggests that 渙 is how danger is left: not climbed out of but lifted off from above, as wind lifts water from a pool. Because the pole within is yang, what gets scattered has a firm core — a crowd, a clot, a hoard — and the 彖's 剛來而不窮 says the firm survives it. The form breaks; the substance goes on.

**For the name.** *Scattering* — the signature supports it and says why it is transitive and why it is good. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Dissolving* — survives: 渙其群 "dissolving his flock", 渙奔其机 (*huàn bēn qí jī*) "in the dissolving, he runs to his bench", though it softens 渙其血, which is not gentle; *Thawing* — fails the lines: "thawing his flock" (渙其群) and "thawing his blood" (渙其血) make the water image the name and lose the verb's object.
