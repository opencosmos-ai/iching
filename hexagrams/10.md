## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake under sky ↔ earth.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) reads the name as a relation between two kinds of
line — 柔履剛也 (*róu lǚ gāng yě*, "the yielding treads on the firm") — and says why the treader
survives: 說而應乎乾 (*yuè ér yìng hū qián*, "releasing, it answers 乾, the sky"), and so 履虎尾，
不咥人 (*lǚ hǔ wěi, bù dié rén*, "treads on the tiger's tail; it does not bite"). The 大象 (*dà
xiàng*) takes the figure as the plainest statement of rank, 上天下澤 (*shàng tiān xià zé*, "sky
above, lake below"), and the noble one 以辨上下，定民志 (*yǐ biàn shàng xià, dìng mín zhì*, "tells
above from below, and settles the people's aims").

**What the polarity implies.** 10 is 9 turned over, and the same fact shows from the other side.
Lake is sky with its top line opened, and it meets the sky with exactly that line: the figure's one
yielding line is line 3, the sole of the foot on the tiger. Everything above it is firm, and nothing
in the figure could shift it. What the lake has is an open face turned upward — 說 (*yuè*,
releasing, speech, delight), a mouth under a weight. The treader is almost what it treads on,
differing only at the surface that touches. The structure says the tread is safe while that surface
stays open, and the lines agree at the exact place where it pretends to be closed: line 3, 眇能視，
跛能履 (*miǎo néng shì, bǒ néng lǚ*, "the one-eyed can see, the lame can tread"), then 履虎尾，咥人，凶
(*lǚ hǔ wěi, dié rén, xiōng*, "treads on the tiger's tail; it bites; ominous"), and 武人為于大君
(*wǔ rén wéi yú dà jūn*, "a warrior acting as a great lord"). The bite falls on the lake's own line.
Against 9: there the soft line holds the firm from above; here it walks beneath it. The same single
open line, as a cover and as a footing.

**For the name.** **Treading** — supports: the figure is a soft surface in contact with a hard one
from below, which is what the word says; the signature adds that the tread keeps contact without
contest. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Footing* — survives: 履道坦坦 (*lǚ dào tǎn tǎn*,
"treading the path, level and smooth", 10.2) and 視履考祥 (*shì lǚ kǎo xiáng*, "look at the
treading, examine the signs", 10.6) both read as footing; *Stepping Softly* — fails the lines: 夬履
(*guài lǚ*, "decisive treading", 10.5) is a hard step, and the line counts it 貞厲 (*zhēn lì*, "firm,
and in peril"), not wrong in kind.
