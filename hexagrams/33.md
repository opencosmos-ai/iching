## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 *gèn* · yang · stopping). Without: **sky** (乾 *qián* · yang · vigour).
**Across** — the yang pole of mountain ↔ lake within, the yang pole of sky ↔ earth without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 reads a picture — 天下有山 (*tiān xià yǒu shān*, "under the sky
there is a mountain") — and draws a conduct from it: 君子以遠小人，不惡而嚴 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yuǎn xiǎorén, bù
wù ér yán*, "the noble one keeps the small people at a distance — not with hatred, but with
strictness"). The 彖 reads the moment: 剛當位而應，與時行也 (*gāng dāng wèi ér yìng, yǔ shí xíng yě*, "the
firm is in its place and is answered; it moves with the time"), while the yielding lines below 浸而長也
(*jìn ér zhǎng yě*, "soak in and grow"). For the tradition this is withdrawal as timing, not defeat:
遯之時義大矣哉 (*dùn zhī shí yì dà yǐ zāi*, "great indeed is the timeliness of withdrawal").

**What the polarity implies.** Two yang trigrams, and neither yields: this is not a weak figure
running. A mountain is the highest thing the earth can do, and the sky is still above it; however far
the mountain climbs, the sky is no nearer. That is this figure's distance — not a gap opened by flight
but one the higher thing keeps simply by being what it is. And the stopping is *inside*. The mountain is
closed at the top, its one firm line a lid, so what withdraws keeps its counsel and its ground; it does
not scatter. The structure suggests that 遯 (*dùn*, "withdrawal") is less a movement than a relation of
heights — vigour going on above, the self within stopping where it stands — and that is how 不惡而嚴
(*bù wù ér yán*, "not hating, but strict") arrives from the figure: distance with no heat in it, the
way a summit is apart from the sky without resenting it.

The lines agree about where it goes wrong: at the tail, 遯尾，厲 (*dùn wěi, lì*, "withdrawal at the tail —
peril"), and when bound, 系遯 (*xì dùn*, "withdrawal while tied"). The good end is the unencumbered one,
肥遯 (*féi dùn*, "an ample withdrawal"). Turned over, the figure is 34, and 雜卦 crosses the pair:
大壯則止，遯則退也 (*dà zhuàng zé zhǐ, dùn zé tuì yě*, "great vigour means stopping; withdrawal means
retreating"). The figure with the mountain in it is the one that goes.

**For the name.** **Withdrawal** — the signature supports it and deepens it: a withdrawal that holds
within, distance kept by height rather than ground given up. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Receding*, *Keeping Distance* — *Receding* survives the lines: 遯尾，厲 (*dùn wěi, lì*, "receding at the
tail — peril"), 嘉遯 (*jiā dùn*, "a fine receding"), 肥遯 (*féi dùn*, "an ample receding"), though in
好遯君子吉 (*hào dùn jūnzǐ jí*, "the noble one fond of withdrawal — auspicious") it makes a chosen act
sound like something that happens; *Keeping Distance*, from the 大象's 遠 (*yuǎn*, "keep at a distance"),
fails the lines — 系遯 (*xì dùn*, "withdrawal while tied") and 遯尾 (*dùn wěi*, "withdrawal's tail") need a
movement, not a stance, and the word would have come from commentary.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi, the 3rd-century commentator, and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**To withdraw is to go while going is still clean — and the farther out it gets, the better it
goes.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 遯 ("Withdrawal") as 逃也 ("to flee"),
and its parts are a foot on the road, 辵 ("walking"), and a young pig, 豚 ("young pig"): the graph is
a piglet bolting. The core text names 遯 ("withdrawal") in five of its six lines and grades them by
where they stand — the tail in peril, the one tied in sickness, then fond, fine and ample withdrawal
as the lines climb. The one line that does not withdraw is the one held fast. This is not a hexagram
about defeat. It is about timing: leaving before one is the last thing out of the gate.

### The polarities in it

The mountain within stops; the sky without keeps its vigour going. So the figure does two things at
once — something stays put and something moves off — and the lines divide exactly along the seam.
The three lines of the mountain are the hard ones: the tail, the one bound in oxhide, the one tied.
The three lines of the sky are the easy ones, and each is easier than the last. **The stopping that
gives the mountain its steadiness is also what snags a withdrawal**; the sky's part goes clean
because nothing in it holds on. The signature above reads the distance as a relation of heights; the
lines add that it must still be walked, and that the first steps are where it catches.

### In nature

Watch animals leave a place. A herd or a flock moving off from trouble is strung out, and the danger
is at the back: the straggler is the one a predator takes. That is 遯尾 ("withdrawal at the tail"),
and the Shuowen glosses 尾 ("tail") as 微也 ("the slight end") — the thinnest part, last through the
gap.

The second line holds another fact of the farmyard: 黃牛之革 ("the hide of a yellow ox"), and the
Shuowen gives 革 ("hide") as a hide with the hair taken off. Rawhide lashed on wet shrinks as it
dries and grips harder than when it was tied; no one works it loose. In the core text yellow
attaches, six times in eight, to a centre line — 黃裳 ("a yellow skirt") at the fifth line of 2, 黃離
("yellow fire") at the second of 30 — and here it is again at a centre.

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, draws it as one picture: 天下有山 ("under the sky there
is a mountain"). Wang Bi hears a bird in the top line: 矰繳不能及 ("the corded arrow cannot reach it") —
a bird flown beyond the fowler's range, no longer worth the shot.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 遯：亨。小利貞 ("Withdrawal: it gets through. Small: advantageous to hold firm.") The
second phrase can be read two ways, and the core text does not choose: *a small advantage in holding
firm*, or *holding firm is advantageous for the small*. The Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, takes
the second and reads the small as the yielding lines below, 浸而長也 ("soaking in and growing") — the
reason withdrawal is timely at all.

**Line 1** is the tail. 遯尾，厲，勿用有攸往 ("withdrawal at the tail — peril; do not set out anywhere"). Too
late to leave cleanly, the line says: stay still. The Small Image, the Wing on each line, puts it
plainly, 不往何災也 ("if you do not go, what harm?").

**Line 2** is the line that does not withdraw. 執之用黃牛之革，莫之勝說 ("grasp it with yellow oxhide; no one
can work it loose"). 說 ("work loose") here is the releasing sense, the knot undone. Whether the
thing grasped is one's purpose or someone who would leave, the Small Image settles for the first:
固志也 ("the will made fast"). The mountain's centre does not move.

**Line 3** is at the seam, and caught on it. 系遯，有疾厲，畜臣妾吉 ("withdrawal while tied — sickness, peril;
keeping bondservants, men and women: auspicious"). Tied to what it should leave, this line cannot
go; it can still keep its household. The Shuowen's 畜 ("keeping") is livestock kept on the field. The
Small Image draws the line: 不可大事也 ("not fit for great affairs").

**Line 4** crosses into the sky. 好遯君子吉，小人否 ("fond of withdrawal — for the noble one, auspicious; for
small people, not so"). Wang Bi reads the difference as being tied: the noble one can let go of what
it answers to below; small people 繫戀 ("stay tied by longing").

**Line 5** is the centre of the sky. 嘉遯，貞吉 ("a fine withdrawal — holding firm, auspicious"). The
Shuowen gives 嘉 ("fine") as 美也 ("fine"), built on a drum: a leaving that could be celebrated.

**Line 6** is furthest out. 肥遯，无不利 ("an ample withdrawal — nothing not advantageous"). 肥 ("ample")
is flesh in plenty; the Small Image adds 无所疑也 ("nothing left to doubt"). Nothing ominous falls
anywhere in the figure: the worst is peril, and peril is at the bottom.

### In a life now

This is the figure for leaving a job before the new management has finished arriving, stepping out
of a movement as it is taken over, handing on a farm or a practice while one can still choose the
terms, letting a friendship go quiet before it curdles. Its counsel is almost entirely about
**when**. Go early and it is a fine withdrawal; go late and one is the tail, and then the text says
something harder — do not go at all, hold still until the moment passes. The third line is the
honest one for most people: tied by illness, by those who depend on them. The text does not blame
it. It says: keep the household well, and do not take on great affairs from there.

The Great Image gives the manner: 君子以遠小人，不惡而嚴 ("the noble one keeps small people at a distance — not
with hatred, but strictly"). Distance with no heat in it.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Thematic, through a Wing.** Chapter 9 ends: *"The work done, the body withdraws. This is the way
of nature."* The graph there is 退 ("withdraw"), and it reaches 遯 ("Withdrawal") only through
commentary — the Xugua, the Wing on the sequence, says 遯者退也 ("Withdrawal is withdrawing"), and the
Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams, the same. The core text never uses 退 ("withdraw") in this
hexagram, so this is an echo in the Wings' layer, not the Zhou's.

And the two withdrawals differ in a way that matters. The Laozi's comes **after** the work is done:
it is the modesty of success. 遯 ("Withdrawal")'s comes while the small are still growing and the
work cannot be finished: it is the judgment of timing under pressure. The Tuan calls that timing
great — 遯之時義大矣哉 ("great indeed is the timeliness of withdrawal") — and 時 ("the moment") is a word
the Laozi uses once.
