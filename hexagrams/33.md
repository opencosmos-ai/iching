## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 *gèn* · yang · stopping). Without: **sky** (乾 *qián* · yang · vigour).
**Across** — the yang pole of mountain ↔ lake within, the yang pole of sky ↔ earth without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 reads a picture — 天下有山 (*tiān xià yǒu shān*, "under the sky
there is a mountain") — and draws a conduct from it: 君子以遠小人，不惡而嚴 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yuǎn xiǎorén, bù
wù ér yán*, "the noble one keeps the small people at a distance — not with hatred, but with
strictness"). The 彖 reads the moment: 剛當位而應，與時行也 (*gāng dāng wèi ér yìng, yǔ shí xíng yě*, "the
firm is in its place and is answered; it moves with the time"), while the yielding lines below 浸而長也
(*jìn ér zhǎng yě*, "soak in and grow"). For the tradition this is withdrawal as timing, not defeat:
遯之時義大矣哉 (*dùn zhī shí yì dà yǐ zāi*, "great indeed is the timeliness of withdrawal").

**What the polarity implies.** Two yang trigrams, and neither yields: this is not a weak figure
running. A mountain is the highest thing the earth can do, and the sky is still above it; however far
the mountain climbs, the sky is no nearer. That is this figure's distance — not a gap opened by flight
but one the higher thing keeps simply by being what it is. And the stopping is *inside*. The mountain is
closed at the top, its one firm line a lid, so what withdraws keeps its counsel and its ground; it does
not scatter. The structure suggests that 遯 (*dùn*, "withdrawal") is less a movement than a relation of
heights — vigour going on above, the self within stopping where it stands — and that is how 不惡而嚴
(*bù wù ér yán*, "not hating, but strict") arrives from the figure: distance with no heat in it, the
way a summit is apart from the sky without resenting it.

The lines agree about where it goes wrong: at the tail, 遯尾，厲 (*dùn wěi, lì*, "withdrawal at the tail —
peril"), and when bound, 系遯 (*xì dùn*, "withdrawal while tied"). The good end is the unencumbered one,
肥遯 (*féi dùn*, "an ample withdrawal"). Turned over, the figure is 34, and 雜卦 crosses the pair:
大壯則止，遯則退也 (*dà zhuàng zé zhǐ, dùn zé tuì yě*, "great vigour means stopping; withdrawal means
retreating"). The figure with the mountain in it is the one that goes.

**For the name.** **Withdrawal** — the signature supports it and deepens it: a withdrawal that holds
within, distance kept by height rather than ground given up. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Receding*, *Keeping Distance* — *Receding* survives the lines: 遯尾，厲 (*dùn wěi, lì*, "receding at the
tail — peril"), 嘉遯 (*jiā dùn*, "a fine receding"), 肥遯 (*féi dùn*, "an ample receding"), though in
好遯君子吉 (*hào dùn jūnzǐ jí*, "the noble one fond of withdrawal — auspicious") it makes a chosen act
sound like something that happens; *Keeping Distance*, from the 大象's 遠 (*yuǎn*, "keep at a distance"),
fails the lines — 系遯 (*xì dùn*, "withdrawal while tied") and 遯尾 (*dùn wěi*, "withdrawal's tail") need a
movement, not a stance, and the word would have come from commentary.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**To withdraw is to go while going is still clean — and the farther out it gets, the better it
goes.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 遯 (*dùn*) as 逃也 (*táo yě*, "to flee"), and its parts are a foot on
the road, 辵 (*chuò*), and a young pig, 豚 (*tún*): the graph is a piglet bolting. The core text
names 遯 in five of its six lines and grades them by where they stand — the tail in peril, the one
tied in sickness, then fond, fine and ample withdrawal as the lines climb. The one line that does
not withdraw is the one held fast. This is not a hexagram about defeat. It is about timing: leaving
before one is the last thing out of the gate.

### The polarities in it

The mountain within stops; the sky without keeps its vigour going. So the figure does two things at
once — something stays put and something moves off — and the lines divide exactly along the seam.
The three lines of the mountain are the hard ones: the tail, the one bound in oxhide, the one tied.
The three lines of the sky are the easy ones, and each is easier than the last. **The stopping that
gives the mountain its steadiness is also what snags a withdrawal**; the sky's part goes clean
because nothing in it holds on. The signature above reads the distance as a relation of heights;
the lines add that it must still be walked, and that the first steps are where it catches.

### In nature

Watch animals leave a place. A herd or a flock moving off from trouble is strung out, and the
danger is at the back: the straggler is the one a predator takes. That is 遯尾 (*dùn wěi*,
"withdrawal at the tail"), and 說文 glosses 尾 (*wěi*, "tail") as 微也 (*wēi yě*, "the slight end") —
the thinnest part, last through the gap.

The second line holds another fact of the farmyard. 黃牛之革 (*huáng niú zhī gé*) is the hide of a
yellow ox, and 說文 gives 革 (*gé*) as a hide with the hair taken off. Rawhide lashed on wet shrinks
as it dries and grips harder than when it was tied; no one works it loose. In the core text yellow
attaches, six times in eight, to a centre line — 黃裳 (*huáng cháng*, "a yellow skirt") at the fifth line of
2, 黃離 (*huáng lí*, "yellow fire") at the second of 30 — and here it is again at a centre.

The 大象 draws the whole figure as one picture: 天下有山 (*tiān xià yǒu shān*, "under the sky
there is a mountain"). 王弼 hears a bird in the top line: 矰繳不能及 (*zēng
zhuó bù néng jí*, "the corded arrow cannot reach it") — a bird flown beyond the fowler's range, no
longer worth the shot.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 遯：亨。小利貞 (*dùn: hēng. xiǎo lì zhēn*) — "Withdrawal: it gets through. Small:
advantageous to hold firm." The second phrase can be read two ways, and the core text does not
choose: *a small advantage in holding firm*, or *holding firm is advantageous for the small*. The
彖 takes the second and reads the small as the yielding lines below, 浸而長也 (*jìn ér zhǎng yě*,
"soaking in and growing") — the reason withdrawal is timely at all.

**Line 1** is the tail. 遯尾，厲，勿用有攸往 (*dùn wěi, lì, wù yòng yǒu yōu wǎng*, "withdrawal at the
tail — peril; do not set out anywhere"). Too late to leave cleanly, the line says: stay still. The
小象 puts it plainly, 不往何災也 (*bù wǎng hé zāi yě*, "if you do not go, what harm?").

**Line 2** is the line that does not withdraw. 執之用黃牛之革，莫之勝說 (*zhí zhī yòng huáng niú zhī gé,
mò zhī shèng tuō*, "grasp it with yellow oxhide; no one can work it loose"). 說 (*tuō*) here is the
releasing sense, the knot undone. Whether the thing grasped is one's purpose or someone who would
leave, the 小象 settles for the first: 固志也 (*gù zhì yě*, "the will made fast"). The mountain's
centre does not move.

**Line 3** is at the seam, and caught on it. 系遯，有疾厲，畜臣妾吉 (*xì dùn, yǒu jí lì, xù chén qiè
jí*, "withdrawal while tied — sickness, peril; keeping bondservants, men and women: auspicious").
Tied to what it should leave, this line cannot go; it can still keep its household. 說文's 畜 (*xù*)
is livestock kept on the field. The 小象 draws the line: 不可大事也 (*bù kě dà shì yě*, "not fit for
great affairs").

**Line 4** crosses into the sky. 好遯君子吉，小人否 (*hào dùn jūnzǐ jí, xiǎorén pǐ*, "fond of
withdrawal — for the noble one, auspicious; for small people, not so"). 王弼 reads the difference as
being tied: the noble one can let go of what it answers to below; small people 繫戀 (*xì liàn*,
"stay tied by longing").

**Line 5** is the centre of the sky. 嘉遯，貞吉 (*jiā dùn, zhēn jí*, "a fine withdrawal — holding
firm, auspicious"). 說文 gives 嘉 (*jiā*) as 美也 (*měi yě*, "fine"), built on a drum: a leaving that
could be celebrated.

**Line 6** is furthest out. 肥遯，无不利 (*féi dùn, wú bù lì*, "an ample withdrawal — nothing not
advantageous"). 肥 (*féi*) is flesh in plenty; the 小象 adds 无所疑也 (*wú suǒ yí yě*, "nothing left
to doubt"). Nothing ominous falls anywhere in the figure: the worst is peril, and peril is at the
bottom.

### In a life now

This is the figure for leaving a job before the new management has finished arriving, stepping out
of a movement as it is taken over, handing on a farm or a practice while one can still choose the
terms, letting a friendship go quiet before it curdles. Its counsel is almost entirely about
**when**. Go early and it is a fine withdrawal; go late and one is the tail, and then the text says
something harder — do not go at all, hold still until the moment passes. The third line is the
honest one for most people: tied by illness, by those who depend on them. The text does not blame
it. It says: keep the household well, and do not take on great affairs from there.

The 大象 gives the manner: 君子以遠小人，不惡而嚴 (*jūnzǐ yǐ yuǎn xiǎorén, bù wù ér yán*, "the noble
one keeps small people at a distance — not with hatred, but strictly"). Distance with no heat in it.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Thematic, through a Wing.** Chapter 9 ends: *"The work done, the body withdraws. This is the way
of nature."* The graph there is 退 (*tuì*, "withdraw"), and it reaches 遯 only through commentary —
序卦 (*xùguà*) says 遯者退也 (*dùn zhě tuì yě*, "遯 is withdrawing"), and 雜卦 (*záguà*) the same.
The core text never uses 退 in this hexagram, so this is an echo in the Wings' layer, not the
Zhou's.

And the two withdrawals differ in a way that matters. The Laozi's comes **after** the work is done:
it is the modesty of success. 遯's comes while the small are still growing and the work cannot be
finished: it is the judgment of timing under pressure. The 彖 calls that timing great — 遯之時義大矣哉
(*dùn zhī shí yì dà yǐ zāi*, "great indeed is the timeliness of withdrawal") — and 時 (*shí*, "the
moment") is a word the Laozi uses once.

### Words kept

- 遯 (*dùn*) — *withdrawal*, the proposed name (not yet approved); *retreat* is left for 退 (*tuì*)
- 執 (*zhí*) — *grasp*, never *cling* (locked)
- 說 (*tuō*) — the releasing sense, *work loose*, here (this project's settled word, sense named)
- 大 (*dà*) — *great*: 不可大事 (*bù kě dà shì*), "not fit for great affairs"; 事 (*shì*) is *affairs* (locked)
- 天下有山 (*tiān xià yǒu shān*) — *under the sky there is a mountain*: 天 as a tier, *sky* (locked); 天 and 下 are two words here, not the compound 天下, *the world*
