## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Across** — water ↔ fire × mountain ↔ lake.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 柔來而文剛 (*róu lái ér wén gāng*), "the yielding comes and
patterns the firm," and 文明以止，人文也 (*wén míng yǐ zhǐ, rén wén yě*), "pattern and clarity,
held: that is the pattern of people." The 大象傳: 山下有火 (*shān xià yǒu huǒ*), "below the mountain
there is fire," and 君子以明庶政，无敢折獄 (*jūnzǐ yǐ míng shù zhèng, wú gǎn zhé yù*), "the noble one
makes the many matters of government clear, and does not venture to decide lawsuits." The tradition
reads adornment as pattern laid over what is firm: culture, with a limit set on what it may do.

**What the polarity implies.** Fire at the foot of a mountain lights the slope from below. It shows
the mountain and does not move it. Fire's hollow is at its middle, and the mountain's firm line is
at its top: an inner opening that attaches, under an outer closure that holds. Adornment is exactly
this, a surface catching light against something that stays. The 大象's limit follows from the
structure. Light is enough for ordinary business but not for lawsuits, because adornment can make
things clear but cannot decide them; deciding needs the thunder that 21 has and 22 lacks. 22 is 21
turned over. The jaw is upside down, the bite's thunder has gone, and the fire has sunk under a
mountain. Where 21 shackles the foot and cuts at the ear, 22 adorns the toes and the beard (賁其趾,
*bì qí zhǐ*; 賁其須, *bì qí xū*). The mountain, stopping at the top, finishes the figure's own
argument. The top line is 白賁 (*bái bì*, "white adornment"), and the 雜卦 has 賁无色也 (*bì wú sè
yě*, "賁 is without colour"). The structure suggests adornment held to its height ends uncoloured:
the fire goes out on the summit, and the pattern turns plain. 序卦 sends it straight to 23: once
ornament is carried to the end, it is used up (則盡矣, *zé jìn yǐ*, "then it is exhausted").

**For the name.** **Adornment**. The signature deepens it, because this is adornment held from
above and ending in white. The name has to survive 白賁, "white adornment," and it does, since the
phrase needs a noun that can be plain. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Pattern* survives the
lines (賁其趾, "pattern his toes"; 白賁, "white pattern"), but it is the 彖傳's 文 (*wén*, "pattern")
and not 賁, which is commentary supplying the word. *Glow* fails the lines: 賁其趾 and 賁其須 need a
transitive verb, and "glow his toes" is not English.
