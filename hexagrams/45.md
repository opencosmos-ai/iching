## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × mountain ↔ lake: both poles yin, a trigram with no odd line under one open
at the top.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 萃，聚也；順以說，剛中而應，故聚也 (*cuì, jù yě; shùn yǐ yuè,
gāng zhōng ér yìng, gù jù yě*, "*cuì* is gathering: going along and releasing, the firm central and
answered — so there is gathering"). It makes the gathering a way of seeing: 觀其所聚，而天地萬物之情可見
矣 (*guān qí suǒ jù, ér tiān dì wàn wù zhī qíng kě jiàn yǐ*, "look at where they gather, and the
nature of sky and earth and the countless things can be seen"). The 大象傳 hears a warning in it: 澤上
于地，萃；君子以除戎器，戒不虞 (*zé shàng yú dì, cuì; jūnzǐ yǐ chú róng qì, jiè bù yú*, "the lake above
the earth: *cuì*; the noble one puts the implements of war in order, to guard against the
unforeseen"). The tradition takes 萃 (*cuì*, "gathering") as people drawn together around a centre,
at a temple, and knows that a crowd needs guarding.

**What the polarity implies.** Two yin trigrams: nothing in this figure pushes. The earth below is all
yielding and the lake above is open at its top; the only firm lines in six are the lake's, side by
side in the fourth and fifth places. This is water where the ground has given way — a gathering that
happens not because anything summons it but because the land goes along and there is a hollow to
fill. The firm lines are what it collects around; the 彖傳's 剛中而應 (*gāng zhōng ér yìng*, "the firm
central and answered") is the fifth line, sitting in the lake's body.

The structure goes further. A lake open at the top is also a mouth, and the lines of this hexagram
are full of the mouth's sounds: 若號一握為笑 (*ruò háo yī wò wéi xiào*, "if there is crying out, one
handful turns to laughter"), 萃如，嗟如 (*cuì rú, jiē rú*, "gathering, sighing"), 齎咨涕洟 (*jī zī tì
yí*, "sighs and laments, tears and running nose"). A gathering is where people make noise together.
And open ground under an open top is exposed at both ends, which is why the 大象傳 reaches for the
implements of war. Turn the figure over and it is 46: what settles on the earth here rises through it
there — 萃聚，而升不來也 (*cuì jù, ér shēng bù lái yě*, "*cuì* gathers, and *shēng* does not come
back"), as the 雜卦 has it.

**For the name.** *Gathering* — the signature supports it, and adds that the gathering is drawn by a
hollow rather than a summons, and that it is loud. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Confluence*,
*Muster* — *Confluence* — water running together into the low place — fails the lines: 萃如，嗟如 (*cuì
rú, jiē rú*, "gathering, sighing") is a person, and water does not sigh; *Muster* survives: 乃亂乃萃
(*nǎi luàn nǎi cuì*, "now disorder, now muster") and 萃有位 (*cuì yǒu wèi*, "the muster has its
places") read, but it imports an army the core text never mentions.
