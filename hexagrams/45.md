## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × mountain ↔ lake: both poles yin, a trigram with no odd line under one open
at the top.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 萃，聚也；順以說，剛中而應，故聚也 (*cuì, jù yě; shùn yǐ yuè,
gāng zhōng ér yìng, gù jù yě*, "*cuì* is gathering: going along and releasing, the firm central and
answered — so there is gathering"). It makes the gathering a way of seeing: 觀其所聚，而天地萬物之情可見
矣 (*guān qí suǒ jù, ér tiān dì wàn wù zhī qíng kě jiàn yǐ*, "look at where they gather, and the
nature of sky and earth and the countless things can be seen"). The 大象傳 hears a warning in it: 澤上
于地，萃；君子以除戎器，戒不虞 (*zé shàng yú dì, cuì; jūnzǐ yǐ chú róng qì, jiè bù yú*, "the lake above
the earth: *cuì*; the noble one puts the implements of war in order, to guard against the
unforeseen"). The tradition takes 萃 (*cuì*, "gathering") as people drawn together around a centre,
at a temple, and knows that a crowd needs guarding.

**What the polarity implies.** Two yin trigrams: nothing in this figure pushes. The earth below is all
yielding and the lake above is open at its top; the only firm lines in six are the lake's, side by
side in the fourth and fifth places. This is water where the ground has given way — a gathering that
happens not because anything summons it but because the land goes along and there is a hollow to
fill. The firm lines are what it collects around; the 彖傳's 剛中而應 (*gāng zhōng ér yìng*, "the firm
central and answered") is the fifth line, sitting in the lake's body.

The structure goes further. A lake open at the top is also a mouth, and the lines of this hexagram
are full of the mouth's sounds: 若號一握為笑 (*ruò háo yī wò wéi xiào*, "if there is crying out, one
handful turns to laughter"), 萃如，嗟如 (*cuì rú, jiē rú*, "gathering, sighing"), 齎咨涕洟 (*jī zī tì
yí*, "sighs and laments, tears and running nose"). A gathering is where people make noise together.
And open ground under an open top is exposed at both ends, which is why the 大象傳 reaches for the
implements of war. Turn the figure over and it is 46: what settles on the earth here rises through it
there — 萃聚，而升不來也 (*cuì jù, ér shēng bù lái yě*, "*cuì* gathers, and *shēng* does not come
back"), as the 雜卦 has it.

**For the name.** *Gathering* — the signature supports it, and adds that the gathering is drawn by a
hollow rather than a summons, and that it is loud. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Confluence*,
*Muster* — *Confluence* — water running together into the low place — fails the lines: 萃如，嗟如 (*cuì
rú, jiē rú*, "gathering, sighing") is a person, and water does not sigh; *Muster* survives: 乃亂乃萃
(*nǎi luàn nǎi cuì*, "now disorder, now muster") and 萃有位 (*cuì yǒu wèi*, "the muster has its
places") read, but it imports an army the core text never mentions.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A gathering is held together by confidence, and confidence comes and goes.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives
萃 (*cuì*, "gathering") as 艸皃 (*cǎo mào*, "the look of grass") — grass standing thick, many stems
from one ground. The core text gathers people at one place, the ancestral temple: 王假有廟 (*wáng gé yǒu
miào*, "the ruler arrives at the temple"). Then it tells the inside of the gathering in two ways.
Three times it turns on 孚 (*fú*, "confidence") — lost in line 1, enough in line 2, missing in line
5. And it is full of sound: a cry, laughter, a sigh, weeping. What holds a crowd is not the temple's
walls but confidence in what it has gathered around, and the text never pretends that is always
there.

### The polarities in it

Earth within, lake without: all going along below, open at the top above (see *The signature*,
above). Nothing in the figure summons; the ground yields, there is a hollow, and things collect in
it, around the only firm lines, the fourth and fifth. **But the text will not let the centre take
its standing for granted.** The fourth line's great fortune comes with the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*, the
line commentary) noting 位不當也 (*wèi bù dāng yě*, "the place is not fitting"), and the fifth — the
centre itself — is told 匪孚 (*fěi fú*, "there is no confidence") and must earn it by 元永貞 (*yuán yǒng
zhēn*, "from the source, holding firm for a long time"). The structure supplies a centre; the lines
say a centre is gathered around only for as long as it keeps holding firm.

### In nature

澤上于地 (*zé shàng yú dì*, "the lake above the earth", 大象) is water standing on ground — not a lake
held in a basin of rock but rain lying where the land is lowest, a seasonal marsh, a flood meadow.
Living things come together there: in a dry season, at dusk, the animals of a whole district arrive
at the one pool. Watch how they drink. A flock of geese grazing a wet field keeps a few heads up
while the rest feed; deer at a pond lift their heads between mouthfuls. Where many gather, a
predator waits, and every gathering animal behaves as if it knows.

The 大象 draws exactly this conclusion: 君子以除戎器，戒不虞 (*jūnzǐ yǐ chú róng qì, jiè bù yú*, "the noble one
puts the tools of war in order, to guard against the unforeseen"). 王弼 gives the reason: 聚而无防則衆生心
(*jù ér wú fáng zé zhòng shēng xīn*, "gathered and unguarded, the crowd's heart brings forth designs
of its own"). 說文's grass is there too, in any wet meadow: tussock sedge, its stems in dense clumps,
each held up by the others, the clump raised a little above the water that feeds it.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 萃：亨。王假有廟，利見大人，亨。利貞。用大牲吉，利有攸往 (*cuì: hēng. wáng gé yǒu miào, lì jiàn dàrén, hēng.
lì zhēn. yòng dà shēng jí, lì yǒu yōu wǎng*) — "Gathering: it gets through. The ruler arrives at the
temple; it is advantageous to see the great person; it gets through; it is advantageous to hold
firm. Using the great offering animal: auspicious; it is advantageous to have somewhere to go." The
temple, 廟 (*miào*), is in 說文 尊先祖皃也 (*zūn xiānzǔ mào yě*, "the look of honouring the ancestors"): the
gathering is at the place of the dead.

**Line 1** is a crowd in its first hour. 有孚不終，乃亂乃萃，若號一握為笑，勿恤，往无咎 (*yǒu fú bù zhōng, nǎi luàn nǎi
cuì, ruò háo yī wò wéi xiào, wù xù, wǎng wú jiù*, "there is confidence, but it does not last to the
end: now disorder, now gathering. If there is a cry, one grip turns it to laughter. Do not worry;
going on, no blame"). 一握 (*yī wò*, "one grip") may be a clasp of hands; 王弼 reads it as smallness
itself. Either way, what turns the cry is small.

**Line 2** is drawn in. 引吉，无咎，孚乃利用禴 (*yǐn jí, wú jiù, fú nǎi lì yòng yuè*, "drawn in: auspicious, no
blame. With confidence, it is advantageous to use the spring offering"). 禴 (*yuè*, "the spring
offering") is, for 王弼, 四時祭之省者也 (*sì shí jì zhī shěng zhě yě*, "the plainest of the four seasons'
offerings"). Set against the judgment's whole ox, the soft centre of the earth says: with
confidence, the plainest offering is enough. 46's second line repeats it word for word.

**Line 3** arrives and cannot join. 萃如，嗟如，无攸利，往无咎，小吝 (*cuì rú, jiē rú, wú yōu lì, wǎng wú jiù, xiǎo
lìn*, "gathering, sighing; nothing advantageous; going on, no blame; a little stinting"). At the
seam between the trigrams, in the crowd and not of it — and still told to go on.

**Line 4** is the shortest line. 大吉，无咎 (*dà jí, wú jiù*, "greatly auspicious; no blame"). The first
firm line, beside the centre but not in it: great fortune, and only then no blame.

**Line 5** is the centre, and its problem. 萃有位，无咎。匪孚，元永貞，悔亡 (*cuì yǒu wèi, wú jiù. fěi fú, yuán yǒng
zhēn, huǐ wáng*, "the gathering has its places; no blame. Where there is no confidence: from the
source, holding firm for a long time — regret goes"). 位 (*wèi*) in 說文 is 列中庭之左右謂之位 (*liè zhōng tíng
zhī zuǒ yòu wèi zhī wèi*, "to stand in rows to left and right of the central court is called 位") — a
court in session. The order can be arranged; the confidence cannot.

**Line 6** weeps. 齎咨涕洟，无咎 (*jī zī tì yí, wú jiù*, "sighs and laments, tears and a running nose: no
blame"). The line is exact about grief — the nose runs too — and at the ancestors' temple, grief is
in its place.

**Every one of the six lines carries 无咎 (*wú jiù*, "no blame"), and none is ominous.** Disorder,
sighing, stinting, tears: none of it is held against anyone. A gathering is allowed to be loud.

### In a life now

A funeral. A family at a holiday table after a bad year. A congregation, a union meeting, a
festival, a team after a merger. The figure grants that a gathering needs a place and a centre, then
says its binding is confidence — which wavers in the first hour (line 1), is shown in small things,
one clasped hand, the plain offering rather than the lavish one (lines 1 and 2), and cannot be
commanded even from the centre, only earned over time (line 5). To the one who stands at the edge
sighing it says: go on anyway (line 3). It does not count tears against a gathering (line 6). The 大象
adds the sober half: where many are gathered, keep the tools in order — stewards at the gate, a plan
for the crush at the door — because the unforeseen comes to crowds.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a graph and a person here, and part over what the person does.** The Laozi's
chapter 31 begins: *"Fine weapons are tools of ill omen,"* and goes on: *"Weapons are tools of ill
omen, not the noble's tools. / Used only when unavoidable, calm detachment is best."* The graph is 器
(*qì*, "vessel, tool"), the one the 大象 uses in 戎器 (*róng qì*, "the tools of war"), and both speak of
君子 (*jūnzǐ*, "the noble one"). The echo passes the two-question test at the Wing layer only: the
core text of 萃 (*cuì*, "gathering") never mentions weapons. Both keep weapons for what cannot be
avoided or foreseen. But the 大象's noble one keeps them in order, ready; the Laozi's does not dwell
among them at all. The difference stands.
