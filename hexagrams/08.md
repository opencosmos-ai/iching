## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking
in). **Across** — sky ↔ earth meets water ↔ fire, yin within and yang without, no odd line within and
the odd line at the middle without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 比，輔也，下順從也 (*bǐ, fǔ yě, xià shùn cóng yě*),
"比 (*bǐ*) is support: those below go along and follow"; and 不寧方來，上下應也 (*bù níng fāng lái,
shàng xià yìng yě*), "the unsettled come from every side: above and below answer each other." The
大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image): 地上有水，比；先王以建萬國，親諸侯 (*dì shàng yǒu shuǐ, bǐ; xiān
wáng yǐ jiàn wàn guó, qīn zhū hóu*), "on the earth there is water: 比; the former rulers, by it, set
up the countless states and kept close to the lords." The tradition reads a gathering round one
centre, joined freely.

**What the polarity implies.** The same lone firm line as 7, now in the ruler's seat: 九五 (*jiǔ wǔ*)
顯比 (*xiǎn bǐ*), "drawing near made visible." Water no longer hides inside the ground; it lies on
it, where water does one thing — runs together and finds the low place, pool joining pool. Every
open line is placed by how it relates to the one: 比之自內 (*bǐ zhī zì nèi*), "draws near to it from
within" (line 2); 外比之 (*wài bǐ zhī*), "draws near to it from outside" (line 4); and line 3, whose
neighbours are all open, 比之匪人 (*bǐ zhī fěi rén*), "draws near to the wrong people." Going along
has no firm line of its own, so it has to go along with what is there, when it is there — which is
why timing enters the judgment: 後夫凶 (*hòu fū xiōng*), "the latecomer: ominous." Once the water has
settled, the one who arrives after it is left dry, and the top line, past the centre, is 比之无首
(*bǐ zhī wú shǒu*), "drawing near with no head."

Against 7: there the many are bound by 律 (*lǜ*, "regulation"); here nobody is compelled. The fifth
line's hunt shows it — 王用三驅，失前禽 (*wáng yòng sān qū, shī qián qín*), "the ruler drives on three
sides and loses the game in front": the side left open is what makes those who come, come freely.

**For the name.** **Drawing Near** — supported and deepened: going along within toward one firm line
without is drawing near, and the signature adds that the drawing is toward a single centre and
unforced. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Rallying* — survives the lines: 比之自內 "rallying
to it from within", 比之匪人 "rallying to the wrong people", 顯比 "open rallying", 比之无首 "rallying
with no head." *Pooling* — the water-on-earth image; fails the lines at 比之匪人 (*bǐ zhī fěi rén*),
"— the wrong people", and at 顯比 (*xiǎn bǐ*), where "open pooling" says nothing.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Drawing near to one centre — from within, from outside, or to the wrong people — and the time to
come is now.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 比 (*bǐ*) as 密也 (*mì yě*, "close"), and draws it as the graph
for following turned round: 二人爲从，反从爲比 (*èr rén wéi cóng, fǎn cóng wéi bǐ*, "two people make
从 (*cóng*), *following*; 从 reversed makes 比"). Not one behind another, then: two close. The core text uses
the word for how each line comes near to what holds it — 比之自內 (*bǐ zhī zì nèi*, "draws near to
it from within"), 外比之 (*wài bǐ zhī*, "draws near to it from outside") — and its judgment puts a
clock on it: 後夫凶 (*hòu fū xiōng*, "the one who comes after: ominous").

### The polarities in it

Within is earth, open through all three lines: going along, with nothing firm of its own. Without is
water, its one firm line at the fifth place, the ruler's seat. So five open lines turn toward a
single firm one, and the text places each by how it draws near to it. It is 7 turned over: there
the one firm line was below, holding the many from inside the ranks; here it is above and in plain
sight — the fifth line calls it 顯比 (*xiǎn bǐ*, "drawing near made visible"). In 7 the many are
held; in 8 they come. The 彖 (*tuàn*) reads the whole back toward following — 下順從也 (*xià shùn
cóng yě*, "those below go along and follow") — which is a Wing's gloss on a graph that is following
reversed. **The two readings sit side by side in this figure, and neither cancels the other.**

### In nature

Watch water on the ground after rain. On a path or a field it does not stay where it fell: beads on
a leaf touch and are one drop at once; puddles lengthen toward each other; rivulets find each other
and the low place, and join. Water lying on earth has one habit, and it is drawing near. That is
the 大象 (*dà xiàng*): 地上有水 (*dì shàng yǒu shuǐ*, "on the earth there is water").

The judgment's 永 (*yǒng*, "lasting") is in 說文 a picture of water too — 象水坙理之長 (*xiàng shuǐ
jīng lǐ zhī cháng*, "the long veins of running water") — and quotes a song: 江之永矣 (*jiāng zhī yǒng
yǐ*, "how long the river runs").

The fifth line has a real hunt in it. 王用三驅，失前禽 (*wáng yòng sān qū, shī qián qín*, "the ruler
drives from three sides and loses the game in front"). Beaters close three sides of a covert and
leave one open; deer and boar break for the gap, and some get away. Anyone who has watched sheep
worked by dogs knows the same thing: animals move toward the open side. The side left open is what
makes the drive work — and what lets those who will not come, go.

And the first line keeps an old greeting. 說文 gives 它 (*tā*, "something else") as a snake, and
explains the idiom: 上古艸居患它，故相問無它乎 (*shàng gǔ cǎo jū huàn tā, gù xiāng wèn wú tā hū*,
"in early times people lived among the grasses and feared snakes, so they asked each other, *No
snake?*").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 比：吉。原筮元永貞，无咎。不寧方來，後夫凶 (*bǐ: jí. yuán shì yuán yǒng zhēn, wú
jiù. bù níng fāng lái, hòu fū xiōng*) — "Drawing near: auspicious. Consult the milfoil again: from the
source, lasting, holding firm — no blame. The unsettled lands come; the one who comes after:
ominous." 筮 (*shì*) is in 說文 the Changes' figures cast with milfoil; the text here names its own
practice, and the older sense of 貞 (*zhēn*) as *the inquiry* reads naturally: "a lasting
inquiry". 不寧方 (*bù níng fāng*) may be "the unsettled regions" or "those not at rest": either way,
they are coming, and the latecomer is not welcome.

**Line 1** begins with confidence. 有孚，比之，无咎。有孚盈缶，終來有它，吉 (*yǒu fú, bǐ zhī, wú jiù.
yǒu fú yíng fǒu, zhōng lái yǒu tā, jí*, "there is confidence: draw near to it, no blame. Confidence
fills the earthen jar; in the end something else comes: auspicious"). 缶 (*fǒu*) is in 說文 a plain
clay wine jar that the people of Qin also beat to keep time for songs. And 有它 (*yǒu tā*), "there is
something else", is elsewhere a trouble — at 28's fourth line it is 吝 (*lìn*, "stinting"). Here what
comes unlooked-for is good.

**Line 2**, the earth's centre, answers the fifth directly. 比之自內，貞吉 (*bǐ zhī zì nèi, zhēn jí*,
"draws near to it from within: holding firm, auspicious").

**Line 3**, at the seam, has only open lines around it. 比之匪人 (*bǐ zhī fěi rén*, "draws near to the
wrong people"). The core text gives no verdict at all — no 凶 (*xiōng*), nothing; the 小象 (*xiǎo
xiàng*) supplies one, 不亦傷乎 (*bù yì shāng hū*, "is it not hurtful?"). The text only shows where
this line has gone.

**Line 4**, just below the fifth. 外比之，貞吉 (*wài bǐ zhī, zhēn jí*, "draws near to it from outside:
holding firm, auspicious"). It sits in the outer trigram, next to the fifth: nearness from outside, and it does well.

**Line 5** is the centre everything turns toward. 顯比。王用三驅，失前禽，邑人不誡，吉 (*xiǎn bǐ.
wáng yòng sān qū, shī qián qín, yì rén bù jiè, jí*, "drawing near made visible. The ruler drives from
three sides and loses the game in front; the townspeople are not put on guard: auspicious"). 說文
gives 顯 (*xiǎn*) as a bright ornament on the head: seen from far off. The centre does not compel,
and no one at home is alarmed by it.

**Line 6** comes last. 比之无首，凶 (*bǐ zhī wú shǒu, xiōng*, "drawing near with no head: ominous").
首 (*shǒu*) is the head and the beginning. 王弼: 无首後也 (*wú shǒu hòu yě*, "no head means coming
after") — this is the judgment's latecomer, arriving when the gathering is done.

### In a life now

Moving to a new town and choosing who to fall in with; joining a union, a choir, a co-operative, a
crew; a marriage; a neighbourhood forming round a school or a well. The figure asks three things.
**Draw near to what actually holds** — a firm centre — rather than to whoever happens to be beside
you (line 3). **Come in time**: the first line begins with a full jar, the last arrives headless
and late. **And if you are the centre, leave a side open**: the hunt that is auspicious is the one
that lets the unwilling go, and alarms no one at home (line 5). 雜卦 (*záguà*) sets the pair in two
words: 比樂師憂 (*bǐ lè shī yōu*, "drawing near is joy, the army is sorrow").

The 大象 turns it to government: 先王以建萬國，親諸侯 (*xiān wáng yǐ jiàn wàn guó, qīn zhū hóu*,
"the former rulers set up the countless states and kept close to the lords"). Order made not by
force, like 7's, but by nearness.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A shared graph, a thematic image, and a real disagreement.** The Laozi's chapter 66: *"Rivers and
seas can rule the hundred valleys / because they are masterful at staying below them."* The graph
of *rule* there is 王 (*wáng*), which stands in the fifth line of 比 (*bǐ*) in the core text. The water gathering
is only thematic: 比's water is the 大象's image, a Wing, not the core.

Both books picture a gathering that no one is forced into. They part over where the centre stands.
The Laozi's gathering place is low, and its best leaders are those of whom *"little is known"* (ch
17). 比's centre is the fifth line, high, and 顯 (*xiǎn*), made visible — a bright ornament on the
head. The naturalist's eye sides with the Laozi, since water on the ground gathers in the low
place; the lines honour the seen centre above. **This reading leaves that tension where the two
books put it.**

### Words kept

- 比 (*bǐ*; traditionally *bì*) — *drawing near*, the proposed name (not yet approved); never *attachment*, which 執 (*zhí*, locked *grasp*) forbids
- 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler*, never *king* (locked); 諸侯 (*zhū hóu*) — *the lords*
- 萬國 (*wàn guó*) — *the countless states*, after the locked 萬物 (*wàn wù*) *the countless things*
- 孚 (*fú*) — *confidence*, working gloss (A6; *trust* belongs to 信)
- 貞 · 元 (*zhēn · yuán*) — *holding firm · from the source*, working glosses; the older *inquiry* matters in 原筮 (*yuán shì*)
- 吉 · 凶 · 无咎 (*jí · xiōng · wú jiù*) — *auspicious · ominous · no blame*, working glosses (A4)
- 永 (*yǒng*) — *lasting*; never *eternal* or *abiding*, which are the locks' words for 常
- 顯 (*xiǎn*) — *made visible*, not *illustrious*
