## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind × sky ↔ earth: both poles yin, a trigram open at the base under one with
no odd line.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 柔以時升，巽而順，剛中而應 (*róu yǐ shí shēng, xùn ér shùn,
gāng zhōng ér yìng*, "the yielding rises with the time; entering, and going along; the firm central
and answered"). The 大象傳: 地中生木，升；君子以順德，積小以高大 (*dì zhōng shēng mù, shēng; jūnzǐ yǐ
shùn dé, jī xiǎo yǐ gāo dà*, "within the earth, wood is brought forth: *shēng*; the noble one goes
along with integrity, heaping up the small to become high and great"). The tradition takes 升
(*shēng*, "ascent") as the slow rise of something soft that does not force its way — a tree climbing
out of the ground, one small addition at a time.

**What the polarity implies.** Wind is the sky with its base opened, so the soft point of the figure
is its root; and the earth above has no firm line at all. This is the figure in which the way up has
no resistance in it. Set it beside 43: there vigour under an open lip overtops and breaks; here
entering under going-along breaks nothing, and the rise is made by working in. The action within is
entering, not moving out — the root going down is what sends the trunk up. The 彖傳's 巽而順 (*xùn ér
shùn*, "entering, and going along") is simply the figure read aloud.

The core text agrees with the structure. 升虛邑 (*shēng xū yì*, "ascend into an empty city") — the
earth above is all open lines, a city with no one on its walls. 冥升 (*míng shēng*, "ascending in the
dark") — a rise that happens inside the earth, before anything shows. The structure suggests that the
danger is not opposition but not knowing when to stop, which the top line meets with 利于不息之貞 (*lì
yú bù xī zhī zhēn*, "favourable for a *zhēn* that does not rest" — 貞, *zhēn*, its English still open,
A6). And it answers 45, its turned-over pair: there the earth was the self, yielding so that things
collect above it, loud and open; here the earth is the world, yielding so that something passes
quietly through, and 不來 (*bù lái*, "does not come back").

**For the name.** *Ascent* — the signature supports it: it describes a rise without resistance, and
*ascent*, unlike *climbing*, does not claim a struggle. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Climbing*, *Sprouting* — *Climbing* survives: 升階 (*shēng jiē*, "climb the steps"), 升虛邑 (*shēng
xū yì*, "climb into an empty city") and 冥升 (*míng shēng*, "climbing in the dark") all read, though it
adds an effort the soft figure lacks; *Sprouting* — the wood in the earth — fails the lines: 升階
(*shēng jiē*, "sprout up the steps") and 升虛邑 (*shēng xū yì*, "sprout into an empty city") are a
person going up, not a plant.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A rise with nothing in its way, taken a step at a time — and the only trouble it names is going on
after the light has gone.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 升 (*shēng*, "ascent") as 十龠也 (*shí yuè yě*, "ten
龠", a measure of grain), made from 斗 (*dǒu*, "the dipper") and also a picture: a ladle lifting
grain. Measured lifting, not a leap. The core text climbs by stages — 允升 (*yǔn shēng*, "a warranted
rise"), 升虛邑 (*shēng xū yì*, "climbing into the empty town"), 升階 (*shēng jiē*, "climbing the steps"),
冥升 (*míng shēng*, "climbing in the dark") — and between them it makes two offerings. No line is
ominous, stinting or regretted.

### The polarities in it

Wind within, earth without: entering beneath, going along above, both yielding (see *The signature*,
above). The self goes in at its open base, and the world above has no firm line to stop it. So the
rise is made by going in — the way a root goes down to send a stem up — and the 彖 (*tuàn*, the
judgment commentary) says so: 柔以時升 (*róu yǐ shí shēng*, "the soft rises with the time"). Structure
and text agree almost line for line; the empty town is the earth trigram's open lines seen from
below. **They part at the top.** An upper trigram all yielding could go on yielding for ever, and
the figure gives no reason to stop. The text does: its last line is dark, and the 序卦 (*xùguà*, the
Sequence) draws the lesson plainly — 升而不已，必困 (*shēng ér bù yǐ, bì kùn*, "rising without stopping,
one is sure to be hemmed in"). That is how 47 comes next.

### In nature

The 大象: 地中生木 (*dì zhōng shēng mù*, "within the earth, wood is brought forth"). The 說卦 (*shuōguà*)
gives the trigram within as wood as well as wind: 巽為木 (*xùn wéi mù*, "the wind trigram is wood").
Watch a seedling become a tree and see the fact that fits the 大象 best: **a tree grows taller only at
its tips.** A nail driven into a trunk at head height stays at head height for the life of the tree;
the trunk thickens round it, but nothing below the growing tip ever rises. Height is each season's
small growth laid on the last — count the rings of bud scars on a winter twig, one for each year.
The 大象 names it: 積小以高大 (*jī xiǎo yǐ gāo dà*, "heaping up the small to become high and great"). 說文
gives 積 (*jī*, "heap up") as 聚也 (*jù yě*, "gathering") — the word 45's 彖 uses for 萃 (*cuì*,
"gathering"). What collects there, heaped up, rises here.

The top line's word has its own picture. 冥 (*míng*, "dark") is in 說文 幽也 (*yōu yě*, "hidden, dim"),
and Xu Shen explains it by the moon: 十六日而月始虧幽也 (*shí liù rì ér yuè shǐ kuī yōu yě*, "on the
sixteenth day the moon begins to wane into darkness"). Watch the moon in the nights after full: it
comes up later each evening, a little smaller, into more of the dark. That is 冥升: a rising that goes
on past its fullness.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 升：元亨，用見大人，勿恤，南征吉 (*shēng: yuán hēng, yòng jiàn dàrén, wù xù, nán zhēng jí*) —
"Ascent: from the source, it gets through. Use it to see the great person; do not worry. Setting out
south: auspicious." 恤 (*xù*) is in 說文 憂也 (*yōu yě*, "to worry") — the one feeling the hexagram
names, and it names it to set it aside. South is read only by the Wings: the 說卦 calls it the quarter
where 萬物皆相見 (*wàn wù jiē xiāng jiàn*, "the countless things all see one another") — full daylight.

**Line 1** is the root. 允升，大吉 (*yǔn shēng, dà jí*, "a warranted rise: greatly auspicious"). 允
(*yǔn*) is in 說文 信也 (*xìn yě*), and 信 (*xìn*) is this project's *trust*: a rise that can be trusted,
welcomed from above — the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*, the line commentary): 上合志也 (*shàng hé zhì yě*, "those
above share the aim"). The soft line at wind's open base carries the best fortune in the figure.

**Line 2** makes the plain offering. 孚乃利用禴，无咎 (*fú nǎi lì yòng yuè, wú jiù*, "with confidence, it is
advantageous to use the spring offering; no blame"). Word for word 45's second line: the pair share
a line. The firm centre within gives the plainest of the seasonal offerings, and it is enough.

**Line 3** walks in. 升虛邑 (*shēng xū yì*, "climbing into the empty town"). The one line with no
verdict: the text reports it, as one reports walking into a place with no one on the walls. 虛 (*xū*,
"empty") is also, in 說文, 大丘也 (*dà qiū yě*, "a great mound") — a town on a rise, or where a town once
stood.

**Line 4** gives thanks at the height. 王用亨于岐山，吉无咎 (*wáng yòng xiǎng yú qí shān, jí wú jiù*, "the
ruler makes offering on Mount Qi: auspicious, no blame"). Here 亨 (*xiǎng*) stands for 享 (*xiǎng*,
"to offer"), as 王弼's text writes it at 47.2 — not the working gloss *gets through*. Mount Qi was the
Zhou people's home mountain: the rise, now in the world above, goes to the ancestors' high place and
gives.

**Line 5** climbs the steps. 貞吉，升階 (*zhēn jí, shēng jiē*, "holding firm, auspicious: climbing the
steps"). 階 (*jiē*) is 說文 陛也 (*bì yě*, "the stair of a hall"). At the soft centre of the earth, the
rise has become ceremony: stair by stair, into the hall.

**Line 6** is still climbing. 冥升，利于不息之貞 (*míng shēng, lì yú bù xī zhī zhēn*, "climbing in the dark:
the advantage is in a holding firm that does not rest"). The line does not say *stop*. It says that
what may go on without rest is 貞 (*zhēn*, "holding firm") — not the climb. (On the older reading of
貞 as *the inquiry*, it is an inquiry kept up without rest; either way, attention, not altitude.) The
小象 names the cost of the other course: 消不富也 (*xiāo bù fù yě*, "dwindling, not enriched").

### In a life now

An apprenticeship, a career that opens, a recovery after illness, a child growing, a young orchard.
The figure says: when the way up is open, go, and do not worry (the judgment). Go by steps that can
be trusted (line 1). Make the plain offering on the way — thanks, a return to where you came from
(lines 2 and 4). And watch the light. The risk of an easy rise is that nothing stops it, so one goes
on climbing in the dark into a place with nothing in it — promoted past the work one loves, building
past what anyone needs. When the light goes, what should continue is holding firm, not the climb.
The 大象 puts the whole of it as practice: 君子以順德，積小以高大 (*jūnzǐ yǐ shùn dé, jī xiǎo yǐ gāo dà*, "the
noble one goes along with integrity, heaping up the small to become high and great").

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a tree and a word, and part over what heaping is for.** The Laozi's chapter
64: *"The tree the width of both arms is born from a hair's tip. / The nine-tiered terrace rises
from a heap of dirt."* Its Chinese has 木 (*mù*, "wood, tree") and 生 (*shēng*, "bear, be born"), the
two graphs of the 大象's 地中生木 — so the echo passes the two-question test, at the Wing layer: the core
text of 升 has no tree. Both see height as the small, added.

But chapter 64 draws a different counsel: *"Handle it before it exists"* — act while things are
small, then leave them to grow of themselves. The 大象 tells the noble one to do the heaping. And
where the 大象 praises 積 (*jī*, "heap up"), the Laozi's chapter 81 uses the same graph against it:
*"The sage does not hoard."* **The graph is shared and the sense divides**: heaping the small toward
height, and hoarding. This reading leaves the difference standing.

- 木 (*mù*) — *wood*, the 大象's image; the trigram 巽's own image is *wind*
- 生 (*shēng*) — *bring forth*, never *generate* (locked)
