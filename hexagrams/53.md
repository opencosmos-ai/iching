## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake and thunder ↔ wind; yang within, yin without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象傳 sees 山上有木 (*shān shàng yǒu mù*, "a tree on the mountain"):
巽 (*xùn*) read as wood, rooted on what does not move. The 彖傳 reads it as advance that keeps its footing: 進得位，往有功也
(*jìn dé wèi, wǎng yǒu gōng yě*, "advancing, it gains its place; going on, it achieves"). And it gives the
two actions outright: 止而巽，動不窮也 (*zhǐ ér xùn, dòng bù qióng yě*, "stopping, and entering: its
movement does not run out"). The judgment ties it to the proper order of a marriage, 女歸吉 (*nǚ guī jí*,
"the woman goes to her husband's house: good fortune").

**What the polarity implies.** Mountain within is closed at its top, the third line. Wind without is open
at its base, the fourth. The two odd lines meet at the centre of the figure: the lid of the hold lies
directly under the open floor of the entering. That is a tree's root on a rock, going in only where the
rock gives. Movement that starts from a hold does not run out, because it never overreaches its ground.
The lines are one wild goose landing by stages — 干 (*gān*, "shore"), 磐 (*pán*, "rock"), 陸 (*lù*, "dry land"), 木 (*mù*, "tree"), 陵 (*líng*, "hill") — and
the seam between the trigrams is where it goes wrong. At the third line, the top of the hold, the goose reaches dry
land and 夫征不復 (*fū zhēng bù fù*, "the husband marches off and does not come back"). The structure
suggests the reason. That is the one line that tries to go straight on from the mountain's closed top, instead
of stepping across into the open floor above. At the fourth line the goose, now in the tree, 或得其桷 (*huò dé qí jué*,
"may find a flat branch"). It does not land well; it lands enough. Turned over, the figure is 54: releasing
first, then moving. Here the order is right, stopping before entering, and the order is the whole of it.

**For the name.** **By Degrees** — the signature supports it: stopping under entering is advance measured by
the ground, and the name keeps the manner rather than the motion. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Gradual Advance* — survives: 鴻漸于陸 (*hóng jiàn yú lù*, "the wild goose advances gradually to the dry
land"); *Rooting* — fails the lines: the tree is the 大象傳's image, but 漸 (*jiàn*) in every line is the goose's
progress, not growth.
