## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out).
**Across** — water ↔ fire and thunder ↔ wind; yin within, yang without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 glosses 豐，大也 (*fēng, dà yě*, "*fēng* is great") and gives its cause as
明以動，故豐 (*míng yǐ dòng, gù fēng*, "clarity, and so moving: hence abundance"). Then it turns at once to
decline: 日中則昃，月盈則食 (*rì zhōng zé zè, yuè yíng zé shí*, "the sun at noon then leans; the moon at
full then is eaten"). The 大象傳 has 雷電皆至 (*léi diàn jiē zhì*, "thunder and lightning arrive together"),
and from it 君子以折獄致刑 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhé yù zhì xíng*, "the noble one decides cases and carries out
penalties").

**What the polarity implies.** Lightning and thunder arriving together means the storm is directly
overhead: no gap between the flash and the report. That is the figure's fullness. Fire within is hollow at
the centre, and its clarity depends on that empty middle. Thunder without puts one firm line down on top
of it, force at the base of the upper trigram, pressing on the light below. So the abundance piles up over
the clarity and covers it. The lines say this more strongly than the 彖傳 does: 豐其蔀，日中見斗 (*fēng qí bù,
rì zhōng jiàn dǒu*, "the screen made thick: at noon you see the Dipper"). Darkness at noon comes from too
much, not too little. By the top the covering is complete: 豐其屋，蔀其家 (*fēng qí wū, bù qí jiā*, "his
house made great, his household screened"), then 闃其无人 (*qù qí wú rén*, "silent, no one there"). The
structure suggests that this figure's abundance is not a harvest but a canopy. The remedy is the one
the judgment gives, 宜日中 (*yí rì zhōng*, "fitting at noon"): act while the light is at its height, before
the fullness shuts it in. 56, its inversion, has lost the house altogether.

**For the name.** **Abundance** — the signature deepens it: the name has to carry *too much* as well as
*much*, and the lines' 豐其蔀 needs exactly that. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Fullness* —
survives: 豐其屋 ("his house made full"), 豐其沛 (*fēng qí pèi*, "the curtain made full"); *Noon* — fails the
lines: 日中 (*rì zhōng*, "noon") is the hour in the second, third and fourth lines, not the subject of the first or the top.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Fullness at its noon — so much of it that it screens the light it came from.** 說文 (*shuōwén*)
gives 豐 (*fēng*, "abundance") as 豆之豐滿者也 (*dòu zhī fēng mǎn zhě yě*, "a 豆 heaped full") — the
豆 (*dòu*) being the raised dish that offerings were piled on. The judgment is bright:
豐：亨。王假之，勿憂，宜日中 (*fēng: hēng. wáng jiǎ zhī, wù yōu, yí rì zhōng*, "Abundance gets
through. The ruler reaches it. Do not grieve: it is fitting at noon."). But in the lines, what is
made abundant is a covering — 豐其蔀 (*fēng qí bù*, "the screen made thick"), 豐其沛 (*fēng qí pèi*,
"the curtain made full"), 豐其屋 (*fēng qí wū*, "the house made great") — and three times the
result, at noon, is stars. **Abundance** *(proposed)* in the core text is not a harvest to enjoy. It
is a height that darkens itself, and the text asks what to do while it is still noon.

### The polarities in it

Fire within, thunder without. The fire is hollow at its centre, and its clarity comes from that
empty middle; the thunder puts a firm line down at its base, directly on top (see *The signature*,
above). The 彖 (*tuàn*) gives the Wings' cause, 明以動，故豐 (*míng yǐ dòng, gù fēng*, "clarity, and
so moving: hence abundance"). The lines give the consequence: force piled on clarity covers it. The
text and the structure agree precisely at the seam. The same words, 豐其蔀，日中見斗 (*fēng qí bù, rì
zhōng jiàn dǒu*, "the screen made thick: at noon one sees the Dipper"), stand at line 2, the centre
of the fire, and at line 4, the thunder's firm base. The screen is on both sides of the join.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 雷電皆至 (*léi diàn jiē zhì*, "thunder and lightning arrive together").
Count the seconds between a flash and its thunder: about three to a kilometre. When they come
together, the storm is directly overhead — nothing between you and it, and the day gone dark under
the cloud.

But a storm does not show stars. 日中見斗 — "at noon one sees the Dipper" — happens under the sky
in one case only: a total eclipse of the sun. In a few minutes the light turns grey and cold, birds
go quiet and fly to roost, and the brightest stars and planets come out at midday. The core text
gives the cause as a screen — 王弼 glosses 蔀 (*bù*) as 覆曖鄣光明之物也 (*fù ài zhàng guāng míng zhī
wù yě*, "a thing that covers, shades and blocks the light") — but the sight it describes is the
sight of an eclipse, and the 彖 knows eclipses: 日中則昃，月盈則食 (*rì zhōng zé zè, yuè yíng zé
shí*, "the sun at noon then leans; the moon at full is then eaten"). That is exact astronomy. The
moon is eclipsed only when it is full. The line 3 star, 沫 (*mèi*), 王弼 reads as 微昧之明 (*wēi mèi
zhī míng*, "a faint, dim light") — the smallest star, the last to show.

說卦 (*shuōguà*) ch 5 places fire in the south: 離也者，明也，萬物皆相見 (*lí yě zhě, míng yě, wànwù
jiē xiāng jiàn*, "fire is clarity: the countless things all see one another"). This figure is that
high summer noon — and the moment the seeing is lost.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 豐：亨。王假之，勿憂，宜日中 — "Abundance gets through. The ruler reaches it. Do
not grieve: it is fitting at noon." Every noon leans. The judgment does not refuse the height; it
says not to grieve over what follows, and to act while the light is overhead.

**Line 1** meets an equal. 遇其配主，雖旬无咎，往有尚 (*yù qí pèi zhǔ, suī xún wú jiù, wǎng yǒu shàng*,
"meets its matching host; though for ten days, no blame; going on is honoured"). 說文: 十日爲旬
(*shí rì wéi xún*, "ten days make a 旬"); 王弼 reads 旬 (*xún*) as 均 (*jūn*, "equal"). The 小象
(*xiǎo xiàng*) sets the limit: 過旬災也 (*guò xún zāi yě*, "past ten days, disaster").

**Line 2** is the fire's centre, screened. 豐其蔀，日中見斗，往得疑疾，有孚發若，吉 (*fēng qí bù, rì
zhōng jiàn dǒu, wǎng dé yí jí, yǒu fú fā ruò, jí*, "the screen made thick: at noon one sees the
Dipper; going brings suspicion and sickness; with confidence it opens — auspicious"). The clarity
is inside; forcing it out makes enemies; confidence lets it out.

**Line 3** is the deepest dark. 豐其沛，日中見沫，折其右肱，无咎 (*fēng qí pèi, rì zhōng jiàn mèi, shé
qí yòu gōng, wú jiù*, "the curtain made full: at noon one sees the faint star; the right arm is
broken — no blame"). The working arm is gone. Nothing can be done, and it is not one's fault.

**Line 4** is the same darkness across the seam, and a way out. 豐其蔀，日中見斗，遇其夷主，吉 (*fēng
qí bù, rì zhōng jiàn dǒu, yù qí yí zhǔ, jí*, "the screen made thick, at noon the Dipper; meets its
level host — auspicious"). 說文 gives 夷 (*yí*) as 平也 (*píng yě*, "level"). Company is the way
through the screen.

**Line 5** draws the light in. 來章，有慶譽，吉 (*lái zhāng, yǒu qìng yù, jí*, "the pattern comes;
celebration and praise — auspicious"). 說文: 樂竟爲一章 (*yuè jìng wéi yī zhāng*, "a piece of music
brought to its end is a 章"). The open centre receives what is bright instead of heaping a cover.

**Line 6** completes the covering. 豐其屋，蔀其家，窺其戶，闃其无人，三歲不觌，凶 (*fēng qí wū, bù qí
jiā, kuī qí hù, qù qí wú rén, sān suì bù dí, xiōng*, "the house made great, the household screened;
peer in at the door — silence, no one there; three years and not seen — ominous"). 說文 gives 闃
(*qù*) as 靜也 (*jìng yě*, "stillness"). The 小象: 自藏也 (*zì cáng yě*, "it hides itself").

### In a life now

This is the figure for success at its height: the firm grown too large to hear itself, the house
behind gates, the household of plenty where no one is at home, the one famous enough that no one
can reach them, the year when everything came in at once. The 雜卦 (*záguà*) puts it in four
characters: 豐多故也 (*fēng duō gù yě*, "abundance: many affairs"). The text does not counsel less.
It counsels acting at noon: meet equals (lines 1, 4), keep confidence rather than force (line 2), let the
bright thing come to you (line 5), and do not build the house so large it becomes a screen (line 6).

The 大象 turns the storm to practice: 君子以折獄致刑 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhé yù zhì xíng*, "the noble one
decides cases and carries out penalties"). When light and force arrive together, decide then — do
not wait for the dark.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books meet over fullness and part over what to do with it.** The Laozi's chapter 9:
*"Rather than keep filling what you hold, stop. … / Gold and jade fill the hall, and no one can
guard them."* The graph 盈 (*yíng*, "full") is chapter 9's first image, and it is in 55's 彖, 月盈則食
(*yuè yíng zé shí*, "the moon at full is eaten") — the Wings, not the core. The core's house made
great and the chapter's hall filled share an image, not a character: thematic only.

And they part. Chapter 9 says stop before full. 55's judgment says the fullness gets through, the
ruler reaches it, and 勿憂 (*wù yōu*, "do not grieve") — it counsels acting at the height, not
refusing it. The graph 豐 itself does occur in the Laozi, chapter 54, as plain good: *"Cultivate
this in your nation, and its integrity becomes bountiful"* — the word there is 豐 (*fēng*, "abundance"). The Laozi's
豐 is integrity grown outward; 55's is a canopy grown over. The difference is left open.

- 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler*, never *king* (locked)
