## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out).
**Across** — water ↔ fire and thunder ↔ wind; yin within, yang without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 glosses 豐，大也 (*fēng, dà yě*, "*fēng* is great") and gives its cause as
明以動，故豐 (*míng yǐ dòng, gù fēng*, "clarity, and so moving: hence abundance"). Then it turns at once to
decline: 日中則昃，月盈則食 (*rì zhōng zé zè, yuè yíng zé shí*, "the sun at noon then leans; the moon at
full then is eaten"). The 大象傳 has 雷電皆至 (*léi diàn jiē zhì*, "thunder and lightning arrive together"),
and from it 君子以折獄致刑 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zhé yù zhì xíng*, "the noble one decides cases and carries out
penalties").

**What the polarity implies.** Lightning and thunder arriving together means the storm is directly
overhead: no gap between the flash and the report. That is the figure's fullness. Fire within is hollow at
the centre, and its clarity depends on that empty middle. Thunder without puts one firm line down on top
of it, force at the base of the upper trigram, pressing on the light below. So the abundance piles up over
the clarity and covers it. The lines say this more strongly than the 彖傳 does: 豐其蔀，日中見斗 (*fēng qí bù,
rì zhōng jiàn dǒu*, "the screen made thick: at noon you see the Dipper"). Darkness at noon comes from too
much, not too little. By the top the covering is complete: 豐其屋，蔀其家 (*fēng qí wū, bù qí jiā*, "his
house made great, his household screened"), then 闃其无人 (*qù qí wú rén*, "silent, no one there"). The
structure suggests that this figure's abundance is not a harvest but a canopy. The remedy is the one
the judgment gives, 宜日中 (*yí rì zhōng*, "fitting at noon"): act while the light is at its height, before
the fullness shuts it in. 56, its inversion, has lost the house altogether.

**For the name.** **Abundance** — the signature deepens it: the name has to carry *too much* as well as
*much*, and the lines' 豐其蔀 needs exactly that. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Fullness* —
survives: 豐其屋 ("his house made full"), 豐其沛 (*fēng qí pèi*, "the curtain made full"); *Noon* — fails the
lines: 日中 (*rì zhōng*, "noon") is the hour in the second, third and fourth lines, not the subject of the first or the top.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Fullness at its noon — so much of it that it screens the light it came from.** The Shuowen, the c.
100 CE character dictionary, gives 豐 ("abundance") as 豆之豐滿者也 ("a dish heaped full") — the 豆 ("dish")
being the raised dish that offerings were piled on. The judgment is bright: 豐：亨。王假之，勿憂，宜日中
("Abundance gets through. The ruler reaches it. Do not grieve: it is fitting at noon."). But in the
lines, what is made abundant is a covering — 豐其蔀 ("the screen made thick"), 豐其沛 ("the curtain made
full"), 豐其屋 ("the house made great") — and three times the result, at noon, is stars. **Abundance**
*(proposed)* in the core text is not a harvest to enjoy. It is a height that darkens itself, and the
text asks what to do while it is still noon.

### The polarities in it

Fire within, thunder without. The fire is hollow at its centre, and its clarity comes from that
empty middle; the thunder puts a firm line down at its base, directly on top (see *The signature*,
above). The Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, gives the Wings' cause, 明以動，故豐 ("clarity, and so moving:
hence abundance"). The lines give the consequence: force piled on clarity covers it. The text and
the structure agree precisely at the seam. The same words, 豐其蔀，日中見斗 ("the screen made thick: at noon
one sees the Dipper"), stand at line 2, the centre of the fire, and at line 4, the thunder's firm
base. The screen is on both sides of the join.

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, sees 雷電皆至 ("thunder and lightning arrive together").
Count the seconds between a flash and its thunder: about three to a kilometre. When they come
together, the storm is directly overhead — nothing between you and it, and the day gone dark under
the cloud.

But a storm does not show stars. 日中見斗 ("at noon one sees the Dipper") happens under the sky in one
case only: a total eclipse of the sun. In a few minutes the light turns grey and cold, birds go
quiet and fly to roost, and the brightest stars and planets come out at midday. The core text gives
the cause as a screen — Wang Bi glosses 蔀 ("screen") as 覆曖鄣光明之物也 ("a thing that covers, shades and
blocks the light") — but the sight it describes is the sight of an eclipse, and the Tuan knows
eclipses: 日中則昃，月盈則食 ("the sun at noon then leans; the moon at full is then eaten"). That is exact
astronomy. The moon is eclipsed only when it is full. The line 3 star, 沫 ("the faint star"), Wang Bi
reads as 微昧之明 ("a faint, dim light") — the smallest star, the last to show.

The Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams, ch 5, places fire in the south: 離也者，明也，萬物皆相見 ("fire is
clarity: the countless things all see one another"). This figure is that high summer noon — and the
moment the seeing is lost.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 豐：亨。王假之，勿憂，宜日中 ("Abundance gets through. The ruler reaches it. Do not grieve: it
is fitting at noon."). Every noon leans. The judgment does not refuse the height; it says not to
grieve over what follows, and to act while the light is overhead.

**Line 1** meets an equal. 遇其配主，雖旬无咎，往有尚 ("meets its matching host; though for ten days, no blame;
going on is honoured"). The Shuowen: 十日爲旬 ("ten days make one ten-day span"); Wang Bi reads 旬 ("ten
days") as 均 ("equal"). The Small Image, the Wing on each line, sets the limit: 過旬災也 ("past ten days,
disaster").

**Line 2** is the fire's centre, screened. 豐其蔀，日中見斗，往得疑疾，有孚發若，吉 ("the screen made thick: at noon one
sees the Dipper; going brings suspicion and sickness; with confidence it opens — auspicious"). The
clarity is inside; forcing it out makes enemies; confidence lets it out.

**Line 3** is the deepest dark. 豐其沛，日中見沫，折其右肱，无咎 ("the curtain made full: at noon one sees the faint
star; the right arm is broken — no blame"). The working arm is gone. Nothing can be done, and it is
not one's fault.

**Line 4** is the same darkness across the seam, and a way out. 豐其蔀，日中見斗，遇其夷主，吉 ("the screen made
thick, at noon the Dipper; meets its level host — auspicious"). The Shuowen gives 夷 ("level") as 平也
("level"). Company is the way through the screen.

**Line 5** draws the light in. 來章，有慶譽，吉 ("the pattern comes; celebration and praise — auspicious").
The Shuowen: 樂竟爲一章 ("a piece of music brought to its end is one movement"). The open centre receives
what is bright instead of heaping a cover.

**Line 6** completes the covering. 豐其屋，蔀其家，窺其戶，闃其无人，三歲不觌，凶 ("the house made great, the household
screened; peer in at the door — silence, no one there; three years and not seen — ominous"). The
Shuowen gives 闃 ("silence") as 靜也 ("stillness"). The Small Image: 自藏也 ("it hides itself").

### In a life now

This is the figure for success at its height: the firm grown too large to hear itself, the house
behind gates, the household of plenty where no one is at home, the one famous enough that no one can
reach them, the year when everything came in at once. The Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams,
puts it in four characters: 豐多故也 ("abundance: many affairs"). The text does not counsel less. It
counsels acting at noon: meet equals (lines 1, 4), keep confidence rather than force (line 2), let
the bright thing come to you (line 5), and do not build the house so large it becomes a screen (line
6).

The Great Image turns the storm to practice: 君子以折獄致刑 ("the noble one decides cases and carries out
penalties"). When light and force arrive together, decide then — do not wait for the dark.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books meet over fullness and part over what to do with it.** The Laozi's chapter 9:
*"Rather than keep filling what you hold, stop. … / Gold and jade fill the hall, and no one can
guard them."* The graph 盈 ("full") is chapter 9's first image, and it is in 55's Tuan, 月盈則食 ("the
moon at full is eaten") — the Wings, not the core. The core's house made great and the chapter's
hall filled share an image, not a character: thematic only.

And they part. Chapter 9 says stop before full. 55's judgment says the fullness gets through, the
ruler reaches it, and 勿憂 ("do not grieve") — it counsels acting at the height, not refusing it. The
graph 豐 ("abundance") itself does occur in the Laozi, chapter 54, as plain good: *"Cultivate this in
your nation, and its integrity becomes bountiful"* — the word there is 豐 ("abundance"). The Laozi's
豐 ("abundance") is integrity grown outward; 55's is a canopy grown over. The difference is left
open.
