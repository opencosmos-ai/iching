## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang ·
stopping). **Across** — water ↔ fire meets mountain ↔ lake, yang on both sides, the odd line at the
middle within and at the top without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 山下有險，險而止，蒙 (*shān xià yǒu xiǎn, xiǎn ér zhǐ,
méng*), "below the mountain there is a hazard; hazard, and a halt: 蒙 (*méng*)." The 大象 (*dà
xiàng*, the great image) turns the hazard into a source: 山下出泉，蒙；君子以果行育德 (*shān xià chū
quán, méng; jūnzǐ yǐ guǒ xíng yù dé*), "a spring comes out below the mountain: 蒙; the noble one, by
it, acts decisively and rears integrity." And the 彖 names the task: 蒙以養正，聖功也 (*méng yǐ
yǎng zhèng, shèng gōng yě*), "to nourish what is right in 蒙 is the sage's work." The tradition reads
the young, covered state and the teaching that meets it.

**What the polarity implies.** Both actions close. Water sinks in; the mountain holds; nothing in
the figure goes out. And both firm lines are guarded: water's is at its centre, wrapped in open
lines, the mountain's is its lid. So the figure is a core with a cap — a spring under a hill before
it has found its way out, which is the 大象's image arrived at from the structure. That changes what
the unknowing is. It is not empty: there is a firm line inside it. The two firm lines are the two
who act on it — 九二 (*jiǔ èr*, the firm second) 包蒙 (*bāo méng*), "enfolding the unknowing", from
inside, and 上九 (*shàng jiǔ*, the firm top) 擊蒙 (*jī méng*), "striking the unknowing", from above.
Everything open between them is the child.

The structure also explains the judgment's odd direction: 匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我 (*fěi wǒ qiú tóng
méng, tóng méng qiú wǒ*), "it is not I who seek the young unknowing; the young unknowing seeks me."
A mountain does not go to the spring; the water must find its own outlet. Against 3: there the force pushes
up from the base into a crowded opening; here it is sunk and capped. 3 is a beginning with too much
push, 4 a beginning with no way out yet.

**For the name.** **Unknowing** — supported and deepened: the signature says the unknowing is full
rather than empty, a spring not yet opened, which is a reason against *Ignorance* (a lack) and for a
word that can be lifted, as *unknowing* can. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Unopened* —
survives the lines: 發蒙 (*fā méng*) "open the unopened", 包蒙 "enfold the unopened", 困蒙 (*kùn
méng*) "the unopened, hemmed in", 童蒙 (*tóng méng*) "the young unopened", 擊蒙 "strike the
unopened." *The Covered Spring* — the image the signature most wants; fails the lines at 童蒙
(*tóng méng*, "the young one's 蒙"), which no spring can be.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**The young, covered state of a thing not yet opened — and how it is rightly opened: when it asks,
once, and by the right hand.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 蒙
("Unknowing") as 王女也 ("sovereign woman"), the name of a plant, with 冡 ("to cover") — itself a graph
for covering — as its sound; the word's everyday sense is *covered over, not yet seeing*. The core
text uses 蒙 ("Unknowing") six times as a condition that others act on: it is opened, enfolded,
hemmed in, young, struck. And its judgment, speaking in the book's own voice, sets the rule for the
one who knows: **wait to be asked, answer the first asking, and do not answer the fourth.**

### The polarities in it

Water within, mountain without. Water sinks in; the mountain stops; nothing in the figure goes out.
The water's firm line is wrapped in its middle, and the mountain's firm line is its lid. The Tuan,
the Wing on the judgment, says it: 山下有險，險而止 ("below the mountain, a hazard; hazard, and stopping"),
and Wang Bi draws out the bind: 退則困險，進則閡山 ("go back and the hazard hems you in; go forward and the
mountain blocks you"). So the unknowing here is not empty. **There is something firm inside it,
capped** — and the two firm lines are the two who act on it: the second line from within, the top
line from above.

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, gives the figure's image: 山下出泉 ("a spring comes out
below the mountain"). This is how springs are made. Rain sinks into a hillside and goes down through
soil and broken rock until it meets a layer it cannot pass; it runs along that layer and comes out
at the foot of the slope — water hidden inside the mountain, finding the one place it can leave.
Walk a valley side after a wet week and look for it: a line of greener grass, rushes, a seep that
becomes a trickle. Wang Bi: 山下出泉未知所適 ("a spring coming out below the mountain does not yet know
where it will go").

A new spring muddies easily. Step in it, stir it again and again, and it clouds; leave it, and it
clears. The judgment's word for asking too often is 瀆 ("muddy"), written with water, which the
Shuowen gives as 溝也 ("a channel"). And the lines hold one more picture from the Shuowen: 包
("enfold"), line 2's *enfold*, is drawn as 象人褢妊，巳在中，象子未成形也 ("a person carrying a child: the unborn
within, the child not yet formed"). The covered thing is not dead. It is carried.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 蒙：亨。匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我。初筮告，再三瀆，瀆則不告。利貞 ("Unknowing *(proposed)*: it gets through. It is
not I who seek the young unknowing; the young unknowing seeks me. The first yarrow-casting is
answered; the second and third muddy it; muddied, it is not answered. Advantageous: holding firm").
The book speaks here as 我 ("I") — the oracle itself — and the Shuowen gives 筮 ("yarrow-casting") as
易卦用蓍也 ("the casting of the Changes with yarrow"). In this setting the Shuowen's older sense of 貞
("the inquiry"), 卜問也 ("asking by the cracked bone"), is close to the surface: the advantage lies in
the inquiry itself, made once.

**Line 1** opens, with a hard hand and a loosening. 發蒙，利用刑人，用說桎梏，以往吝 ("opening the unknowing:
advantageous to use discipline on a person, and to take off the fetters; going on that way —
stinting"). 說 ("taking off") is here *taking off*, not *delight*; the Shuowen's 桎 ("foot fetter")
and 梏 ("hand fetter") are the fetters of the feet and of the hands. The three old translations split
over whether the fetters come off; Wang Bi holds both, and adds the limit: 刑不可長 ("discipline cannot
be kept up").

**Line 2** is the firm line at the lower centre — the teacher inside the water. 包蒙吉，納婦吉，子克家
("enfolding the unknowing: auspicious; taking in a wife: auspicious; the child can run the house").
Held as a child is carried before it has a shape, the unknowing ends as the one who runs the
household. Two auspicious verdicts, the most in the figure.

**Line 3** is at the seam, soft in a firm seat. 勿用取女，見金夫，不有躬，无攸利 ("do not take this woman: she sees
a man of gold and does not keep hold of herself; nothing advantageous"). The image is a Zhou match;
what it names is unknowing that goes after the first bright thing and loses its own person, 躬
("one's own person"). Wang Bi reads the fault as seeking in the wrong direction: 見剛夫而求之 ("it sees a
firm man and seeks him").

**Line 4** is alone. 困蒙，吝 ("unknowing, hemmed in: stinting"). The Shuowen draws 困 ("hemmed in") as
从木在囗中 ("a tree inside an enclosure"). The Small Image, the Wing on each line: 獨遠實也 ("alone, far from
the solid"). The structure agrees exactly: line 4 is the one line with no firm neighbour and no firm
partner below. No teacher is in reach.

**Line 5** is the ruler's seat, held by the child — and the verdict is good. 童蒙，吉 ("the young
unknowing: auspicious"). Wang Bi: 不自任察而委於二 ("it does not trust its own scrutiny, and entrusts itself
to the second line"). Unknowing that knows it is young, and asks.

**Line 6** is the mountain's lid, the firm line at the top. 擊蒙，不利為寇，利禦寇 ("striking the unknowing: no
advantage in being the raider; advantage in warding off raiders"). The blow is allowed only as a
guard — against what would prey on the young, never preying on them.

### In a life now

This is the figure for teaching and being taught: a parent and a child, a master and an apprentice,
a new hand on the first day, a doctor with a frightened patient, anyone meeting a diagnosis or a
craft or a country they do not yet understand. For the one who knows, it counsels **waiting to be
asked**, answering the first real question fully, and not answering the fourth version of it — the
one asked in the hope of a different reply. Discipline at the start, and the fetters off; hold the
not-knowing the way a child is carried; if you strike, strike what threatens. For the one who does
not know, it counsels going to ask, asking once, not running after what glitters, and not staying
alone at a distance from anyone solid. It also describes something common now: asking an adviser, a
search engine, an oracle, again and again until it says what one hoped. The judgment's word for that
is 瀆 ("muddy"): the spring stirred until it will not clear.

The Great Image turns it to practice: 君子以果行育德 ("the noble one, by it, acts decisively and rears
integrity"). Wang Bi ties the first half to the judgment: 果行者初筮之義也 ("acting decisively is the
meaning of the first casting") — ask once, then act.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share an English word here, not a Chinese one — and that is the thing to see.** This
project's Laozi already uses *unknowing*: *"Loving the people and governing the state, can you be
unknowing?"* (chapter 10), and *"so the people are ever unknowing, ever desireless"* (chapter 3).
Its graph is 無知 ("without knowing"), a state the Laozi asks **of** the sage. 蒙 ("Unknowing") is
another graph, and in its lines it is a state to be opened, enfolded, struck. The two-question test
fails at the second question: *unknowing* in the Laozi answers to 無知 ("without knowing"), not to 蒙
("Unknowing").

Wang Bi does build the bridge, in his own layer. The Tuan says 蒙以養正，聖功也 ("to rear what is right in
the unknowing is the sage's work"), and Wang Bi adds 然則養正以明，失其道矣 ("so to rear it by making it clear
is to go off its road"). That is chapter 65 in his mouth — *"did not use it to make the people
clever"* — the 明 ("clarity") that the Laozi translation treats as a declared exception. It is Wang
Bi's reading, not the core's; the most the core grants him is line 5, 童蒙，吉 ("the young unknowing:
auspicious"). Where the Laozi returns to the infant, 蒙 ("Unknowing") sends the child to ask. **That
difference is the point, and if *Unknowing* is kept as the name, it should be kept knowing that a
reader of both books will hear chapter 10 in it.**
