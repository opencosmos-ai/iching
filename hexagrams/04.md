## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang ·
stopping). **Across** — water ↔ fire meets mountain ↔ lake, yang on both sides, the odd line at the
middle within and at the top without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 山下有險，險而止，蒙 (*shān xià yǒu xiǎn, xiǎn ér zhǐ,
méng*), "below the mountain there is a hazard; hazard, and a halt: 蒙 (*méng*)." The 大象 (*dà
xiàng*, the great image) turns the hazard into a source: 山下出泉，蒙；君子以果行育德 (*shān xià chū
quán, méng; jūnzǐ yǐ guǒ xíng yù dé*), "a spring comes out below the mountain: 蒙; the noble one, by
it, acts decisively and rears integrity." And the 彖 names the task: 蒙以養正，聖功也 (*méng yǐ
yǎng zhèng, shèng gōng yě*), "to nourish what is right in 蒙 is the sage's work." The tradition reads
the young, covered state and the teaching that meets it.

**What the polarity implies.** Both actions close. Water sinks in; the mountain holds; nothing in
the figure goes out. And both firm lines are guarded: water's is at its centre, wrapped in open
lines, the mountain's is its lid. So the figure is a core with a cap — a spring under a hill before
it has found its way out, which is the 大象's image arrived at from the structure. That changes what
the unknowing is. It is not empty: there is a firm line inside it. The two firm lines are the two
who act on it — 九二 (*jiǔ èr*, the firm second) 包蒙 (*bāo méng*), "enfolding the unknowing", from
inside, and 上九 (*shàng jiǔ*, the firm top) 擊蒙 (*jī méng*), "striking the unknowing", from above.
Everything open between them is the child.

The structure also explains the judgment's odd direction: 匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我 (*fěi wǒ qiú tóng
méng, tóng méng qiú wǒ*), "it is not I who seek the young unknowing; the young unknowing seeks me."
A mountain does not go to the spring; the water must find its own outlet. Against 3: there the force pushes
up from the base into a crowded opening; here it is sunk and capped. 3 is a beginning with too much
push, 4 a beginning with no way out yet.

**For the name.** **Unknowing** — supported and deepened: the signature says the unknowing is full
rather than empty, a spring not yet opened, which is a reason against *Ignorance* (a lack) and for a
word that can be lifted, as *unknowing* can. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Unopened* —
survives the lines: 發蒙 (*fā méng*) "open the unopened", 包蒙 "enfold the unopened", 困蒙 (*kùn
méng*) "the unopened, hemmed in", 童蒙 (*tóng méng*) "the young unopened", 擊蒙 "strike the
unopened." *The Covered Spring* — the image the signature most wants; fails the lines at 童蒙
(*tóng méng*, "the young one's 蒙"), which no spring can be.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**The young, covered state of a thing not yet opened — and how it is rightly opened: when it asks,
once, and by the right hand.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 蒙 (*méng*) as 王女也 (*wáng nǚ yě*), the name of
a plant, with 冡 (*méng*) — itself a graph for covering — as its sound; the word's everyday sense is
*covered over, not yet seeing*. The core text uses 蒙 (*méng*) six times as a condition that others
act on: it is opened, enfolded, hemmed in, young, struck. And its judgment, speaking in the book's
own voice, sets the rule for the one who knows: **wait to be asked, answer the first asking, and do
not answer the fourth.**

### The polarities in it

Water within, mountain without. Water sinks in; the mountain stops; nothing in the figure goes out.
The water's firm line is wrapped in its middle, and the mountain's firm line is its lid. The 彖
(*tuàn*) says it: 山下有險，險而止 (*shān xià yǒu xiǎn, xiǎn ér zhǐ*, "below the mountain, a hazard;
hazard, and stopping"), and 王弼 draws out the bind: 退則困險，進則閡山 (*tuì zé kùn xiǎn, jìn zé hé
shān*, "go back and the hazard hems you in; go forward and the mountain blocks you"). So the
unknowing here is not empty. **There is something firm inside it, capped** — and the two firm lines
are the two who act on it: the second line from within, the top line from above.

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) gives the figure's image: 山下出泉 (*shān xià chū quán*, "a spring comes out
below the mountain"). This is how springs are made. Rain sinks into a hillside and goes down through
soil and broken rock until it meets a layer it cannot pass; it runs along that layer and comes out
at the foot of the slope — water hidden inside the mountain, finding the one place it can leave. Walk
a valley side after a wet week and look for it: a line of greener grass, rushes, a seep that becomes
a trickle. 王弼: 山下出泉未知所適 (*shān xià chū quán wèi zhī suǒ shì*, "a spring coming out below the
mountain does not yet know where it will go").

A new spring muddies easily. Step in it, stir it again and again, and it clouds; leave it, and it
clears. The judgment's word for asking too often is 瀆 (*dú*), written with water, which 說文 gives as
溝也 (*gōu yě*, "a channel"). And the lines hold one more picture from 說文: 包 (*bāo*), line 2's
*enfold*, is drawn as 象人褢妊，巳在中，象子未成形也 (*xiàng rén huái rèn, sì zài zhōng, xiàng zǐ wèi
chéng xíng yě*, "a person carrying a child: 巳 within, the child not yet formed"). The covered thing is
not dead. It is carried.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 蒙：亨。匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我。初筮告，再三瀆，瀆則不告。利貞 (*méng: hēng. fěi wǒ qiú
tóng méng, tóng méng qiú wǒ. chū shì gào, zài sān dú, dú zé bù gào. lì zhēn*) — "Unknowing
*(proposed)*: it gets through. It is not I who seek the young unknowing; the young unknowing seeks
me. The first yarrow-casting is answered; the second and third muddy it; muddied, it is not
answered. Advantageous: holding firm." The book speaks here as 我 (*wǒ*, "I") — the oracle itself —
and 說文 gives 筮 (*shì*) as 《易》卦用蓍也 (*yì guà yòng shī yě*, "the casting of the Changes with
yarrow"). In this setting 說文's older sense of 貞 (*zhēn*), 卜問也 (*bǔ wèn yě*, "asking by the cracked
bone"), is close to the surface: the advantage lies in the inquiry itself, made once.

**Line 1** opens, with a hard hand and a loosening. 發蒙，利用刑人，用說桎梏，以往吝 (*fā méng, lì yòng
xíng rén, yòng tuō zhì gù, yǐ wǎng lìn*, "opening the unknowing: advantageous to use discipline on a
person, and to take off the fetters; going on that way — stinting"). 說 (*tuō*) is here *taking off*,
not *delight*; 說文's 桎 (*zhì*) and 梏 (*gù*) are the fetters of the feet and of the hands. The three
old translations split over whether the fetters come off; 王弼 holds both, and adds the limit:
刑不可長 (*xíng bù kě cháng*, "discipline cannot be kept up").

**Line 2** is the firm line at the lower centre — the teacher inside the water. 包蒙吉，納婦吉，子克家
(*bāo méng jí, nà fù jí, zǐ kè jiā*, "enfolding the unknowing: auspicious; taking in a wife: auspicious;
the child can run the house"). Held as a child is carried before it has a shape, the unknowing ends
as the one who runs the household. Two auspicious verdicts, the most in the figure.

**Line 3** is at the seam, soft in a firm seat. 勿用取女，見金夫，不有躬，无攸利 (*wù yòng qǔ nǚ, jiàn
jīn fū, bù yǒu gōng, wú yōu lì*, "do not take this woman: she sees a man of gold and does not keep
hold of herself; nothing advantageous"). The image is a Zhou match; what it names is unknowing that
goes after the first bright thing and loses its own person, 躬 (*gōng*). 王弼 reads the fault as
seeking in the wrong direction: 見剛夫而求之 (*jiàn gāng fū ér qiú zhī*, "it sees a firm man and
seeks him").

**Line 4** is alone. 困蒙，吝 (*kùn méng, lìn*, "unknowing, hemmed in: stinting"). 說文 draws 困 (*kùn*)
as 从木在囗中 (*cóng mù zài wéi zhōng*, "a tree inside an enclosure"). The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*):
獨遠實也 (*dú yuǎn shí yě*, "alone, far from the solid"). The structure agrees exactly: line 4 is the
one line with no firm neighbour and no firm partner below. No teacher is in reach.

**Line 5** is the ruler's seat, held by the child — and the verdict is good. 童蒙，吉 (*tóng méng, jí*,
"the young unknowing: auspicious"). 王弼: 不自任察而委於二 (*bù zì rèn chá ér wěi yú èr*, "it does not
trust its own scrutiny, and entrusts itself to the second line"). Unknowing that knows it is young,
and asks.

**Line 6** is the mountain's lid, the firm line at the top. 擊蒙，不利為寇，利禦寇 (*jī méng, bù lì wéi
kòu, lì yù kòu*, "striking the unknowing: no advantage in being the raider; advantage in warding off
raiders"). The blow is allowed only as a guard — against what would prey on the young, never
preying on them.

### In a life now

This is the figure for teaching and being taught: a parent and a child, a master and an apprentice,
a new hand on the first day, a doctor with a frightened patient, anyone meeting a diagnosis or a
craft or a country they do not yet understand. For the one who knows, it counsels **waiting to be
asked**, answering the first real question fully, and not answering the fourth version of it —
the one asked in the hope of a different reply. Discipline at the start, and the fetters off; hold
the not-knowing the way a child is carried; if you strike, strike what threatens. For the one who
does not know, it counsels going to ask, asking once, not running after what glitters, and not
staying alone at a distance from anyone solid. It also describes something common now: asking an
adviser, a search engine, an oracle, again and again until it says what one hoped. The judgment's
word for that is 瀆 (*dú*): the spring stirred until it will not clear.

The 大象 turns it to practice: 君子以果行育德 (*jūnzǐ yǐ guǒ xíng yù dé*, "the noble one, by it, acts
decisively and rears integrity"). 王弼 ties the first half to the judgment: 果行者初筮之義也 (*guǒ xíng
zhě chū shì zhī yì yě*, "acting decisively is the meaning of the first casting") — ask once, then act.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share an English word here, not a Chinese one — and that is the thing to see.**
This project's Laozi already uses *unknowing*: *"Loving the people and governing the state, can you
be unknowing?"* (chapter 10), and *"so the people are ever unknowing, ever desireless"* (chapter 3).
Its graph is 無知 (*wú zhī*, "without knowing"), a state the Laozi asks **of** the sage. 蒙 (*méng*)
is another graph, and in its lines it is a state to be opened, enfolded, struck. The two-question
test fails at the second question: *unknowing* in the Laozi answers to 無知 (*wú zhī*), not to 蒙.

王弼 does build the bridge, in his own layer. The 彖 says 蒙以養正，聖功也 (*méng yǐ yǎng zhèng, shèng
gōng yě*, "to rear what is right in the unknowing is the sage's work"), and 王弼 adds
然則養正以明，失其道矣 (*rán zé yǎng zhèng yǐ míng, shī qí dào yǐ*, "so to rear it by making it clear
is to go off its road"). That is chapter 65 in his mouth — *"did not use it to make the people
clever"* — the 明 (*míng*) that the Laozi translation treats as a declared exception. It is 王弼's
reading, not the core's; the most the core grants him is line 5, 童蒙，吉 (*tóng méng, jí*, "the young
unknowing: auspicious"). Where the Laozi returns to the infant, 蒙 (*méng*) sends the child to ask.
**That difference is the point, and if *Unknowing* is kept as the name, it should be kept knowing
that a reader of both books will hear chapter 10 in it.**

### Words kept

- 蒙 (*méng*) — *unknowing*, the proposed name (not yet approved); the same English is the Laozi translation's word for 無知 (*wú zhī*)
- 童 (*tóng*) — *young*; 說文's entry gives a bound servant, the lines need only the child
- 筮 (*shì*) — *yarrow-casting*; 瀆 (*dú*) — *muddy*, a water graph
- 說 (*tuō*) — *taking off*, here; not *delight*, not *releasing*
- 包 (*bāo*) — *enfold*; 困 (*kùn*) — *hemmed in*; 擊 (*jī*) — *strike*; 寇 (*kòu*) — *raider*
- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked); 明 (*míng*) — *clarity* (locked)
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss (A6); in the judgment the older *inquiry* is close to the surface
- 亨 (*hēng*) — *gets through*, working gloss (A6)
- 吉 · 吝 (*jí · lìn*) — *auspicious · stinting*, working glosses (A4)
