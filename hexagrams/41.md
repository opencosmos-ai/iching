## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Crossed** — both poles of mountain ↔ lake, the spectrum whose odd line is at the top.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 reads the figure as a transfer — 損下益上 (*sǔn xià yì shàng*,
"decrease below, increase above") — and will not let the transfer become a rule: 損剛益柔有時 (*sǔn
gāng yì róu yǒu shí*, "decreasing the firm to increase the yielding has its time"); 損益盈虛，與時偕行
(*sǔn yì yíng xū, yǔ shí xié xíng*, "decrease and increase, filling and emptying, go along with the
time"). The 大象傳 turns it inward: 山下有澤，損；君子以懲忿窒欲 (*shān xià yǒu zé, sǔn; jūnzǐ yǐ
chéng fèn zhì yù*, "below the mountain there is a lake: decrease; the noble one checks anger and stops
up desire"). The tradition takes 損 (*sǔn*, "decrease") as a giving-up from below that is right when
it is timely, and as self-restraint.

**What the polarity implies.** The two trigrams are one line apart, and it is the same line. The lake
is the sky with its top line opened; the mountain is the earth with its top line closed. Set one
under the other and the lake's missing summit is the mountain's summit — what the hollow lost, the
height gained. That is 損下益上 (*sǔn xià yì shàng*, "decrease below, increase above") drawn rather
than argued: dig the lake and the spoil is the mountain. The exchange happens at the top of each
trigram, at the mouth and the peak, where it can be seen.

Releasing within, stopping without: what the self would let out — the lake's 說 (*yuè*, delight,
release, speech) — meets a situation that closes over it. So the 大象傳 can read restraint without
leaving the figure; anger and desire are the lake's outflow, and the mountain caps it. The structure
suggests what the moral reading skips: the capping is not waste. The mountain is built of what the
lake gave up, so the restraint below is what stands above. The core text puts the moved line in its
own third place: 三人行，則損一人 (*sān rén xíng, zé sǔn yī rén*, "three walk together, and one is
subtracted"). And 31 咸 (*xián*, the figure of being moved) is these two trigrams the other way up;
損 (*sǔn*, "decrease") is its reverse, the figure of giving.

**For the name.** *Decrease* — the signature deepens it: a decrease that is also the building of
something above, which is why the lines can say 弗損益之 (*fú sǔn yì zhī*, "without decreasing it,
increase it") twice without contradicting the name. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Paring*,
*Tribute* — *Paring* survives: 酌損之 (*zhuó sǔn zhī*, "weigh how much to pare it"), 損其疾 (*sǔn qí
jí*, "pare away the sickness") and 三人行，則損一人 (*sān rén xíng, zé sǔn yī rén*, "three walk
together, and one is pared off") all read, though it loses the pairing with 42's *Increase*;
*Tribute* — the lake giving up to the mountain — fails the lines: 損其疾 (*sǔn qí jí*) cannot be
"tribute the sickness", and 弗損益之 (*fú sǔn yì zhī*) makes no sense as "not tributing, increase it".

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Less, measured out with care, is enough — and a decrease made with confidence turns, by the top of
the figure, into increase.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 損 (*sǔn*) as 減也 (*jiǎn yě*, "to lessen"), with
the hand (手, *shǒu*) as its radical: decrease here is something done, not something that befalls
you. The judgment asks its own question and answers it at the plainest level there is: 曷之用？二簋可用享
(*hé zhī yòng? èr guǐ kě yòng xiǎng*, "what is to be used? two bowls will serve for the offering").
The 簋 (*guǐ*) is, in 說文, 黍稷方器也 (*shǔ jì fāng qì yě*, "the square vessel for millet") — an
everyday grain dish. And the lines keep finding that the giving leaves no one
poorer: 弗損益之 (*fú sǔn yì zhī*, "without decreasing, increase it"), twice.

### The polarities in it

Lake within, mountain without: both poles of the spectrum whose odd line is at the top. The lake is
open at its lip and the mountain closed at its summit, so the one line the lake lacks is the one the
mountain has (see *The signature*, above). The figure draws what the 彖 says, 損下益上 (*sǔn xià yì
shàng*, "decrease below, increase above"): what the self would let out is stopped, and the stopping
is built of what was given up. Restraint here is not waste. But the Wings will not let the direction
harden into a rule — 損剛益柔有時 (*sǔn gāng yì róu yǒu shí*, "decreasing the firm to increase the
yielding has its time", 彖) — and the core text agrees in its own way: its two firm lines, the second
and the top, give by **not** decreasing themselves. Not every giving is a giving away.

### In nature

Look at a tarn under a peak in high country. The bowl the water lies in and the steep wall above it
were cut by the same ice: one removal made the hollow and the height. On a cool still morning, mist
lifts off the water and drifts up the slope as the sun warms it — the lake going up into the
mountain. That is the 大象's picture, 山下有澤 (*shān xià yǒu zé*, "below the mountain there is a
lake").

The lines hold a vessel and a creature. 酌 (*zhuó*, line 1) is in 說文 盛酒行觴也 (*chéng jiǔ xíng
shāng yě*, "filling the wine and passing the cup") — the measure a host uses, ladling enough for
each guest, and the jar goes round lighter. The tortoise of line 5, 十朋之龜 (*shí péng zhī guī*, "a
tortoise worth ten strings of shells"), is in 說文 舊也 (*jiù yě*, "old"), and 外骨内肉 (*wài gǔ nèi
ròu*, "bone outside, flesh within"); its shell was cracked to settle doubt — 王弼, 龜者決疑之物也 (*guī zhě jué yí zhī wù yě*, "the tortoise
is the thing that settles doubt"). Anyone who thins the young fruit on a tree in early summer knows
the rest of this hexagram without reading it: fewer, and the ones left grow.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 損：有孚，元吉。无咎，可貞，利有攸往 (*sǔn: yǒu fú, yuán jí. wú jiù, kě zhēn, lì yǒu
yōu wǎng*, "decrease: there is confidence — from the source, auspicious; no blame; one can hold firm;
advantageous to have somewhere to go"). Nearly every favourable formula the book has, heaped on a
word for less — and then the two plain bowls. 孚 (*fú*, "confidence") is the condition: a decrease
made without it is only loss. The 彖 adds 二簋應有時 (*èr guǐ yìng yǒu shí*, "two bowls answer to a
time").

**Line 1** goes at once, and measures. 已事遄往，无咎，酌損之 (*yǐ shì chuán wǎng, wú jiù, zhuó sǔn
zhī*, "the affair done, go quickly — no blame; weigh out how much to decrease it"). Speed and measure
together: pour, do not empty the jar.

**Line 2**, the firm centre below, keeps its place. 利貞，征凶，弗損益之 (*lì zhēn, zhēng xiōng, fú sǔn
yì zhī*, "advantageous to hold firm; marching out, ominous; without decreasing, increase it"). The
only 凶 (*xiōng*, "ominous") in the figure falls here, on the centre that would leave the centre.

**Line 3** is the seam, and the line the signature says moved. 三人行，則損一人；一人行，則得其友 (*sān
rén xíng, zé sǔn yī rén; yī rén xíng, zé dé qí yǒu*, "three walk together, and one is subtracted; one
walks alone, and finds a friend"). The 小象 gives the reason: 三則疑也 (*sān zé yí yě*, "with three,
there is doubt"). Decrease as finding the right number.

**Line 4** is the first line of the receiving trigram, and what it has to lose is its illness. 損其疾，
使遄有喜，无咎 (*sǔn qí jí, shǐ chuán yǒu xǐ, wú jiù*, "decrease the sickness; make it quick, and
there is gladness — no blame"). For the one above, decrease is a cure.

**Line 5**, the yielding centre above, receives without asking. 或益之，十朋之龜弗克違，元吉 (*huò yì
zhī, shí péng zhī guī fú kè wéi, yuán jí*, "someone increases it; a tortoise worth ten strings, not to
be refused — from the source, auspicious"). Turn the figure over and this line is 42's second, word
for word: the same gift, seen from below.

**Line 6** is decrease completed, and it has become increase. 弗損益之，无咎，貞吉，利有攸往，得臣无家
(*fú sǔn yì zhī, wú jiù, zhēn jí, lì yǒu yōu wǎng, dé chén wú jiā*, "without decreasing, increase it —
no blame; holding firm, auspicious; advantageous to have somewhere to go; one gains servants who
belong to no household"). The 序卦 draws the next figure from here: 損而不已，必益 (*sǔn ér bù yǐ, bì
yì*, "decrease that does not stop must become increase").

### In a life now

This is the lean year met on purpose: the household that sells the second car, the manuscript
that loses a third of its pages and gets better, the friend who steps out of a three-way
partnership so the other two can work, the plain funeral that is whole with two bowls. It is also
line 4's decrease, the most personal: the drink given up, the grievance put down. The text counsels
**measure** (pour, do not empty), **speed once the matter is settled**, and **confidence**; it warns
the firm centre against giving itself away (line 2). What it promises is not merit but a turn: by
the top, what was given has come back as increase.

The 大象 turns it inward: 君子以懲忿窒欲 (*jūnzǐ yǐ chéng fèn zhì yù*, "the noble one checks anger
and stops up desire"). 窒 (*zhì*) is in 說文 塞也 (*sè yě*, "to block"), with a hole (穴, *xué*) as its
radical: the first thing to decrease is what leaks out of oneself.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One graph shared, and one direction disputed.** The Laozi's chapter 48: *"In pursuit of learning,
you increase daily; / in pursuit of the Tao, you decrease daily. / Decreasing and decreasing, until
you reach non-doing."* The graph is 損 (*sǔn*, "decrease"), in the core text here, and *decrease*
answers to it in both books; even 損之 (*sǔn zhī*, "decrease it") is shared with line 1. But line 1
**measures** — 酌 (*zhuó*, "weigh out as wine is poured") — and the 彖 ties decrease to its time;
chapter 48's decrease has no measure and no season, and goes on until nothing is being done.

And the direction divides them. In chapter 77 the way of people *"takes from those who have too
little / and offers it up to those who have too much."* The 彖's 損下益上 (*sǔn xià yì shàng*,
"decrease below, increase above") looks like exactly that, and 王弼 saw it:
損下益上非補不足也 (*sǔn xià yì shàng fēi bǔ bù zú yě*, "decreasing below to increase above is not
mending what is too little"). He saves 41 only by 孚 (*fú*, "confidence") and by time. **The books part
here and this reading leaves them parted**: the Laozi condemns the upward flow; the Wings permit it
when it is timely and made with confidence.
