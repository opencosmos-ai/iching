## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — thunder ↔ wind under sky ↔ earth, both poles yang.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) finds the inner trigram's one firm line arriving from
beyond: 剛自外來，而為主於內 (*gāng zì wài lái, ér wéi zhǔ yú nèi*, "the firm comes from outside and
becomes the master within"), then 動而健 (*dòng ér jiàn*, "moving out, and vigour"), and calls the
whole 天之命也 (*tiān zhī mìng yě*, "the allotted course of the sky"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 天下雷行，物與无妄
(*tiān xià léi xíng, wù yǔ wú wàng*, "under the sky thunder goes, and each thing is given its
without-wildness"). The tradition reads a motion that adds nothing of its own — and 雜卦 (*záguà*)
reads the other face of it: 无妄災也 (*wú wàng zāi yě*, "无妄 is calamity").

**What the polarity implies.** Thunder is sky's first draw — 震一索而得男 (*zhèn yī suǒ ér dé nán*,
"thunder is the first draw, and gets a son", 說卦 ch 10): one firm line at the base, the rest open.
Here that line sits directly under three firm ones, so the impulse at the root is made of the same
stuff as everything above it. It has no author but the sky; that is what the 彖 means by the firm
"coming from outside". Nothing is schemed between the start and what governs it — 不耕穫
(*bù gēng huò*, "reaping without ploughing", 25.2).

The same shape explains the calamity. Both poles yang means there is no yielding trigram anywhere to
take a shock — no earth to receive the thunder, no wind to spread it. Turn the two trigrams the
other way and you have 34 大壯 (*dà zhuàng*, "great vigour"), strength inside pushing out; here the
push is inside and the strength is the world, and the push cannot choose what it meets. The
structure suggests 无妄's harm is the blow that lands on someone who did nothing — 邑人之災 (*yì rén
zhī zāi*, "the townsman's calamity", 25.3) — and that it is not to be corrected: 无妄之疾，勿藥有喜
(*wú wàng zhī jí, wù yào yǒu xǐ*, "an illness of 无妄: take no medicine, and there will be joy", 25.5).

**For the name.** **Innocence** — deepens: the signature makes the innocent not merely guiltless but
unauthored, a motion that is the sky's before it is one's own, and the undeserved calamity follows
from the same figure. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *The Unplanned* — survives: 无妄，往吉
(*wú wàng, wǎng jí*, "unplanned: going is auspicious", 25.1) and 无妄之災 (*wú wàng zhī zāi*, "the
calamity of the unplanned", 25.3) both read, and it keeps 雜卦's calamity; *Spontaneity* — fails the
lines: 无妄之疾 (*wú wàng zhī jí*) as "the illness of spontaneity" makes a fault of what 25.5 says is
none, and the word is on the 自然 (*zì rán*, "of itself") lock's forbidden list. The name waits on
WORKLIST A11 (does the lock on 無, *wú*, bind 无), which can confirm it but not decide it.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Things arrive that nobody schemed — a harvest, a theft, an illness — and the counsel is to add
nothing wild of one's own.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 妄 ("wildness")
as 亂也 ("disorder"), the same 亂 ("disorder") that the Tao Te Ching's lock renders *disorder*: acting
beyond what fits. In the core text 无妄 ("without wildness") qualifies four different things — a going
(lines 1 and 6), a calamity (line 3), an illness (line 5). Two of those are good fortune and two are
harm, and none of them was brought on. The hexagram is about what comes **unauthored**, and about
keeping one's own hand out of it.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, sky without. Thunder's one firm line sits at the base, two open lines rest on it,
and above them the sky is firm all the way up — so the impulse at the root is made of the same stuff
as what governs it, and the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, can say 剛自外來，而為主於內 ("the firm comes from
outside and becomes the master within"; see *The signature*, above). Nothing is contrived between
the start and the whole; that is the good face. The hard face is that no yielding trigram stands
anywhere to take a blow. **What moves in this figure moves because the world moves, and it cannot
choose what it strikes.**

### In nature

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, sees 天下雷行 ("under the sky, thunder goes"). Stand out
in a spring storm and notice two things. The lightning strikes the tallest tree on the ridge, or a
beast in the open field — not the guilty one; it has no aim but height. And the storm leaves the
ground richer than it found it: the discharge fixes nitrogen out of the air, the rain carries it
down, and the grass greens after thunder that nobody sowed for. The same storm is harvest and
calamity. The Great Image goes on: 先王以茂對時，育萬物 ("the former rulers, flourishing, met the season, and
reared the countless things") — met it, not forced it.

Line 2's fields are exact. The Shuowen has 菑 ("a newly cleared field") as 不耕田也 ("a field not yet
ploughed") — ground newly cleared — and 畬 ("a third-year field") as 三歲治田也 ("a field worked three
years"). Look at an old clearing left alone: it fills with self-sown seedlings, volunteers from last
year's crop and the hedge. Harvest without the ploughing.

And look at the judgment's word for harm. 眚 ("a clouding") is in the Shuowen 目病生翳也 ("an eye
sickness: a film growing over it"). Beside it, line 3 uses 災 ("calamity") and line 5 疾 ("illness").
The core text keeps two kinds of harm apart — the film that grows in one's own seeing, and the thing
that simply arrives.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 无妄：元亨。利貞。其匪正有眚，不利有攸往 ("Without wildness: from the source, it gets through; holding
firm is advantageous. If it is not upright there is a clouding, and it is not advantageous to have
somewhere to go."). The weight is on the condition: the good holds only while nothing crooked is
added. 眚 ("a clouding") is the harm that is one's own.

**Line 1** is thunder's firm line, the root. 无妄，往吉 ("without wildness — going is auspicious"). The
first move, made without scheme, goes well.

**Line 2**, open, in the centre below. 不耕穫，不菑畬，則利有攸往 ("not ploughing, yet reaping; not clearing, yet
the third-year field — then it is advantageous to have somewhere to go"). The harvest was not
engineered. The Small Image, the Wing on each line, adds 未富也 ("not yet rich"): taking it is not the
same as having earned it. Wang Bi reads the line as not claiming the credit — 不擅其美 ("not taking the
fine thing as one's own").

**Line 3**, at the seam, open in a firm place. 无妄之災，或系之牛，行人之得，邑人之災 ("the calamity of the unwild:
someone tethers an ox; the passer-by's gain is the townsfolk's calamity"). The ox is taken by one
who is gone by nightfall; suspicion falls on the ones who stay. No one here did anything.

**Line 4**, firm, on the sky's threshold. 可貞，无咎 ("one can hold firm — no blame"). The Small Image:
固有之也 ("it is firmly had"). Hold what is already there.

**Line 5** is the centre above, and the crux. 无妄之疾，勿藥有喜 ("an illness not brought on: no medicine,
and there will be joy"). The Shuowen gives 藥 ("medicine") as 治病艸 ("the herb that treats illness").
The Small Image says 不可試也 ("it is not to be tried"), and Wang Bi is blunter: 非妄而藥之則凶 ("to medicate
what is not wildness is ominous"). Meddling is the wildness the figure warns against.

**Line 6**, firm at the top, the end of the sky. 无妄，行有眚，无攸利 ("without wildness — going brings a
clouding; nothing is advantageous"). The same unschemed going that was auspicious at line 1 goes
wrong at the end. The Small Image names why: 窮之災也 ("the calamity of reaching the end"). It is a
matter of when.

Notice what is missing: 凶 ("ominous") is nowhere in the core text of this hexagram. Its harms are
named as a clouding, a calamity, an illness — events, not verdicts.

### In a life now

The ox is the neighbour's suspicion after a theft, the layoff that follows someone else's error, the
diagnosis that runs in a family, the windfall — an inheritance, a lucky meeting, a crop from a field
one never tended. The figure does not ask why. It asks what one adds. It counsels going forward
cleanly at the start (line 1); taking the unearned harvest without calling it one's own (line 2);
holding what one already has (line 4); and, hardest, leaving alone what will pass of itself — the
fever that runs its course, the quarrel that needs no answer, the grief that is not an illness (line
5). The line is not a rule against medicine; it names an illness *not brought on* and warns against
the reflex to intervene. And at the end of a run of good fortune it says stop (line 6).

The Great Image's 茂對時 ("flourishing, meet the season") turns it to practice: do the thing the season
asks, and nothing it does not.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The graph is shared, and the books part over it.** The Laozi's chapter 16 says: *"Not knowing the
ever-present brings reckless action and disaster."* The Chinese there is 妄作 ("acting wildly") and 凶
("ominous") — 妄 ("wildness") is the very word this hexagram is built on, in the core text, and
Shalom's *reckless* answers to it. In both books wildness is what a person adds to what already
moves.

But the Laozi's line runs one way: wildness brings disaster. 无妄 ("without wildness") refuses the
converse. Line 3's 无妄之災 ("the calamity of the unwild") says that keeping one's hand out does not
keep harm away. The Laozi promises that knowing the ever-present means *"no danger will find you"*;
the ox is taken all the same. **The I Ching does not make that promise, and this reading does not
lend it one.**

### Words kept

- 无妄 ("Innocence") — the proposed name (not yet approved; waits on WORKLIST A11, whether the lock on
  無 ("absence") binds 无 ("without")); "without wildness" as a working phrase
- 妄 ("wildness") — the Shuowen 亂也 ("disorder"), and 亂 ("disorder") is locked as *disorder*, never
  *chaos*
- 眚 ("a clouding") — proposed, to keep the Shuowen's film over the eye; distinct from 災 ("calamity")
  and 疾 ("illness")
- 萬物 ("the countless things") — locked; 王 ("ruler") — never *king* (locked)
- 元亨 ("from the source, gets through") · 利貞 ("holding firm is advantageous") — working glosses (A6)
- 无咎 ("no blame") · 吉 ("auspicious") — working glosses (A4); *blameless* is not free for 无妄
  ("without wildness"), because 无咎 ("no blame") stands in line 4
- 天之命 ("the allotted course of the sky"; the Tuan) — 命 ("the allotted course") is never *mandate* or
  *fate*
