## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang ·
stopping). **Across** — thunder ↔ wind under mountain ↔ lake, both poles yang.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 養正則吉也 (*yǎng zhèng zé jí yě*, "nourishing what
is right is auspicious") and 觀頤，觀其所養也 (*guān yí, guān qí suǒ yǎng yě*, "look at the jaws: look
at what they nourish"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 山下有雷 (*shān xià yǒu léi*, "below the mountain,
thunder"), and the noble one 慎言語，節飲食 (*shèn yán yǔ, jié yǐn shí*, "is careful in speech, and
measured in eating and drinking"). The tradition reads the jaws as nourishment rightly taken, and 雜卦
(*záguà*) agrees: 頤養正也 (*yí yǎng zhèng yě*, "頤 is nourishing rightly").

**What the polarity implies.** The figure draws itself. Its two odd lines sit as far apart as six
places allow — thunder's firm line at the very bottom, mountain's at the very top — with four open
lines between: a jaw, open. Thunder is the lower jaw, which moves; mountain is the upper, which holds
still; chewing is exactly that, motion worked against a stop. The same stop governs what goes in and
what comes out, which is why the 大象 puts speech and food in one sentence.

And the lines divide on the trigram line, cleanly. Every line within, on the moving side, is ominous:
觀我朵頤，凶 (*guān wǒ duǒ yí, xiōng*, "watching my working jaws — ominous", 27.1), 征凶 (*zhēng
xiōng*, "setting out — ominous", 27.2), 貞凶 (*zhēn xiōng*, "holding firm — ominous", 27.3). Every line
without, on the stopping side, is auspicious — even the tiger, 虎視眈眈，其欲逐逐 (*hǔ shì dān dān, qí
yù zhú zhú*, "the tiger stares and stares, its wanting chases on", 27.4) — and the top line, the
upper jaw itself, is 由頤 (*yóu yí*, "where the nourishing comes from"). The structure suggests that
here appetite alone is the danger and the stop is what feeds: the moving jaw is nourished only by the
one that holds.

**For the name.** **Jaws** — deepens: the signature makes the figure a jaw in fact, the lower moving
and the upper stopping, so the name, the figure and the lines say one thing. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Nourishing* — fails the lines: 觀我朵頤 (*guān wǒ duǒ yí*, "watching my
working jaws", 27.1) needs the body part, not the act; *The Mouth* — fails the lines: it collides with
自求口實 (*zì qiú kǒu shí*, "seek for yourself what fills the mouth") in the hexagram's own judgment.
The signature finds nothing better than the name it already has.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Look at the jaws: what fills the mouth has to be sought for oneself, and the jaw that moves is fed
only by the one that holds still.** 頤 (*yí*, "jaws") is on 說文's (*shuōwén*) unmatched list here,
so this reads the graph as it stands: 頁 (*yè*, "the head") beside an older drawing of the jaw. The
judgment's instruction is to look — 觀頤 (*guān yí*, "look at the jaws") — and the whole hexagram is
about where a person's eye goes while they eat: to their own food, or to someone else's mouth.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, mountain without, and the figure is a picture of itself: a firm line at the bottom,
a firm line at the top, four open lines between — a jaw, open (see *The signature*, above). Thunder
is the lower jaw, which moves; the mountain is the upper, which stops. The core text divides on
exactly that seam. Every line of the moving trigram is ominous; every line of the still one is
auspicious. **Appetite alone goes wrong. What feeds is motion worked against a stop.**

### In nature

Put a finger on your own jaw and chew. Only the lower jaw moves; the upper is part of the skull and
does not. Every mammal eats this way. Watch a cow at the cud: it has no upper front teeth at all,
only a hard pad, and the lower jaw grinds sideways against it for hours. The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees
山下有雷 (*shān xià yǒu léi*, "below the mountain, thunder") — the rumble under the still mass. It is
anatomy before it is weather.

Line 1's creature is the tortoise, 龜 (*guī*). A tortoise eats slowly and can go months without food
through a cold season; it was kept in Zhou for divining, its shell read under heat. 靈 (*líng*,
"numinous") is in 說文 as the word for one who 以玉事神 (*yǐ yù shì shén*, "serves the numinous with
jade"). Line 4's creature is the tiger, and 說文 describes its look exactly: 眈 (*dān*) is 視近而志遠
(*shì jìn ér zhì yuǎn*, "looking at what is near, with the aim far off"). A tiger stalks close under
cover, holds still, stares, and most of its hunts still fail; it waits and tries again. The line's
逐 (*zhú*, "chase") is in 說文 追也 (*zhuī yě*, "to pursue"), drawn as feet after a young pig.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 頤：貞吉。觀頤，自求口實 (*yí: zhēn jí. guān yí, zì qiú kǒu shí*) — "Jaws. Holding firm:
auspicious. Look at the jaws; seek for yourself what fills the mouth." 口實 (*kǒu shí*, "what fills
the mouth") is food. The counsel is to look, and then to seek one's own.

**Line 1** is the lower jaw itself. 舍爾靈龜，觀我朵頤，凶 (*shě ěr líng guī, guān wǒ duǒ yí, xiōng*,
"you set aside your numinous tortoise and watch my working jaws — ominous"). 王弼: 朶頥者嚼也 (*duǒ yí
zhě jiáo yě*, "working jaws means chewing"; his printing writes the variant graphs 朶頥 for 朵頤). The one who had the creature that needs almost nothing
now watches another eat.

**Line 2**, open, in the lower centre. 顛頤，拂經，于丘頤，征凶 (*diān yí, fú jīng, yú qiū yí, zhēng
xiōng*, "the jaws upended, going against the warp; to the hill for feeding — setting out is
ominous"). 經 (*jīng*) is the warp thread, the standing line; 王弼 reads 顛 (*diān*, "upended") as
feeding from below, and 丘 (*qiū*, "hill") — in 說文 土之高也，非人所爲也 (*tǔ zhī gāo yě, fēi rén suǒ wéi
yě*, "earth's height, not made by people") — as the high place one goes up to for food.

**Line 3**, open, at the seam. 拂頤，貞凶，十年勿用，无攸利 (*fú yí, zhēn xiōng, shí nián wù yòng, wú yōu
lì*, "going against the jaws: holding firm is ominous; ten years, do not act; nothing is
advantageous"). The worst line: feeding itself turned wrong, and held to.

**Line 4**, open, the base of the mountain. 顛頤吉，虎視眈眈，其欲逐逐，无咎 (*diān yí jí, hǔ shì dān dān, qí
yù zhú zhú, wú jiù*, "the jaws upended: auspicious. The tiger looks, stares and stares; its wanting
chases on and on — no blame"). The same upending that was ominous at line 2 is auspicious here,
because now the high feeds the low. And the tiger's wanting is not condemned: it is steady, and it
waits.

**Line 5**, open, the upper centre. 拂經，居貞吉，不可涉大川 (*fú jīng, jū zhēn jí, bù kě shè dà chuān*,
"going against the warp: staying put and holding firm is auspicious; the great river cannot be
crossed"). Line 2 and line 5 share 拂經 (*fú jīng*, "going against the warp"). Line 2 sets out and it
is ominous; line 5 stays and it is auspicious. The deviation is the same; the movement decides.

**Line 6** is the upper jaw. 由頤，厲吉，利涉大川 (*yóu yí, lì jí, lì shè dà chuān*, "where the feeding
comes from: peril, and auspicious; it is advantageous to cross the great river"). The still jaw is
the source, and to be the source is peril.

### In a life now

This is the figure for everything a person takes in — food first, then money, praise, news, other
people's attention — and for everything they give out, since the 大象 puts both in one mouth. Line
1 is the scroll through other lives while one's own table goes unset; line 2 is looking to the
wrong quarter for support; line 3 is the habit that feeds nothing, kept up. Line 4 is a parent or a
teacher or an employer feeding those below with the tiger's patience, and line 6 is the burden of
being the one others eat from. The core text counsels looking before taking, seeking one's own, and
staying put when one's feeding is out of line.

The 大象 makes it daily practice: 君子以慎言語，節飲食 (*jūnzǐ yǐ shèn yán yǔ, jié yǐn shí*, "the noble
one is careful in speech, and measured in eating and drinking"). One jaw governs both.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One graph shared, and a disagreement about who fills the mouth.** The Laozi's chapter 3 has the
sage governing by *"emptying their hearts and filling their bellies."* *Filling* is 實 (*shí*) — the
same graph as the judgment's 口實 (*kǒu shí*, "what fills the mouth"), in the core text, and Shalom's
English answers to it. Chapter 12's *"the sage acts for the belly, not for the eye"* reads close to
line 1's eye on another's jaws, but that is thematic only — 目 (*mù*, "eye") is not in this hexagram.

They part on the subject. In chapter 3 the sage fills other people's bellies. The core text of 頤
(*yí*, "jaws") says 自求 (*zì qiú*, "seek for yourself"). It is the 彖 (*tuàn*), a Wing, that comes
round to the Laozi's side — 聖人養賢，以及萬民 (*shèngrén yǎng xián, yǐ jí wàn mín*, "the sage nourishes
the worthy, and so reaches the countless people"). **The core asks each mouth to seek its own;
this reading keeps that, and marks where the Wing moves toward the Laozi.**

### Words kept

- 頤 (*yí*) — *Jaws*, the proposed name (not yet approved); not *Nourishing*, which loses the body part line 1 needs, and not *The Mouth*, which collides with 口實
- 口實 (*kǒu shí*) — *what fills the mouth*
- 養 (*yǎng*) — *nourish* (Wings); 說文 供養也 (*gōng yǎng yě*, "to provide for")
- 靈 (*líng*) — *numinous*; 神 (*shén*) is locked *numinous*, never *divine*, and 靈龜 (*líng guī*, "the numinous tortoise") must not become a *sacred* tortoise
- 顛 · 拂 · 經 (*diān · fú · jīng*) — *upended · going against · the warp*, working
- 萬 (*wàn*) — *countless* (locked with 萬物)
- 貞吉 · 凶 · 厲 · 无咎 (*zhēn jí · xiōng · lì · wú jiù*) — *holding firm: auspicious · ominous · peril · no blame*, working glosses (A4, A6)
