## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang ·
holding). **Across** — thunder ↔ wind under mountain ↔ lake, both poles yang.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 養正則吉也 (*yǎng zhèng zé jí yě*, "nourishing what
is right is auspicious") and 觀頤，觀其所養也 (*guān yí, guān qí suǒ yǎng yě*, "look at the jaws: look
at what they nourish"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 山下有雷 (*shān xià yǒu léi*, "below the mountain,
thunder"), and the noble one 慎言語，節飲食 (*shèn yán yǔ, jié yǐn shí*, "is careful in speech, and
measured in eating and drinking"). The tradition reads the jaws as nourishment rightly taken, and 雜卦
(*záguà*) agrees: 頤養正也 (*yí yǎng zhèng yě*, "頤 is nourishing rightly").

**What the polarity implies.** The figure draws itself. Its two odd lines sit as far apart as six
places allow — thunder's firm line at the very bottom, mountain's at the very top — with four open
lines between: a jaw, open. Thunder is the lower jaw, which moves; mountain is the upper, which holds
still; chewing is exactly that, motion worked against a stop. The same stop governs what goes in and
what comes out, which is why the 大象 puts speech and food in one sentence.

And the lines divide on the trigram line, cleanly. Every line within, on the moving side, is ominous:
觀我朵頤，凶 (*guān wǒ duǒ yí, xiōng*, "watching my working jaws — ominous", 27.1), 征凶 (*zhēng
xiōng*, "setting out — ominous", 27.2), 貞凶 (*zhēn xiōng*, "perseverance — ominous", 27.3). Every line
without, on the holding side, is auspicious — even the tiger, 虎視眈眈，其欲逐逐 (*hǔ shì dān dān, qí
yù zhú zhú*, "the tiger stares and stares, its wanting chases on", 27.4) — and the top line, the
upper jaw itself, is 由頤 (*yóu yí*, "where the nourishing comes from"). The structure suggests that
here appetite alone is the danger and the stop is what feeds: the moving jaw is nourished only by the
one that holds.

**For the name.** **Jaws** — deepens: the signature makes the figure a jaw in fact, the lower moving
and the upper holding, so the name, the figure and the lines say one thing. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Nourishing* — fails the lines: 觀我朵頤 (*guān wǒ duǒ yí*, "watching my
working jaws", 27.1) needs the body part, not the act; *The Mouth* — fails the lines: it collides with
自求口實 (*zì qiú kǒu shí*, "seek for yourself what fills the mouth") in the hexagram's own judgment.
The signature finds nothing better than the name it already has.
