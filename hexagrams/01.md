## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Doubled** — the yang pole of sky ↔ earth taken twice, with no odd line anywhere in the six.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image) gives the plainest version:
天行健，君子以自強不息 (*tiān xíng jiàn, jūnzǐ yǐ zì qiáng bù xī*), "the sky moves with vigour; the
noble one, by it, makes the self strong and does not stop." The 彖 (*tuàn*) makes the figure the
start of everything: 大哉乾元，萬物資始，乃統天 (*dà zāi qián yuán, wànwù zī shǐ, nǎi tǒng tiān*),
"great is 乾's (*qián*'s) origin — the countless things draw their start from it, and so it runs
through the sky" — and orders that start in time: 時乘六龍以御天 (*shí chéng liù lóng yǐ yù tiān*),
"in its season it rides six dragons to drive the sky." The tradition reads pure initiating force,
paced by the six positions.

**What the polarity implies.** Every trigram but 乾 (*qián*) and 坤 (*kūn*) is defined by where its odd line stands;
here there is no odd line inside or out, so nothing in the figure marks a place. With no height to
read, the six lines can only be read as *time*: the dragon hidden (潛龍 *qián lóng*), seen in the
field, striving all day, perhaps leaping in the deep, flying in the sky, overreaching. The six
dragons of the 彖 arrive straight from the structure. Vigour within meets vigour without, so
nothing pushes back — and the structure suggests the figure's hazard is therefore not an obstacle
but the absence of one. A force with no counter-pole in its own figure has to supply its own check,
which is exactly what line 3 does: 夕惕若 (*xī tì ruò*), "wary at dusk." When it does not, the top
line has it: 亢龍有悔 (*kàng lóng yǒu huǐ*), "the overreaching dragon has regret."

Read with 2, the pair is not strong against weak but beginning against bearing: 乾 (*qián*) 資始
(*zī shǐ*), "draws the start", where 坤 (*kūn*) 資生 (*zī shēng*), "draws the birth". Each, doubled
to its top, runs into the other — 乾's dragon overreaches; 坤's top line is a dragon fighting in the
open country. And their two extra lines part ways: 乾's 用九 (*yòng jiǔ*, "using the nines") ends in
見羣龍无首 (*jiàn qún lóng wú shǒu*), "seeing a flock of dragons with no head" — pure vigour, led by
nobody, is auspicious; 坤's ends in duration.

**For the name.** **Striving** — the signature deepens it: 乾乾 (*qián qián*) in line 3 doubles the
graph as the figure doubles the trigram, and an unopposed force is one that has to keep itself
going and keep itself wary, which *striving* carries and *Vigour* (kept for 34, and the action-word
of this very trigram) would not. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Unresting* — from 自強不息
(*zì qiáng bù xī*, "makes the self strong and does not stop"); fails the lines, since the first is
潛龍勿用 (*qián lóng wù yòng*), "hidden dragon: do not act." *The Dragon* — survives five of the six
lines and the 用九, but not line 3, whose 乾乾 has no dragon, and it names the lines' image rather
than the graph.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**A force that begins everything and meets nothing, so it has to find its own pace and its own
limit.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 乾 (*qián*) as 上出也 (*shàng chū yě*, "going up and out"), with 乙
(*yǐ*) at its side, glossed 物之達也 (*wù zhī dá yě*, "things getting through"). The core text never
says what 乾 (*qián*) does. It follows one creature, the dragon, through six stations — under water,
seen in the field, gone while a person strives, leaping, flying, overreaching — and ends with a
flock that has no head. The strength never changes from line to line. What changes is the moment,
and the same strength is right at one station and ruinous at another. **This is the book's first
lesson, and it is about timing.**

### The polarities in it

Sky within, sky without: the firm pole twice, and not one open line in the six. Every other trigram
is marked by the height of its odd line; here nothing marks a height, so the six places can only be
read as moments (see *The signature*, above). Nothing in the figure pushes back. That is its gift
and its hazard at once. **With nothing outside to check it, the check has to come from inside** —
line 3's wariness at dusk — or it comes too late, at the top, as regret.

### In nature

Two natural things are in this figure. The first is the sky, which the 大象 (*dà xiàng*) names:
天行健 (*tiān xíng jiàn*, "the sky moves with vigour"). Watch it through one clear evening. The stars
rise in the east, cross, and set in the west, the whole bowl wheeling round a still point in the
north, and it has never once stopped. That is the 大象's picture — not a force that strikes, but a
going that does not halt.

The second is the dragon, and 說文 reads it by the sky's own calendar: 龍 (*lóng*) 能幽，能明，能細，能巨
(*néng yōu, néng míng, néng xì, néng jù*, "it can be dark, it can be clear, it can be small, it can be
huge") … 春分而登天，秋分而潛淵 (*chūnfēn ér dēng tiān, qiūfēn ér qián yuān*, "at the spring equinox
it climbs the sky; at the autumn equinox it sinks into the deep"). A modern reading, not in this
project's sources, takes the lines' dragon for the band of stars early Chinese astronomy called the
Dragon — whose second lodge is named 亢 (*kàng*), the word of line 6, which 說文 glosses 人頸也 (*rén
jǐng yě*, "a person's neck"). Look east an hour after sunset in April: the bright star Spica, the
Dragon's horn, lifts off the horizon — a dragon seen in the field. On midsummer evenings the long
body, with red Antares at its heart, lies low across the south; in autumn it slides down into the
west at dusk; in winter it is under the earth with the sun. Whether or not the line-writers meant
it, the sky does what the lines say, and anyone can watch it.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 乾：元亨。利貞 (*qián: yuán hēng. lì zhēn*) — "Striving *(proposed)*: from the source,
it gets through; advantageous: holding firm." Four words, and no image and no condition. 說文 gives
貞 (*zhēn*) as 卜問也 (*bǔ wèn yě*, "asking by the cracked bone"), so the older sense is "an
advantageous inquiry". The 文言 (*wényán*) turns the four words into four qualities of the noble one,
and gives 貞 (*zhēn*) its later weight: 貞者，事之幹也 (*zhēn zhě, shì zhī gàn yě*, "貞 is the trunk of
affairs"). The working gloss *holding firm* comes from that Wing, and it is labelled as such.

**Line 1** is under water. 潛龍勿用 (*qián lóng wù yòng*, "the dragon submerged: do not act"). 說文
adds to 潛 (*qián*) 一曰藏也 (*yī yuē cáng yě*, "also: to store away"). The strength is already whole;
the time is not. The 文言 gives the line its stance: 確乎其不可拔 (*què hū qí bù kě bá*, "firm — it
cannot be pulled up"), like a root, not a sulk.

**Line 2** is the lower centre. 見龍在田，利見大人 (*xiàn lóng zài tián, lì jiàn dà rén*, "the dragon
appears in the field; advantageous to see the great person"). 說文's 田 (*tián*) is ground planted with
grain, its cross the paths between plots: the dragon first shows itself where people work.

**Line 3** is the seam, and the dragon is gone. 君子終日乾乾，夕惕若；厲，无咎 (*jūnzǐ zhōng rì qián
qián, xī tì ruò; lì, wú jiù*, "the noble one strives and strives all day, and at dusk is wary; peril
— no blame"). 說文 gives 惕 (*tì*) as 敬也 (*jìng yě*, "attentive care"). 王弼 says why the creature
has left the line: 乾乾夕惕非龍德也 (*qián qián xī tì fēi lóng dé yě*, "striving and dusk-wariness are
not a dragon's integrity"). At the top of the lower sky, with no centre to stand on, the force needs
a person — effort by day, a check at nightfall. This is the figure's own brake.

**Line 4** crosses into the upper sky and tests it. 或躍在淵，无咎 (*huò yuè zài yuān, wú jiù*,
"perhaps leaping in the deep — no blame"). 說文 gives 淵 (*yuān*) as 回水也 (*huí shuǐ yě*, "water
that turns back on itself"), a pool where the current circles. The 文言 reads the first word:
或之者，疑之也 (*huò zhī zhě, yí zhī yě*, "'perhaps' means in doubt"). A trial leap, from the water
and back into it — blameless because it is a test.

**Line 5** is the upper centre. 飛龍在天，利見大人 (*fēi lóng zài tiān, lì jiàn dà rén*, "the dragon
flies in the sky; advantageous to see the great person"). Both centres get the same verdict — seen on
the ground, seen in the sky. The 文言 answers with its list of kinds that find each other:
水流濕，火就燥，雲從龍，風從虎 (*shuǐ liú shī, huǒ jiù zào, yún cóng lóng, fēng cóng hǔ*, "water flows
to the wet, fire goes to the dry, clouds follow the dragon, wind follows the tiger").

**Line 6** is past the height. 亢龍，有悔 (*kàng lóng, yǒu huǐ*, "the overreaching dragon: there is
regret"). The 小象: 盈不可久也 (*yíng bù kě jiǔ yě*, "fullness cannot last"); the 文言: 知進而不知退
(*zhī jìn ér bù zhī tuì*, "it knows advancing and not withdrawing"). Not a punishment — only a force
that has not noticed the station changed.

**Using the nines.** 見羣龍无首，吉 (*jiàn qún lóng wú shǒu, jí*, "seeing a flock of dragons with no
head: auspicious"). 羣 (*qún*) is written with 羊 (*yáng*, "sheep") — a flock's word, given to
dragons. The 小象: 天德不可為首也 (*tiān dé bù kě wéi shǒu yě*, "the sky's integrity cannot be made a
head"). Pure strength is well only when no one part of it rules the rest.

### In a life now

This is the figure for a beginning with nothing yet against it: a first real talent, a venture with
money and goodwill behind it, a movement with no rival, a founder's early years — and also the long
strong middle of a working life. It does not tell anyone to be less strong. It tells them where they
are. **Stay under while the time is not come**; show yourself where the work is done, and find the
person who can see you; through the long middle, **work all day and take stock at dusk**; test a
leap before trusting it; and know that the top has regret — the founder who cannot hand on, the
campaign that has won and will not stop, the leader past their season. The extra line imagines the
alternative: a company of capable people in which no one needs to be the head.

The 大象 turns all of it toward practice: 天行健，君子以自強不息 (*tiān xíng jiàn, jūnzǐ yǐ zì qiáng bù
xī*, "the sky moves with vigour; the noble one, by it, makes the self strong and does not stop"). Read
beside line 3, *not stopping* is not *never resting*. The sky does not halt, but it keeps its
seasons; the day's striving ends in wariness, not in collapse.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The Wings and the Laozi share two graphs at the top of this figure, and part over the rest of
it.** Chapter 9: *"Rather than keep filling what you hold, stop"* … *"The work done, the body
withdraws. This is the way of nature."* The graphs are 盈 (*yíng*, "full") and 退 (*tuì*,
"withdraw") — the same two the Wings use for the overreaching dragon: 盈不可久也 (*yíng bù kě jiǔ yě*,
"fullness cannot last", 小象) and 知進而不知退 (*zhī jìn ér bù zhī tuì*, "it knows advancing and not
withdrawing", 文言). Both are Wings. The core text has only 亢 (*kàng*, "overreaching") and 悔 (*huǐ*,
"regret").

Where they part: the Laozi makes withdrawal the rule, and 乾 (*qián*) makes it the last station.
Five of its six lines say go on — appear, strive, leap, fly — and its 大象 says do not stop. The I
Ching does not ask the dragon to be less a dragon; it asks it to know where it is. The one core-text
phrase the books seem to share, 君子終日 (*jūnzǐ zhōng rì*, "the noble one, all day"), in line 3 and
chapter 26, is not leaned on here: two of the Laozi's editions read 聖人 (*shèngrén*, "the sage") in
that line ([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 4).
