## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin · releasing).
**Across** — sky ↔ earth × mountain ↔ lake: a trigram with no odd line under one open only at the top.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳: 夬，決也，剛決柔也 (*guài, jué yě, gāng jué róu yě*, "*guài*
is breaking through: the firm breaks through the yielding"), and at once it tempers it — 健而說，決而和
(*jiàn ér yuè, jué ér hé*, "vigour, and releasing; breaking through, and in accord"). It counts the
lines, 柔乘五剛也 (*róu chéng wǔ gāng yě*, "the yielding rides on five firm lines"), and warns off
force: 告自邑，不利即戎，所尚乃窮也 (*gào zì yì, bù lì jí róng, suǒ shàng nǎi qióng yě*, "announce it
from the town; it is not favourable to go straight to arms — what that prizes comes to an end"). The
大象傳: 澤上于天，夬；君子以施祿及下，居德則忌 (*zé shàng yú tiān, guài; jūnzǐ yǐ shī lù jí xià, jū dé
zé jì*, "the lake has risen above the sky: *guài*; the noble one gives out stipends to reach those
below, and holds that sitting on integrity is to be shunned"). The tradition reads a decisive
clearing-out of the last weak line, done openly and by word rather than by arms.

**What the polarity implies.** A trigram with nothing open in it, under a trigram open only at its
lip. All the force of the figure has one exit, and it is at the very top: five firm lines press up
and the sixth gives. The lake raised above the sky is a body of water held up at the height of the
figure, full to the brim, with vigour still rising beneath it. What comes next is not a push but an
overtopping — the break happens at the place already open.

The structure suggests why the 彖傳 insists on 決而和 (*jué ér hé*, "breaking through, and in
accord"), and why the judgement goes to the court and not the field. The outlet is the lake, and the
lake's action is 說 (*yuè*) — release, and also speech. Vigour within, speech without: resolve that
becomes a proclamation, 揚于王庭 (*yáng yú wáng tíng*, "raise it in the ruler's court"). The same
reading accounts for the top line's warning, 无號，終有凶 (*wú háo, zhōng yǒu xiōng*, "no crying out:
in the end, misfortune") — if the one open place does not speak, the force has nowhere to go. Turn
the figure over and it is 44: the open line at the bottom, arriving instead of leaving, and the
strength no longer the self's but the world's.

**For the name.** *Resolve* — the signature supports it and deepens it: resolve here is firmness that
has found its one outlet, and the outlet is a voice. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Clearing*, *Breakthrough* — *Clearing* survives: 君子夬夬 (*jūnzǐ guài guài*, "the noble one,
clearing, clearing") and 莧陸夬夬 (*xiàn lù guài guài*, "the purslane, cleared, cleared") both carry
the doubled word; *Breakthrough* — the overtopping the figure draws — fails the lines as a noun: 君子
夬夬 (*jūnzǐ guài guài*) must double as a description of a person, and "the noble one, breakthrough,
breakthrough" does not.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**The break is all but made, and what finishes it is a voice raised in the open — not force of
arms.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 夬 (*guài*) as 分決也 (*fēn jué yě*, "to part, to breach"), a hand at
work, 象決形 (*xiàng jué xíng*, "the shape of the breach"); 決 (*jué*) is the same graph with water
added, 行流也 (*xíng liú yě*, "to set water flowing") — a bank breached. Five firm lines press up
under one yielding line: the bank is nearly through. And the core text is full of the voice. The
judgment says 揚于王庭 (*yáng yú wáng tíng*, "raise it in the ruler's court"), 孚號 (*fú háo*, "cry
out, with confidence"), 告自邑 (*gào zì yì*, "announce it from one's own town"); the only thing it
counsels against is 即戎 (*jí róng*, "going straight to arms"). 號 (*háo*, "to cry out") is in 說文
呼也 (*hū yě*, "to call"), with a tiger (虎, *hǔ*) in the graph — a roar. It sounds three times in the
figure, and its absence is the last line's ill fortune.

### The polarities in it

Sky within, lake without: a trigram with no opening under one open only at its lip. All the
figure's force has a single exit, at the very top — and the lake's action is 說 (*yuè*), releasing,
which is also speech (see *The signature*, above). So resolve becomes proclamation. The 彖 holds both
halves together: 健而說，決而和 (*jiàn ér yuè, jué ér hé*, "vigour, and releasing; breaking through,
and in accord"). Structure and text agree on where this ends — 剛長乃終也 (*gāng zhǎng nǎi zhōng yě*,
"the firm grow, and so it comes to an end", 彖). But they differ in weight. The figure makes the
outcome look certain; the lines spend most of their words on how the firm go wrong on the way.

### In nature

The 大象: 澤上于天 (*zé shàng yú tiān*, "the lake has risen above the sky"). A lake stands above the
sky only as cloud. Watch a thunderhead build on a summer afternoon: water carried up in a column
higher than any mountain, the top spreading flat where it can rise no further. It will come down.
王弼 saw the rain in it — 澤上於天，必來下潤 (*zé shàng yú tiān, bì lái xià rùn*, "a lake above the sky
must come down and moisten") — and the rain is in the core text too, at line 3.

Line 5 has a weed: 莧陸夬夬 (*xiàn lù guài guài*, "the purslane, parting, parting"). 說文 gives 莧
(*xiàn*) as 莧菜 (*xiàn cài*, "a pot-herb"), and 王弼 calls it 草之柔脆者也 (*cǎo zhī róu cuì zhě yě*, "a
plant soft and brittle"). Purslane is a low succulent of dug ground and path edges, its reddish
stems snapping at a touch; it is the easiest weed to pull. But the broken pieces root again, and one
plant sets seed by the thousand. Anyone who has weeded it knows why the word is doubled.

Line 4 has sheep: 牽羊悔亡 (*qiān yáng huǐ wáng*, "lead the sheep, and regret goes"). 王弼 calls the
sheep 抵很難移之物 (*dǐ hěn nán yí zhī wù*, "a thing that butts and resists, hard to move"). A sheep
hauled forward by the neck plants its feet; the same sheep walks when it can follow the flock.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 夬：揚于王庭，孚號，有厲，告自邑，不利即戎，利有攸往 (*guài: yáng yú wáng tíng, fú háo,
yǒu lì, gào zì yì, bù lì jí róng, lì yǒu yōu wǎng*, "parting: raise it in the ruler's court; cry out
with confidence — there is peril; announce it from one's own town; not advantageous to go straight to
arms; advantageous to have somewhere to go"). At the centre of power and at home, openly, with the
peril admitted rather than hidden.

**Line 1** is the foot. 壯于前趾，往不勝為咎 (*zhuàng yú qián zhǐ, wǎng bù shèng wéi jiù*, "vigour in the
forward toes; going, and not prevailing, is blame"). Strength that sets out before the body.

**Line 2**, the firm centre below, is the call that works. 惕號，莫夜有戎，勿恤 (*tì háo, mù yè yǒu
róng, wù xù*, "alarm, and crying out; at dusk and in the night there are arms — do not worry"). The
one who raised the alarm in time need not fear the attack in the dark.

**Line 3** is the seam, and the only firm line whose partner is the yielding top. 壯于頄，有凶。君子夬夬，
獨行遇雨，若濡有慍，无咎 (*zhuàng yú qiú, yǒu xiōng. jūnzǐ guài guài, dú xíng yù yǔ, ruò rú yǒu yùn,
wú jiù*, "vigour in the cheekbones — ominous. The noble one, parting, parting, walks alone and meets
rain; as if soaked, and resented — no blame"). Resolve worn on the face goes badly; resolve that
walks apart, gets wet with what the others press against, and is suspected for it, is clear.

**Line 4** can neither sit nor walk. 臀无膚，其行次且。牽羊悔亡，聞言不信 (*tún wú fū, qí xíng zī jū. qiān
yáng huǐ wáng, wén yán bù xìn*, "buttocks without skin, the walking halting. Lead the sheep, and
regret goes; hearing the words, one does not trust them"). The advice is to follow, and it goes
unheeded. 44's third line is this line, turned over, in the same words.

**Line 5** is beside the last yielding line. 莧陸夬夬，中行无咎 (*xiàn lù guài guài, zhōng xíng wú jiù*,
"the purslane, parting, parting; walking the middle, no blame"). The clearing is easy and near, so the
risk is excess; the 小象 withholds praise: 中未光也 (*zhōng wèi guāng yě*, "the centre is not yet
bright").

**Line 6** is the yielding line itself. 无號，終有凶 (*wú háo, zhōng yǒu xiōng*, "no crying out; in the
end, ominous"). What cannot call out is finished. The judgment's 孚號 (*fú háo*, "crying out with
confidence") and this 无號 (*wú háo*) frame the whole.

### In a life now

This is the figure for the last push: the whistle blown on a fraud that everyone half knows, the
family that finally names a parent's drinking, the partnership ended, the official removed. The text
counsels **say it openly** — in the place of authority and in your own town — and **name the peril**;
do not go to arms. It warns against strength that runs ahead (the toes) and strength put on show
(the face). It allows that the one who must walk apart may be soaked and resented and still be
without blame. It knows that what is cleared comes back, like purslane. And it says that whoever
cannot or will not call out comes to an ill end.

The 大象 turns the resolve to giving: 君子以施祿及下，居德則忌 (*jūnzǐ yǐ shī lù jí xià, jū dé zé jì*,
"the noble one gives out stipends to reach those below, and holds that sitting on integrity is to be
shunned"). 王弼 reads 忌 (*jì*) as 禁 (*jìn*, "a prohibition") — integrity made into clear bans. Either
way, what is held high comes down, as rain does.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One graph shared, and a refusal of arms that is only thematic.** Chapter 30: *"One who rules in
alignment with the Tao does not use military force to dominate the world. … When things reach their
prime, they decay. / This is called not-Tao."* 壯 (*zhuàng*, "vigour, prime") stands in the core text
at lines 1 and 3, and Shalom's *reach their prime* answers to it in the Laozi. The arms are another
matter: 43's word is 戎 (*róng*, "arms"), the Laozi's is 兵 (*bīng*, "weapons, soldiers"), so the
shared refusal of force is thematic, not textual. **Where they part:** the Laozi says vigour at its
height is itself not-Tao, and the masterful halt after succeeding. 43 does not halt. It lets the firm
grow to their end and faults only vigour in the wrong place — in the toes, in the face — and it
finishes the clearing, by voice.
