## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin ·
releasing). **Crossed** — mountain ↔ lake, the yang pole within, the yin pole without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 咸，感也 (*xián, gǎn yě*, "咸 is being moved"),
柔上而剛下，二氣感應以相與 (*róu shàng ér gāng xià, èr qì gǎn yìng yǐ xiāng yǔ*, "the yielding above,
the firm below: the two breaths stir and answer, and give to each other"), and 止而說，男下女 (*zhǐ ér
yuè, nán xià nǚ*, "stopping, and releasing; the man goes below the woman"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees
山上有澤 (*shān shàng yǒu zé*, "on the mountain, a lake"), and the noble one 以虛受人 (*yǐ xū shòu
rén*, "receives others by being empty"). The tradition reads mutual feeling as courtship — the
youngest son, mountain, placing himself under the youngest daughter, lake.

**What the polarity implies.** Both odd lines are top lines — the mountain's firm lid at the third
place, the lake's open mouth at the sixth. A mountain with a lake on its summit: the height carries
the hollow, and what the height is closed against, the hollow is open to. The lines climb a body as
the figure climbs — 咸其拇 (*xián qí mǔ*, "touch his big toe", 31.1), the calf, the thigh, the back —
and at the lake's open top reach 咸其輔，頰，舌 (*xián qí fǔ, jiá, shé*, "touch his jaws, cheeks,
tongue", 31.6): the mouth, where 說 (*yuè*, "releasing") is also 說 (*shuō*, "speech"). On the
mountain's side the lines praise stillness — 居吉 (*jū jí*, "staying put is auspicious", 31.2) — and at
the lid warn against holding on: 執其隨，往吝 (*zhí qí suí, wǎng lìn*, "grasping what it follows —
going brings regret", 31.3). The structure suggests why touch needs this signature: a thing that moves
can be struck but not touched. Only what holds still below can feel what opens above.

And it opens the lower canon, which 序卦 (*xùguà*) begins 有男女然後有夫婦 (*yǒu nán nǚ rán hòu yǒu fū
fù*, "there are men and women, and then there are husbands and wives"). The upper canon closed on
water and fire, each pole alone and twice. The lower opens on the two spectrums whose odd line sits at
an end, and crosses them — the youngest pair here, the eldest in 32. The structure makes the hinge a
turn from each pole kept to itself to the two poles meeting.

**For the name.** **Touch** — deepens: stillness below and openness above is the condition of touch,
and the lines' climb from toe to tongue is the figure's climb from mountain to lake. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Quickening* — survives: 咸其拇 reads "quickens his big toe" and 咸其輔，頰，舌
"quickens his jaws, cheeks, tongue", and 雜卦 (*záguà*) is behind it — 咸速也 (*xián sù yě*, "咸 is
quick") — though it strains at 咸其脢 (*xián qí méi*, "the flesh of his back", 31.5); *Response* —
fails the lines: 咸其股 (*xián qí gǔ*, "touch his thigh", 31.3) cannot be "respond his thigh", and the
彖's 感應 (*gǎn yìng*, "stir and answer") is commentary.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**To be touched is to be moved — and the whole counsel is to let it reach you without being carried
off by it.** The core text writes 咸 ("touch", proposed) five times, each time on a part of the body,
and climbs: the big toe, the calf, the thigh, the flesh of the back, and at the top 輔，頰，舌 ("jaws,
cheeks, tongue"). The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 咸 ("touch") as 皆也 ("all,
together"). The Wings read it as 感 ("being moved"), which the Shuowen defines as 動人心也 ("what moves a
person's heart") — the same graph with 心 ("heart") written under it. The core text's graph has no
heart. And the one line of the six that does not write 咸 ("touch") — line 4, where the heart would
fall in the climb — is the one crowded with heart-graphs: 悔 ("regret"), 憧 ("unsettled"), 思
("thought").

### The polarities in it

Mountain within, lake without: the two poles whose odd line is at the top, crossed. The mountain is
closed at its top; the lake is open at its top. The lower lines are the mountain's, and the text
tells them to keep still — 凶，居吉 ("ominous; staying put, auspicious", line 2) — and at the mountain's
closed lid, line 3, not to go after what draws them. The upper lines are the lake's, and at its open
mouth, line 6, touch turns into talk. The Tuan, the Wing on the judgment, says it in two words, 止而說
("stopping, and releasing"). **Touch needs a still place to land: the mountain stops so that what
lies above it can be stirred.**

### In nature

The image in the Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, is 山上有澤 ("on the mountain, a lake"): a
tarn in a high corrie, a crater lake, water held up in the rock because the rock beneath is closed
and does not drain. Go to one on a calm morning. The surface is so still that the lightest air
writes on it — dark patches of ripple that slide across and are gone — while the mountain under it
does not stir at all. The lake shows every touch because it lies on something that does not move. On
a cold autumn dawn, mist lifts off the water where the air is colder than the lake: the Shuogua's,
the Wing on the trigrams, 山澤通氣 ("mountain and lake exchange breath").

The lines' own ground is the body, and it can be watched too. Stand to walk and notice what moves
first: the calf tightens before the foot leaves the floor. Wang Bi saw it — 腓體動躁者也 ("the calf is the
part of the body that moves restlessly"). A touch felt in the calf is already a step. And the flesh
of the back is the one place a person cannot see or reach alone: what touches there is received,
never chased.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 咸：亨。利貞。取女吉 ("Touch. It gets through. Advantageous to hold firm. Taking a wife —
auspicious."). Courtship is in the core text, not only in the Tuan: the touch that climbs the body
is heading for a marriage, and the judgment approves.

**Line 1** — 咸其拇 ("touch at the big toe"). No verdict: the first stir, at the extremity, too slight
to judge. The Small Image, the Wing on each line: 志在外也 ("its aim is outside").

**Line 2** — 咸其腓，凶，居吉 ("touch at the calf — ominous; staying put, auspicious"). One line, both
verdicts, and the difference is whether one moves. The Shuowen gives 居 ("staying put") as 蹲也 ("to
squat"): sit on your heels.

**Line 3** — 咸其股，執其隨，往吝 ("touch at the thigh; grasping what it follows — going on is stinting").
Wang Bi: 股之爲物隨足者也 ("the thigh is the thing that follows the foot"). At the mountain's lid, feeling
has become pursuit, and pursuit is what the text marks down.

**Line 4** — 貞吉悔亡，憧憧往來，朋從爾思 ("holding firm, auspicious; regret goes. Unsettled, coming and going —
friends follow your thought"). No body part, no 咸 ("touch"): this is the place of the heart, and
Wang Bi names it, 心神始感者也 ("where heart and the numinous are first moved"). The Shuowen gives 憧
("unsettled") as 意不定也 ("intent not settled"). Held firm, the centre of feeling is auspicious;
restless, it draws only those who already think as you do.

**Line 5** — 咸其脢，无悔 ("touch at the flesh of the back — no regret"). The Shuowen gives 脢 ("the flesh
of the back") as 背肉也 ("the flesh of the back") and quotes this line; Wang Bi places it 心之上口之下
("above the heart, below the mouth"). The firm centre of the lake, and a modest verdict: no regret,
and no more.

**Line 6** — 咸其輔，頰，舌 ("touch at the jaws, cheeks, tongue"). No verdict. The Small Image calls it
滕口說也 ("a mouth running over with speech"). At the lake's open top, feeling has gone into words.

The verdicts fall on the parts that move — the calf, the thigh — and on the heart. The two
extremities, toe and tongue, get none: the first stir and the last word are not where anything is
decided.

### In a life now

This is the figure for attraction — to a person, a place, a piece of work, a cause — and for any
first stir of feeling: a friendship starting, a speech that moves a room, the pull of someone met
once. It counsels timing. **Do not leap up at the first stir** (the calf); sit on your heels. **Do
not grasp after what draws you** (the thigh). **Hold firm at the heart**, where restlessness gathers
only an echo of yourself. **Let yourself be touched where you cannot see** (the back). **Distrust
feeling that has gone straight to the mouth** — the declaration, the post, the promise made in the
first hour. None of this is coldness; the judgment ends in a wedding, and calls it auspicious.

The Great Image turns it toward practice: 君子以虛受人 ("the noble one receives others by being empty"). A
lake on a mountain can take in what falls into it because it is not already full. To be touched,
leave room.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a heart and move it in opposite directions.** The Tuan says 聖人感人心而天下和平 ("the
sage moves people's hearts, and the world is at peace"), and the Great Image makes 虛 ("empty") the
noble one's way to receive others. The Laozi's chapter 3 has the same three things — the sage, the
heart, the empty — and turns them round: *"By not displaying the desirable, the people's hearts are
not thrown into disorder. / This is how the sage governs: / emptying their hearts and filling their
bellies."* The characters, 心 ("heart") and 虛 ("empty"), are in the layer being read, and the English
answers to them. But the layer is the Wings: the core text's 咸 ("touch") has no heart in it.

And they part. The Changes' sage stirs hearts, and that is how the world comes to peace; the Laozi's
sage keeps hearts from being stirred. The Great Image's empty place is the noble one's own, kept so
as to take others in; the Laozi's is done to the people. **This reading leaves that difference
standing** ([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 2).

### Words kept

- 咸 ("Touch") — the proposed name (not yet approved); 感 ("being moved") is the Tuan's gloss, not the
  core text's word
- 心 ("heart") — never *mind* (locked); in the Wings and Wang Bi here, not in the core text
- 虛 ("empty") — never *emptiness* (the Tao Te Ching's entry, open there)
- 執 ("grasp") — never *cling* (locked)
- 說 ("releasing"; "speech") — *releasing* in the Tuan's 止而說 ("stopping, and releasing"); *speech* in
  the Small Image's 滕口說也 ("a mouth running over with speech")
- 君子 ("the noble one"); 聖人 ("the sage") — as in the Tao Te Ching translation
- 貞 ("holding firm") — working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 利貞 ("advantageous to hold firm")
  is "an advantageous inquiry"
- 吉 ("auspicious") · 凶 ("ominous") · 吝 ("stinting"); 悔亡 ("regret goes"); 无悔 ("no regret") — working
  glosses (A4)
