## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **lake** (兌 · yin ·
releasing). **Crossed** — mountain ↔ lake, the yang pole within, the yin pole without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 咸，感也 (*xián, gǎn yě*, "咸 is being moved"),
柔上而剛下，二氣感應以相與 (*róu shàng ér gāng xià, èr qì gǎn yìng yǐ xiāng yǔ*, "the yielding above,
the firm below: the two breaths stir and answer, and give to each other"), and 止而說，男下女 (*zhǐ ér
yuè, nán xià nǚ*, "stopping, and releasing; the man goes below the woman"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees
山上有澤 (*shān shàng yǒu zé*, "on the mountain, a lake"), and the noble one 以虛受人 (*yǐ xū shòu
rén*, "receives others by being empty"). The tradition reads mutual feeling as courtship — the
youngest son, mountain, placing himself under the youngest daughter, lake.

**What the polarity implies.** Both odd lines are top lines — the mountain's firm lid at the third
place, the lake's open mouth at the sixth. A mountain with a lake on its summit: the height carries
the hollow, and what the height is closed against, the hollow is open to. The lines climb a body as
the figure climbs — 咸其拇 (*xián qí mǔ*, "touch his big toe", 31.1), the calf, the thigh, the back —
and at the lake's open top reach 咸其輔，頰，舌 (*xián qí fǔ, jiá, shé*, "touch his jaws, cheeks,
tongue", 31.6): the mouth, where 說 (*yuè*, "releasing") is also 說 (*shuō*, "speech"). On the
mountain's side the lines praise stillness — 居吉 (*jū jí*, "staying put is auspicious", 31.2) — and at
the lid warn against holding on: 執其隨，往吝 (*zhí qí suí, wǎng lìn*, "grasping what it follows —
going brings regret", 31.3). The structure suggests why touch needs this signature: a thing that moves
can be struck but not touched. Only what holds still below can feel what opens above.

And it opens the lower canon, which 序卦 (*xùguà*) begins 有男女然後有夫婦 (*yǒu nán nǚ rán hòu yǒu fū
fù*, "there are men and women, and then there are husbands and wives"). The upper canon closed on
water and fire, each pole alone and twice. The lower opens on the two spectrums whose odd line sits at
an end, and crosses them — the youngest pair here, the eldest in 32. The structure makes the hinge a
turn from each pole kept to itself to the two poles meeting.

**For the name.** **Touch** — deepens: stillness below and openness above is the condition of touch,
and the lines' climb from toe to tongue is the figure's climb from mountain to lake. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Quickening* — survives: 咸其拇 reads "quickens his big toe" and 咸其輔，頰，舌
"quickens his jaws, cheeks, tongue", and 雜卦 (*záguà*) is behind it — 咸速也 (*xián sù yě*, "咸 is
quick") — though it strains at 咸其脢 (*xián qí méi*, "the flesh of his back", 31.5); *Response* —
fails the lines: 咸其股 (*xián qí gǔ*, "touch his thigh", 31.3) cannot be "respond his thigh", and the
彖's 感應 (*gǎn yìng*, "stir and answer") is commentary.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**To be touched is to be moved — and the whole counsel is to let it reach you without being carried
off by it.** The core text writes 咸 (*xián*, "touch", proposed) five times, each time on a part of
the body, and climbs: the big toe, the calf, the thigh, the flesh of the back, and at the top 輔，頰，舌
(*fǔ, jiá, shé*, "jaws, cheeks, tongue"). 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 咸 (*xián*, "touch") as 皆也 (*jiē
yě*, "all, together"). The Wings read it as 感 (*gǎn*, "being moved"), which 說文 defines as 動人心也
(*dòng rén xīn yě*, "what moves a person's heart") — the same graph with 心 (*xīn*, "heart") written
under it. The core text's graph has no heart. And the one line of the six that does not write 咸
(*xián*, "touch") — line 4, where the heart would fall in the climb — is the one crowded with
heart-graphs: 悔 (*huǐ*, "regret"), 憧 (*chōng*, "unsettled"), 思 (*sī*, "thought").

### The polarities in it

Mountain within, lake without: the two poles whose odd line is at the top, crossed. The mountain is
closed at its top; the lake is open at its top. The lower lines are the mountain's, and the text tells
them to keep still — 凶，居吉 (*xiōng, jū jí*, "ominous; staying put, auspicious", line 2) — and at the
mountain's closed lid, line 3, not to go after what draws them. The upper lines are the lake's, and at
its open mouth, line 6, touch turns into talk. The 彖 (*tuàn*) says it in two words, 止而說 (*zhǐ ér
yuè*, "stopping, and releasing"). **Touch needs a still place to land: the mountain stops so
that what lies above it can be stirred.**

### In nature

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) image is 山上有澤 (*shān shàng yǒu zé*, "on the mountain, a lake"): a tarn in a
high corrie, a crater lake, water held up in the rock because the rock beneath is closed and does
not drain. Go to one on a calm morning. The surface is so still that the lightest air writes on it —
dark patches of ripple that slide across and are gone — while the mountain under it does not stir at
all. The lake shows every touch because it lies on something that does not move. On a cold autumn
dawn, mist lifts off the water where the air is colder than the lake: 說卦's (*shuōguà*) 山澤通氣
(*shān zé tōng qì*, "mountain and lake exchange breath").

The lines' own ground is the body, and it can be watched too. Stand to walk and notice what moves
first: the calf tightens before the foot leaves the floor. 王弼 saw it — 腓體動躁者也 (*féi tǐ dòng zào
zhě yě*, "the calf is the part of the body that moves restlessly"). A touch felt in the calf is
already a step. And the flesh of the back is the one place a person cannot see or reach alone: what
touches there is received, never chased.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 咸：亨。利貞。取女吉 (*xián: hēng. lì zhēn. qǔ nǚ jí*) — "Touch. It gets through.
Advantageous to hold firm. Taking a wife — auspicious." Courtship is in the core text, not only in the
彖: the touch that climbs the body is heading for a marriage, and the judgment approves.

**Line 1** — 咸其拇 (*xián qí mǔ*, "touch at the big toe"). No verdict: the first stir, at the
extremity, too slight to judge. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*): 志在外也 (*zhì zài wài yě*, "its aim is
outside").

**Line 2** — 咸其腓，凶，居吉 (*xián qí féi, xiōng, jū jí*, "touch at the calf — ominous; staying put,
auspicious"). One line, both verdicts, and the difference is whether one moves. 說文 gives 居 (*jū*)
as 蹲也 (*dūn yě*, "to squat"): sit on your heels.

**Line 3** — 咸其股，執其隨，往吝 (*xián qí gǔ, zhí qí suí, wǎng lìn*, "touch at the thigh; grasping
what it follows — going on is stinting"). 王弼: 股之爲物隨足者也 (*gǔ zhī wéi wù suí zú zhě yě*, "the
thigh is the thing that follows the foot"). At the mountain's lid, feeling has become pursuit, and
pursuit is what the text marks down.

**Line 4** — 貞吉悔亡，憧憧往來，朋從爾思 (*zhēn jí huǐ wáng, chōng chōng wǎng lái, péng cóng ěr sī*,
"holding firm, auspicious; regret goes. Unsettled, coming and going — friends follow your thought").
No body part, no 咸 (*xián*, "touch"): this is the place of the heart, and 王弼 names it, 心神始感者也
(*xīn shén shǐ gǎn zhě yě*, "where heart and the numinous are first moved"). 說文 gives 憧 (*chōng*)
as 意不定也 (*yì bù dìng yě*, "intent not settled"). Held firm, the centre of feeling is auspicious;
restless, it draws only those who already think as you do.

**Line 5** — 咸其脢，无悔 (*xián qí méi, wú huǐ*, "touch at the flesh of the back — no regret"). 說文
gives 脢 (*méi*) as 背肉也 (*bèi ròu yě*, "the flesh of the back") and quotes this line; 王弼 places it
心之上口之下 (*xīn zhī shàng kǒu zhī xià*, "above the heart, below the mouth"). The firm centre of the
lake, and a modest verdict: no regret, and no more.

**Line 6** — 咸其輔，頰，舌 (*xián qí fǔ, jiá, shé*, "touch at the jaws, cheeks, tongue"). No verdict.
The 小象 calls it 滕口說也 (*téng kǒu shuō yě*, "a mouth running over with speech"). At the lake's
open top, feeling has gone into words.

The verdicts fall on the parts that move — the calf, the thigh — and on the heart. The two
extremities, toe and tongue, get none: the first stir and the last word are not where anything is
decided.

### In a life now

This is the figure for attraction — to a person, a place, a piece of work, a cause — and for any
first stir of feeling: a friendship starting, a speech that moves a room, the pull of someone met
once. It counsels timing. **Do not leap up at the first stir** (the calf); sit on your heels.
**Do not grasp after what draws you** (the thigh). **Hold firm at the heart**, where restlessness
gathers only an echo of yourself. **Let yourself be touched where you cannot see** (the back).
**Distrust feeling that has gone straight to the mouth** — the declaration, the post, the promise made
in the first hour. None of this is coldness; the judgment ends in a wedding, and calls it auspicious.

The 大象 turns it toward practice: 君子以虛受人 (*jūnzǐ yǐ xū shòu rén*, "the noble one receives others
by being empty"). A lake on a mountain can take in what falls into it because it is not already full.
To be touched, leave room.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a heart and move it in opposite directions.** The 彖 says 聖人感人心而天下和平
(*shèngrén gǎn rén xīn ér tiānxià hé píng*, "the sage moves people's hearts, and the world is at
peace"), and the 大象 makes 虛 (*xū*, "empty") the noble one's way to receive others. The Laozi's
chapter 3 has the same three things — the sage, the heart, the empty — and turns them round: *"By not
displaying the desirable, the people's hearts are not thrown into disorder. / This is how the sage
governs: / emptying their hearts and filling their bellies."* The characters, 心 (*xīn*, "heart") and
虛 (*xū*, "empty"), are in the layer being read, and the English answers to them. But the layer is the
Wings: the core text's 咸 (*xián*, "touch") has no heart in it.

And they part. The Changes' sage stirs hearts, and that is how the world comes to peace; the Laozi's
sage keeps hearts from being stirred. The 大象's empty place is the noble one's own, kept so as to take
others in; the Laozi's is done to the people. **This reading leaves that difference standing**
([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 2).

### Words kept

- 咸 (*xián*) — *touch*, the proposed name (not yet approved); 感 (*gǎn*, "being moved") is the 彖's gloss, not the core text's word
- 心 (*xīn*) — *heart*, never *mind* (locked); in the Wings and 王弼 here, not in the core text
- 虛 (*xū*) — *empty*, never *emptiness* (the Tao Te Ching's entry, open there)
- 執 (*zhí*) — *grasp*, never *cling* (locked)
- 說 (*yuè / shuō*) — *releasing* in the 彖's 止而說 (*zhǐ ér yuè*); *speech* in the 小象's 滕口說也 (*téng kǒu shuō yě*)
- 君子 (*jūnzǐ*) — *the noble one*; 聖人 (*shèngrén*) — *the sage*, as in the Tao Te Ching translation
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 利貞 (*lì zhēn*) is "an advantageous inquiry"
- 吉 · 凶 · 吝 (*jí · xiōng · lìn*) — *auspicious · ominous · stinting*; 悔亡 (*huǐ wáng*) *regret goes*; 无悔 (*wú huǐ*) *no regret* — working glosses (A4)
