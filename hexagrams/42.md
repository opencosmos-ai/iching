## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Crossed** — both poles of thunder ↔ wind, the spectrum whose odd line is at the base.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 reverses 41's sentence: 益，損上益下，民說无疆 (*yì, sǔn shàng
yì xià, mín yuè wú jiāng*, "increase: decrease above, increase below, and the people's delight has no
bound"); 自上下下 (*zì shàng xià xià*, "from above, it goes down to those below"). It names the two
actions — 益動而巽，日進无疆 (*yì dòng ér xùn, rì jìn wú jiāng*, "increase moves and enters; it
advances daily without bound") — and widens them: 天施地生，其益无方 (*tiān shī dì shēng, qí yì wú
fāng*, "the sky bestows, the earth brings forth; its increase keeps to no one direction"). The 大象傳:
風雷，益；君子以見善則遷，有過則改 (*fēng léi, yì; jūnzǐ yǐ jiàn shàn zé qiān, yǒu guò zé gǎi*, "wind
and thunder: increase; the noble one, seeing the good, moves to it, and having a fault, changes it").
The tradition takes 益 (*yì*, "increase") as a gift that travels downward, from those who have to
those who lack, and as a readiness to change.

**What the polarity implies.** Here the shared line is at the base. Thunder is the earth with its
bottom line made firm; wind is the sky with its bottom line opened. The firm line the wind lacks at
its root is the firm line thunder has at its root — so where 41 traded at the summits, 42 trades at
the foundations. The two are one figure turned over: 41's mountain, inverted, is 42's thunder, and a
height given up becomes a base laid down. A decrease you can see, at the top, becomes an increase put
underneath, where no one sees it.

Moving out within, entering without: a force starts at the base of the self and the world lets it
in. Thunder alone startles and is gone; wind alone disperses; together the start is carried. That is
益動而巽 (*yì dòng ér xùn*, "increase moves and enters") arrived at from the figure, and it is why the
大象傳 speaks of change rather than of wealth — "seeing the good, moving to it" is a start that finds
a way in. The 雜卦 pairs the two, 損、益，盛衰之始也 (*sǔn, yì, shèng shuāi zhī shǐ yě*, "decrease and
increase: where flourishing and decline begin"), without saying which begins which. The structure
suggests each begins the other: 41's gift at the top is 42's base, and 42's base, turned over, is
41's summit again.

**For the name.** *Increase* — the signature deepens it: an increase that moves downward and goes in
at the root, the reverse of accumulation. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Bestowal*,
*Groundwork* — *Bestowal* survives: 或益之 (*huò yì zhī*, "someone bestows on it") and 莫益之，或擊之
(*mò yì zhī, huò jī zhī*, "no one bestows on it; someone strikes it") read, though 益之用凶事 (*yì zhī
yòng xiōng shì*, "bestow on it by way of calamitous affairs") strains, and it breaks the pair with
*Decrease*; *Groundwork* — the gift laid at the base — fails the lines: 或益之，十朋之龜弗克違 (*huò yì
zhī, shí péng zhī guī fú kè wéi*, "someone increases it; a tortoise worth ten strings cannot oppose
it") is receiving, not laying a foundation.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Increase is the vessel running over: it comes in from outside, and it stays increase only while it
is passed on.** The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 益 ("increase") as 饒也
("plenty") and draws it 从水、皿 ("from water and a vessel") — water standing above the rim of a dish,
the graph that later took a second water radical as 溢 ("to overflow"). The judgment has nothing to
say about keeping: 利有攸往。利涉大川 ("advantageous to have somewhere to go; advantageous to cross the great
river"). In the lines the gift arrives from elsewhere, is spent on disaster and on moving a whole
people, is given with a kind heart — and the one line that wants increase for itself is struck.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, wind without: both poles of the spectrum whose odd line is at the base. Thunder is
firm at its root and wind open there, so the two trade where no one sees — at the foundations, as 41
traded at the summits (see *The signature*, above). A start from inside finds the world letting it
in: 益動而巽 ("increase moves and enters", the Tuan, the Wing on the judgment). The Tuan names the
direction as 41's reversed, 損上益下 ("decrease above, increase below"), and here the core text and the
structure agree without strain: the gifts go down and outward, and trouble comes only at the top.

### In nature

The image of the Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, is 風雷 ("wind and thunder") — a spring
squall. Thunder first, then wind driving rain across ground that has been dry; the surface loosens
and the water goes in rather than running off. The Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams, in ch 5 sets
these two at the start of the year's round: 帝出乎震，齊乎巽 ("god comes out in thunder, and is made even in
wind"), with thunder in the east and wind in the south-east.

Then watch the graph itself in a downpour: a stone basin under a spout fills, and the water over its
lip goes on to wet the next ground. A basin kept full does nothing more; the increase is in the
overflow. Wang Bi says it on the Tuan: 滿而益之，害之道也 ("to increase what is already full is the way of
harm").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 益：利有攸往。利涉大川 ("increase: advantageous to have somewhere to go; advantageous to
cross the great river"). All movement: increase is a means of going, not a place to stop.

**Line 1**, the firm root of thunder, is where the gift lands. 利用為大作，元吉，无咎 ("advantageous to
undertake a great work; from the source, auspicious — no blame"). 作 ("work") is in the Shuowen 起也
("to rise up"). The Small Image, the Wing on each line, adds the catch: 下不厚事也 ("those below do not
ordinarily carry heavy affairs") — only a great result clears the lowest line of blame.

**Line 2**, the yielding centre below, receives. 或益之，十朋之龜弗克違，永貞吉。王用享于帝，吉 ("someone increases it; a
tortoise worth ten strings, not to be refused; holding firm long, auspicious. The ruler makes
offering to a god — auspicious"). This is 41's fifth line, turned over into 42's second: the same
gift, now arriving below. And it is sent straight up again as offering.

**Line 3** spends it on disaster. 益之用凶事，无咎。有孚中行，告公用圭 ("increase it by way of ominous affairs — no
blame; with confidence, walking the middle, report to the lord with the jade tablet"). 凶事 ("ominous
affairs") are, in Zhou usage, mourning and calamity — here an occasion, not a verdict. The 圭 ("jade
tablet") is in the Shuowen 瑞玉也。上圜下方 ("a jade of credence, round above, square below"): the errand of
relief carries its warrant.

**Line 4** moves a people. 中行，告公從。利用為依遷國 ("walking the middle; report to the lord, and the lord
follows; advantageous to be the support in moving the state"). Lines 3 and 4 are the seam and
neither is a centre, yet both 中行 ("walk the middle"): they keep a centre they do not hold.

**Line 5**, the firm centre above, gives without asking. 有孚惠心，勿問元吉。有孚惠我德 ("there is confidence and a
kind heart; do not ask — from the source, auspicious. There is confidence: kindness to my
integrity"). The Shuowen gives 惠 ("kindness") as 仁也 ("humaneness"), with 心 ("heart") in the graph.
No oracle is needed — 勿問 ("do not ask") — and the kindness comes back as regard for one's integrity.

**Line 6** wants increase for itself. 莫益之，或擊之，立心勿恆，凶 ("no one increases it; someone strikes it; the
heart set, and not held to — ominous"). The Small Image uses the same words for the blow as for line
2's gift: 或擊之，自外來也 ("someone strikes it: it comes from outside"), as 或益之，自外來也 ("someone increases
it: it comes from outside"). Both come from beyond; what differs is where the heart stands. The
figure's only verdict of 凶 ("ominous") is here.

### In a life now

This is the windfall, the inheritance, the grant, the season when help arrives unasked — and what to
do then. Take it; do not refuse the tortoise. Turn it at once into work (line 1) or offering (line
2). Spend it where there is flood or fire or a death in the family (line 3); let it carry people to
new ground — the family that moves for a child's schooling, the town that rebuilds on higher land
(line 4). Give without asking whether the giving will be repaid (line 5). What goes wrong is not the
gift but the heart set on more of it, which cannot keep its setting (line 6): the full basin, kept
full.

The Great Image turns it to practice: 君子以見善則遷，有過則改 ("the noble one, seeing the good, moves to it,
and having a fault, changes it"). 遷 ("to move") is line 4's word for moving the state: the same
increase that moves a people moves a person.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Three graphs shared, and not the sentence.** Chapter 42: *"For so it is: what is decreased is
thereby increased, and what is increased is thereby decreased."* The Laozi's line contains 或益之
("someone increases it") — the very string of 42's second line and 41's fifth, in the core text, and
*increase* answers to 益 ("increase") in both. But the grammar is not the same. In the Zhouyi 或
("someone; perhaps") is "someone" — the Small Image's 自外來也 ("it comes from outside") shows an agent;
in the Laozi it is "it may be". The string is a coincidence of wording
([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 4); the shared ground is the
reversal. Line 6 is increased-and-thereby-decreased, and 41 into 42 is decreased-and-thereby-
increased. **Where they part:** the Laozi states a law of things; the I Ching lays out a turn in
time and ties it to the moment — 凡益之道，與時偕行 ("the way of every increase goes along with its time",
the Tuan).
