## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **thunder** (震 · yang · moving out). Without: **wind** (巽 · yin · entering).
**Crossed** — both poles of thunder ↔ wind, the spectrum whose odd line is at the base.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 reverses 41's sentence: 益，損上益下，民說无疆 (*yì, sǔn shàng
yì xià, mín yuè wú jiāng*, "increase: decrease above, increase below, and the people's delight has no
bound"); 自上下下 (*zì shàng xià xià*, "from above, it goes down to those below"). It names the two
actions — 益動而巽，日進无疆 (*yì dòng ér xùn, rì jìn wú jiāng*, "increase moves and enters; it
advances daily without bound") — and widens them: 天施地生，其益无方 (*tiān shī dì shēng, qí yì wú
fāng*, "the sky bestows, the earth brings forth; its increase keeps to no one direction"). The 大象傳:
風雷，益；君子以見善則遷，有過則改 (*fēng léi, yì; jūnzǐ yǐ jiàn shàn zé qiān, yǒu guò zé gǎi*, "wind
and thunder: increase; the noble one, seeing the good, moves to it, and having a fault, changes it").
The tradition takes 益 (*yì*, "increase") as a gift that travels downward, from those who have to
those who lack, and as a readiness to change.

**What the polarity implies.** Here the shared line is at the base. Thunder is the earth with its
bottom line made firm; wind is the sky with its bottom line opened. The firm line the wind lacks at
its root is the firm line thunder has at its root — so where 41 traded at the summits, 42 trades at
the foundations. The two are one figure turned over: 41's mountain, inverted, is 42's thunder, and a
height given up becomes a base laid down. A decrease you can see, at the top, becomes an increase put
underneath, where no one sees it.

Moving out within, entering without: a force starts at the base of the self and the world lets it
in. Thunder alone startles and is gone; wind alone disperses; together the start is carried. That is
益動而巽 (*yì dòng ér xùn*, "increase moves and enters") arrived at from the figure, and it is why the
大象傳 speaks of change rather than of wealth — "seeing the good, moving to it" is a start that finds
a way in. The 雜卦 pairs the two, 損、益，盛衰之始也 (*sǔn, yì, shèng shuāi zhī shǐ yě*, "decrease and
increase: where flourishing and decline begin"), without saying which begins which. The structure
suggests each begins the other: 41's gift at the top is 42's base, and 42's base, turned over, is
41's summit again.

**For the name.** *Increase* — the signature deepens it: an increase that moves downward and goes in
at the root, the reverse of accumulation. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Bestowal*,
*Groundwork* — *Bestowal* survives: 或益之 (*huò yì zhī*, "someone bestows on it") and 莫益之，或擊之
(*mò yì zhī, huò jī zhī*, "no one bestows on it; someone strikes it") read, though 益之用凶事 (*yì zhī
yòng xiōng shì*, "bestow on it by way of calamitous affairs") strains, and it breaks the pair with
*Decrease*; *Groundwork* — the gift laid at the base — fails the lines: 或益之，十朋之龜弗克違 (*huò yì
zhī, shí péng zhī guī fú kè wéi*, "someone increases it; a tortoise worth ten strings cannot oppose
it") is receiving, not laying a foundation.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Increase is the vessel running over: it comes in from outside, and it stays increase only while
it is passed on.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 益 (*yì*) as 饒也 (*ráo yě*, "plenty") and draws it 从水、皿
(*cóng shuǐ, mǐn*, "from water and a vessel") — water standing above the rim of a dish, the graph
that later took a second water radical as 溢 (*yì*, "to overflow"). The judgment has nothing to say
about keeping: 利有攸往。利涉大川 (*lì yǒu yōu wǎng. lì shè dà chuān*, "advantageous to have
somewhere to go; advantageous to cross the great river"). In the lines the gift arrives from
elsewhere, is spent on disaster and on moving a whole people, is given with a kind heart — and the
one line that wants increase for itself is struck.

### The polarities in it

Thunder within, wind without: both poles of the spectrum whose odd line is at the base. Thunder is
firm at its root and wind open there, so the two trade where no one sees — at the foundations, as 41
traded at the summits (see *The signature*, above). A start from inside finds the world letting it in: 益動而巽 (*yì dòng ér xùn*,
"increase moves and enters", 彖). The 彖 names the direction as 41's reversed, 損上益下 (*sǔn shàng yì
xià*, "decrease above, increase below"), and here the core text and the structure agree without
strain: the gifts go down and outward, and trouble comes only at the top.

### In nature

The 大象's image is 風雷 (*fēng léi*, "wind and thunder") — a spring squall. Thunder first, then wind
driving rain across ground that has been dry; the surface loosens and the water goes in rather than
running off. 說卦 ch 5 sets these two at the start of the year's round: 帝出乎震，齊乎巽 (*dì chū hū
zhèn, qí hū xùn*, "god comes out in thunder, and is made even in wind"), with thunder in the east and
wind in the south-east.

Then watch the graph itself in a downpour: a stone basin under a spout fills, and the water over its
lip goes on to wet the next ground. A basin kept full does nothing more; the increase is in the
overflow. 王弼 says it on the 彖: 滿而益之，害之道也 (*mǎn ér yì zhī, hài zhī dào yě*, "to increase
what is already full is the way of harm").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 益：利有攸往。利涉大川 (*yì: lì yǒu yōu wǎng. lì shè dà chuān*, "increase:
advantageous to have somewhere to go; advantageous to cross the great river"). All movement: increase
is a means of going, not a place to stop.

**Line 1**, the firm root of thunder, is where the gift lands. 利用為大作，元吉，无咎 (*lì yòng wéi dà
zuò, yuán jí, wú jiù*, "advantageous to undertake a great work; from the source, auspicious — no
blame"). 作 (*zuò*) is in 說文 起也 (*qǐ yě*, "to rise up"). The 小象 adds the catch: 下不厚事也 (*xià
bù hòu shì yě*, "those below do not ordinarily carry heavy affairs") — only a great result clears the
lowest line of blame.

**Line 2**, the yielding centre below, receives. 或益之，十朋之龜弗克違，永貞吉。王用享于帝，吉 (*huò yì
zhī, shí péng zhī guī fú kè wéi, yǒng zhēn jí. wáng yòng xiǎng yú dì, jí*, "someone increases it; a
tortoise worth ten strings, not to be refused; holding firm long, auspicious. The ruler makes
offering to 帝, *dì*, a god — auspicious"). This is 41's fifth line, turned over into 42's second: the
same gift, now arriving below. And it is sent straight up again as offering.

**Line 3** spends it on disaster. 益之用凶事，无咎。有孚中行，告公用圭 (*yì zhī yòng xiōng shì, wú jiù.
yǒu fú zhōng xíng, gào gōng yòng guī*, "increase it by way of ominous affairs — no blame; with
confidence, walking the middle, report to the lord with the jade tablet"). 凶事 (*xiōng shì*,
"ominous affairs") are, in Zhou usage, mourning and calamity — here an occasion, not a verdict. The
圭 (*guī*) is in 說文 瑞玉也。上圜下方 (*ruì yù yě. shàng yuán xià fāng*, "a jade of credence, round
above, square below"): the errand of relief carries its warrant.

**Line 4** moves a people. 中行，告公從。利用為依遷國 (*zhōng xíng, gào gōng cóng. lì yòng wéi yī qiān
guó*, "walking the middle; report to the lord, and the lord follows; advantageous to be the support
in moving the state"). Lines 3 and 4 are the seam and
neither is a centre, yet both 中行 (*zhōng xíng*, "walk the middle"): they keep a centre they do not
hold.

**Line 5**, the firm centre above, gives without asking. 有孚惠心，勿問元吉。有孚惠我德 (*yǒu fú huì
xīn, wù wèn yuán jí. yǒu fú huì wǒ dé*, "there is confidence and a kind heart; do not ask — from the
source, auspicious. There is confidence: kindness to my integrity"). 說文 gives 惠 (*huì*) as 仁也 (*rén
yě*, "humaneness"), with 心 (*xīn*, heart) in the graph. No oracle is needed — 勿問 (*wù wèn*, "do not
ask") — and the kindness comes back as regard for one's integrity.

**Line 6** wants increase for itself. 莫益之，或擊之，立心勿恆，凶 (*mò yì zhī, huò jī zhī, lì xīn wù
héng, xiōng*, "no one increases it; someone strikes it; the heart set, and not held to — ominous").
The 小象 uses the same words for the blow as for line 2's gift: 或擊之，自外來也 (*huò jī zhī, zì wài
lái yě*, "someone strikes it: it comes from outside"), as 或益之，自外來也 (*huò yì zhī, zì wài lái
yě*, "someone increases it: it comes from outside"). Both come from beyond; what differs is where the
heart stands. The figure's only verdict of 凶 (*xiōng*, "ominous") is here.

### In a life now

This is the windfall, the inheritance, the grant, the season when help arrives unasked — and what
to do then. Take it; do not refuse the tortoise. Turn it at once into work (line 1) or offering
(line 2). Spend it where there is flood or
fire or a death in the family (line 3); let it carry people to new ground — the family that moves
for a child's schooling, the town that rebuilds on higher land (line 4). Give without asking whether
the giving will be repaid (line 5). What goes wrong is not the gift but the heart set on more of it,
which cannot keep its setting (line 6): the full basin, kept full.

The 大象 turns it to practice: 君子以見善則遷，有過則改 (*jūnzǐ yǐ jiàn shàn zé qiān, yǒu guò zé gǎi*,
"the noble one, seeing the good, moves to it, and having a fault, changes it"). 遷 (*qiān*, "to
move") is line 4's word for moving the state: the same increase that moves a people moves a person.

### An echo from the Laozi

**Three graphs shared, and not the sentence.** Chapter 42: *"For so it is: what is decreased is
thereby increased, and what is increased is thereby decreased."* The Laozi's line contains 或益之
(*huò yì zhī*) — the very string of 42's second line and 41's fifth, in the core text, and *increase*
answers to 益 (*yì*) in both. But the grammar is not the same. In the Zhouyi 或 (*huò*) is "someone" —
the 小象's 自外來也 (*zì wài lái yě*, "it comes from outside") shows an agent; in the Laozi it is "it
may be". The string is a coincidence of wording
([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 4); the shared ground is the
reversal. Line 6 is increased-and-thereby-decreased, and 41 into 42 is decreased-and-thereby-
increased. **Where they part:** the Laozi states a law of things; the I Ching lays out a turn in time
and ties it to the moment — 凡益之道，與時偕行 (*fán yì zhī dào, yǔ shí xié xíng*, "the way of every
increase goes along with its time", 彖).

### Words kept

- 益 (*yì*) — *increase*, the proposed name (not yet approved), paired with 41's *decrease* because
  the Chinese pairs them; not *benefit* (too near 利, *lì*, "advantage") nor *gain* (得, *dé*)
- 帝 (*dì*) — *god*, any god, never *God* (locked); 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler* (locked); 公 (*gōng*) —
  *lord* (locked)
- 心 (*xīn*) — *heart* (locked); 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked)
- 善 (*shàn*) — *the good* in the 大象, the lock's own exception: here the text names the category,
  set against 過 (*guò*, "fault")
- 惠 (*huì*) — *kindness*, working; 恆 (*héng*) — *held to*, 32's word (proposed *Constancy*)
- 大川 (*dà chuān*) — *the great river*, working gloss
- 孚 · 元吉 · 凶 · 无咎 (*fú · yuán jí · xiōng · wú jiù*) — *confidence · from the source, auspicious ·
  ominous · no blame*, working glosses (A4, A6)
