## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping).
**Doubled** — the yang pole of mountain ↔ lake, twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖傳 glosses the figure in one word, 艮，止也 (*gèn, zhǐ yě*, "*gèn* is
stopping"), and then refuses to let stopping be the whole of it: 時止則止，時行則行 (*shí zhǐ zé zhǐ, shí xíng
zé xíng*, "when it is time to stop, stop; when it is time to go, go"). It reads the doubling as two equals
that do not join: 上下敵應，不相與也 (*shàng xià dí yìng, bù xiāng yǔ yě*, "above and below answer as
equals and do not join"). The 大象傳 has 兼山 (*jiān shān*, "mountain joined to mountain"), and from it
君子以思不出其位 (*jūnzǐ yǐ sī bù chū qí wèi*, "the noble one's thought does not go beyond its place").

**What the polarity implies.** This is 51 inverted in every sense: one firm line again, but at the top of
each trigram, a lid over two open lines rather than a drive beneath them. The lines climb a body — toes,
calves, waist, trunk, jaws — so the two lids fall at the waist and at the end. The lower lid is the one line
that hurts. 艮其限，列其夤 (*gèn qí xiàn, liè qí yín*, "stilling his waist, tearing the flesh along his
spine"), with 厲薰心 (*lì xūn xīn*, "peril: the heart smoulders"). The upper lid is the one line wholly
good: 敦艮，吉 (*dūn gèn, jí*, "stilling made solid: good fortune"). The structure suggests the difference.
A stop imposed in the middle of a body splits it at the joint; a stop that comes at the top is simply where
the body ends. Read with 51 it is exact: thunder's first strike is good and its second bogs down; the
mountain's first closure is dangerous and its second is good fortune. 雜卦 (*záguà*, "the Mixed Figures") pairs them in two phrases,
震起也，艮止也 (*zhèn qǐ yě, gèn zhǐ yě*, "*zhèn* is rising; *gèn* is stopping"). But the lines show each pole
comes right at its own end of the figure.

**For the name.** **Stilling** — the signature deepens it: the lid is laid on a moving part, toes and jaw,
not on a thing already still, and *stilling* keeps that act. It must stay clear of *stopping*, which is
this project's reading of 止 (*zhǐ*) and not available as a name. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Keeping
Still* — survives: 艮其趾 (*gèn qí zhǐ*, "keeping his toes still"), 艮其輔，言有序 (*gèn qí fǔ, yán yǒu xù*,
"keeping his jaws still: his words come in order"); *Summit* — fails the lines: 艮其趾 puts the stilling at
the feet, and only the top line is at the top.
