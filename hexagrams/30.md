## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Doubled** — water ↔ fire, the yin pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 離，麗也 (*lí, lì yě*, "離 is attaching"),
日月麗乎天，百谷草木麗乎土 (*rì yuè lì hū tiān, bǎi gǔ cǎo mù lì hū tǔ*, "sun and moon attach to the
sky; the hundred grains, the grasses and trees attach to the soil"), and 柔麗乎中正，故亨 (*róu lì hū
zhōng zhèng, gù hēng*, "the yielding attaches to what is central and right, and so gets through").
The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 明兩作離 (*míng liǎng zuò lí*, "clarity rises twice: the blaze"), and the
great person 以繼明照于四方 (*yǐ jì míng zhào yú sì fāng*, "carries clarity on, and shines on the four
quarters"). The tradition reads fire as what lives by what it holds to, and doubled fire as clarity
handed on.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 29 turned inside out, line for line: the two centres are open
and every edge is firm. Fire's hollow middle means it has no substance of its own — bright at the
edges, empty where water keeps its strength — so it must attach, and doubled it must attach twice.
The figure puts its good fortune exactly in those hollow centres: 黃離，元吉 (*huáng lí, yuán jí*,
"yellow blaze — auspicious from the source", 30.2), yellow being the colour of the centre, and 出涕沱若，戚嗟若，吉
(*chū tì tuó ruò, qī jiē ruò, jí*, "tears pouring down, grieving and sighing — auspicious", 30.5).
The yielding centre wins by being moved.

The seam shows the difference from 29 most sharply. There, where two open lines meet between the
pits, a gift is handed in through a window. Here two firm lines meet at the join, and the fourth
thrusts in: 突如其來如，焚如，死如，棄如 (*tū rú qí lái rú, fén rú, sǐ rú, qì rú*, "sudden, its coming —
burning, dying, thrown away", 30.4). The structure suggests that what fire cannot survive is firmness
forced into it: it lives by holding to something, and dies when something is shoved into it. Water at
its seam receives; fire at its seam consumes. With that the upper canon ends — the middle son and the
middle daughter (說卦 ch 10: 再索, *zài suǒ*, "the second draw"), each kept to itself.

**For the name.** **Blaze** — supports: doubled fire is fire at full height, and the lines' 離 is a
burning — 日昃之離 (*rì zè zhī lí*, "the blaze of the declining sun", 30.3) — which a word for attaching
could not carry. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Flame* — survives: "yellow flame", "the flame
of the declining sun" both read, though it is smaller than two fires rising; *Attaching* — fails the
lines: 黃離 (*huáng lí*) as "yellow attaching" is not English — and it is the 彖's word and the
signature's action, which may check a name but not supply one.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Fire has nothing of its own: it shines by what it holds to, and every blaze leans toward
evening.** In the core text 離 ("the blaze", proposed) is a burning seen at two heights — 黃離 ("the
yellow blaze", line 2) and 日昃之離 ("the blaze of the sun leaning west", line 3) — and the lines around
them flare, burn out, weep and go to war. The Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives the
graph not as fire but as a bird: 黃倉庚也 ("the yellow oriole"), and adds 鳴則蠶生 ("when it sings, the
silkworms hatch"). The Wings give the other half: 離，麗也 ("the blaze: it is attaching", the Tuan, the
Wing on the judgment, and the Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams). So the hexagram holds something
radiant and dependent — a light that must be fed and does not last — and asks how to live inside it.
The judgment's answer is not to burn harder. It is 畜牝牛 ("rear a cow").

### The polarities in it

Fire within, fire without: one pole, twice, and 29 turned inside out. Each trigram is hollow at its
centre and firm at its rims, so doubled, all the strength is at the edges and both middles are open
(see *The signature*, above). Here the core text and the structure agree closely. The two lines the
text calls auspicious without condition are those open centres — the yellow blaze (line 2) and the
tears (line 5). The one line with no verdict at all, line 4, is a firm line thrust into the seam
where the two fires meet. **Fire holds by being soft at its heart; whatever is forced into it
burns.**

### In nature

Light a candle and look into the flame rather than at it. Round the wick sits a dim, darker cone:
wax vapour that has not yet caught, and cooler than the rest. The burning happens at the flame's
skin, where vapour meets air. A flame is hollow at its heart and alight at its edges — the trigram's
`101` in wax and air — and it exists only while it has a wick below and air around it. Take either
away and nothing is left to be fire. This is what the Tuan means by attaching: 日月麗乎天，百谷草木麗乎土 ("sun
and moon attach to the sky; the hundred grains, the grasses and trees attach to the soil").

The core text's own fire is the sun. The Shuowen gives 昃 ("leaning west") as 日在西方時 ("the time when
the sun is in the west") and quotes this very line. Watch a clear afternoon: the shadows lengthen
long before the sky dims, and the light that stood overhead at noon now comes in sideways and gold.
The Small Image, the Wing on each line, asks the only question there is: 日昃之離，何可久也 ("the blaze of
the leaning sun — how could it last?").

Then listen for the Shuowen's bird. The black-naped oriole reaches north China in late spring, as
the mulberry comes into leaf and the silkworm eggs hatch; it keeps to the canopy and is heard — a
liquid, fluting whistle — far more often than it is seen, a flash of gold when it crosses a gap.
Whether the line's author heard 黃離 ("the yellow blaze") as the yellow bird cannot be shown; the pun
sits in the graph. And 黃 ("yellow") is in the Shuowen 地之色也 ("the colour of the earth"), drawn from a
field, 田 ("field"), and an old form of light, 炗 ("light"). Light lying on a field: that is where
this fire is safest.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 離：利貞。亨。畜牝牛，吉 ("The blaze. Advantageous to hold firm. It gets through. Rearing a
cow — auspicious."). The Shuowen gives 牝 ("female") as 畜母也 ("the mother among livestock") and quotes
this judgment. Fire's counsel is the least fiery thing in the text: a slow, female, patient animal,
raised over years. Wang Bi reads the ox as 外強而内順 ("strong without, going along within") — the
figure's firm rims and soft heart.

**Line 1** — 履錯然，敬之无咎 ("footsteps crossing every way; take care with them — no blame"). First light,
tracks going in all directions before the day has a shape. Wang Bi places it 處離之始 ("at the blaze's
beginning"): watch where you tread.

**Line 2** — 黃離，元吉 ("the yellow blaze — auspicious from the source"). The soft centre of the lower
fire: noon, the earth's colour, the middle. The best line in the figure is the one that does not
blaze outward at all.

**Line 3** — 日昃之離，不鼓缶而歌，則大耋之嗟，凶 ("the blaze of the leaning sun: if one does not beat the clay jar
and sing, there is the sighing of great old age — ominous"). The Shuowen's 缶 ("clay jar") is a 瓦器
("earthenware vessel") that 秦人鼓之以節謌 ("the people of Qin beat to keep time for song"). Plain pottery
again, as in 29's fourth line. The ominous verdict does not fall on evening, which comes regardless;
it falls on meeting evening with a groan instead of a song. Wang Bi reads the line through the
Laozi's own phrase — hand the work to others and 養志无爲 ("nourish one's aim in non-doing"). That is
his cross-reading, not the line's.

**Line 4** — 突如其來如，焚如，死如，棄如 ("sudden, its coming — burning, dying, cast away"). The Shuowen draws 突
("sudden") as 犬从穴中暫出也 ("a dog bursting out of a hole"). The firm line shoved into the seam, where
the second fire starts: it flares and is gone, and the text gives it no verdict — nothing is left to
judge. The Small Image: 无所容也 ("there is nowhere that holds it").

**Line 5** — 出涕沱若，戚嗟若，吉 ("tears pouring out in streams, grieving and sighing — auspicious"). The
Shuowen's 沱 ("streams") is 江別流也 ("a channel branching off the river"): tears like floodwater finding
a second course. This is the surprise of the hexagram. The sighing, 嗟 ("sighing"), that was ominous
at line 3 is auspicious here. The core text does not say why; the position does — this is the upper
centre, soft in a soft place. The Small Image ties it to what the line holds to: 離王公也 ("it attaches
to ruler and lord").

**Line 6** — 王用出征，有嘉折首，獲匪其醜，无咎 ("the ruler goes out on campaign; praise for breaking the head; what
is taken is not of their kind — no blame"). The last clause is contested. The old translators read
it as the chief struck down and the followers spared; Wang Bi as clearing away 非類 ("what does not
belong"). Either way the fire at the top has become the power to tell one thing from another, and to
strike narrowly.

The lower fire is one day: first light, noon, the leaning sun. The upper is a second fire that comes
on all at once — Wang Bi's 昬而始暁 ("dusk, then the first dawning") — and it burns through at line 4,
is washed at line 5, and ends in judgement at line 6.

### In a life now

This is the figure for whatever shines by what it is fed: a reputation, a first love, a company in
its best year, a gift at its height, a public career. It is also the figure for seeing a parent into
old age, or meeting one's own. It counsels stance. **Begin with care** (line 1's crossing tracks).
**Keep to the yellow middle** — the plain, earthen centre, not the flare. **Sing at sunset**: the
decline comes either way, and only the answer to it — the song or the groan — is one's own (line 3).
**Distrust the sudden flare** — the overnight fame, the boom, the rage — which burns, dies and is
thrown out (line 4). **Let grief move** (line 5). And rear a cow: whatever is to give light for
years has to be tended like an animal, not stoked.

The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, turns it toward the long run: 明兩作離，大人以繼明照于四方
("clarity rises twice: the blaze; the great person carries clarity on, and shines on the four
quarters"). The Shuowen gives 繼 ("carry on") as 續也 ("to join on", as thread is), and Wang Bi as
繼謂不絶也 ("carrying on means not broken off"). No fire lasts. Clarity lasts by being handed on — the
teacher to the student, the parent to the child.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One shared phrase, and it parts the books.** Chapter 10 asks: *"Clarity reaching the four
quarters, can you be non-doing?"* The Great Image has the same two things — 明 ("clarity") and 四方
("the four quarters") — and the English answers to them. But the layer is a Wing: the core text of
this hexagram never writes 明 ("clarity").

And the light goes the other way. In the Great Image the great person 照 ("shines") clarity outward
and keeps it going. The Laozi asks the clarity that reaches everywhere to do nothing, and chapter 52
keeps 光 ("light") apart from 明 ("clarity"): *"Use the outer light, return to your inner clarity."*
What the Great Image calls 明 ("clarity") shining is nearer what the Laozi calls 光 ("light"). **The
books share a word and disagree about the direction of it.** Wang Bi bent line 3 toward the Laozi;
this reading leaves the difference open ([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) §
6, failure 2).

### Words kept

- 離 ("the blaze") — the proposed name (not yet approved); *fire* is the trigram's image; *attaching*
  (麗 ("attaching")) is the Wings' gloss, not the core text's word. The graph can also mean "to part"
  (the Zagua, the Wing that pairs the hexagrams: 渙離也 ("Scattering is parting")) — not the sense
  here. Never *the clinging* or *brightness* ([`glossary/離-li.md`](../glossary/離-li.md))
- 明 ("clarity") — locked: never *illumination*, never *brilliance*, never *enlightenment*; here only
  in the Wings
- 大人 ("the great person") — working; 大 ("great") is never *mighty* (locked)
- 王 ("ruler") · 公 ("lord") — locked; never *king*
- 牝牛 ("a cow") — female, the Shuowen's "mother among livestock"
- 貞 ("holding firm") — working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 利貞 ("advantageous to hold firm")
  is "an advantageous inquiry"
- 元吉 ("auspicious from the source") — working gloss (A4)
- 凶 ("ominous") · 无咎 ("no blame") — working glosses (A4)
- 无爲 ("non-doing") — locked in the Tao Te Ching, and only in Wang Bi here
