## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching). Without: **fire** (離 · yin · attaching).
**Doubled** — water ↔ fire, the yin pole twice.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) says 離，麗也 (*lí, lì yě*, "離 is attaching"),
日月麗乎天，百谷草木麗乎土 (*rì yuè lì hū tiān, bǎi gǔ cǎo mù lì hū tǔ*, "sun and moon attach to the
sky; the hundred grains, the grasses and trees attach to the soil"), and 柔麗乎中正，故亨 (*róu lì hū
zhōng zhèng, gù hēng*, "the yielding attaches to what is central and right, and so gets through").
The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) sees 明兩作離 (*míng liǎng zuò lí*, "clarity rises twice: the blaze"), and the
great person 以繼明照于四方 (*yǐ jì míng zhào yú sì fāng*, "carries clarity on, and shines on the four
quarters"). The tradition reads fire as what lives by what it holds to, and doubled fire as clarity
handed on.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 29 turned inside out, line for line: the two centres are open
and every edge is firm. Fire's hollow middle means it has no substance of its own — bright at the
edges, empty where water keeps its strength — so it must attach, and doubled it must attach twice.
The figure puts its good fortune exactly in those hollow centres: 黃離，元吉 (*huáng lí, yuán jí*,
"yellow blaze — auspicious from the source", 30.2), yellow being the colour of the centre, and 出涕沱若，戚嗟若，吉
(*chū tì tuó ruò, qī jiē ruò, jí*, "tears pouring down, grieving and sighing — auspicious", 30.5).
The yielding centre wins by being moved.

The seam shows the difference from 29 most sharply. There, where two open lines meet between the
pits, a gift is handed in through a window. Here two firm lines meet at the join, and the fourth
thrusts in: 突如其來如，焚如，死如，棄如 (*tū rú qí lái rú, fén rú, sǐ rú, qì rú*, "sudden, its coming —
burning, dying, thrown away", 30.4). The structure suggests that what fire cannot survive is firmness
forced into it: it lives by holding to something, and dies when something is shoved into it. Water at
its seam receives; fire at its seam consumes. With that the upper canon ends — the middle son and the
middle daughter (說卦 ch 10: 再索, *zài suǒ*, "the second draw"), each kept to itself.

**For the name.** **Blaze** — supports: doubled fire is fire at full height, and the lines' 離 is a
burning — 日昃之離 (*rì zè zhī lí*, "the blaze of the declining sun", 30.3) — which a word for attaching
could not carry. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Flame* — survives: "yellow flame", "the flame
of the declining sun" both read, though it is smaller than two fires rising; *Attaching* — fails the
lines: 黃離 (*huáng lí*) as "yellow attaching" is not English — and it is the 彖's word and the
signature's action, which may check a name but not supply one.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Fire has nothing of its own: it shines by what it holds to, and every blaze leans toward
evening.** In the core text 離 (*lí*, "the blaze", proposed) is a burning seen at two heights — 黃離
(*huáng lí*, "the yellow blaze", line 2) and 日昃之離 (*rì zè zhī lí*, "the blaze of the sun leaning
west", line 3) — and the lines around them flare, burn out, weep and go to war. 說文 (*shuōwén*)
gives the graph not as fire but as a bird: 黃倉庚也 (*huáng cānggēng yě*, "the yellow oriole"), and
adds 鳴則蠶生 (*míng zé cán shēng*, "when it sings, the silkworms hatch"). The Wings give the other
half: 離，麗也 (*lí, lì yě*, "離 is attaching", 彖 and 說卦). So the hexagram holds something radiant
and dependent — a light that must be fed and does not last — and asks how to live inside it. The
judgment's answer is not to burn harder. It is 畜牝牛 (*xù pìn niú*, "rear a cow").

### The polarities in it

Fire within, fire without: one pole, twice, and 29 turned inside out. Each trigram is hollow at its
centre and firm at its rims, so doubled, all the strength is at the edges and both middles are open
(see *The signature*, above). Here the core text and the structure agree closely. The two lines the
text calls auspicious without condition are those open centres — the yellow blaze (line 2) and the
tears (line 5). The one line with no verdict at all, line 4, is a firm line thrust into the seam
where the two fires meet. **Fire holds by being soft at its heart; whatever is forced into it
burns.**

### In nature

Light a candle and look into the flame rather than at it. Round the wick sits a dim, darker cone:
wax vapour that has not yet caught, and cooler than the rest. The burning happens at the flame's
skin, where vapour meets air. A flame is hollow at its heart and alight at its edges — the
trigram's `101` in wax and air — and it exists only while it has a wick below and air around it.
Take either away and nothing is left to be fire. This is what the 彖 (*tuàn*) means by attaching:
日月麗乎天，百谷草木麗乎土 (*rì yuè lì hū tiān, bǎi gǔ cǎo mù lì hū tǔ*, "sun and moon attach to the
sky; the hundred grains, the grasses and trees attach to the soil").

The core text's own fire is the sun. 說文 gives 昃 (*zè*) as 日在西方時 (*rì zài xīfāng shí*, "the
time when the sun is in the west") and quotes this very line. Watch a clear afternoon: the
shadows lengthen long before the sky dims, and the light that stood overhead at noon now comes in
sideways and gold. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) asks the only question there is: 日昃之離，何可久也 (*rì zè
zhī lí, hé kě jiǔ yě*, "the blaze of the leaning sun — how could it last?").

Then listen for 說文's bird. The black-naped oriole reaches north China in late spring, as the
mulberry comes into leaf and the silkworm eggs hatch; it keeps to the canopy and is heard — a
liquid, fluting whistle — far more often than it is seen, a flash of gold when it crosses a gap.
Whether the line's author heard 黃離 (*huáng lí*, "the yellow blaze") as the yellow bird cannot be
shown; the pun sits in the graph. And 黃 (*huáng*, "yellow") is in 說文 地之色也 (*dì zhī sè yě*,
"the colour of the earth"), drawn from a field (田, *tián*) and an old form of light (炗, *guāng*).
Light lying on a field: that is where this fire is safest.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 離：利貞。亨。畜牝牛，吉 (*lí: lì zhēn. hēng. xù pìn niú, jí*) — "The blaze.
Advantageous to hold firm. It gets through. Rearing a cow — auspicious." 說文 gives 牝 (*pìn*) as
畜母也 (*xù mǔ yě*, "the mother among livestock") and quotes this judgment. Fire's counsel is the
least fiery thing in the text: a slow, female, patient animal, raised over years. 王弼 reads the ox
as 外強而内順 (*wài qiáng ér nèi shùn*, "strong without, going along within") — the figure's firm
rims and soft heart.

**Line 1** — 履錯然，敬之无咎 (*lǚ cuò rán, jìng zhī wú jiù*, "footsteps crossing every way; take
care with them — no blame"). First light, tracks going in all directions before the day has a
shape. 王弼 places it 處離之始 (*chǔ lí zhī shǐ*, "at the blaze's beginning"): watch where you tread.

**Line 2** — 黃離，元吉 (*huáng lí, yuán jí*, "the yellow blaze — auspicious from the source"). The
soft centre of the lower fire: noon, the earth's colour, the middle. The best line in the figure is
the one that does not blaze outward at all.

**Line 3** — 日昃之離，不鼓缶而歌，則大耋之嗟，凶 (*rì zè zhī lí, bù gǔ fǒu ér gē, zé dà dié zhī jiē,
xiōng*, "the blaze of the leaning sun: if one does not beat the clay jar and sing, there is the
sighing of great old age — ominous"). 說文's 缶 (*fǒu*) is a 瓦器 (*wǎ qì*, "earthenware vessel") that
秦人鼓之以節謌 (*Qín rén gǔ zhī yǐ jié gē*, "the people of Qin beat to keep time for song"). Plain
pottery again, as in 29's fourth line. The ominous verdict does not fall on evening, which comes
regardless; it falls on meeting evening with a groan instead of a song. 王弼 reads the line through
the Laozi's own phrase — hand the work to others and 養志无爲 (*yǎng zhì wú wéi*, "nourish one's aim
in non-doing"). That is his cross-reading, not the line's.

**Line 4** — 突如其來如，焚如，死如，棄如 (*tū rú qí lái rú, fén rú, sǐ rú, qì rú*, "sudden, its
coming — burning, dying, cast away"). 說文 draws 突 (*tū*) as 犬从穴中暫出也 (*quǎn cóng xué zhōng zàn
chū yě*, "a dog bursting out of a hole"). The firm line shoved into the seam, where the second fire
starts: it flares and is gone, and the text gives it no verdict — nothing is left to judge. The 小象:
无所容也 (*wú suǒ róng yě*, "there is nowhere that holds it").

**Line 5** — 出涕沱若，戚嗟若，吉 (*chū tì tuó ruò, qī jiē ruò, jí*, "tears pouring out in streams,
grieving and sighing — auspicious"). 說文's 沱 (*tuó*) is 江別流也 (*jiāng bié liú yě*, "a channel
branching off the river"): tears like floodwater finding a second course. This is the surprise of
the hexagram. The sighing, 嗟 (*jiē*), that was ominous at line 3 is auspicious here. The core text
does not say why; the position does — this is the upper centre, soft in a soft place. The 小象 ties
it to what the line holds to: 離王公也 (*lí wáng gōng yě*, "it attaches to ruler and lord").

**Line 6** — 王用出征，有嘉折首，獲匪其醜，无咎 (*wáng yòng chū zhēng, yǒu jiā zhé shǒu, huò fěi qí
chǒu, wú jiù*, "the ruler goes out on campaign; praise for breaking the head; what is taken is not
of their kind — no blame"). The last clause is contested. The old translators read it as the chief
struck down and the followers spared; 王弼 as clearing away 非類 (*fēi lèi*, "what does not belong").
Either way the fire at the top has become the power to tell one thing from another, and to strike
narrowly.

The lower fire is one day: first light, noon, the leaning sun. The upper is a second fire that
comes on all at once — 王弼's 昬而始暁 (*hūn ér shǐ xiǎo*, "dusk, then the first dawning") — and it
burns through at line 4, is washed at line 5, and ends in judgement at line 6.

### In a life now

This is the figure for whatever shines by what it is fed: a reputation, a first love, a company in
its best year, a gift at its height, a public career. It is also the figure for seeing a parent
into old age, or meeting one's own. It counsels stance. **Begin with care** (line 1's crossing
tracks). **Keep to the yellow middle** — the plain, earthen centre, not the flare. **Sing at
sunset**: the decline comes either way, and only the answer to it — the song or the groan — is
one's own (line 3). **Distrust the sudden flare** — the overnight fame, the boom, the rage — which
burns, dies and is thrown out (line 4). **Let grief move** (line 5). And rear a cow: whatever is to
give light for years has to be tended like an animal, not stoked.

The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) turns it toward the long run: 明兩作離，大人以繼明照于四方 (*míng liǎng zuò lí,
dà rén yǐ jì míng zhào yú sì fāng*, "clarity rises twice: the blaze; the great person carries
clarity on, and shines on the four quarters"). 說文 gives 繼 (*jì*) as 續也 (*xù yě*, "to join on", as
thread is), and 王弼 as 繼謂不絶也 (*jì wèi bù jué yě*, "繼 means not broken off"). No fire lasts.
Clarity lasts by being handed on — the teacher to the student, the parent to the child.

### An echo from the Laozi

**One shared phrase, and it parts the books.** Chapter 10 asks: *"Clarity reaching the four
quarters, can you be non-doing?"* The 大象 has the same two things — 明 (*míng*, "clarity") and 四方
(*sì fāng*, "the four quarters") — and the English answers to them. But the layer is a Wing: the core
text of this hexagram never writes 明 (*míng*, "clarity").

And the light goes the other way. In the 大象 the great person 照 (*zhào*, "shines") clarity outward
and keeps it going. The Laozi asks the clarity that reaches everywhere to do nothing, and chapter 52
keeps 光 (*guāng*, "light") apart from 明 (*míng*, "clarity"): *"Use the outer light, return to your
inner clarity."* What the 大象 calls 明 (*míng*, "clarity") shining is nearer what the Laozi calls
光 (*guāng*, "light"). **The books share a word and disagree about the direction of it.** 王弼 bent
line 3 toward the Laozi; this reading leaves the difference open
([`tao-te-ching-relation.md`](../tao-te-ching-relation.md) § 6, failure 2).

### Words kept

- 離 (*lí*) — *the blaze*, the proposed name (not yet approved); *fire* is the trigram's image; *attaching* (麗, *lì*) is the Wings' gloss, not the core text's word. The graph can also mean "to part" (雜卦's 渙離也, *huàn lí yě*) — not the sense here. Never *the clinging* or *brightness* ([`glossary/離-li.md`](../glossary/離-li.md))
- 明 (*míng*) — *clarity* (locked): never *illumination*, never *brilliance*, never *enlightenment*; here only in the Wings
- 大人 (*dà rén*) — *the great person* (working); 大 (*dà*) is *great*, never *mighty* (locked)
- 王 · 公 (*wáng · gōng*) — *ruler · lord* (locked); never *king*
- 牝牛 (*pìn niú*) — *a cow*: female, 說文's "mother among livestock"
- 貞 (*zhēn*) — *holding firm*, working gloss (A6); read as "the inquiry", 利貞 (*lì zhēn*) is "an advantageous inquiry"
- 元吉 (*yuán jí*) — *auspicious from the source*, working gloss (A4)
- 凶 · 无咎 (*xiōng · wú jiù*) — *ominous · no blame*, working glosses (A4)
- 无爲 (*wú wéi*) — *non-doing* (locked in the Tao Te Ching), and only in 王弼 here
