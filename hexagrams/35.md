## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 *kūn* · yin · going along). Without: **fire** (離 *lí* · yin · attaching).
**Across** — the yin pole of sky ↔ earth within, the yin pole of water ↔ fire without.

**What the tradition reads.** Both Wings read a sunrise. The 大象: 明出地上，晉；君子以自昭明德 (*míng chū
dì shàng, jìn; jūnzǐ yǐ zì zhāo míng dé*, "clarity comes out above the earth: progress; the noble one
makes their own clear integrity shine"). The 彖 glosses the name 晉，進也 (*jìn, jìn yě*, "晉 is going
forward") and reads the two actions in order: 順而麗乎大明，柔進而上行 (*shùn ér lì hū dà míng, róu jìn ér
shàng xíng*, "going along, and attaching to great clarity; the yielding goes forward and moves up"). The
tradition takes the figure as advance that is also a coming into view — the lord 晝日三接 (*zhòu rì sān
jiē*, "received three times in a single day").

**What the polarity implies.** Both trigrams are yin, and no firm line anywhere pushes from below: the
earth is all open, and the only firm lines are the fire's two outer ones, framing a hollow centre. So
what rises here is not driven; it is carried. The earth goes along, and on its back the sun clears the
horizon. That is this figure's progress — the dawn, the one ascent in which nothing strains. Fire's
action is attaching: light has no body and must cling to something to be seen, and here what it clings
to is the whole open earth beneath. The structure suggests that to go forward, in 35, is to become
visible — which is why 雜卦 can gloss it with a time of day, 晉晝也 (*jìn zhòu yě*, "progress is
daytime").

The lines ask for patience, not force: 晉如，摧如 (*jìn rú, cuī rú*, "going forward — and pushed back"),
失得勿恤 (*shī dé wù xù*, "loss and gain — do not fret"). And the one firm line that sits low, at the base
of the fire, is the figure's warning: 晉如鼫鼠 (*jìn rú shí shǔ*, "going forward like a rat") — the
grasping thing caught in daylight. Turn the figure over and the same sun is under the earth: 36.
35 is clarity as the world one walks into; 36 is clarity as the self one hides.

**For the name.** **Progress** — the signature supports it and deepens it toward emergence: advance by
degrees, carried and seen, not forced. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Rising*, *Sunrise* —
*Rising* survives the lines: 晉如，摧如 (*jìn rú, cuī rú*, "rising — and pushed back"), 晉如鼫鼠 (*jìn rú shí
shǔ*, "rising like a rat"), 晉其角 (*jìn qí jiǎo*, "rising on its horns"); *Sunrise*, from 明出地上 (*míng
chū dì shàng*, "clarity comes out above the earth"), fails the lines — 晉如，摧如 cannot be "sunrise-like,
pushed back", and 晉其角 cannot be "sunrise on its horns".

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Going forward in full view, by degrees — received rather than taken.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 晉
(*jìn*) as 進也 (*jìn yě*, "going forward") and says why: 日出萬物進 (*rì chū wàn wù jìn*, "the sun
comes out and the countless things go forward"). The graph is the sun, 日 (*rì*), under a doubled 至
(*zhì*), which 說文 draws as a bird dropping from height to reach the ground; the doubled form is not
itself in the vendored entries, so that reading is from the graph as it stands. The judgment is a
scene of favour, not of effort — a lord given horses in great numbers and received three times in a
day. And the lines say what it costs to get there: going forward and being pushed back, going
forward and grieving, until the crowd assents.

### The polarities in it

Earth within goes along; fire without attaches, and has nothing of its own to burn except what it
clings to. So the rise here is carried, not driven — the signature's dawn. But the text is harder
than the picture, and it wins. The first two lines are not carried at all: they are pushed back and
sorrowing, and the ease comes only with the third, when the whole of the earth has risen together.
And the two firm lines, the only ones in the figure that could push, are the two the text warns: the
rat at the fourth line, the horns at the top. **What goes forward well here is what goes along; what
forces its way forward is what the light shows up.**

### In nature

Watch a clear dawn from high ground. The light comes long before the sun: the sky greys, then takes
colour, and things come into view in order — the tops of trees, then roofs, then the fields — before
the rim of the sun shows at all. Nothing climbs. The 說卦 (*shuōguà*) catches exactly this about
fire: 離也者，明也，萬物皆相見 (*lí yě zhě, míng yě, wàn wù jiē xiāng jiàn*, "fire is clarity: the
countless things all see one another").

The fourth line holds the book's strangest animal. 說文 gives 鼫 (*shí*) as 五技鼠也 (*wǔ jì shǔ yě*,
"the rat of five skills") and lists them: 能飛，不能過屋 (*néng fēi, bù néng guò wū*, "it can fly, but
not over a roof"), and so on — it can climb but not to the top of a tree, swim but not across a
gorge, dig but not hide its body, run but not outrun a person.

王弼, reading the second line, reaches for a bird from another hexagram: 鳴鶴在隂則其子和之 (*míng hè
zài yīn zé qí zǐ hè zhī*, "the crane calls in the shade, and its young answer it"). Cranes do this:
a call from a bird out of sight, and a reply from across the marsh. Integrity done where no one sees
is still heard.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 晉：康侯用錫馬蕃庶，晝日三接 (*jìn: kāng hóu yòng xī mǎ fán shù, zhòu rì sān jiē*) —
"Going forward: the lord of Kang, given horses in great numbers, received three times in a single
day." 康侯 (*kāng hóu*) is most likely a person, a lord of the early Zhou; 王弼 takes 康 (*kāng*) as a
name for *fine*: 康美之名也 (*kāng měi zhī míng yě*). 蕃 (*fán*) is in 說文 plants growing thick, and
the horses may be bred, not only given.

**Line 1.** 晉如，摧如，貞吉。罔孚，裕无咎 (*jìn rú, cuī rú, zhēn jí. wǎng fú, yù wú jiù*, "going
forward — and pushed back; holding firm, auspicious. No confidence yet: be roomy, no blame"). 說文's
裕 (*yù*) is 衣物饒也 (*yī wù ráo yě*, "clothes with plenty of room"). Not yet trusted, give it room.

**Line 2**, at the earth's centre. 晉如，愁如，貞吉。受茲介福，于其王母 (*jìn rú, chóu rú, zhēn jí. shòu
zī jiè fú, yú qí wáng mǔ*, "going forward — and grieving; holding firm, auspicious. One receives this
great blessing from one's grandmother"). The blessing comes from within the house, from the elder
who is not seen at court.

**Line 3** completes the earth. 眾允，悔亡 (*zhòng yǔn, huǐ wáng*, "the crowd assents; regret goes").
說文 glosses 允 (*yǔn*) with 信 (*xìn*, "trust"). The three yielding lines have risen together, and now
they are believed.

**Line 4**, the first firm line, sits at the fire's base. 晉如鼫鼠，貞厲 (*jìn rú shí shǔ, zhēn lì*,
"going forward like the five-skill rat; holding firm, peril"). The one line that pushes alone is the
one caught in the light.

**Line 5** is the hollow centre of the fire, a yielding line in the ruler's seat. 悔亡，失得勿恤，往吉
无不利 (*huǐ wáng, shī dé wù xù, wǎng jí wú bù lì*, "regret goes; loss and gain — do not fret; going on
is auspicious, nothing not advantageous"). 王弼 says this ruler 能不用察 (*néng bù yòng chá*, "is able
not to scrutinise") — the sun does not inspect what it lights.

**Line 6** is past noon. 晉其角，維用伐邑，厲吉无咎，貞吝 (*jìn qí jiǎo, wéi yòng fá yì, lì jí wú jiù,
zhēn lìn*, "going forward on its horns — use it only to strike a town; peril, auspicious, no blame;
holding firm, stinting"). The advance has become horns. 王弼: 明將夷焉 (*míng jiāng yí yān*, "clarity is
about to be wounded") — which is the next hexagram.

### In a life now

This is the figure for the first year somewhere new: a job, a village, a school, a field of work
where no one yet knows your name. It is the artist whose work is starting to be seen, the newcomer
whose standing comes a little at a time. The text is unsentimental about the beginning: one is
pushed back, one grieves, and there is no confidence yet. Its counsel is to be roomy — to leave
space for others to come round — and to trust the help that comes from home. Standing arrives as
the crowd's assent, not as a thing seized. The warnings fall on the forcing: the rat's many half-
skills in daylight, the horns at the top. And at the centre, the ruler's line gives the best advice
the figure has: stop counting what was lost and gained.

The 大象 makes it a practice: 君子以自昭明德 (*jūnzǐ yǐ zì zhāo míng dé*, "the noble one makes their
own clear integrity shine"). What shines is not the person. It is the integrity.

### An echo from the Laozi

**A graph shared in the Wings' layer, and a real disagreement.** Chapter 20 sets the speaker apart
from the crowd: *"The worldly are bright. I alone am clouded."* The *bright* there is 昭 (*zhāo*) —
the same graph as the 大象's 自昭 (*zì zhāo*, "makes shine"). The character is in the layer (a Wing,
not the core text), and the English answers to it; the echo passes both questions.

And the two books point opposite ways with it. In the Laozi, brightness belongs to the worldly, and
chapter 24 adds *"Those who show themselves off do not see clearly."* The 大象 of 晉 asks the noble
one to make their clarity shine, as the sun does. **The I Ching does not settle this by choosing a
side; it gives each side a hexagram.** 35 is clarity shown; 36, its reverse, is clarity hidden. The
Laozi is closer to 36. This reading does not pull 35 toward it.

### Words kept

- 晉 (*jìn*) — *progress*, the proposed name (not yet approved); *advance* is left for 進 (*jìn*)
- 明 (*míng*) — *clarity*, *clear*, never *enlightenment*, never *illumination*, never *brilliance* (locked)
- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked): 明德 (*míng dé*), "clear integrity"
- 眾 (*zhòng*) — *the crowd*, never *the multitude* (locked)
- 萬物 (*wàn wù*) — *the countless things* (locked)
- 王母 (*wáng mǔ*) — *grandmother*: in the kinship terms of the time, a father's mother. The lock gives 王 (*wáng*) *ruler* and 母 (*mǔ*) *mother*; read by the letter, *the ruler's mother*. **For Shalom to rule.**
- 侯 (*hóu*) — *lord* here, which touches the lock that gives 公 (*gōng*) *lord*; the two are different ranks, and one of them will need another word
- 允 (*yǔn*) — *assent*; 說文 glosses it with 信 (*xìn*), which is locked to *trust*
- 孚 (*fú*) — *confidence*, working gloss (A6)
- 悔亡 · 厲 · 吝 (*huǐ wáng · lì · lìn*) — *regret goes · peril · stinting*, working glosses (A4)
