## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **mountain** (艮 · yang · stopping). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along).
**Across** — mountain ↔ lake under sky ↔ earth.

**What the tradition reads.** The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) reads the figure as a picture: 地中有山 (*dì
zhōng yǒu shān*, "within the earth there is a mountain"), and the noble one 以裒多益寡，稱物平施
(*yǐ póu duō yì guǎ, chēng wù píng shī*, "takes from the much, adds to the little, weighs things
and gives evenly"). The 彖 (*tuàn*) makes it a law of both poles: 天道下濟而光明，地道卑而上行
(*tiān dào xià jì ér guāng míng, dì dào bēi ér shàng xíng*, "the Tao of the sky comes down to help
and shines with clarity; the Tao of earth is low and moves upward"), and 謙尊而光，卑而不可逾
(*qiān zūn ér guāng, bēi ér bù kě yú*, "modest, it is honoured and shines; low, and it cannot be
stepped over").

**What the polarity implies.** The mountain is height, and here the height is inside, under the
ground. Its odd line — the closed top — is the one firm line of the whole figure, at line 3, pressed
up against the earth's yielding underside. Stopping (止, *zhǐ*) turns inward: the mountain keeps its
own peak in, and the earth over it goes along and lies level. So modesty here is not smallness. It
is the single strong thing in the figure, carrying from underneath, and the lines give that line
the best word in the hexagram: 勞謙君子，有終吉 (*láo qiān jūnzǐ, yǒu zhōng jí*, "the toiling,
modest noble one: there is an ending; auspicious", 15.3). The 彖's (*tuàn*) 卑而不可逾 (*bēi ér bù kě yú*, "low, and it cannot be stepped over") is the structure
exactly: you can walk over earth, but not over the mountain inside it. It is also why the figure's
modesty can fight — 利用侵伐 (*lì yòng qīn fá*, "favourable to invade and strike", 15.5) — which no
meekness could. Against 16, its figure turned over: the same single firm line, held under the
surface here, breaking out above it there.

**For the name.** **Modesty** — deepens: *modus*, measure, is what the signature shows — height
kept at the level of the ground, 稱物平施 (*chēng wù píng shī*, "weigh things and give evenly") made into a figure. **Candidates the signature suggests:**
*Lowliness* — survives, with strain: 卑以自牧 (*bēi yǐ zì mù*, "low, it tends itself", the 小象
(*xiǎo xiàng*) on 15.1) fits, but 利用行師 (*lì yòng xíng shī*, "favourable to march the army",
15.6) sits badly with it; *Hidden Height* — fails the lines: 謙謙君子 (*qiān qiān jūnzǐ*, "modest,
modest noble one", 15.1) and 鳴謙 (*míng qiān*, "modesty that calls out", 15.2) use 謙 (*qiān*, "modest") as a quality
of a person, and a buried peak cannot be doubled or made to call.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Modesty is height that keeps itself level with the ground — and in this figure it is the
strength that sees things through.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 謙 (*qiān*) as 敬也 (*jìng yě*,
"respect"), and writes it with the speech radical: it is a way of speaking and bearing oneself toward
others, not a feeling about oneself. The judgment gives it one promise: 謙：亨，君子有終 (*qiān:
hēng, jūnzǐ yǒu zhōng*, "modesty: gets through; the noble one has an ending") — that is, carries
what is begun through to its end. And the lines refuse to make it meek. The modest one crosses the
great river, toils, directs, and in the last two lines marches an army. The 序卦 (*xùguà*) says why
it follows 14: 有大者不可以盈 (*yǒu dà zhě bù kě yǐ yíng*, "one who has the great cannot be full").

### The polarities in it

Mountain within, earth without. The mountain's odd line is its closed top, and here that top —
line 3 — is the only firm line in the figure, pressed up under five yielding ones. The height is
real, and it is kept inside. The mountain stops at its own peak and does not push through; the earth
above goes along, lies flat over it, and hides it. **What results is height that no one sees and no
one can walk over** — the 彖's 卑而不可逾 (*bēi ér bù kě yú*, "low, and it cannot be stepped over").

### In nature

地中有山 (*dì zhōng yǒu shān*, "within the earth there is a mountain", 大象). There is a way this is
simply true. Stand on a river plain and look at the soil under your feet: it is mountain, ground
down and carried. Frost and rain loosen rock on the heights, streams carry it, floods lay it out
flat, and a floodplain is built of hills. The 彖 sees the process: 地道變盈而流謙 (*dì dào biàn yíng
ér liú qiān*, "the Tao of earth changes the full and flows toward the modest"). The 大象 makes a
practice of it: 裒多益寡，稱物平施 (*póu duō yì guǎ, chēng wù píng shī*, "take from the much, add to
the little; weigh things and give evenly") — which is what water does to a mountain, given time.

Watch the moon, too. The 彖's first law is 天道虧盈而益謙 (*tiān dào kuī yíng ér yì qiān*, "the Tao
of the sky wanes the full and adds to the modest"). The full moon starts to lose its edge the next
night; the thin new crescent only grows.

Two of the line words are pictures. 鳴 (*míng*), in 鳴謙 (*míng qiān*, "modesty that calls out",
lines 2 and 6), is in 說文 鳥聲也 (*niǎo shēng yě*, "a bird's voice") — the call by which a bird is
found by its own kind. And 勞 (*láo*, "toil"), in line 3, 說文 explains through fire: 熒，火燒冂，用力者勞
(*yíng, huǒ shāo jiōng, yòng lì zhě láo*, "fire burning a building; those who put their strength to
it toil"). Toil, in the graph, is the work of fighting a house fire.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 謙：亨，君子有終 — "modesty: gets through; the noble one has an ending." 有終
(*yǒu zhōng*) is bringing a thing to its close, and the 彖 ends on it: 君子之終也 (*jūnzǐ zhī zhōng
yě*, "this is the noble one's ending").

**Line 1.** 謙謙君子，用涉大川，吉 (*qiān qiān jūnzǐ, yòng shè dà chuān, jí*, "modest, modest noble
one: with this, cross the great river — auspicious"). The lowest place, modesty doubled, and it can
cross the widest water. The 小象: 卑以自牧也 (*bēi yǐ zì mù yě*, "low, it tends itself") — 說文's 牧
(*mù*) is a herder with cattle.

**Line 2.** 鳴謙，貞吉 (*míng qiān, zhēn jí*, "modesty that calls out: holding firm, auspicious").
The centre of the lower figure. 王弼: 鳴者聲名聞之謂也 (*míng zhě shēng míng wén zhī wèi yě*, "鳴
means one's name is heard"). Modesty that has become known, and does not change for it. The 小象:
中心得也 (*zhōng xīn dé yě*, "it is gained at the centre of the heart").

**Line 3.** 勞謙君子，有終吉 (*láo qiān jūnzǐ, yǒu zhōng jí*, "the toiling, modest noble one: an
ending — auspicious"). The one firm line, the hidden peak, carrying all the rest; and the judgment's
own words, 有終 (*yǒu zhōng*, "an ending"), come back here and nowhere else. The 繫辭上 comments:
勞而不伐，有功而不德 (*láo ér bù fá, yǒu gōng ér bù dé*, "toils and does not boast; has the
achievement and does not claim it"). The 小象: 萬民服也 (*wàn mín fú yě*, "the ten thousand people
accept it").

**Line 4.** 无不利，撝謙 (*wú bù lì, huī qiān*, "nothing not advantageous: modesty that directs").
說文 gives 撝 (*huī*) two senses — 裂也 (*liè yě*, "to tear apart") and 手指也 (*shǒu zhǐ yě*, "to point
with the hand"); 王弼 takes the second: 指撝皆謙 (*zhǐ huī jiē qiān*, "pointing and directing, all
modest"). Just above the firm line, at the seam: modesty in telling others where to go.

**Line 5.** 不富，以其鄰，利用侵伐，无不利 (*bù fù, yǐ qí lín, lì yòng qīn fá, wú bù lì*, "not
wealthy; it goes with its neighbours; advantageous to advance and strike; nothing not
advantageous"). The ruler's place, held by a yielding line with no wealth, only neighbours — 說文's
鄰 (*lín*) is 五家爲鄰 (*wǔ jiā wéi lín*, "five households make a neighbourhood"). 說文's 侵 (*qīn*) is
a hand with a broom, advancing as one sweeps: 漸進也 (*jiàn jìn yě*, "advancing by degrees"). The
小象 names the target: 征不服也 (*zhēng bù fú yě*, "setting out against those who will not accept").

**Line 6.** 鳴謙，利用行師，征邑國 (*míng qiān, lì yòng xíng shī, zhēng yì guó*, "modesty that
calls out: advantageous to march the army, to set out against towns and states"). The outer edge,
and modesty has had to raise its voice. The 小象: 志未得也 (*zhì wèi dé yě*, "the aim is not yet
reached").

Not one line carries 凶 (*xiōng*, "ominous"), 吝 (*lìn*, "stinting"), 悔 (*huǐ*, "regret"), 咎 (*jiù*,
"blame") or 厲 (*lì*, "peril"). 王弼 noticed, and gave the reason: 皆无凶咎悔吝者以謙爲主也 (*jiē wú
xiōng jiù huǐ lìn zhě yǐ qiān wéi zhǔ yě*, "none has ominous, blame, regret or stinting, because
modesty is the master").

### In a life now

The colleague who does the unglamorous work and finishes it. The person whose name gets known and
who does not change for it (line 2). The head of a project who points at the task rather than at
themselves (4). The one with no money and good neighbours (5). The hexagram will not let modesty
mean weakness: its last two lines advance, strike and march, and the 小象 says against whom — those
who will not accept. Modesty may have to fight; it does not boast when it wins. Wherever fortune is
uneven — in a family, a workplace, a country, a watershed — the 大象's measure is exact: take from
the much, add to the little, weigh, give evenly. It is the level a river makes of a mountain.

### An echo from the Laozi

**No shared word; a shared idea, and a sharp parting.** The Laozi never writes 謙 (*qiān*) — it is
about little else, and never names it. The nearest contacts are in the Wings, not the core.
Chapter 77, in Shalom's English: *"The way of nature / takes from where there is too much / and mends
where there is too little"* — the 大象's 裒多益寡 (*póu duō yì guǎ*, "take from the much, add to the
little") in other graphs. And chapter 22: *"They do not claim the work, and so the work is theirs"* —
which shares 伐 (*fá*, "boast") and 功 (*gōng*, "achievement") with the 繫辭上's comment on line 3.
In the core, at 15.5, 伐 is a blow with a weapon.

**The parting is the finding.** Chapter 77 goes on: *"The way of people is not like this."* The 彖
says the opposite — 人道惡盈而好謙 (*rén dào wù yíng ér hào qiān*, "the Tao of people hates the full
and loves the modest") — and sets people in one law with sky, earth and 鬼神 (*guǐ shén*, "ghosts and
the numinous"). The Laozi sets nature against human habit; the Wings put humans under the same law.
And the Laozi's low place is water's, while this hexagram's low place carries an army. This echo is
thematic, at the Wing layer, and does not reach the core.
