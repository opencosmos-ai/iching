## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **water** (坎 · yang · sinking in). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Across** — water ↔ fire meets sky ↔ earth, yang on both sides, the odd line at the middle within
and none without.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*): 訟，上剛下險，險而健，訟 (*sòng, shàng gāng xià xiǎn,
xiǎn ér jiàn, sòng*), "訟 (*sòng*): firm above, hazard below; hazard, and vigour: 訟." It adds
終凶，訟不可成也 (*zhōng xiōng, sòng bù kě chéng yě*), "the end is ominous: a dispute cannot be
carried through." The 大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image): 天與水違行，訟；君子以作事謀始 (*tiān yǔ
shuǐ wéi xíng, sòng; jūnzǐ yǐ zuò shì móu shǐ*), "sky and water go opposite ways: 訟; the noble one,
by it, plans at the start of an undertaking." The tradition reads a quarrel that should be settled
early or not pursued.

**What the polarity implies.** This is 5 turned over, and the turn reverses the direction: now the
sinking pole is below and the rising pole above, so they draw apart — the 大象's 違行 (*wéi xíng*,
"go opposite ways") is the structure stated as an image. Within is a hazard with a firm line closed
up at its centre: a grievance lodged in the self, hard and hidden. Without is all firm and rising
away: a counterpart that does not bend and does not come down. No amount of pressure from below
closes a gap that widens by the figure's own motion. So the lines reward those who let go — 初六
(*chū liù*) 不永所事 (*bù yǒng suǒ shì*), "does not prolong the affair"; 九二 (*jiǔ èr*) 不克訟，歸而逋
(*bù kè sòng, guī ér bū*), "cannot win the dispute, goes home and slips away" — and punish the one who
wins: the top line is given a belt and 終朝三褫之 (*zhōng zhāo sān chǐ zhī*), "has it stripped three
times in a morning."

The one line where 訟 is auspicious is 九五 (*jiǔ wǔ*), the centre of the sky: the dispute resolved
from above, by a third party — which is why the judgment says 利見大人 (*lì jiàn dà rén*), "favourable
to see the great person." In 5 the vigour inside waits and is fed; here the hazard inside presses
and is stripped. Same two trigrams; what differs is which one is the self.

**For the name.** **Dispute** — supported and deepened: two forces moving apart explain why the
dispute 不可成 (*bù kě chéng*, "cannot be carried through") and why winning it is empty; *dispute*
keeps the sense that it is argued before someone, not fought out. **Candidates the signature
suggests:** *Grievance* — the hazard within; survives the lines, if stiffly: 不克訟 (*bù kè sòng*)
"cannot carry the grievance", 訟，元吉 (*sòng, yuán jí*) "the grievance: fundamentally auspicious" —
readable only as the grievance heard. *Going Apart* — from 違行 (*wéi xíng*, "go opposite ways");
fails the lines at 不克訟 (*bù kè sòng*), "cannot win the —", since a going-apart is not won or lost.
