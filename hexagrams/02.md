## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going
along). **Doubled** — the yin pole of sky ↔ earth taken twice, with no odd line and no firm line at
all.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) answers 1 phrase for phrase:
至哉坤元，萬物資生，乃順承天 (*zhì zāi kūn yuán, wànwù zī shēng, nǎi shùn chéng tiān*), "how far it
reaches, 坤's (*kūn*'s) origin — the countless things draw their birth from it, and so it goes along
with the sky and receives it"; and 坤厚載物 (*kūn hòu zài wù*), "坤 is thick and carries things." The
大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image): 地勢坤，君子以厚德載物 (*dì shì kūn, jūnzǐ yǐ hòu dé zài wù*),
"the lie of the land is 坤; the noble one, by it, carries things with a thick integrity." The
tradition reads the receiving, bearing counterpart to 乾 (*qián*) — not its absence.

**What the polarity implies.** Open within and open without: whatever comes is let in, and nothing
in the figure starts anything. So the lines are about what accumulates in an open place — 履霜，堅冰至
(*lǚ shuāng, jiān bīng zhì*), "treading frost: hard ice comes"; 含章 (*hán zhāng*), "holding the
pattern inside"; 括囊 (*kuò náng*), "tying up the sack." It is a figure of storage and slow
consequence, where 1 is a figure of timing. And because there is no firm line to go along *with*,
the going-along has to find its object outside the figure: 先迷後得主 (*xiān mí hòu dé zhǔ*), "first
lost, later finds a master." The 文言 (*wényán*) refuses to call this weakness —
坤至柔而動也剛 (*kūn zhì róu ér dòng yě gāng*), "坤 is yielding to the last degree, yet its moving is
firm" — and the structure agrees: six open lines are also six lines that hold whatever is put in
them.

Read with 1: where 乾's hazard is overreach, 坤's is to go along so far that nothing is left to go
along with. At the top the yin pole, with no yang to follow, takes the yang's shape:
龍戰于野，其血玄黃 (*lóng zhàn yú yě, qí xuè xuán huáng*), "dragons fight in the open country, their
blood dark and yellow" — and the 文言 says why, 陰疑於陽必戰 (*yīn yí yú yáng bì zhàn*), "yin taken
for yang must fight." 乾's extra line ends in a leaderless flock; 坤's in 利永貞 (*lì yǒng zhēn*),
"favourable: lasting steadiness" (貞 *zhēn* not yet ruled).

**For the name.** **Carrying** — supported and deepened: doubled going-along with no ruling line is
a load-bearing that does not choose its load, which is what 載物 (*zài wù*, "carries things")
says twice in the Wings; and it answers *Striving* as bearing answers beginning. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Receiving* — from 順承天 (*shùn chéng tiān*, "goes along with the sky and
receives it"); survives the lines, where 履霜 ("treading frost"), 含章 ("holding the pattern
inside") and 括囊 ("tying up the sack") are all receiving and keeping. *Following* — from 順
(*shùn*, "going along") and 得主 (*dé zhǔ*, "finds a master"); fails the lines at the top, where
龍戰于野 (*lóng zhàn yú yě*, "dragons fight in the open country") is yin that has stopped following.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**The ground that takes whatever is given and carries it to its end without making it its own.**
說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 坤 (*kūn*) in two words, 地也 (*dì yě*, "the earth"), written with 土 (*tǔ*,
"soil"). Where 乾's (*qián*'s) lines are moments, 坤's (*kūn*'s) are what accumulates: frost that
becomes ice, a pattern kept inside, a sack tied shut, a yellow skirt. And its judgment says how it
moves — as a mare, not a dragon; second, not first. **The text never calls this weakness.** Its one
full verdict, 元吉 (*yuán jí*, "from the source, auspicious"), goes to the line that keeps its beauty
low and in the middle.

### The polarities in it

Earth within, earth without: the open pole twice, and no firm line anywhere. Nothing in the figure
starts anything; everything that comes is let in. So the going-along has to find what it goes along
with outside itself — the judgment's 先迷後得主 (*xiān mí hòu dé zhǔ*, "going first, lost; coming
after, finds a master"). Its hazard is 乾's (*qián*'s) turned over: not overreach, but going along
until nothing is left to follow, and then taking the other pole's shape — at the top the open figure
grows a dragon. The 文言 (*wényán*) says the pole is not slack: 坤至柔而動也剛 (*kūn zhì róu ér dòng yě
gāng*, "坤 is soft to the last degree, and its moving is firm").

### In nature

Start with the first line: 履霜，堅冰至 (*lǚ shuāng, jiān bīng zhì*, "treading on frost: hard ice
comes"). The first hoarfrost comes on a clear, windless autumn night, when the ground gives up its
warmth to an open sky; you find it at dawn on the grass in the low places, and it is gone by
mid-morning. It comes weeks before any ice that will hold a person's weight. But it does not lie:
once it has come, the freeze is on its way. 說文 gives 霜 (*shuāng*) as 喪也。成物者 (*sàng yě. chéng wù
zhě*, "a loss; that which completes things") — the frost that ends the growing year is also what
finishes it. The 文言 reads the line as a law of accumulation: 其所由來者漸矣 (*qí suǒ yóu lái zhě jiàn
yǐ*, "where it came from, it came by degrees").

Then the land. The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) has 地勢坤 (*dì shì kūn*, "the lie of the land: 坤"), and line 2
has its shape: 直方大 (*zhí fāng dà*, "straight, square, great"). Stand at the edge of a plain after
harvest and that is what it is. And the judgment's animal, the 牝馬 (*pìn mǎ*, "mare"), which the 彖
(*tuàn*) glosses 牝馬地類，行地无疆 (*pìn mǎ dì lèi, xíng dì wú jiāng*, "the mare is of the earth's
kind; she travels the land without bound"). Horses left to themselves graze across miles of ground a
day, and watchers of free-ranging herds often see an older mare start the walk to water, the others
falling in behind. The book chose the mare, and a mare's way of leading is to go where the herd
needs to go.

Last, the colours. 說文 gives 黃 (*huáng*) as 地之色也 (*dì zhī sè yě*, "the colour of the earth") —
the loess of the Yellow River country, the soil the Zhou farmed — and 玄 (*xuán*) as 黑而有赤色者
(*hēi ér yǒu chì sè zhě*, "black with red in it"). The 文言 makes them the two halves of the world:
天玄而地黃 (*tiān xuán ér dì huáng*, "the sky dark, the earth yellow").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 坤：元亨。利牝馬之貞 (*kūn: yuán hēng. lì pìn mǎ zhī zhēn*) — "Carrying
*(proposed)*: from the source, it gets through; advantageous: a mare's holding firm." Then
君子有攸往，先迷後得主 (*jūnzǐ yǒu yōu wǎng, xiān mí hòu dé zhǔ*, "the noble one has somewhere to go:
going first, lost; coming after, finds a master"), and 利西南得朋，東北喪朋 (*lì xīnán dé péng, dōngběi
sàng péng*, "advantageous: in the southwest, finding companions; in the northeast, losing them"), and
安貞，吉 (*ān zhēn, jí*, "settled, holding firm: auspicious"). The core gives no key to the directions.
說卦 (*shuōguà*) ch 5 later sets 坤 (*kūn*) in the southwest and the mountain in the northeast, and
王弼 calls the southwest 致養之地 (*zhì yǎng zhī dì*, "the ground where nourishing is done") —
companions found among one's own kind. That is the Wings' key and 王弼's, not the text's.

**Line 1** is the whole figure in small: 履霜，堅冰至 (*lǚ shuāng, jiān bīng zhì*, "treading on frost:
hard ice comes"). What the open ground lets in, it keeps, and the end is readable in the beginning.

**Line 2** is the lower centre, and the one soft line sitting in its own soft place. 直方大，不習无不利
(*zhí fāng dà, bù xí wú bù lì*, "straight, square, great: without practising, nothing that is not
advantageous"). 說文 gives 習 (*xí*) as 數飛也 (*shuò fēi yě*, "flying again and again"), the
fledgling's practice. The earth needs none; it does not rehearse being level. 王弼:
任其自然而物自生 (*rèn qí zì rán ér wù zì shēng*, "it leaves them to be of themselves, and things
are born of themselves").

**Line 3** is at the seam. 含章可貞。或從王事，无成有終 (*hán zhāng kě zhēn. huò cóng wáng shì, wú
chéng yǒu zhōng*, "holding the pattern inside: it can hold firm. Perhaps following the ruler's
affairs: no completion, and an end"). 說文's 章 (*zhāng*) is 樂竟爲一章 (*yuè jìng wéi yī zhāng*, "a
piece of music brought to its close"). The work is finished and not claimed. The 文言 gives this to
地道也，妻道也，臣道也 (*dì dào yě, qī dào yě, chén dào yě*, "the Tao of earth, of a wife, of a
minister") — the Wings' social reading, noted here and not taken up.

**Line 4** crosses into the outer earth and closes. 括囊，无咎无譽 (*kuò náng, wú jiù wú yù*, "tying up
the sack: no blame, no praise"). The 文言: 天地閉，賢人隱 (*tiān dì bì, xián rén yǐn*, "when sky and
earth close, worthy people hide"). The grain is in the sack; nothing shows, so nothing is praised.

**Line 5** is the upper centre, and the fullest verdict in the figure. 黃裳，元吉 (*huáng cháng, yuán
jí*, "a yellow lower garment: from the source, auspicious"). 王弼: 黃中之色也，裳下之飾也 (*huáng zhōng
zhī sè yě, cháng xià zhī shì yě*, "yellow is the colour of the middle; the lower garment is the
adornment below"). The ruler's seat, taken in earth's colour and the garment of the lower body.

**Line 6** is past the edge. 龍戰于野，其血玄黃 (*lóng zhàn yú yě, qí xuè xuán huáng*, "dragons fight in
the open country; their blood is dark and yellow"). The only dragon in 坤 (*kūn*): the open pole at
its limit taking the firm pole's shape. The 文言: 陰疑於陽必戰 (*yīn yí yú yáng bì zhàn*, "yin taken
for yang must fight"). The core gives no verdict; it leaves the blood on the field.

**Using the sixes.** 利永貞 (*lì yǒng zhēn*, "advantageous: holding firm for long"). 說文 draws 永
(*yǒng*) as 象水坙理之長 (*xiàng shuǐ jīng lǐ zhī cháng*, "the long veins of running water"). 乾
(*qián*) ends in no head; 坤 (*kūn*) ends in length.

### In a life now

This is the figure for work that carries other people's lives: the carer, the nurse, the parent,
the farmer, the editor, the deputy who runs the place while someone else's name is on the door. It
counsels going second, letting things be what they are, keeping what is fine in oneself inside and
in the middle, and closing the sack when the times close. It is also a figure for **reading small
signs early** — the first cold word in a marriage, the first missed payment, the first symptom:
frost is not ice, and it tells you ice is coming. Its warning is line 6's: the one who has gone
along so long that they take the shape of what they served — the deputy who fights for the chair,
the carer who breaks — and there is blood on both sides.

The 大象 turns it toward practice: 地勢坤，君子以厚德載物 (*dì shì kūn, jūnzǐ yǐ hòu dé zài wù*, "the
lie of the land is 坤; the noble one, by it, carries things with a thick integrity"). Thickness, not
height: the capacity to hold weight.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a graph for the female, and part over what she wins.** Chapter 61: *"The
female always overcomes the male by stillness. / By stillness she is underneath."* The graph is 牝
(*pìn*, "female") — in 坤's (*kūn*'s) own judgment, 利牝馬之貞 (*lì pìn mǎ zhī zhēn*, "advantageous: a
mare's holding firm") — and the 文言 adds the Laozi's other word, 至靜 (*zhì jìng*, "still to the last
degree"). Both books put endurance in the female and in being below.

Then they part. The Laozi's female overcomes; 坤 (*kūn*) completes nothing of its own — 无成有終 (*wú
chéng yǒu zhōng*, "no completion, and an end") — and when it forgets that, at the top, it does not win
by stillness: it fights, and bleeds. And 玄 (*xuán*, "dark"), which chapter 6 gives to the female
herself — *"This is the dark female"* — is in 坤 (*kūn*) the colour of the blood, and the 文言 hands it
to the other pole: 天玄而地黃 (*tiān xuán ér dì huáng*, "the sky dark, the earth yellow"). **In the
Laozi the dark belongs to the female; in 坤 it belongs to the sky.** The difference is kept.

### Words kept

- 坤 (*kūn*) — *carrying*, the proposed name (not yet approved); *earth* is the trigram's image; never *the receptive*
- 牝馬 (*pìn mǎ*) — *a mare*; 牝 alone is *female*
- 主 (*zhǔ*) — *a master*; 王 (*wáng*) — *ruler*, never *king* (locked)
- 柔 (*róu*) — *soft* (弱 is *yielding*); 靜 (*jìng*) — *stillness*
- 玄 (*xuán*) — *dark*, never *mystery* (locked); 黃 (*huáng*) — *yellow*
- 德 (*dé*) — *integrity*, never *virtue* (locked); 自然 (*zì rán*) — *of itself* (locked)
- 道 (*dào*) — *the Tao* in the 文言; the core text of 坤 does not use it
- 元 · 亨 · 貞 (*yuán · hēng · zhēn*) — *from the source · gets through · holding firm*, working glosses (A6)
