## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **earth** (坤 · yin · going
along). **Doubled** — the yin pole of sky ↔ earth taken twice, with no odd line and no firm line at
all.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) answers 1 phrase for phrase:
至哉坤元，萬物資生，乃順承天 (*zhì zāi kūn yuán, wànwù zī shēng, nǎi shùn chéng tiān*), "how far it
reaches, 坤's (*kūn*'s) origin — the countless things draw their birth from it, and so it goes along
with the sky and receives it"; and 坤厚載物 (*kūn hòu zài wù*), "坤 is thick and carries things." The
大象 (*dà xiàng*, the great image): 地勢坤，君子以厚德載物 (*dì shì kūn, jūnzǐ yǐ hòu dé zài wù*),
"the lie of the land is 坤; the noble one, by it, carries things with a thick integrity." The
tradition reads the receiving, bearing counterpart to 乾 (*qián*) — not its absence.

**What the polarity implies.** Open within and open without: whatever comes is let in, and nothing
in the figure starts anything. So the lines are about what accumulates in an open place — 履霜，堅冰至
(*lǚ shuāng, jiān bīng zhì*), "treading frost: hard ice comes"; 含章 (*hán zhāng*), "holding the
pattern inside"; 括囊 (*kuò náng*), "tying up the sack." It is a figure of storage and slow
consequence, where 1 is a figure of timing. And because there is no firm line to go along *with*,
the going-along has to find its object outside the figure: 先迷後得主 (*xiān mí hòu dé zhǔ*), "first
lost, later finds a master." The 文言 (*wényán*) refuses to call this weakness —
坤至柔而動也剛 (*kūn zhì róu ér dòng yě gāng*), "坤 is yielding to the last degree, yet its moving is
firm" — and the structure agrees: six open lines are also six lines that hold whatever is put in
them.

Read with 1: where 乾's hazard is overreach, 坤's is to go along so far that nothing is left to go
along with. At the top the yin pole, with no yang to follow, takes the yang's shape:
龍戰于野，其血玄黃 (*lóng zhàn yú yě, qí xuè xuán huáng*), "dragons fight in the open country, their
blood dark and yellow" — and the 文言 says why, 陰疑於陽必戰 (*yīn yí yú yáng bì zhàn*), "yin taken
for yang must fight." 乾's extra line ends in a leaderless flock; 坤's in 利永貞 (*lì yǒng zhēn*),
"favourable: lasting steadiness" (貞 *zhēn* not yet ruled).

**For the name.** **Carrying** — supported and deepened: doubled going-along with no ruling line is
a load-bearing that does not choose its load, which is what 載物 (*zài wù*, "carries things")
says twice in the Wings; and it answers *Striving* as bearing answers beginning. **Candidates the
signature suggests:** *Receiving* — from 順承天 (*shùn chéng tiān*, "goes along with the sky and
receives it"); survives the lines, where 履霜 ("treading frost"), 含章 ("holding the pattern
inside") and 括囊 ("tying up the sack") are all receiving and keeping. *Following* — from 順
(*shùn*, "going along") and 得主 (*dé zhǔ*, "finds a master"); fails the lines at the top, where
龍戰于野 (*lóng zhàn yú yě*, "dragons fight in the open country") is yin that has stopped following.

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), Wang Bi (the 3rd-century commentator), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own
translation. The formula words — 吉 ("auspicious"), 凶 ("ominous"), 无咎 ("no blame"), 亨 ("gets
through"), 孚 ("confidence"), 貞 ("holding firm") — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6
rule on them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**The ground that takes whatever is given and carries it to its end without making it its own.** The
Shuowen, the c. 100 CE character dictionary, gives 坤 ("Carrying") in two words, 地也 ("the earth"),
written with 土 ("soil"). Where the lines of 乾 ("Striving") are moments, those of 坤 ("Carrying") are
what accumulates: frost that becomes ice, a pattern kept inside, a sack tied shut, a yellow skirt.
And its judgment says how it moves — as a mare, not a dragon; second, not first. **The text never
calls this weakness.** Its one full verdict, 元吉 ("from the source, auspicious"), goes to the line
that keeps its beauty low and in the middle.

### The polarities in it

Earth within, earth without: the open pole twice, and no firm line anywhere. Nothing in the figure
starts anything; everything that comes is let in. So the going-along has to find what it goes along
with outside itself — the judgment's 先迷後得主 ("going first, lost; coming after, finds a master"). Its
hazard is that of 乾 ("Striving") turned over: not overreach, but going along until nothing is left
to follow, and then taking the other pole's shape — at the top the open figure grows a dragon. The
Wenyan, the Wing on hexagrams 1 and 2, says the pole is not slack: 坤至柔而動也剛 ("Carrying is soft to the
last degree, and its moving is firm").

### In nature

Start with the first line: 履霜，堅冰至 ("treading on frost: hard ice comes"). The first hoarfrost comes
on a clear, windless autumn night, when the ground gives up its warmth to an open sky; you find it
at dawn on the grass in the low places, and it is gone by mid-morning. It comes weeks before any ice
that will hold a person's weight. But it does not lie: once it has come, the freeze is on its way.
The Shuowen gives 霜 ("frost") as 喪也。成物者 ("a loss; that which completes things") — the frost that
ends the growing year is also what finishes it. The Wenyan reads the line as a law of accumulation:
其所由來者漸矣 ("where it came from, it came by degrees").

Then the land. The Great Image, the Wing on the whole figure, has 地勢坤 ("the lie of the land:
Carrying"), and line 2 has its shape: 直方大 ("straight, square, great"). Stand at the edge of a plain
after harvest and that is what it is. And the judgment's animal, the 牝馬 ("mare"), which the Tuan,
the Wing on the judgment, glosses 牝馬地類，行地无疆 ("the mare is of the earth's kind; she travels the land
without bound"). Horses left to themselves graze across miles of ground a day, and watchers of
free-ranging herds often see an older mare start the walk to water, the others falling in behind.
The book chose the mare, and a mare's way of leading is to go where the herd needs to go.

Last, the colours. The Shuowen gives 黃 ("yellow") as 地之色也 ("the colour of the earth") — the loess of
the Yellow River country, the soil the Zhou farmed — and 玄 ("dark") as 黑而有赤色者 ("black with red in
it"). The Wenyan makes them the two halves of the world: 天玄而地黃 ("the sky dark, the earth yellow").

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 坤：元亨。利牝馬之貞 ("Carrying *(proposed)*: from the source, it gets through;
advantageous: a mare's holding firm"). Then 君子有攸往，先迷後得主 ("the noble one has somewhere to go: going
first, lost; coming after, finds a master"), and 利西南得朋，東北喪朋 ("advantageous: in the southwest,
finding companions; in the northeast, losing them"), and 安貞，吉 ("settled, holding firm: auspicious").
The core gives no key to the directions. The Shuogua, the Wing on the trigrams, ch 5 later sets 坤
("earth") in the southwest and the mountain in the northeast, and Wang Bi calls the southwest 致養之地
("the ground where nourishing is done") — companions found among one's own kind. That is the Wings'
key and Wang Bi's, not the text's.

**Line 1** is the whole figure in small: 履霜，堅冰至 ("treading on frost: hard ice comes"). What the open
ground lets in, it keeps, and the end is readable in the beginning.

**Line 2** is the lower centre, and the one soft line sitting in its own soft place. 直方大，不習无不利
("straight, square, great: without practising, nothing that is not advantageous"). The Shuowen gives
習 ("practising") as 數飛也 ("flying again and again"), the fledgling's practice. The earth needs none;
it does not rehearse being level. Wang Bi: 任其自然而物自生 ("it leaves them to be of themselves, and things
are born of themselves").

**Line 3** is at the seam. 含章可貞。或從王事，无成有終 ("holding the pattern inside: it can hold firm. Perhaps
following the ruler's affairs: no completion, and an end"). The Shuowen's 章 ("pattern") is 樂竟爲一章 ("a
piece of music brought to its close"). The work is finished and not claimed. The Wenyan gives this
to 地道也，妻道也，臣道也 ("the Tao of earth, of a wife, of a minister") — the Wings' social reading, noted
here and not taken up.

**Line 4** crosses into the outer earth and closes. 括囊，无咎无譽 ("tying up the sack: no blame, no
praise"). The Wenyan: 天地閉，賢人隱 ("when sky and earth close, worthy people hide"). The grain is in the
sack; nothing shows, so nothing is praised.

**Line 5** is the upper centre, and the fullest verdict in the figure. 黃裳，元吉 ("a yellow lower
garment: from the source, auspicious"). Wang Bi: 黃中之色也，裳下之飾也 ("yellow is the colour of the middle;
the lower garment is the adornment below"). The ruler's seat, taken in earth's colour and the
garment of the lower body.

**Line 6** is past the edge. 龍戰于野，其血玄黃 ("dragons fight in the open country; their blood is dark and
yellow"). The only dragon in 坤 ("Carrying"): the open pole at its limit taking the firm pole's
shape. The Wenyan: 陰疑於陽必戰 ("yin taken for yang must fight"). The core gives no verdict; it leaves
the blood on the field.

**Using the sixes.** 利永貞 ("advantageous: holding firm for long"). The Shuowen draws 永 ("long") as
象水坙理之長 ("the long veins of running water"). 乾 ("Striving") ends in no head; 坤 ("Carrying") ends in
length.

### In a life now

This is the figure for work that carries other people's lives: the carer, the nurse, the parent, the
farmer, the editor, the deputy who runs the place while someone else's name is on the door. It
counsels going second, letting things be what they are, keeping what is fine in oneself inside and
in the middle, and closing the sack when the times close. It is also a figure for **reading small
signs early** — the first cold word in a marriage, the first missed payment, the first symptom:
frost is not ice, and it tells you ice is coming. Its warning is line 6's: the one who has gone
along so long that they take the shape of what they served — the deputy who fights for the chair,
the carer who breaks — and there is blood on both sides.

The Great Image turns it toward practice: 地勢坤，君子以厚德載物 ("the lie of the land is Carrying; the noble
one, by it, carries things with a thick integrity"). Thickness, not height: the capacity to hold
weight.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The two books share a graph for the female, and part over what she wins.** Chapter 61: *"The
female always overcomes the male by stillness. / By stillness she is underneath."* The graph is 牝
("female") — in the judgment of 坤 ("Carrying") itself, 利牝馬之貞 ("advantageous: a mare's holding firm")
— and the Wenyan adds the Laozi's other word, 至靜 ("still to the last degree"). Both books put
endurance in the female and in being below.

Then they part. The Laozi's female overcomes; 坤 ("Carrying") completes nothing of its own — 无成有終
("no completion, and an end") — and when it forgets that, at the top, it does not win by stillness:
it fights, and bleeds. And 玄 ("dark"), which chapter 6 gives to the female herself — *"This is the
dark female"* — is in 坤 ("Carrying") the colour of the blood, and the Wenyan hands it to the other
pole: 天玄而地黃 ("the sky dark, the earth yellow"). **In the Laozi the dark belongs to the female; in 坤
("Carrying") it belongs to the sky.** The difference is kept.
