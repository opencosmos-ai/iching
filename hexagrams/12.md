## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Crossed** — sky ↔ earth, the sinking pole below and the rising pole above, so they move apart.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) is 11's, negated clause by clause:
天地不交而萬物不通也 (*tiān dì bù jiāo ér wàn wù bù tōng yě*, "sky and earth do not meet, and the
countless things do not get through"), 上下不交而天下无邦也 (*shàng xià bù jiāo ér tiān xià wú bāng
yě*, "above and below do not meet, and under the sky there are no states"), 內陰而外陽，內柔而外剛
(*nèi yīn ér wài yáng, nèi róu ér wài gāng*, "yin within and yang without; yielding within, firm
without"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) has the noble one 以儉德辟難，不可榮以祿 (*yǐ jiǎn dé bì nán, bù kě
róng yǐ lù*, "keeps integrity spare to avoid trouble, and cannot be honoured with a salary"): a time
when the channels are shut, and the answer is to go lean and stay out of office.

**What the polarity implies.** Here everything is in its right place — sky above, earth below,
exactly as 天地定位 (*tiān dì dìng wèi*, "sky and earth set the positions") has them — and that is
the trouble. Each trigram, doing what it does, goes away from the other: earth settles, sky climbs,
and every move widens the gap. Nothing stands between them; there is no wall to break. The blockage
is distance, and correctness produces it. Inside, the yielding lines go along with whatever is
nearest, and the lines show the cost: 包承 (*bāo chéng*, "wrapped up, carried along", 12.2), then
包羞 (*bāo xiū*, "wrapped up in shame", 12.3). The way out does not come from below. It comes from
the sky, which, having risen as far as it can, has nowhere left to go but over: 休否 (*xiū pǐ*, "the
blockage rests", 12.5), 傾否，先否後喜 (*qīng pǐ, xiān pǐ hòu xǐ*, "topple the blockage: first
blocked, then glad", 12.6), and the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) 否終則傾 (*pǐ zhōng zé qīng*, "blockage at
its end topples"). The two figures open on the same line — 拔茅茹以其彙 (*bá máo rú yǐ qí huì*,
"pull up the couch grass, roots and all, with its kind") — and part on one character: 11 has 征吉
(*zhēng jí*, "setting out: auspicious"), 12 has 貞吉 (*zhēn jí*, "holding firm: auspicious"). In the
meeting you go; in the parting you stay rooted.

**For the name.** **Blockage** — strains, then holds: the signature has no obstacle in it, only
distance, which argues for a parting word; but the lines treat 否 (*pǐ*) as a thing that can rest
and be toppled, and only a blockage topples. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Parting* —
fails the lines: 傾否 (*qīng pǐ*, "topple the 否", 12.6) cannot take it, and 休否 (*xiū pǐ*, "the parting
rests") says the opposite of what the line means; *Standstill* — survives: 先否後喜 (*xiān pǐ hòu xǐ*, "first at a
standstill, then glad") and 大人否 (*dà rén pǐ*, "the great person at a standstill", 12.2) both read,
and it keeps the signature's picture of two bodies no longer moving toward each other. The name
waits on 亨 (*hēng*, not yet ruled — A6), which stands twice in this figure's own lines (12.1, 12.2).
