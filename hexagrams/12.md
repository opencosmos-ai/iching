## The signature — a reading

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-24. This reads the figure through the Wings — the declared lens
([`README.md`](../README.md#the-lens)) — and it is a reading, not a ruling. The name still comes from
the lines ([`read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums`](../principles/read-a-figure-through-its-spectrums.md)).*

**The signature.** Within: **earth** (坤 · yin · going along). Without: **sky** (乾 · yang · vigour).
**Crossed** — sky ↔ earth, the sinking pole below and the rising pole above, so they move apart.

**What the tradition reads.** The 彖 (*tuàn*) is 11's, negated clause by clause:
天地不交而萬物不通也 (*tiān dì bù jiāo ér wàn wù bù tōng yě*, "sky and earth do not meet, and the
countless things do not get through"), 上下不交而天下无邦也 (*shàng xià bù jiāo ér tiān xià wú bāng
yě*, "above and below do not meet, and under the sky there are no states"), 內陰而外陽，內柔而外剛
(*nèi yīn ér wài yáng, nèi róu ér wài gāng*, "yin within and yang without; yielding within, firm
without"). The 大象 (*dà xiàng*) has the noble one 以儉德辟難，不可榮以祿 (*yǐ jiǎn dé bì nán, bù kě
róng yǐ lù*, "keeps integrity spare to avoid trouble, and cannot be honoured with a salary"): a time
when the channels are shut, and the answer is to go lean and stay out of office.

**What the polarity implies.** Here everything is in its right place — sky above, earth below,
exactly as 天地定位 (*tiān dì dìng wèi*, "sky and earth set the positions") has them — and that is
the trouble. Each trigram, doing what it does, goes away from the other: earth settles, sky climbs,
and every move widens the gap. Nothing stands between them; there is no wall to break. The blockage
is distance, and correctness produces it. Inside, the yielding lines go along with whatever is
nearest, and the lines show the cost: 包承 (*bāo chéng*, "wrapped up, carried along", 12.2), then
包羞 (*bāo xiū*, "wrapped up in shame", 12.3). The way out does not come from below. It comes from
the sky, which, having risen as far as it can, has nowhere left to go but over: 休否 (*xiū pǐ*, "the
blockage rests", 12.5), 傾否，先否後喜 (*qīng pǐ, xiān pǐ hòu xǐ*, "topple the blockage: first
blocked, then glad", 12.6), and the 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) 否終則傾 (*pǐ zhōng zé qīng*, "blockage at
its end topples"). The two figures open on the same line — 拔茅茹以其彙 (*bá máo rú yǐ qí huì*,
"pull up the couch grass, roots and all, with its kind") — and part on one character: 11 has 征吉
(*zhēng jí*, "setting out: auspicious"), 12 has 貞吉 (*zhēn jí*, "holding firm: auspicious"). In the
meeting you go; in the parting you stay rooted.

**For the name.** **Blockage** — strains, then holds: the signature has no obstacle in it, only
distance, which argues for a parting word; but the lines treat 否 (*pǐ*) as a thing that can rest
and be toppled, and only a blockage topples. **Candidates the signature suggests:** *Parting* —
fails the lines: 傾否 (*qīng pǐ*, "topple the 否", 12.6) cannot take it, and 休否 (*xiū pǐ*, "the parting
rests") says the opposite of what the line means; *Standstill* — survives: 先否後喜 (*xiān pǐ hòu xǐ*, "first at a
standstill, then glad") and 大人否 (*dà rén pǐ*, "the great person at a standstill", 12.2) both read,
and it keeps the signature's picture of two bodies no longer moving toward each other. The name
waits on 亨 (*hēng*, not yet ruled — A6), which stands twice in this figure's own lines (12.1, 12.2).

## The heart of it

*Draft for Shalom, 2026-09-25 — an interpretive reading, not a translation. Each claim is marked by
layer: the core text (the judgment and the six lines, Western Zhou), the Wings (Warring States to
Han), 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, 3rd century), and echoes of the Tao Te Ching in this project's own translation.
The formula words — 吉, 凶, 无咎, 亨, 孚, 貞 — carry working glosses until WORKLIST A4 and A6 rule on
them ([`hexagram-names.md`](../hexagram-names.md)).*

### Essence

**Sky and earth each in their proper place, turned away from one another, and nothing passes
between them: a time to stay rooted, and to wait for it to tip.** 說文 (*shuōwén*) gives 否 (*pǐ*)
as 不也 (*bù yě*, "no") — a mouth and the negative, a refusal spoken. The judgment opens with the
wrong company, 否之匪人 (*pǐ zhī fěi rén*, "blocked by what is not the right people"), and ends 大往小來
(*dà wǎng xiǎo lái*, "the great goes, the small comes"). But the lines do not stay blocked. The
blockage rests at line 5 and topples at line 6, and before either, at the very bottom, the text
twice says 亨 (*hēng*, "it gets through"). *Blockage*, the lead candidate for the name, is a condition
of the time, not a sentence on the person in it.

### The polarities in it

Earth within, sky without — each where 說卦 (*shuōguà*) says it belongs, and so each, moving by its
own nature, moves away: the earth settles, the sky climbs, and the gap widens with every right move
(see *The signature*, above). No wall stands between them; the blockage is distance. **Here too
the text corrects the structure.** By the figure nothing should get through, yet the two lowest
lines — the earth's, the most bound — each end on 亨. The time is shut; the person in it is not.

### In nature

This is the drought sky. Day after day the air is high, clear and still; no cloud forms, because
nothing rises from the ground to make it, and the ground cracks because nothing comes down.
Sky and earth are both entirely themselves, and there is no weather between them. The 大象 (*dà
xiàng*) says it in four characters: 天地不交 (*tiān dì bù jiāo*, "sky and earth do not meet").

The first line has the same grass as 11: 拔茅茹以其彙 (*bá máo rú yǐ qí huì*, "pull up the couch
grass, roots and all, with its kind"). Grasses that spread by runners outlast drought and fire in
the ground: the tops die back, the joined roots wait. In 11 the line goes, 征吉 (*zhēng jí*,
"setting out — auspicious"); here it stays, 貞吉 (*zhēn jí*, "holding firm — auspicious").

Then line 5's tree. 說文 gives 休 (*xiū*, "rest") as 人依木 (*rén yī mù*, "a person leaning on a
tree"), and 桑 (*sāng*, "mulberry") as 蠶所食葉木 (*cán suǒ shí yè mù*, "the tree whose leaves
silkworms eat"). Mulberry for silkworms is cut back hard and grows from the stool into a dense
clump, and an old stool, many years cut, sits in the field like a knot of roots. That is what the
line ties to.

### The movement of the lines

**The judgment.** 否之匪人，不利君子貞，大往小來 (*pǐ zhī fěi rén, bù lì jūnzǐ zhēn, dà wǎng xiǎo lái*) —
"Blocked by the wrong people; holding firm is not advantageous for the noble one; the great goes,
the small comes." Unlike 11, no 亨 in the judgment. 匪 (*fěi*) is in 說文 a bamboo basket, borrowed
here for "not".

**Line 1** 拔茅茹以其彙，貞吉。亨 (*bá máo rú yǐ qí huì, zhēn jí. hēng*, "pull up the couch grass,
roots and all, with its kind; holding firm — auspicious. It gets through"). Stay rooted with your
own; that gets through. 王弼: 貞而不諂則吉亨 (*zhēn ér bù chǎn zé jí hēng*, "holding firm and not
fawning, it is auspicious and gets through").

**Line 2** 包承，小人吉，大人否。亨 (*bāo chéng, xiǎo rén jí, dà rén pǐ. hēng*, "wrapped, carried
along: for small people, auspicious; for the great person, blocked. It gets through"). 承 (*chéng*)
is 說文's 奉也 (*fèng yě*, "to hold up to"). Going along with those above works for small people.
The great person refuses it, and that refusal is what gets through — 王弼: 大人否之其道乃亨 (*dà rén
pǐ zhī qí dào nǎi hēng*, "the great person blocks it, and their road then gets through").

**Line 3** 包羞 (*bāo xiū*, "wrapped: shame"). The last of the earth's lines, at the seam, has gone
along as far as going along goes. 說文 gives 羞 (*xiū*) as 進獻也 (*jìn xiàn yě*, "to present food as
an offering"); with 亨's old kinship to 享 (*xiǎng*, "offer"), lines 1–3 might once have been read as
offerings. The 小象 (*xiǎo xiàng*) reads it as a position not fitting.

**Line 4** 有命，无咎，疇離祉 (*yǒu mìng, wú jiù, chóu lí zhǐ*, "there is an allotted course — no
blame; those of its kind share the blessing"). The sky's first line receives a charge, and the time
begins to turn. 說文 makes 疇 (*chóu*) 耕治之田也 (*gēng zhì zhī tián yě*, "tilled fields"); 王弼 reads
it as the line's own kind.

**Line 5** 休否，大人吉。其亡其亡，繫于苞桑 (*xiū pǐ, dà rén jí. qí wáng qí wáng, xì yú bāo sāng*, "the
blockage rests; for the great person, auspicious. 'It may be lost, it may be lost' — tie it to the
mulberry clump"). The blockage leans back like a person against a tree, and the line's safety is a
tree. The one who says "it may be lost" is the one who ties it down; 王弼: 心存將危乃得固也 (*xīn cún
jiāng wēi nǎi dé gù yě*, "the heart keeps danger in view, and so holds fast").

**Line 6** 傾否，先否後喜 (*qīng pǐ, xiān pǐ hòu xǐ*, "topple the blockage: first blocked, then
glad"). 傾 (*qīng*) is 說文's 仄也 (*zè yě*, "tilted"). The sky, risen as far as it can go, tips over.
The 小象: 否終則傾 (*pǐ zhōng zé qīng*, "a blockage at its end topples").

### In a life now

This is the figure for a shut season: a workplace run by the wrong people, a political time when
honest work is not rewarded, a family where no one is speaking, a long depression in which nothing
goes in or out. The text does not ask for a fight. It asks: stay rooted with your own kind (line
1); do not buy comfort by going along (line 2); know when going along has become shame (line 3);
take up the charge when it comes (line 4); when the blockage eases, keep saying "it may be lost" and
tie it down (line 5). And trust the shape of the time: a blockage at its height tips.

The 大象 makes it a practice: 君子以儉德辟難，不可榮以祿 (*jūnzǐ yǐ jiǎn dé bì nán, bù kě róng yǐ lù*,
"the noble one keeps their integrity frugal to avoid trouble, and cannot be honoured with a
salary"). Live lean, and do not take the wage that would bind you to the blockage.

### An echo from the Laozi

**The echo is in a Wing, not the core text.** 儉 (*jiǎn*, "frugal") is in 12's 大象, and the Laozi's
chapter 67 has it among its treasures: *"The second is frugality ... Frugality, and so you can
reach far."* Same graph, same English, and in both books frugality is how one outlasts what one
cannot overcome.

They part on time. The Laozi keeps frugality always, as a treasure; the 大象 makes it the stance of a
shut season, paired with refusing office — a way of keeping integrity intact until the sky tips.
And the core text's own counsel is harder than either: the one image of safety it gives is a
person tying what they hold to a rooted tree, saying "it may be lost".
